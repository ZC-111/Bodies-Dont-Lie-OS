## Section C — Fasciolopsis & Praziquantel (6 questions)

### Q9. Where does Fasciolopsis buski preferentially live, and why does that matter for digestion?
**Answer:** Near the **duodenum / ampulla of Vater**, impairing alkaline bile delivery. Pancreatic enzymes then fail to engage → carb fermentation, protein putrefaction, fat rancidity → gas, bloating, food sensitivity, leaky-gut chemistry.

### Q10. What is Gerber’s standard adult praziquantel dosing for fasciolopsis?
- A) 5 mg/kg once
- B) **25 mg/kg in three divided doses over one day** ✓
- C) 100 mg BID for 30 days
- D) 1 g nightly for 7 days

### Q11. A 72-year-old woman had 25 years of IBS with 7 years of daily vomiting and negative tertiary-center workups. What happened after one day of praziquantel?
**Answer:** **Complete resolution the next day** — nausea/vomiting stopped, no recurrence, ER visits ceased. She had been labeled psychiatric and given benzos/antidepressants.

### Q12. A 36-year-old bow hunter with bilirubin 11.6 and negative hepatitis/autoimmune workup — what exposure clue mattered, and what was the outcome?
**Answer:** Rare **wild boar/venison** plus heavy animal exposure. One-day praziquantel → bilirubin ~**1 within 10 days**, jaundice resolved, planned pancreatic biopsy canceled.

### Q13. Why does Gerber often follow praziquantel with a liver–gallbladder flush?
**Answer:** First-day die-off can leave patients tired/spacey/nauseated; a short flush (magnesium citrate + olive oil/grapefruit) helps clear debris. Advise **not driving** until clear.

### Q14. Compared with Hulda Clark’s herbal fluke protocol, what is Gerber’s main practical argument for praziquantel?
**Answer:** Herbs (black walnut, cloves, wormwood) need **~3 months TID** dosing; praziquantel often works in **1–2 days** with far better compliance — though he still loves herbs and always EAV-tests readiness first.
