## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Black Cohosh Myth-Bust (12:28–19:29)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario (Speaker 4)

### ★ Core Thesis
> Black cohosh is **not a phytoestrogen** that binds estrogen receptors. It modulates neurotransmitter pathways (dopamine, serotonin, GABA, opioid) controlling thermoregulation — and may be **protective**, not contraindicated, in ER+ breast cancer survivors.

### Evidence Cited
| Study / Finding | Result |
|---|---|
| Tamoxifen + black cohosh, 136 ER+ women, 12 months | ~50% completely free of hot flashes vs controls; zero serious AEs |
| Hormonal suppression trial, 85 women, 12 weeks | Symptom improvement; **no change** in estradiol, LH, FSH, or uterine lining |
| Case-control (~2,500 women) | **~50% lower** breast cancer risk among black cohosh users |
| Survivor recurrence data | Recurrence delayed **2 years → 6.5 years** in black cohosh group |
| Preclinical | Antiproliferative effects on breast cancer cells |

### Practical Protocol
- Dose: **20–40 mg** standard extract daily
- Safe with tamoxifen; loop in oncologist
- Routine monitoring still appropriate

### Related Hot-Flash Options Mentioned
- **Veozah (fezolinetant)** — non-hormonal VMS drug (drug rep visit noted)
- Relizen / Bonafide (Swedish bee pollen)
- Myo-chiro-inositol (perimenopause; may regularize cycles if ovarian function remains)
- Combos historically used: chasteberry, black walnut seed, Swedish bee pollen with black cohosh

## Segment 4 — Fasciolopsis buski & Landmark Cases (41:39–55:01)

**Speaker:** Michael Gerber

### ★ Core Thesis
> **Fasciolopsis buski** (giant intestinal fluke) is commonly missed, frequently drives “IBS,” and often resolves dramatically after **one day of praziquantel**.

### Organism Facts
- Up to **4–6 cm**; found in **51 countries**; endemic SE Asia; now seen in US practice
- Sources: undercooked sea vegetables, fish, pork, rare meats
- Lives near ampulla of Vater / duodenum → impairs alkaline bile flow → pancreatic enzymes fail → carb fermentation, protein putrefaction, fat rancidity → gas, bloating, food sensitivities, leaky gut
- Distended bowel under sternum may press vagus → tachycardia, SOB, anxiety (sympathetic overlay)

### Hulda Clark Link
- *The Cure for All Cancers* (1991): central tenet — kill the fluke; ~100 solved cancer cases described; metal-free dentistry emphasized

### Case 1 — 72yo Woman, 25 Years “IBS”
- Daily vomiting × 7 years; workups at UC Davis, UCSF, Stanford, Mayo — no cause; labeled psychiatric (benzos/antidepressants)
- **One-day praziquantel** (TID, 4–6h apart with food) → complete resolution next day; no recurrence; ER visits stopped

### Case 2 — 36yo Male, Bilirubin 11.6
- Hep A/B/C and autoimmune hepatitis negative; no biliary obstruction; pancreatic biopsy for cancer being considered
- Clue: avid bow hunter; rare wild boar/venison; large animal exposure (59 dogs + farm animals)
- One-day praziquantel → bilirubin **normalized to ~1 within 10 days**; jaundice resolved; biopsy canceled

### Treatment Protocol — Fasciolopsis
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Drug | **Praziquantel** (brand Biltricide) |
| Dose | **25 mg/kg** in 3 divided doses (one day) |
| Aftercare | Often a short **liver–gallbladder flush** next |
| Side effects | Don’t drive day-of; nausea, dizziness, drowsiness, fatigue, diarrhea; occasional PTSD-like reaction |
| Natural alternative | Hulda Clark: black walnut, cloves, wormwood — TID × **3 months** (compliance harder) |
| Pediatrics | Used in toddlers who vomit/won’t eat — often via solution in honey/applesauce after EAV clearance |
| Prep | Some patients need thyroid/adrenal/nutrient support before EAV “clears” for praziquantel |

### Diagnostic Yield Claim
- Identifies fasciolopsis in roughly **1 of every 2–3 IBS patients** in Gerber’s practice
- Standard GI labs/endoscopy routinely miss it
- ~40–50% of gallbladder symptom patients still symptomatic after cholecystectomy (cited research)

## Segment 5 — Other Flukes, Babesia, Scabies, Worms, Giardia (55:01–1:08:13)

**Speaker:** Michael Gerber

### Paragonimus (Lung Fluke)
- Linked to venison / sea vegetables history
- Chronic asthma, COPD, granulomatous lesions, possible pulmonary hypertension turnarounds
- Praziquantel **2–3 days** (Gerber usually tries 2); recheck EAV at ~2 weeks

### Echinococcus (Nasal/Sinus Fluke Framing)
- Chronic refractory sinusitis after ENT surgery, mold Rx, antibiotics, allergy Rx fail
- One-day praziquantel protocol

### Babesia (Often Worse Than Lyme)
- Fatigue, malaise, fever, sweats, myalgia, SOB; any insect bite (not only ticks)
- **Azithromycin 250 mg BID × 10 days** + **atovaquone** 750 mg/5 mL BID × 10 days with fatty meal (olive oil/avocado; not always dairy)
- Compounding/pharmacy access issues for atovaquone noted

### Schistosoma mansoni
- Freshwater / lake exposure (even “pristine” lakes cited via Clark); urinary tract preference
- UTI, hematuria, stool findings, infertility concern, hives, fevers, myalgia, dry cough
- Dark-field microscopy / blood tests; high index of suspicion

### Scabies
- Intense itching (groin, axilla, wrists, waist); often missed by dermatology as “dermatitis/allergy”
- Triggers: cleaning grandmother’s house, old furniture/carpets
- **Permethrin 5%** head-to-toe 10–12 hours; weekly (bad cases twice weekly)
- **Ivermectin** oral: women ~12 mg, men ~15 mg, often twice weekly; cream option ± B vitamins
- Spinosad: mixed results
- **Clean Green enzyme spray** — digests mite calcium carapace; laundry additive (¼ cup concentrate)

### Roundworms / Tapeworms / Pinworms / Whipworms
- OTC pyrantel (Pin-X); **albendazole 200 mg BID × 10 days** (some pinworm protocols shorter + repeat in 1 week)
- Mebendazole: “Cadillac” but costly ($1,500–$2,000) and harder to source in US
- Can underlie ulcerative colitis / Crohn’s presentations; barefoot + dog feces → pinworms

### Giardia
- Fatty, floaty, frothy stools; travel / contaminated water
- Azithromycin, Bactrim, or Flagyl — EAV to choose which antibiotic balances best

## Segment 6 — Lyme, H. pylori, EBV, Fungi/Mycotoxins (1:08:13–1:20:41)

**Speaker:** Michael Gerber

### Lyme (Borrelia) — “Great Imitator”
- Possible man-made origin speculation (Connecticut River / Plum Island era) noted as historical context
- Tick, spider, deer fly, mosquito possible vectors
- ELISA/Western blot 5-band criteria often inadequate; false neg/pos common
- Protocol: **azithromycin 250 mg daily × 30 days** + **Cowden protocol**
  - Uncaria tomentosa (cat’s claw / Samento), Cumanda (*Campsiandra angustifolia*), Desmodium (Burbur) drainage
  - Start 1 drop in 3–4 oz water 2–3×/day; ↑ 1 drop/day to 15 drops; 12 days on / 2 off
  - Herxheimer: drop dose + Desmodium ½ dropper → symptoms often ease in 30–40 minutes
- Typical course ~1 month; may repeat

### H. pylori
- Breath/blood tests imperfect; linked to gastritis, ulcers, autoimmunity, gastric cancer, possibly rosacea
- Clinic protocol (~1 month BID): **mastic gum + berberine HCl + bismuth citrate + zinc carnosine** (Pylori-Cil / Ortho Molecular)

### EBV & Viral Load
- Often co-travel with adenovirus, coronavirus, Coxsackie, CMV, Echo, enterovirus, HSV 1/2, VZV
- Treatments: IV H₂O₂, IV ozone, IV vitamin C 25–50 g
- Homeopathic multi-virus combo (6C–200C frequencies, no live virus) — **0.1 cc SQ at SP-6** daily alternating sides until improved (~2–4 days energy shift)

### Fungi & Mycotoxins
- Andrew Campbell frame: “mold is the gun; mycotoxins are the bullets”; prefers **azoles** over binders long-term
- Targets: Aspergillus, Candida, *Penicillium*, *Stachybotrys*, *Coccidioides* (valley fever — Central Valley, Phoenix, also Reno)
- Sweet-tooth homeopathic: Argentum nitricum 6C/12C/30C
- **Itraconazole 100 mg BID** preferred (often 2–6 months); fluconazole if itraconazole doesn’t balance (fluorine concern)
