## Segment 2 — Mindset from Ultramarathon: Libyan Desert → Badwater (19:45–35:00)

**Speaker:** Lisa Tamati

### Learning Objectives Stated
1. Mindset / mental toughness transferable from extreme sport → life & rehab
2. Case study: mother’s aneurysm/stroke recovery
3. Brief hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) context (clinic owner)

### Libyan Desert Expedition (~1997)
- Illegal 4-person unsupported crossing; ~250 km; ~35 kg packs; ~2 L water/day; 40°C+ heat
- Abusive relationship ended mid-expedition → **learned cognitive compartmentalization** under life threat

- Turning point: chose structured ultramarathon racing over unsupported extremes

### Moroccan Sahara Ultra
- First ultra (~240 km); full race support vs. desert expedition
- Hooked by camaraderie / shared mission → 25-year career

### Death Valley / Badwater (~217 km)
- Took ~13 years to qualify; start below sea level → Mount Whitney (~2,500 m) + intermediate passes
- Narrated documentary clip: heat risk, multi-day effort, climb after multiple marathon-equivalents
- **Pearl:** finishing reframes perceived capability; success rests on **crew/team**, not solo heroics
- Personal vindication after years of being told she was “useless”; prior asthma + L1/back injury with “never run again” prognosis — disproven

### Clinical Pearl
> Extreme adversity training builds transferable skills: compartmentalize crisis emotion, keep moving one step at a time, and lean on a dedicated team.

## Segment 3 — Himalayas Race: Hypoxia, Injury, Finish (35:00–55:00)

**Speaker:** Lisa Tamati

### Race Profile
- ~222 km; altitude ~3,600 → ~5,800 m (~18,000 ft) **twice**; ~⅓ sea-level O₂ at high camps
- Prior year: only 1 finisher (Mark); first-year runners hospitalized; Lisa motivated by “no woman has finished”
- Anemia (heavy menses) + asthma made altitude adaptation harder

### Hypoxic Tent Misadventure
- Sponsored altitude tent; ignored titration → slept at equivalent of ~6,500 m nightly
- SpO₂ ~**70%** overnight → hypoxic brain injury / “hypoxic concussion,” infections (orificial bacterial proliferation), memory gaps
- Sports physician: exit tent, pull race — she refused due to sponsors/film crew; recovered partially then reinjured

### Ankle Ligament Rupture (~10 weeks pre-race)
- Post-100k Blue Mountains race + North Face photo shoot → bilateral ligament rupture, left ankle
- Docs: ~7 weeks no running — she pivoted to swimming, weights, mindset work (“what *can* I do?”)

### Race Day Reality
- 7 starters (5 women); military tried to stop race (tourism/liability); US doctors lectured on HAPE/HACE
- Strategy: crew as **gatekeepers** against fear-mongering; start-number ritual as “armor”
- Course crises: asthma attack (brief medical hold), heatstroke/pass-out in valley (~40°C), hypothermia + diesel-fume panic on second climb, snowstorm collapse
- Crew first gave “permission to fail,” then one member **refused failure** — shook her, demanded another step → summit Tanglang La; finished **53h 5m** (2nd woman; beaten by 55yo asthmatic English grandmother)

### Clinical / Coaching Pearls
- Aggressive hypoxic pre-acclimatization can cause brain injury — titrate, monitor SpO₂
- Team can either soft-exit or hard-push; know which the athlete needs in the moment
- Age, sex, ethnicity, asthma ≠ absolute barriers when motivation + support are extreme

## Segment 4 — Case Study: Isabelle — Aneurysm, Stroke, Advocacy (55:00–1:13:00)

**Speaker:** Lisa Tamati | **Patient:** Mother Isabelle (age 74 at event)

### Acute Course — System Failures
1. Collapse at home; paramedic suspected neuro event
2. ED labeled **migraine**; left on analgesics **~4–6 hours** despite “worst pain of life” (SAH-type presentation)
3. Friend-paramedic forced **CT** → blood throughout brain; survival doubted
4. Regional hospital → capital (Wellington): **+12 h** for air ambulance (age-related deprioritization) → **~18 h** to surgery (vs. “golden hour” ideal)
5. Coil/stent; peri-op stroke → right hemiplegia; days of coma; inflammatory cascade; massive brain damage; “no quality of life” prognosis

### Family Response
- Lisa to father (preparing funeral): “She’s still breathing — I will bring her back” (mirrors Himalayan crew intervention)
- 24h bedside vigil despite staff resistance
- Day 3: full coma → ICU (more capability than ward) — another advocacy lesson

### Sleep Apnea Discovery (Critical)
- Noticed better status on O₂ in Wellington vs. deterioration in rehab town
- Recalled own hypoxic injury pattern (bacterial proliferation, etc.)
- Requested sleep study → refused → **smuggled sleep consultant** overnight
- Result: **severe OSA**, SpO₂ to **~70%**, Cheyne-Stokes, hundreds of apneas/night → **CPAP** initiated — likely life-saving

### Home vs Institution Fight
- Hospital pushed 24/7 institutional care; social worker predicted failure in 2 weeks
- Lisa fought to keep mother in home budget + loving environment
- Day of discharge: commercial dive company HBOT chamber (forklift entry); legal waiver

### Hyperbaric + Relentless Rehab
- Response after **~33 HBOT sessions**: words returning, right-hand flicker, “intelligence” returning
- Lost chamber access → **mortgaged house**, bought home mild HBOT unit; family transfers
- Studied functional neurology, vestibular rehab, keto, supplements — stayed “one step ahead”
- Rejected “neural fatigue = only minutes of therapy” dogma → rotate modalities when brain tires (passive ROM, talk, etc.)
- Ultramarathon skill: **grind repetition** (teach same motor/cognitive task 1000×/day)
- Timeline: ~**13 months** out of wheelchair (few steps); ~**2.5 years** to driver’s license, full POA, gym 5×/week, social life
- Book: *Relentless* — not 100% reserve capacity (infections still “take brain offline”) but high QoL at ~76

### Clinical Pearls
> Never accept migraine for thunderclap/worst-pain neuro presentations without imaging.  
> Post-SAH/stroke: screen aggressively for sleep apnea — nocturnal hypoxia sabotages recovery.  
> Home environment + family intensity can outperform institutional minimal rehab when resources allow.  
> HBOT (Paul Harch–informed) may contribute to late neuro recovery even after grim prognosis.

## Segment 5 — CNS Lymphoma & Metabolic Oncology Stack (embedded in case → ~1:13)

**Speaker:** Lisa Tamati

### Diagnosis Path
- Age ~80: facial droop → ED ×2 sent home after CT “fine”
- Fought **3 weeks** for MRI → grapefruit-sized left tumor + frontal lesions
- Private neurosurgery resected; pathology: aggressive **CNS lymphoma**
- Public oncology: chemo would kill her; “weeks to live”

### Protocol (self-assembled)
- Advanced testing: **RGCC** (chemo/immuno/supplement sensitivity)
- Private oncologist open to RGCC → temozolomide + rituximab obtained (full-dose only; no IPT available in NZ)
- Stack: doubled HBOT, IV vitamin C, peptides (esp. **thymosin α1** for immune support), strict keto, ~**28 supplements**, 15-doctor team
- Sold house to fund care
- **Tumors gone on MRI in ~12 weeks**; cancer-free ~3 years at time of talk (cautious language — never say “cured”)
- Book/interview series: *What Your Oncologist Isn’t Telling You* (21 metabolic-cancer experts); now coaches families

### Subsequent Near-Death Events
- 3 GI bleeds (one with Hb reported ~49; surgery averted when labs misread); E. coli; hip fracture Feb (same year) → walking independently again at ~6 months rehab

### Message
- Do not write off elderly patients who still want to fight
- Empower families as advocates; hope ≠ false hope when options remain
