# Quiz — Uveal Melanoma: Molecular Drivers, Therapy Gaps & Translational Targets

**Source:** Clearfield Webinar 2025-02-05 — Evangelica Papasterio lecture + clinical panel  
**Level:** Integrative/functional medicine practitioner  
**Format:** 20 questions (multiple choice + short answer)

## Section A — Disease Foundations (8 questions)

### Q1. What fraction of all melanomas is uveal melanoma (UM)?
- A) <1%
- B) **3–5%** ✓
- C) 15–20%
- D) ~50%

> *Most common primary intraocular malignancy in adults, but rare among melanomas overall. Incidence cited: 5–7 per million/year.*

### Q2. Which anatomic structures compose the uveal tract?
**Answer:** Iris, ciliary body, and choroid (intermediate vascular/pigmented layer between sclera and retina).

### Q3. Approximately what percentage of primary UM arises in the choroid?
- A) 50%
- B) 70%
- C) **~90%** ✓
- D) Nearly 100%

### Q4. Why does rising UM incidence from southern → northern latitude argue against UV as the main driver?
**Answer:** If UV were causal like cutaneous melanoma, rates should fall (not rise) at higher latitudes with less intense UV. Presenter used this geography pattern plus limited UV-signature data (mainly iris subset) to separate UM from cutaneous disease.

### Q5. After primary UM, roughly what fraction of patients develop metastasis, and where does it preferentially go?
- A) 10%; lung
- B) 25%; brain
- C) **~50%; liver** ✓
- D) ~80%; bone

> *Once metastatic, presenter cited 80–90% mortality within about a year — rapid lethal course.*

### Q6. Which initiating mutations dominate UM and are typically mutually exclusive?
- A) BRAF and NRAS
- B) **GNAQ and GNA11** ✓
- C) EGFR and KRAS
- D) BRCA1 and BRCA2

> *Cutaneous melanoma’s BRAF/NRAS drivers are rare/absent in UM — a core reason therapies don’t cross over.*

### Q7. Which alteration pair most clearly marks high metastatic risk in the stratification framework presented?
- A) Disomy 3 + EIF1AX
- B) **Monosomy 3 + BAP1 loss** ✓
- C) SF3B1 alone with PRAME-low
- D) CYSLTR2 + PLCB4 co-mutation

### Q8. What commercial prognostic assay was recommended for US clinicians, and what does it use?
**Answer:** **DecisionDx-UM** (Castle Biosciences) — a **15-gene expression signature** stratifying metastatic risk (with optional PRAME / related markers).

## Section B — Therapy & Translational Science (6 questions)

### Q9. What is tebentafusp (Kimmtrak), and what limits its reach?
- A) Oral kinase inhibitor for all UM genotypes
- B) **Bispecific fusion protein linking gp100+ UM cells to CD3+ T cells; requires a specific HLA allele (~9% eligible/responsive as cited)** ✓
- C) Anti–PD-1 monotherapy with >50% response
- D) Proton-beam radiosensitizer for primary choroidal tumors only

### Q10. True or False: MAPK/PI3K pathway inhibitors that work in UM cell culture generally translate into strong clinical benefit.
**Answer:** **False.** Presenter emphasized in-vitro efficacy that fails to reflect patient reality; metastatic UM still lacks a standard therapy for most patients.

### Q11. In the EMBO Molecular Medicine 2023 lab story, loss of which tumor-suppressor kinase axis drives metastatic UM proliferation?
**Answer:** **LKB1–SIK2** loss → proliferative advantage (validated by CRISPR hits, rescue with WT but not kinase-dead LKB1, and larger xenografts).

### Q12. What downstream transporter/channel-related gene rose after LKB1 loss and sits in the calcium-metabolism hub?
- A) SERCA2
- B) **SLC8A1** ✓
- C) TRPV1
- D) CFTR

### Q13. Which preclinical combination shrank LKB1-knockout xenografts better than either agent alone?
**Answer:** **SLC8A1 inhibition (e.g. KB-R7943) + mitochondrial antioxidant MitoQ** — combination pushed cells toward apoptosis and reduced tumor size vs single agents.

### Q14. Presenter’s unpublished PhD functional readouts included approximately:
- A) 20% proliferation drop only
- B) **~70–80% lower proliferation and ~60% lower Transwell migration** ✓
- C) Complete in-vivo cure in mice
- D) Phase 2 radiographic responses in humans

> *Still preclinical mechanism work — not clinical trial results.*

## Section C — Clinic Bridging & Biotech (6 questions)

### Q15. Why did panelists caution against managing UM solely by RGCC circulating tumor cell (CTC) counts?
**Answer:** CTCs are imperfect diagnostics; **clinical presentation should trump CTC number**. Melanoma also lacks highly specific classic serum markers (CEA can reflect nonspecific inflammation).

### Q16. Name two globe-preserving primary UM treatment categories discussed.
**Answer:** **Radiotherapy** (plaque brachytherapy; charged-particle / proton beam) and **local resection** — with a modern preference to avoid enucleation when possible.

### Q17. Orphan biotech solutions highlighted for rare cancers include all EXCEPT:
- A) FDA/EMA orphan designation incentives
- B) Rare-disease consortia and adaptive trials
- C) Mission-driven investors and incubators
- D) **Relying only on large commercial markets without academic collaboration** ✓

### Q18. What ethical challenge did the Roca Therapeutics co-founder highlight as especially hard?
**Answer:** Being contacted by patients/families at therapeutic dead ends asking when a treatment will be available — needing to stay positive while clarifying she is not their treating physician.

### Q19. Integrative panel ideas that remain investigational / not established UM SOC in this talk include:
**Answer (any two):** nanoparticle-loaded tebentafusp; ICG + methylene blue photodynamic therapy; nifedipine/verapamil repurposing for “calcium addiction”; melatonin for micromets; vitamin D protocols extrapolated from cutaneous melanoma talks.

### Q20. Collectively, orphan/rare diseases were framed as affecting roughly what share of the world population?
- A) <0.1%
- B) **~3.5–7% (≈300–500 million people)** ✓
- C) 25%
- D) Majority of adults over 65
