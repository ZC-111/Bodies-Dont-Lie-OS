## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Rosemary as Cognitive Enhancer (06:43–13:04)

**Speaker:** Dr. Melvin Nario (Speaker 2) — herbal module, Andrew Weil Integrative Medicine fellowship

### Botanical Snapshot
- **Rosmarinus officinalis** (mint family) — traditional Western herbal memory tonic
- Key constituents: volatile oils (**1,8-cineole**, **camphor**), **rosmarinic acid**
- Mechanism: natural **acetylcholinesterase inhibition** (same class of action as Donepezil); cineole stimulates CNS / shortens reaction times (brainwave measures)

### Evidence Highlights (as presented)
- Double-blind trial in ~20 adults (mean age 75): **750 mg** dried rosemary powder improved cognitive performance vs placebo; **6,000 mg** impaired performance → **more is not better**
- Japanese study: ~1 g/day → better mental function, energy, sleep quality, less daytime sleepiness
- Night-shift nurses: one drop rosemary EO on mask → less sleepiness, more alertness vs controls

### Practical Dosing
| Form | Typical use |
|---|---|
| Culinary / powder | ~750 mg (~½ tsp dried) — therapeutic culinary dose |
| Tea | ½–1 tsp dried herb per cup, up to 3 cups/day |
| Tincture | 2–4 mL three times daily |
| Topical / aroma | Effective; limit to ≤3×/day |

### Safety
- **Never ingest isolated essential oils** → gastric irritation, kidney injury, seizures
- May inhibit platelet aggregation → caution with anticoagulants
- Long-term high-dose effects unknown

### Clinical Pearl
> Effective cognitive support is often sitting in the spice cabinet — culinary doses beat megadoses.

## Segment 3 — Beyond Pharmacogenomics: Root-Cause Genomics for Depression & Anxiety (13:04–50:35)

**Speaker:** Sharon Houseman Cohen, MD — family + integrative medicine; Intellx DNA

### ★ Core Thesis
> Pharmacogenomics helps **dose and metabolism**; it does **not** reveal why the patient is depressed or anxious. Root-cause / clinical genomics maps etiology (inflammation, cortisol, nutrients, neurotransmitters, detox) and enables genomically targeted interventions.

### 3A. Why Pharmacogenomics Alone Falls Short

| Study context | Remission with PGx | Usual care |
|---|---|---|
| 2013 add-on PGx trial (~10 weeks) | ~20% | ~9% |
| 2022 treatment-resistant, more detailed PGx (~8 weeks) | ~16% | ~9% |

- Even with PGx, **~80% do not achieve remission** — missing root causes
- PGx assumes neurotransmitter imbalance is the primary driver; integrative care requires oxidative stress, cortisol/adrenaline, nutrient cofactors (Zn, C, Mg, B6), inflammation, gut, toxins, growth factors, etc.

### 3B. Clinical Genomics vs Pharmacogenomics
- **Pharmacogenomics:** gene–drug interactions, metabolizer status (e.g., rapid fluoxetine metabolizer → higher dose)
- **Clinical / root-cause genomics:** SNPs with documented odds ratios for depression/anxiety → map etiology → diet / lifestyle / supplements / meds matched to the map
- Tool must do both: **identify contributing variants** and **offer genomically targeted solutions** (avoid endless trial-and-error stacks)

### 3C. Top Genes Covered (of Houseman Cohen’s “top 10”)

#### 1) HTR1A — “Faulty serotonin thermostat”
- Presynaptic **auto-receptor**; overactive variant → brain “thinks” serotonin is high → **stops synthesis**
- Highest odds ratios for depression **and** anxiety; **2 copies** in ~**17%** of population; ~**2.8×** risk (lecture claim)
- Phenotype: more fear, bipolar signal, suicide risk, severe depression, **slower/weaker SSRI response**
- Blunted cortisol response to acute stress → apathy, difficulty initiating
- **Pearl:** SSRIs often “take 8–10 weeks” because many patients lack serotonin to recycle — rebuild first
- **Interventions:** saffron, 5-HTP, SAMe (selected), vitamin D, *L. helveticus* + *B. longum*, 10,000-lux light; **SNRIs often outperform SSRIs** for this genotype

**Case — Candy (64):** severe depression/SAD on sertraline 150 mg; 2× HTR1A + 2× MTHFR + low Zn/C recycling + gut histamine SNPs. Plan: methylfolate, L-glutamine + DAO / low-histamine awareness, vitamin C + zinc + 5-HTP, Mood Support (probiotics + saffron), taper sertraline → 50 mg. Outcome: mood, Christmas enjoyment, gut improved.

#### 2) IL1B — Systemic → brain inflammation
- High / moderate producers (~8% very high; ~15% moderate) → “brain on fire”
- Linked to depression, bipolar, autism (~**3×** with one copy — lecture), post-traumatic epilepsy, TBI swelling, cognitive decline; especially relevant in **seniors**
- Pathway: IL-1β → **NLRP3 inflammasomes** → inflammatory signal to brain; amyloid, Lyme, mold, viruses/bacteria/fungi also trigger inflammasomes
- Antidepressants generally **do not** address brain inflammation → failure to remit
- **Interventions:** resolvins, omega-3s, sulforaphane, resveratrol, apigenin, luteolin, quercetin — match agent to phenotype (e.g., sulforaphane for autism/ADHD/schizophrenia/pain; apigenin for anxiety/sleep)

#### 3) GPR50 — “Not a good pairing”: high cortisol + low melatonin
- Overactivates HPA; lowers melatonin signaling; **X chromosome** → stronger effects in women (~**1.77×** depression risk — lecture)
- Lowers **β-arrestin** → cannot “arrest” fear/anxiety behaviors (steroid-like panic without exogenous steroids)
- Hogging melatonin receptor → poor sleep
- **Ashwagandha** 300–500 mg BID × 6 weeks: perceived stress ↓44% vs 5% placebo; GHQ depression/anxiety subscale ↓72%; cortisol ↓~30%; p < 0.0001 (lecture citation)
- **Relora** (Magnolia + Phellodendron): athletes — depression ↓20%, stress ↓11%, fatigue ↓31%, vigor ↑18%

#### 4) ADORA2A — “Adore your caffeine; it doesn’t adore you”
- Low-functioning adenosine A2A receptor; **2 copies ~16%**; ~50% ↑ panic/agoraphobia risk
- Caffeine blocks A2A → worsens anxiety; ~**100–150 mg** can trigger in carriers; Grande blonde roast ~360 mg; decaf up to ~50 mg
- Also: sleep disturbance (~40% higher risk), concentration issues, seizures/tics risk; ↑ glutamate / ↓ GABA / ↑ dopamine / ↓ BDNF
- **Interventions:** DHA, genistein, theanine (↑ GABA / ↓ glutamate), NAC (esp. nail-biting/OCD-type), shift to tea; fix B12/D if deficient

**Case — Anxious Alan (25):** catastrophizing, tachycardia, energy drinks, B12 + D deficiency; ADORA2A + ADRB2 + TNF-α + IL1B. Plan: nutrient repletion, DHA/genistein/theanine/NAC, caffeine education.

#### 5) GCH1 — “Little-known big sister to MTHFR”
- Rate-limiting for **BH4 (tetrahydrobiopterin)** → serotonin, NE, dopamine, **and** eNOS / nitric oxide
- **2 copies ~4%** of population; linked to treatment-resistant mental health, autism, PMDD, severe anxiety, behavioral crises (pending *Frontiers in Neuroscience* publication — lecture)
- **BH4 dosing (clinical, not PKU):** start ~**2.5 mg daily**; typical adult ~**5 mg BID** (occasionally 10 mg BID) — ~1/20 of PKU/Kuvan doses
- Single-copy supports: resveratrol (↑ transcription BH4/eNOS), sauna, vigorous exercise

**Case — Jasmine (13):** homebound anxiety since early school years; failed fluoxetine/counseling; 2× GCH1 + rare low-GABA SNPs + low Zn absorption. BH4 key → 100% anxiety-free, school attendance, track team, sleepovers.

### 3D. Closing Framework
- Map top 5–6 SNPs with meaningful ORs (anxiety/depression + inflammation/detox/nutrient/gut)
- Genomics useless without knowing **how to modify** each SNP → clinical decision support (Intellx DNA)
- Same principles apply to cognition (Bredesen research context), complex pediatrics (autism/PANS/PANDAS), environmental illness, longevity

## Segment 5 — Off-Camera Deep Dive: Intellx DNA Demo + Annie Case (56:36–1:08+)

**Speakers:** Houseman Cohen, Clearfield, Nario

### Nutrigenomics position
- Included, but often **least necessary** when blood nutrient levels are measurable
- Genomics most useful for **receptors/transporters** (e.g., **TCN2** — ~18% poor B12 transport into brain)

### Workflow tips
- Start with **hot spots** (variants in ~2–15% of population) → top ~20 SNPs matter more than browsing 600–800 markers
- Tool = **clinical decision support** for licensed clinicians (MD/DO/ND/NP/PA/trained chiro) — includes meds + referenced dosing; not sold to lay/health coaches

### Case — Annie (“adrenaline”)
- Hot spots: pesticide metabolism → anxiety with exposure; mast-cell pathways; **2× COMT** → catecholamines last ~4× longer → ~**2.4×** panic disorder risk
- **DIO2**: impaired brain T4→T3 conversion despite OK peripheral T3 → ~60% ↑ anxiety risk → low-dose **liothyronine** (e.g., 2× 5 mcg before breakfast)
- **BDNF** variant: poor proBDNF → mature BDNF conversion → SSRIs less effective; **moderate–intense exercise** prioritized
- Mast-cell gene specificity: different SNPs → different interventions (e.g., FCER1A → resveratrol)
- Platform builds anxiety action plan (ashwagandha, liothyronine, exercise, nutrients) into a clinician-facing report; also has cognition, cardiometabolic, anesthesia, macular degeneration, osteoporosis modules
