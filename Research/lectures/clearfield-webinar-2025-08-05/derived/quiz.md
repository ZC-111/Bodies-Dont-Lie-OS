## Section A — Framework & Pharmacogenomics Limits (5 questions)

### Q1. According to Houseman Cohen, what is the main limitation of pharmacogenomics in depression/anxiety care?
- A) It is illegal in most states
- B) **It clarifies drug metabolism but not the root etiology of symptoms** ✓
- C) It cannot identify CYP2D6 variants
- D) It always worsens SSRI side effects

> *PGx answers “how do I dose this drug?” — not “why is this patient depressed?”*

### Q2. In the 2013 trial adding pharmacogenomics to usual care, approximately what fraction of patients achieved remission by ~10 weeks with PGx?
- A) 80%
- B) 50%
- C) **20%** ✓
- D) 9%

> *Better than usual care (~9%), but ~80% still did not remit.*

### Q3. True or False: The 2022 treatment-resistant depression PGx trial showed that more detailed pharmacogenomics largely solved non-response.
**Answer:** **False.** Remission was still only ~16% at 8 weeks vs ~9% usual care.

### Q4. Which statement best distinguishes clinical/root-cause genomics from pharmacogenomics?
- A) Clinical genomics only looks at CYP genes
- B) **Clinical genomics maps etiology SNPs (inflammation, cortisol, nutrients, etc.) and suggests targeted interventions** ✓
- C) Pharmacogenomics includes BH4 dosing algorithms
- D) They are identical; branding differs

### Q5. Why does Houseman Cohen argue blood tests alone are often inadequate for mental-health root-cause workup?
**Answer:** Blood–brain barrier limits insight; organic acids/metabolites help somewhat, but genomics can reveal predispositions (inflammation, cortisol, neurotransmitter synthesis, transporters) that labs miss.

### Q8. Why might SSRIs appear to “take 8–10 weeks” in many patients with HTR1A-related low serotonin synthesis?
