## Segment 3 — Tip of the Week: Low-Dose Colchicine for CV Prevention (13:54–20:57)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario, DO (Andrew Weil / University of Arizona Integrative Medicine fellowship context)

### ★ Core Thesis
> Coronary disease is not only a cholesterol storage disorder — plaque progression/rupture involves neutrophils, macrophages, IL-1β, IL-6, and the **NLRP inflammasome**. Low-dose colchicine targets **residual inflammatory risk** after LDL optimization.

### Landmark trials cited
| Trial | Population | Dose | Outcome (as stated) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LoDoCo2** | >5,500 stable CAD | Colchicine 0.5 mg | **31%** relative ↓ MACE (MI, ischemic stroke, revascularization) |
| **COLCOT** | Post-MI (early start) | Low-dose colchicine | **23%** ↓ major CV events |

### Integrative framing
- Dual benefit: gout flare reduction + CVD secondary prevention
- Biomarkers: ↓ hsCRP, IL-6, IL-1β
- Observational signal: ~12–23% ↓ joint-replacement risk in OA/gout overlap cohorts (not definitive)
- Use case highlighted: patients with **high CAC scores (hundreds)** when next steps unclear

### Practical pearl — brand vs generic
- Branded 0.5 mg (LoDoCo/COLCOT dosing) often **$200–300/month** cash after patent branding
- Clinical workaround discussed: **generic 0.6 mg daily** (~few dollars/month)
- Duration framing: chronic adjunct “like aspirin” but different mechanism (inflammasome vs platelets)
- Historical note (Clearfield): old acute-gout q2h 0.6 mg regimens; price spike after branded monopoly era
- Carol add-on: colchicine as **zinc ionophore** (with ivermectin, HCQ, azithromycin, green tea) — FLCCC-era discussion
