## 4. 60-second video script

**Hook (0–5s):** “If your SIBO came back — you’re not alone. About half relapse by nine months.”  
**Beat 1 (5–20s):** Kill-only care fails because secretions, motility, and nutrients never get fixed.  
**Beat 2 (20–35s):** Breath tests can miss distal disease — symptoms still matter.  
**Beat 3 (35–50s):** Hill’s model: ~30-day antimicrobials (± *H. pylori*), then motility/HCl/bile, then ≥6 months nutrient rebuild.  
**CTA (50–60s):** “Ask: what’s my Phase 2 and Phase 3 — not just my next antibiotic.”  
**On-screen text cues:** `~½ relapse @ 9 mo` · `3 phases` · `FODMAP ≠ SIBO diet`

## 6. Patient takeaway card

**Title:** If SIBO keeps coming back  

**Plain-language bullets (5):**
1. Relapse is common — plan for root causes, not endless “kill” rounds.  
2. A negative breath test does not always rule SIBO out.  
3. After antimicrobials, ask about stomach acid, enzymes, bile, and motility.  
4. Nutrient rebuild often takes months (iron, B12, A/D/E/K, zinc…).  
5. Long strict FODMAP is usually an IBS tool — not forever SIBO care.

**Ask your clinician:** “What is my Phase 2 (secretions/motility) and Phase 3 (nutrients) plan?”  
**Avoid / myth bust:** “More antibiotics alone will permanently fix this.”  
**Disclaimer:** use block above.

## 7. Clinician pearl card

**Title:** SIBO relapse prevention — Hill three-phase  

**Pearls (5, denser):**
1. Counsel ~50% nine-month relapse risk at intake.  
2. Prefer clinical ± empiric therapy over breath-test vetoes (glucose ~54% / lactulose ~42%).  
3. Same ~30-day multi-botanical stack for SIBO ± *H. pylori* when both suspected.  
4. Phase 2: HCl/enzymes/bile + MMC (stop grazing); products named Hydrozyme / Beta Plus / Motilityzyme.  
5. Phase 3 nutrients ≥6 months; probiotics day 31 — strain-specific, not megadose blends.

**Monitor:** symptoms, B12/Fe/fat-soluble vitamins, AST/CRP as indicated; compliance before blaming “resistant bugs.”  
**Related pathway:** `protocol-pathway.md`

## 8. Do-not-say list

- Don’t promise a named kit cures all SIBO.  
- Don’t tell patients to ignore red-flag GI symptoms.  
- Don’t present FODMAP rejection as universal dogma without “speaker stance” framing.  
- Don’t publish exact DIY antimicrobial stacks as consumer instructions.  
- Don’t imply rifaximin is useless — Hill’s non-use is practice-scope/cost, not a universal ban.
