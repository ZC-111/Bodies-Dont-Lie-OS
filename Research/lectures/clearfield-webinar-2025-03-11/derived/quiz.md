## Section B — Diagnostics & Lab Interpretation (6 questions)

### Q7. Cited sensitivity for glucose vs lactulose breath testing was approximately:
- A) 90% / 85%
- B) **54% / 42%** ✓
- C) 20% / 10%
- D) 75% / 75%

### Q8. Why does Hill argue glucose breath testing can miss distal SIBO?
**Answer:** Glucose is absorbed in the proximal ~3–6 feet of small bowel, leaving ~13–16 feet unsampled; colonization can occur anywhere along ~19 feet of small intestine.

### Q9. Breath-test thresholds mentioned for methane and hydrogen elevations were:
- A) CH₄ > 5 ppm; H₂ > 10 ppm
- B) **CH₄ > 10 ppm; H₂ > 20 ppm** ✓
- C) CH₄ > 50 ppm; H₂ > 100 ppm
- D) Any detectable gas is diagnostic

### Q10. A symptomatic patient has no B12 deficiency. Which overgrowth pattern does Hill say to consider more strongly?
- A) Classic SIBO only
- B) **Intestinal methanogen overgrowth (IMO)** ✓
- C) Always H. pylori alone
- D) Celiac disease exclusively

> *B12 deficiency points more toward classic SIBO in his framing; methanogens often don’t drive B12 deficit the same way.*

### Q11. What is stool testing’s proper role in SIBO workup per Hill?
- A) Gold-standard SIBO diagnosis
- B) **Not diagnostic for SIBO, but useful for patterns (e.g., staph/strep → hypochlorhydria clues; fat/elastase/pH → digestive dynamics)** ✓
- C) Replaces clinical history
- D) Useless in all cases

### Q12. True or False: Small-bowel aspirate always outperforms breath testing clinically because it is invasive “gold standard.”
**Answer:** **False.** Hill notes major technique/handling limitations and proximal sampling bias (~1–2 feet), so invasive testing has as many practical problems as non-invasive methods.

## Section C — Mechanisms & Three-Phase Treatment (5 questions)

### Q13. Hill’s three-phase SIBO model is:
- A) Diet → probiotics → antibiotics only
- B) **Antimicrobials → motility/secretions → correct nutrient collateral damage** ✓
- C) Surgery → TPN → transplant
- D) FODMAP lifelong → fiber → rifaximin cycles

### Q14. Why does constant grazing worsen SIBO risk in this framework?
**Answer:** Frequent eating suppresses the **migrating motor complex (MMC / Phase III peristalsis)** that clears the small bowel between meals; Hill suggests leaving ~1–2 hours between feedings.

### Q15. Which probiotic bacterium does Hill lean on most for SIBO based on the collective literature he cites?
- A) Lactobacillus acidophilus high-dose blends
- B) **Lactobacillus plantarum (targeted, often lower dose)** ✓
- C) Bifidobacterium infantis only
- D) Any multi-strain 100+ billion CFU product

### Q16. Which statement about *Saccharomyces boulardii* matches the lecture?
- A) It permanently colonizes the ileum
- B) **It may outperform metronidazole in a cited comparison and clears the body in ~3–5 days** ✓
- C) It prevents all future SIBO when used lifelong
- D) It must never be used after antimicrobials

### Q17. How long does Hill generally recommend correcting nutrient deficiencies after SIBO, and why?
**Answer:** At least **~6 months**, partly so nutrients can rebuild in **lymphocytes** and patients don’t rebound into deficiency soon after stopping.
