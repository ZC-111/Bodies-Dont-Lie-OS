### Tip of the Week — Zinc : Copper Ratio (Dr. Nario / Andrew Weil fellowship material)
- **Biomarker:** Plasma **copper-to-zinc ratio** (standard labs; no specialty panel required)
- **Optimal Cu:Zn:** **0.7–1.0**
  - **> 1.0** → relative high Cu / low Zn; linked with inflammation, oxidative stress, elderly mortality risk, anxiety/depression, CVD
  - **< 0.7** → copper deficiency → anemia, immune dysfunction, connective tissue issues, hormonal imbalance
- Antagonistic minerals: excess of one suppresses the other (analogous to E2:P4 ratio thinking)
- **COVID context:** Zinc popular for antiviral support; high-dose zinc (>~40 mg) or chronic zinc induces intestinal **metallothionein** → binds copper → secondary Cu deficiency
- **Supplementation ratio:** ~**8–15 mg zinc per 1 mg copper**; separate doses by several hours for better absorption
- Clinical frame: nonspecific inflammatory window (like ESR/CRP), not a standalone diagnosis

## Segment 2 — Michael Hartford: TBI in Military / Veterans — Models of Care (18:12–53:00)

**Speaker:** Michael Hartford — special projects healthcare consultant, Avalon Action Alliance; former Chief, Marcus Institute for Brain Health (Aurora, CO); retired US Navy / multi-system VA–DoD executive

### ★ Core Thesis
> Invisible wounds (TBI) are often misread as primary mental illness → psychotropic meds that can worsen outcomes and fuel suicidal ideation. Treat the physiologic brain injury and comorbidities with **interdisciplinary** care, not symptom-only psychiatry.

### Objectives (stated)
1. Correlation: TBI ↔ mental health ↔ substance abuse ↔ suicide  
2. DoD / VA / private-sector interdisciplinary TBI care models  
3. Medical + moral imperatives for a paradigm shift

### Invisible Wound Clinical Picture
- Looks “fine,” walks/talks OK — yet: visual disturbance, memory issues, dizziness, pain, cognitive fatigue, communication trouble, balance problems
- PCP often routes to mental health → PTSD label → meds that may be **counterproductive** when TBI is undiagnosed
- Expanded injury mechanisms beyond classic blast: heavy artillery training, blast overpressure, repetitive trauma, carrier landings, parachute jumping, **sub-concussive** accumulation (LOC not required)

### National Intrepid Center of Excellence (NICoE)
- Fisher Foundation gifted DoD **$70M** (~2007–08) for invisible wounds → NICoE at Bethesda
- Founding director: **Jim Kelly, MD** (behavioral neurologist; Chicago Blackhawks / NCAA concussion work)
- **30-day interdisciplinary program** (not full inpatient); ~1,200 service members in first 3–4 years
- **>90%** excellent satisfaction; most able to **“stay in the fight”** (not medically discharged as NPQ)

### Veteran-Centric Networks
- **Avalon Action Alliance** network (replicates NICoE model); Marcus Institute for Brain Health first site (Bernie Marcus / Home Depot philanthropy ~$38M start; ~$100M with partners incl. Gary Sinise Foundation / Arthur Blank)
- Focus: **TBI as primary** + comorbid MH / substance / SI — vs. Warrior Care Network sites that skew more mental-health primary (UCLA, Emory, Rush, Mass General–affiliated)

### Suicide Epidemiology (cited Journal of Neurology ~2022–23)
- Veterans **with TBI**: suicide rate rose over ~15 years from **<10/100,000 → >90/100,000**
- Broader veteran suicide burden remains epidemic despite large VA/DoD prevention spend concentrated on mental health
- Hartford thesis: many suicides may have undiagnosed/misdiagnosed brain injury → psych meds → SI; substance use as pain mask

### Emerging / Non-Traditional Modalities

- Hyperbaric oxygen (FL ~$25M USF veteran study, year 2)

### Policy & System Imperatives

- DoD baseline **cognitive testing** policy for new recruits + high-risk designators (Deputy Secretary, ~Aug prior year / effective Jan)

## Segment 4 — Bill Clearfield: Hormones & Traumatic Brain Injury (1:19:00–1:40:00)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield, DO

### ★ Core Thesis
> The brain has its own steroidogenic pathway (neurosteroids discovered ~1985, Paris). Post-TBI hormone collapse is common, measurable, and treatable — SSRIs alone often fail because they don’t restore neurosteroids and high-dose psychotropics can further suppress LH/FSH/GH.

### Neurosteroids Named
Pregnenolone, progesterone, allopregnanolone, DHEA, estradiol, testosterone, cortisol

### Protective Biology
- Progesterone / allopregnanolone / DHEA: protect vs oxidative stress, promote neuroregeneration & myelin, ↓ inflammatory cytokines / ILs
- TBI and dementia both show ↓ allopregnanolone in frontal lobes; allopregnanolone triggers **GABA** calming tone
- Brain concentrations of P4 / pregnenolone can exceed periphery (esp. luteal / postmenopause contrast slides)

### Acute Hormone Collapse (24h vs 96h patterns cited)
- Cortisol ↓ >50%, free T3 ↓ >50%, nearly all males show testosterone change, LH ↓ ~50%
- **Growth hormone** first/most common acute hit (~20%); if untreated → **35–40%** GH deficiency by 12 months
- GH deficiency phenotype: memory/concentration, executive dysfunction, anxiety/OCD traits, rapid weight gain, depression, attention/mood issues
- Cash lab panel ~$350 for ~10 hormones; normalizing GH associated with ↓ IL-6, IL-1, CRP, homocysteine; better concentration, memory, fatigue, lean mass

### Labs & Remedies — GH Axis
- Prefer **IGF-1** (~**200–250**) over direct GH (handling/timing fragile)
- IGFBP-3 transport protein target ~**4000**
- Peptides: sermorelin; **CJC-1295** (± DAC: weekly vs 5×/week); homeopathic sprays ~15–20% GH rise / 3 mo; **ibutamoren (MK-677)** 10 mg♀ / 25 mg♂

### Thyroid Post-TBI
- **10–30%** develop hypothyroidism; ↑ reverse T3 (stress/cortisol driven ~90–95% of ↑ rT3)
- Prefer T4/T3 combos: NP Thyroid preferred over Armour (gluten/dairy coating sensitivity)
- Classic hypo symptoms + cognitive/psychosis risk

### Cortisol / DHEA
- AM ACTH/cortisol surge pattern; screen with cheap rT3 (~$10) vs 4-point saliva ($90–200)
- Symptom pattern: morning fatigue → afternoon crash → late-night second wind → insomnia; sugar/salt craving
- DHEA to temper cortisol: **10–25 mg♀** (up to 25); **25 mg♂** start (up to 100 if needed)

### Testosterone, Prolactin, Estrogen
- Total T **<300** → depression risk; **~150** → frank depression (♀ thresholds much lower: ~22 / 11 total; free ~1 / 0.5)
- Suicide attempts inversely related to T; ♂ 4× suicide risk vs ♀
- Adequate T: ↓ inflammatory cytokines, mitochondrial protection, better seizure control, anti-anxiety, cognition
- **Prolactin >6.0** tip for treatment-resistant anxiety/depression (high DA suppresses PRL; adenomas at very high levels)
- Estradiol: brain blood flow, ↓ PTSD risk after trauma, pain modulation, cell-death prevention, ↑ HDL; atypical-antipsychotic–like monoamine effects
- Historical TRT error: T to 1500 with E2 to 0 → memory/balance/hearing/tinnitus issues

### Progesterone / Pregnenolone / Allopregnanolone
- P4: myelin protection, ↓ IL-1β / TNF-α, prevents neuronal loss
- Cited acute care: **8 mg/kg IV P4 within 24h** of TBI → up to **50%** reduction in 30-day TBI deaths (research framing)
- Pregnenolone: “hormone of memory/learning”; clinic sweet spot ~**90–110**; does **not** meaningfully raise downstream sex hormones (Nario); watch mild glucose rise
- ↓ Allopregnanolone → panic, anxiety, major depression, long-term AD risk

### Depression Hormone Toolkit (Clearfield)
GH, thyroid, testosterone; estrogen as adjunct (not standalone); pregnenolone + DHEA

### Timeline Imperative
- Anterior pituitary insufficiency common early: all deficits ~**56%** by 3 months → still ~**36%** at 1 year if untreated
- Gonadotropins / ACTH also decline; **GH deficiency can increase** over the first year if ignored
- Treat early — don’t wait a year

### Zinc–Copper Link (ties to tip)
- Unbalanced Zn:Cu with aluminum accumulation → ↑ beta-amyloid risk
- Clearfield dosing frame: Zn **30–60 mg** with concurrent **15:1** copper
