## Section A — Zinc–Copper & Systems Context (3 questions)

### Q1. What is the optimal plasma copper-to-zinc ratio taught in the tip of the week?
- A) 0.3–0.5
- B) **0.7–1.0** ✓
- C) 1.5–2.0
- D) Exactly 2:1

> *Ratio >1 suggests relative Cu excess / Zn deficiency and inflammatory risk; <0.7 suggests copper deficiency. (Nario)*

### Q2. Why can chronic high-dose zinc create copper deficiency?
**Answer:** High zinc induces intestinal **metallothionein**, which binds copper and blocks absorption. Separate Zn and Cu by several hours; supplement ~8–15 mg Zn per 1 mg Cu.

### Q3. True or False: The copper-to-zinc ratio requires a specialty functional medicine lab panel.
**Answer:** **False.** It is measured on standard plasma/blood mineral labs.

## Section D — Hormones After TBI (7 questions)

### Q14. Which hormone axis is typically hit first / most commonly after TBI in Clearfield’s framing?
- A) Aldosterone only
- B) **Growth hormone** ✓
- C) Melatonin exclusively
- D) Calcitonin

> *~20% acute; untreated survivors may reach ~35–40% GH deficiency by 12 months.*

### Q15. Why does Clearfield prefer IGF-1 over random growth hormone levels?
**Answer:** GH assays are timing- and handling-sensitive (fragile samples). IGF-1 is more stable; target roughly **200–250**. IGFBP-3 ~**4000**.

### Q16. Elevated reverse T3 after TBI is most often a marker of:
- A) Iodine excess alone

- C) Pure T4 overproduction
- D) Vitamin D deficiency only

- A) Risk <500; frank depression <400

- C) Risk <150; frank depression <50

### Q18. What prolactin threshold is a tip-off for treatment-resistant anxiety/depression in this lecture?
- A) Prolactin must be >50
- B) **Prolactin ideally >6.0 (normal often cited ~11–14; very high suggests adenoma workup)** ✓
- C) Prolactin should be undetectable
- D) Prolactin is irrelevant if TSH is normal

### Q19. Match the neurosteroid to its clinical pearl from the lecture:
**Answer:**
- **Pregnenolone** — memory/learning; ~90–110 sweet spot; little downstream sex-hormone conversion; watch glucose
- **Progesterone** — myelin / anti-cytokine / neuronal protection; acute IV research dosing 8 mg/kg within 24h cited
- **Allopregnanolone** — GABA trigger; low levels → panic/anxiety/depression/AD risk
- **DHEA** — tempers high cortisol at low doses (10–25 mg♀ / 25–100 mg♂)

### Q20. A veteran 9 months post-blast has brain fog, weight gain, low motivation, “failed” two SSRIs, and never had a hormone panel. Using this webinar, what is your first-pass plan?
**Answer (model answer):**
1. Reframe as possible **physiologic TBI + pituitary injury**, not solely primary psychiatric disease  
2. Order a practical hormone set (cash ~$350 cited): IGF-1 (± IGFBP-3), thyroid with **rT3**, cortisol context, total/free T, estradiol, progesterone, pregnenolone, DHEA, prolactin  
3. Treat deficits early (GH peptides/secretagogues, NP Thyroid if hypo, DHEA for high-cortisol pattern, T optimization, consider P4/pregnenolone)  
4. Add interdisciplinary tools as indicated (HBOT/TMS/neural therapy/Synapsin/HBOT, cervical care) — not more stacked psychotropics first  
5. Connect to VA Whole Health / disability advocacy and avoid normalizing “this is just who I am now”
