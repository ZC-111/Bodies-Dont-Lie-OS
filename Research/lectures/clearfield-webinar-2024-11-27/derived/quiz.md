- C) **12.7 million** ✓

### Q3. True or False: According to Smurz, amyloid plaques are the root cause of Alzheimer’s disease.
**Answer:** **False.** She frames plaques as a **symptom** of upstream processes (vascular injury, neuroinflammation, toxins, oxidative stress, mito failure, protein misfolding)—not the initiating cause that drugs should solely chase.

### Q4. When do pathologic brain changes typically begin relative to clinical dementia onset, per Smurz?
- A) 1–2 years before diagnosis
- B) 5 years before diagnosis
- C) **~30 years before symptoms** ✓
- D) Only after age 70

> *If clinical change is noticeable ~65, abnormal biomarkers/neuroplastic shifts may start ~35.*

### Q5. Why does Smurz reject “women get more dementia because they live longer”?
**Answer:** Women’s brains differ biologically—pregnancy-related volume changes, lifetime estrogen exposure, menopause-related glucose→lipid fuel shifts (including white-matter catabolism, worse in APOE4), hot-flash–related gray-matter microintensities, and menopausal cortical/hippocampal shrinkage. Risk is ~2× vs men for these reasons, not longevity alone.

### Q6. What did the 2022 umbrella review conclude about the serotonin hypothesis of depression?
- A) It is definitively proven
- B) SSRIs reverse the biochemical deficit in most patients
- C) **The huge research effort has not produced convincing evidence of a biochemical serotonin basis for depression** ✓
- D) Serotonin excess causes dementia

### Q7. In the calcium hypothesis discussion, what clinical caution does Smurz raise for older women?
**Answer:** Excess neuronal calcium may drive dysfunction/death; a dietary calcium-reduction study in elderly (mainly women) showed memory improvement. Reflex high-dose calcium “for bones” may not be brain-neutral—consider magnesium and individualized mineral strategy.

### Q8. Alzheimer’s is sometimes called “type 3 diabetes.” Which downstream effects of brain insulin resistance does Smurz emphasize?
- A) Only elevated peripheral glucose
- B) **↓ acetylcholine signaling, ↑ TNF/inflammation, ↑ amyloid retention, ↑ tau phosphorylation** ✓
- C) Isolated HDL elevation
- D) Purely genetic tau mutations

### Q12. Which activity pattern best grows brain reserve according to the lecture?

### Q13. What is the key limitation of crossword puzzles and Sudoku for dementia prevention?
**Answer:** Learning is **domain-specific**—you get better at those games, but gains do not reliably transfer to global memory/cognition. Prefer complex novel tasks and social engagement for cognitive reserve.

### Q14. Which stimulus does Smurz highlight as the strongest practical way to raise BDNF?

- C) Benzodiazepines at bedtime
- D) High-dose calcium

> *Example: ≥40 min at ≥65% max HR raised BDNF with memory benefit up to 24 hours.*

## Section C — Hormones, PANDAS/PANS & Clinical Pearls (6 questions)

### Q15. What bone-protection timing window around menopause does Smurz emphasize for starting HRT?
**Answer:** Roughly **1.5 years before to 1.5 years after** menopause (≈3-year window) for maximal bone benefit; she likes to start just before menopause when possible.

### Q16. A patient is >10 years past menopause and requests estrogen for cognition. What approach aligns with the panel discussion?
- A) Absolute contraindication forever
- B) Full physiologic replacement immediately
- C) **Individualized risk–benefit; if treating, start low/slow and monitor cognition + metabolic health** ✓
- D) Oral contraceptives as first-line

### Q17. What clinical feature most raises suspicion for PANDAS/PANS versus typical psychiatric illness (Dr. Stone)?
**Answer:** **Sudden/overnight** onset of OCD, psychosis, severe insomnia, or other neuropsychiatric change in a previously stable person—especially after infection. Ordinary mental illness rarely appears that abruptly; treat as possible delirium/autoimmune trigger and pursue infection workup.

### Q18. For menopausal women in the “sandwich generation,” which dietary approach does Smurz usually prefer first?
- A) Strict long-term ketogenic diet for everyone
- B) **Mediterranean or Paleo-style cleanup: ↑ protein/fat, ↓ processed carbs, prioritize insulin resistance** ✓
- C) Very low-fat Ornish only
- D) No dietary guidance—supplements only

### Q19. Smurz’s practical omega-3 titration starting point and upper experience range?
**Answer:** Often start ~**2 g/day**, commonly titrate toward **~3 g**; she personally may use up to **~5 g** if GI-tolerant (stop/back off if greasy stools). Extra caution if on anticoagulants; panel notes AF literature signals near 4–5 g.

### Q20. Name two clinic tools Smurz uses for brain assessment/support beyond labs, and one use-case each.
**Answer (any two with correct use):**
- **P300 EEG cap** — baseline energy/ADHD–mood tendencies; concussion return-to-play
- **Trail Making Test** — cheap repeatable cognitive metric (“brain age”)
- **Visual contrast sensitivity** — mold screen + possible amyloid/tau-related signal
- **Nitric oxide saliva strip** — vascular/synaptic support proxy
- **Nano V (inhaled hydrogenated water)** — mito/protein-folding support; case of post-stroke writing recovery
