> *Cautionary tale: don’t assume postmenopausal osteoporosis drugs work for PPI-induced bone loss.*

### Q9. Why can glucocorticoid-related fracture risk rise before DEXA worsens?
**Answer:** Glucocorticoids damage bone collagen (↓ production; remaining collagen stiffer/more friable) and impair muscle microfibrils → falls. Collagen quality loss raises fracture risk before mineral density falls on DEXA.

### Q15. What practical protein intake floor does Neustadt recommend for US patients using pounds?
**Answer:** Body weight in **pounds × 0.6** = minimum grams of protein per day (aligned with aiming above the inadequate 0.8 g/kg RDA; research often cites ~1–1.3 g/kg). Prefer protein earlier in the day, not stacked only at dinner. Also target **≥30 g** dietary fiber/day.

### Q19. Why should clinicians be cautious prescribing second-generation nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates if they value endogenous MK-4 physiology?
**Answer:** These agents (e.g., alendronate/Fosamax class) **block production of MK-4**, potentially compounding vitamin K–related bone pathophysiology even as they are used for “bone density.”

### Q20. Short answer: Name two non-DEXA priorities Neustadt emphasizes for reducing real-world fracture risk in GI / osteoporotic patients.
**Sample answer:** (1) **Fall prevention** — strength, balance, stability (95% of fractures follow falls); (2) Address root drivers — stop/minimize PPIs when possible, treat dysbiosis/inflammation, correct protein/fiber/micronutrients, and use outcome-proven aids such as **MK-4 ± vitamin D** rather than chasing surrogates alone.
