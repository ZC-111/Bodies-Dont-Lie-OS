## Section B — Delivery Systems & Dosing (5 questions)

### Q5. What approximate dose fraction of immunotherapy / pharma agents does Halasa commonly load into platelet nanoparticles?
- A) 100% of the labeled high dose
- B) **About 10–20% (up to ~25% max)** ✓
- C) 1% only

### Q6. Why does a ~≤100 nm package preferentially enter tumor vs healthy tissue (passive targeting)?
**Answer:** Tumor vessels are **leaky**; healthy endothelium is tighter and less likely to admit packages up to ~100 nm (Doxil ~80 nm liposome cited as precedent).

### Q7. Name the two advanced delivery platforms Halasa emphasizes for concentrating agents in tumor rather than “everywhere.”
**Answer:** **Cyclodextrin** loading and **platelet nanoparticles** (exosomes also discussed as complementary carriers).

### Q8. What advantage does Halasa attribute to NK/umbilical exosomes vs cheaper platelet nanoparticles regarding p53?
- A) Exosomes irreversibly destroy all mitochondria
- B) **Exosomes may carry intact/wild-type p53 useful when tumor p53 is mutated** ✓
- C) Exosomes never contain growth factors
- D) Exosomes cannot be loaded with chemo

### Q9. True or False: Halasa says hydroxychloroquine is mandatory whenever using platelet nanoparticles.
**Answer:** **False.** Platelet NPs are endogenous and framed as solving macrophage interception without requiring HCQ — but HCQ (with ivermectin, methylene blue, thymosin α1) is still valued for **Th17 modulation**.

## Section C — TME, Mitochondria & Resistance (6 questions)

### Q10. How can a tumor be “highly vascular” yet hypoxic in this lecture?
- A) Tumors have no VEGF
- B) **Fibrosis kinks vessels so RBCs/lymphocytes cannot infiltrate despite many vessels** ✓
- C) Arterial oxygen is always zero in cancer patients
- D) Only calcified plaque causes hypoxia

### Q11. Extracellular acidosis from Warburg metabolism favors which immune skew?
- A) Pure Th1 dominance
- B) **Treg / Th2 / Th17 polarization away from cytotoxic Th1** ✓
- C) Only NK hyperactivation
- D) Immediate sterile clearance

### Q12. How does platelet-nanoparticle endocytosis help defeat ABC-transporter multi-drug resistance?
**Answer:** Exporters on the endocytosed membrane pump payload **from the endosome into the tumor cytosol**, concentrating drug instead of ejecting naked extracellular chemo.

### Q13. Why does Halasa argue ozone / oxygen support can *help* rather than antagonize chemo/RT?
**Answer:** Ultimate apoptotic effector of chemo/RT is **free radicals**, which require oxygen; hypoxic TME otherwise blunts efficacy — ozone dissolves O₂ into plasma to bypass RBC exclusion.

### Q14. What infrared-absorbing FDA-approved photosensitizer story is linked to Dr. Quinn’s nanoparticle work?
- A) Hypericin only
- B) **Indocyanine green (ICG)** ✓
- C) Psoralen only
- D) Fluorescein alone

> *Methylene blue / red light limited by depth; ICG enables deeper IR activation.*

### Q15. Which antifibrotic / antifibrin tools are highlighted to open the fibrotic–fibrin cloak?
**Answer:** **Nattokinase** (and related fibrinolytics), **losartan**, Klotho framing, low-dose **aspirin**, plus ozone/HBOT and nanoparticle delivery into remodeled matrix.

### Q16. When should tumor markers / D-dimer ideally be compared to baseline during cytoreductive therapy?

### Q17. Why avoid reduced glutathione *during* oxidative chemo/RT/ozone cycles?
**Answer:** Reduced GSH can **neutralize the ROS** you are using to drive tumor apoptosis; consider after recovery, and recycle glutamate with **NAC + glycine** if used.
