# Key Takeaways — Clearfield Webinar 2025-07-01

## Top 10 Clinical Pearls

1. **Ascorbate is terrain infrastructure, not a vitamin afterthought.** Optimal physiological levels determine whether HRT, ozone, peptides, exosomes, and IVC actually perform.

2. **Prefer sodium ascorbate (SA) over ascorbic acid (AA) for clinical work.** SA is pH-neutral (~7.4–7.8), SVCT-transported, more stable, and far better tolerated IV, orally, and in the mouth.

3. **SIP 7-to-7 beats sporadic megadoses.** ~4 g/day sipped every 20–30 minutes maintains stable levels; acute illness needs loading + q2–4h to gut tolerance, then **≥72 hours** after symptoms clear.

4. **G6PD before high-dose IVC is non-negotiable** (especially >~25 g). Screen kidneys, iron overload, and fluid-overload states too.

5. **Adrenals hold the body’s highest ascorbate density** — stress, infection, and inflammation burn it fast; you cannot “bank” weeks of C.

6. **Hormone patients should be on ascorbate by default.** Cholesterol→pregnenolone and catecholamine synthesis are C-dependent; Nario will switch AA patients to SA after this talk.

7. **Pharmacologic IVC is a sequenced pro-oxidant tool in oncology** (H₂O₂ / Fenton). Do not casually stack glutathione into the cytotoxic window.

8. **Many “IV-first” infections are oral-SA problems.** UTI and similar flares often resolve in 30–60 minutes with distributed pharmacologic oral dosing.

9. **Garlic is a legitimate chelator with human RCT parity to D-penicillamine for lead** (3.6 g/day tablets) — fewer AEs, spares essential minerals vs EDTA.

10. **Skip calcium ascorbate and don’t over-index on liposomal hype** for pharmacologic goals. Pure medical-grade SA wins on tolerability, cost (~$0.35/g vs >$1/g liposomal), and delivery versatility.

## Comparison Table: Ascorbic Acid vs Sodium Ascorbate

| Dimension | Ascorbic acid (AA) | Sodium ascorbate (SA) |
|---|---|---|
| **pH** | ~2.2–2.4 (buffered ~5.2–5.4) | ~7.4–7.8 |
| **Transport** | Passive GLUT + energy-costly conversion | Active SVCT1/SVCT2 |
| **IV tolerability** | Phlebitis, sclerosis, acidosis risk | Superior |
| **Dental / mucosa** | Enamel erosion | Preferred |
| **Oncology microenv.** | May worsen acidification | Better biocompatibility |
| **Historical protocols** | Modern default / often wrong tool | Klenner / Cathcart standard |
| **Best use** | Avoid for high-dose clinical work | Oral SIP, IV, regenerative, infection |
