**Answer:** Ketamine’s mTOR activation is **brain-selective** via glutamatergic signaling; it does not turn on systemic organ mTOR the way longevity/cancer concerns about global mTOR activation imply. Clinic uses include cancer-related depression/adjustment and cancer pain.

## Section D — Safety, Integration & Systems (4 questions)

### Q17. Relative addictive potential of ketamine on the Gable-style profile shown:
- A) Higher than heroin
- B) Equal to benzodiazepines
- C) **Lower than caffeine** ✓

### Q18. Name three absolute or strong contraindications listed for ketamine candidates.
**Answer (any three):** pregnancy; psychosis/schizophrenia/schizoaffective; active mania; prior severe ketamine allergy; brain aneurysm / intracranial bleed history; uncontrolled severe hypertension; Child-Pugh C liver disease; unstable uncontrolled active SUD (relative/strong caution).

### Q19. How does VA coverage split between esketamine and IV ketamine?
**Answer:** **CCN (Community Care Network / TriWest)** covers Spravato/esketamine. **VCA (Veterans Care Agreement)** is a separate carve-out contract that can cover **IV ketamine**. Awareness and local advocacy affect referral success; turnaround often ~2–4 weeks.

### Q20. A plateauing patient improves from severe to moderate QIDS but stalls. What next steps did Boco suggest?
**Answer:** Recognize the plateau pattern; escalate thoughtfully — e.g., partner with **TMS** (synergistic), adjust dose/schedule within safe range, or add other modalities; continue measurement-based care (PHQ-9/QIDS/PCL-5) and address ongoing psychosocial stressors; consider Spravato bridge for insured maintenance after IV induction gains.
