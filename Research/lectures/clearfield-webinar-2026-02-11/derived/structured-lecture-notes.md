## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Ibogaine (11:25–16:26)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario, DO (University of Arizona Integrative Medicine fellowship content)

### ★ Framing
> Mechanism + evidence update for clinicians — **not an endorsement**. Dedicated to John Burgess (ibogaine advocate). Primer for NMDA/ketamine discussion; follows prior kratom tip.

### Source & Pharmacology
- From root bark of *Tabernanthe iboga* (Central Africa); metabolized to longer-acting **noribogaine**
- Traditional Bwiti ritual use; modern addiction-interruption narrative from Howard Lotsof (1962) → US patent 1985 for opioid dependence
- Schedule I (US, 1970) → limited mainstream research
- Half-life up to ~72 hours; onset ~30 min–3 h; dream-like introspective peak ~24 h
- Low dose: stimulant-like; higher dose: psychedelic
- **Polypharmacology:** NMDA antagonism (shared pathway with ketamine), kappa/mu opioid modulation, serotonergic effects, dopaminergic normalization, ↑ BDNF/GDNF neurotrophic signaling

### Clinical Evidence Signals
- Best studied for **addiction interruption** (especially OUD): single-dose withdrawal/craving suppression; Marsh et al. 2018 observational; Brown & Alper 2017 reviews — highly variable durability
- **TBI/PTSD:** 2024 Stanford veterans study (~30) — ~80% reduction in PTSD symptoms at 1 month with magnesium co-administration for cardiac protection (Nario framing)
- Emerging case reports (MS lesion volume reduction; Parkinson’s tremor) — do **not** establish efficacy; illustrate circuit-modifying capacity

### Safety — Non-Negotiables
- **Cardiac toxicity:** QT prolongation, ventricular arrhythmias
- Magnesium co-admin under study to blunt QT effect — **experimental, not a green light**
- Requires EKG screening, electrolyte optimization, strict exclusion criteria, **supervised medical environment only**

### Clinical Pearl
> Ibogaine = **neuroplastic disruptor**, high-risk / low-infrastructure compound — shows what rapid neural reorganization can look like, **not** what is clinically scalable yet.

## Segment 4 — Erwin Boco: Ketamine for Treatment-Resistant Depression (23:33–1:30:09)

**Speaker:** Erwin Boco, MD — founder, Zen Ketamine; IPI ketamine fellowship; ASKPP member  
**Disclosure:** No pharma relationships other than Zen Ketamine ownership

### ★ Core Thesis
> Depression is not only a “chemical imbalance” — it is also a **wiring/connectivity problem**. Ketamine is a high-level signal that surges glutamate → activates AMPA/NMDA cascades → **mTOR** (brain growth switch) → synaptogenesis and rapid mood change.

### 4A. The TRD Problem (STAR*D & FDA definition)

| Antidepressant trial # | Approx. response rate |
|---|---|
| 1st | ~27.5% |
| 2nd | ~21% |
| 3rd | ~16% |
| 4th | <7% |
| Cumulative | ~65–70% respond; **~30% remain TRD** |

- Depression prevalence post-COVID: ~19% → ~29%
- FDA TRD: failure of **≥2** adequate antidepressant regimens (dose + 8–12 weeks + adherence)
- >50% of depression treated in primary care

### 4B. Landmark Evidence
- **Berman 2000 (Yale):** single IV ketamine → >50% depression score drop within 72 h; durability 1–2 weeks
- Now **>160** placebo-controlled RCTs; specialty-community response ~**80%** (range ~65–85%)
- **CANMAT 2021:** single IV ketamine = **Level 1** evidence for adult TRD; multiple infusions Level 3; non-IV Level 3–4; multiple > single for response/remission
- **Albott VA 2015 (MDD + PTSD):** TRD response **93.3%**; PTSD full remission ~**80%** → VA adoption/coverage
- **ELEKT-D 2023:** ketamine **non-inferior** (superior at 3/5 centers) vs ECT for depression improvement; fewer cognitive AEs
- Colorado Medicaid covering IV ketamine (~2 years prior to talk); commercial still mostly off-label/uncovered

### 4C. Cases (Zen Ketamine)
1. **40yo musician (EF):** severe depression/anxiety, 7+ SSRIs failed, sober 7 years post polydrug use → early responder; PHQ-9/GAD-7 to remission after induction
2. **68yo female:** TRD since age 18, PTSD, TMS failure, duloxetine 60 mg → remission in ~4 weeks; PCL-5 60 → 9
3. **53yo pastry chef:** severe anxiety predictor of slower response; IV induction → moderate; Spravato bridge; remission by ~6 months; returned to classes/hiking/travel

### 4D. Molecule, Isomers & Bioavailability

| Route | Approx. bioavailability |
|---|---|
| IV | ~100% |
| IM | ~90–95% |
| Intranasal | ~15–48% |
| Oral | ~10% (first-pass) |

- Racemic ketamine = R + S; Janssen isolated **S-ketamine (esketamine / SPRAVATO)** 2019
- Half-life ~7–15 min; soft landing ~20 min; observe ≥30 min post-pump before discharge
- Oral high doses → more bladder exposure (ulceration risk); IV uses lower absolute doses at full bioavailability
- SPRAVATO FDA indications: TRD + suicidality; **not** PTSD/anxiety/chronic pain (vs off-label IV flexibility)
- Jan (prior year): esketamine approved as **monotherapy** for TRD (first/only) — can wean antidepressants for intolerant patients

### 4E. Mechanism — Glutamate → mTOR
- Ketamine blocks NMDA on inhibitory interneuron → **glutamate surge**
- Glutamate ~70–80% of brain neurotransmission vs serotonin ~2%
- Postsynaptic AMPA/NMDA → Ca²⁺ → kinases/BDNF/Akt/ERK → **mTOR ON** → new spines/synapses
- Suicidality can drop in **4–5 hours**; effect lasts ~7–10 days after single infusion
- Classic psychedelics (ayahuasca/DMT/LSD/ibogaine path) reach mTOR via **5-HT2A**; ketamine does **not** — dissociation via DMN/salience/thalamocortical disruption (dissociative ≠ classic psychedelic)
- Neuroplasticity window: ketamine ~24–72 h; ayahuasca reports weeks–months
- Too little **or** too much glutamate harms; insomnia → high glutamate → maladaptive plasticity
- Natural modulators: short-burst exercise (↑), trained athletes/meditators (lower baseline), sleep
- GLP-1 receptors in brain; nitrous oxide also NMDA-active (emerging mood RCTs)
- Dubbed “first resilience drug” at ASKPP (first-responder 12-month burnout study — one IV vs placebo) — **not yet guideline first-line** outside exceptions

### 4F. Clinical Protocol (Zen / IPI / CANMAT / VA informed)

**Third-line default** after ≥2 failed trials — **exceptions:** active suicidality; selected first-responder/military contexts

**Contraindications / cautions:**
- Pregnancy; psychosis/schizophrenia/schizoaffective; active mania
- Prior severe ketamine allergy; brain aneurysm / intracranial bleed history
- Uncontrolled severe HTN (stabilize first); Child-Pugh C liver disease
- Unstable active SUD (stable SUD often treatable; addiction outcomes often improve with mood)

**Workup:** LFTs, kidneys, UDS, pregnancy test, PDMP (C-III)

**Induction:** 6 IV infusions, non-consecutive (typically 2×/week × 3 weeks)  
- Start **0.5 mg/kg over 40 min** → titrate toward **0.7–1.0 mg/kg** over 40–60 min  
- Partial responders: individualized up to ~1.25–1.75 mg/kg; avoid ≥2 mg/kg (anesthetic range)

**Optimization:** weekly boosters × 4 → complete **10** total → response rates ~85–87% (Florida ~10K infusions / 1K patients cited)  
Then individualized PRN / monthly / q2–3 months

**Monitoring:** continuous VS (± EKG as indicated); RASS; no driving same day; return next day after sleep

**Transient AEs (<5% typically):** dissociation (usually welcomed), BP rise ~40 min mark, urgency, N/V (Zofran), mild headache; OSA → bring CPAP

### 4G. Real-World Outcomes
- São Paulo: PHQ-9 item 9 (suicidality) → near zero by ~3 months with more infusions
- Harvard/Mass Brigham (Dr. Kritzer): 471 patients; most 6–10 IVs (some up to 80); start 0.5 → avg **1.3 mg/kg** / 40 min; QIDS **16–17 → 9–10**; “plateauing” → consider TMS synergy
- Addiction potential (Gable): ketamine **lower than caffeine**
- “Social prescriptions” once improving: meditation, walking, nutrition, connection

### 4H. Pipeline
- Selective 5-HT2A agonists → mTOR without psychedelic arm (pill, 2 doses 4 weeks apart, ~12-month durability) — Phase 2 done, Phase 3 starting
- NMDA-targeted: D-cycloserine class, NRX-1074 (apimostinel), zelquistinel — Phase 2 positive signals

## Segment 5 — Q&A: Practice, Safety, Coverage, mTOR Balance (1:30:09–2:07)

**Speakers:** Erwin Boco, Melvin Nario, Bill Clearfield, panel

### Cancer & mTOR
- Cancer **not** a contraindication — used for cancer-related depression/adjustment and cancer pain
- Ketamine’s mTOR activation is **brain-selective** via glutamatergic path — not systemic organ mTOR

### Compounding & Oral / At-Home
- Prefer established compounding pharmacies; post-COVID shortage forced compounding (branded limited to hospitals)
- Compounded intranasal ~**$39** vs Spravato ~**$1,300** (speaker quote)
- Oral/sublingual: Level 3–4 evidence; bridge/access use OK; **not recommended at home** (FDA guidance; Mindbloom litigation cited)
- Australian remote model: first sessions in clinic, then supervised Zoom with family present

### ADHD / Neurodivergence
- Patients **on ADHD meds** — positive outcome predictor (conference + clinic experience)

### Longevity mTOR Paradox
- Balance: activate brain mTOR episodically for repair; inhibit peripheral mTOR for aging — yin/yang (Nario: AMPK vs mTOR equilibrium)

### Midazolam
- Not universal; blunts intense high-dose/pain-protocol experiences; useful for high anxiety/panic
- Harvard: ~**86%** received IV midazolam (lorazepam ASR in places) to tolerate 1.3–1.5 mg/kg

### Cost & Durability
- Zen out-of-pocket: **~$600/infusion**
- 1 infusion durability ~7–10 days; after 6 → ~5–7 months; after 10 → up to ~12 months in some

### Safety Network Data
- REMS network ~450 clinics: 2 minor AEs (O₂ desats), no deaths/MIs in cited data
- Hypersalivation / laryngospasm mainly at very high doses
- Avoid opioids/benzos/muscle relaxants ~12 h pre-treatment

### Naltrexone
- Full-dose naltrexone challenge historically linked to partial blockade of antidepressant effect (classification controversy); give **6–12 h window** — not LDN discussion dose

### VA Coverage
- **CCN (TriWest):** Spravato/esketamine  
- **VCA (Veterans Care Agreement):** IV ketamine (separate carve-out contract)  
- National CPG updated Oct 2025; referral turnaround often 2–4 weeks when advocates present (e.g., Mather/Sacramento Dr. Gellerman)

### Concurrent Psychedelics
- Not absolute contraindication; counsel safety-first, abstain during ketamine series to attribute response; non-judgment / radical acceptance

### Closing Logistics
- Spravato response ~**65%** vs IV ~**80%** — complementary
