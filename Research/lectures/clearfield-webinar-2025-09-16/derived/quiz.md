## Section C — AI Bias, Hallucinations & Safe Use (8 questions)

### Q13. What is the “error of omission” in medical AI as James defined it?
- A) AI invents fake labs
- B) **AI leaves out knowledge not present in its database/training** ✓
- C) AI always refuses traditional queries
- D) AI deletes the chart

### Q14. Which statement best captures the panel’s stance on clinician liability when using AI?
- A) Liability transfers to the software vendor
- B) Liability transfers to the EHR vendor
- C) **The practitioner retains full responsibility for outcomes** ✓
- D) Liability only applies if no prompt engineer was consulted

### Q15. What real-world dermatology bias example did Tano give?
**Answer:** Skin-cancer / lesion AI trained mainly on lighter-skinned datasets performs poorly on **dark-skinned** patients → missed or delayed diagnoses.

### Q16. The 2019 *Science* hospital algorithm study (discussed by Nario) underestimated Black patients’ needs because it used:
- A) BMI as a proxy for acuity
- B) **Healthcare spending as a proxy for healthcare need** ✓
- C) Zip code only
- D) Self-reported pain scores

### Q17. Regarding AI scribes (e.g., Freed), the panel recommends all EXCEPT:
- A) Informed consent for recording
- B) Careful human review of every transcript
- C) **Blind trust because scribes are HIPAA-certified** ✓
- D) Awareness that scribes can hallucinate chart content

### Q18. What prompt failure did James illustrate with the cancer patient who brought a 5-page AI protocol?
**Answer:** An overly vague prompt (essentially “best cancer treatment for [complex diagnosis]”) without clinical context, integrative constraints, or patient-specific “fingerprint” — yielding an output the patient over-trusted relative to the clinician.

### Q19. Which prompting practice did Tano/James endorse for better integrative outputs?
- A) One-line ChatGPT queries only
- B) **Layered, logic-flow prompts that specify integrative/plant-based/cultural options and reference prior context** ✓
- C) Always demand references without verification
- D) Never mention the patient’s cultural background

### Q20. True or False: Prompt engineering alone is sufficient for safe clinical AI use, even if the user lacks disease knowledge.
**Answer:** **False.** Without domain expertise you cannot recognize errors; hallucinations and bad logic flow can harm patients. Knowledge first, then tool use.
