## Segment 3 — Eight Principles of Health-Supportive Food Selection (24:00–54:00)

**Speaker:** Laura Polt

### The Eight Principles
1. **Whole quality**
2. **Fresh / frozen / fermented / canned / dehydrated** (quality forms)
3. **Natural**
4. **Seasonal**
5. **Local**
6. **In harmony with tradition**
7. **Balance**
8. **Delicious**

### 3A. Whole Foods
- Nature’s edible parts intact: whole apple vs apple juice; bone-in chicken → stocks from bones + peels
- **Whole grain anatomy:** bran + germ + endosperm; refining can remove up to **~95%** of bran/germ nutrients then “enrich”
- Whole-grain examples: quinoa, millet, brown rice, oats, lightly pearled barley, bulgur
- **Soak overnight** with acid (yogurt/kefir/vinegar/lemon): ↑ digestibility, ↑ mineral absorption, ↓ phytates, faster cook / less water
- **Quinoa** = only grain that is a complete protein; **soy** = only bean that is a complete protein
- Plant foods that lower glycemic load / inflammation: most fruits/veg, nuts/seeds, beans

### 3B. Quality Forms
- Fresh preferred for flavor; frozen/canned now retain more nutrients than historically — pantry OK when fresh unavailable
- Dehydrated / naturally pickled / fermented leave most nutrients intact

### 3C. Natural & Organic
- Grow/raise with minimal pesticides, chemicals, hormones; eat close to how food left the plant/animal
- Organic may be more nutritious because stressed plants produce protective phytonutrients (quinoa bitterness = natural “pesticide” → wash well)
- Pasture-raised vs “all natural” chicken-liver visual: liver color/appearance as teaching metaphor for animal diet quality
- **Not everything must be organic** — use **EWG Dirty Dozen / Clean 15** app; e.g., asparagus rarely needs organic pricing
- Label literacy: “if you can’t pronounce it, renounce it”; peanut butter = peanuts only vs sugar + hydrogenated oils; “plant-based” packaging ≠ whole-food healthy

### 3D. Local & Seasonal
- Local: lower footprint, fewer preservatives for long transit, supports farmers; farmers markets may take SNAP / senior discounts
- Seasonal: raw-food Florida retreat ≠ winter Pennsylvania — out-of-season raw foods can leave patients cold/unwell; winter menus favor roots, dark greens, warming grains (e.g., barley soup)
- Swiss chard vs short spinach season — ask growers how to cook unfamiliar greens

### 3E. Tradition / Culture
- Ancestral foodways often suit digestion better than suddenly adopting unfamiliar refined-food replacements
- Ask family/community: beans, grains, seasonings, fats of the “old country”
- Northern European cue: Great Northern beans; rye, buckwheat
- **Soul Savory** cookbook (Smith Center): African American / African heritage / Caribbean / African island recipes; ingredients available in community groceries; donation via smithcenter.org
- **Oldways.org** packaged courses: Mediterranean, African heritage, Latin American, Asian, vegetarian/vegan — Polt taught African heritage diet class (~20 attendees)

### 3F. Balance & Quantity
- Dynamic needs: growth, pregnancy, illness, athletics — “can’t eat dogma”
- Variety: complex carbs + protein + healthy fat; **rainbow** produce (pistachio ice cream ≠ green vegetable)
- Balance textures, tastes, cooking methods (client who boiled everything → teach alternative methods)
- **AICR New American Plate:** ~⅔ plants; ≤⅓ animal protein (deck-of-cards size) — or fully plant with quinoa + beans
- No single food is demon or panacea; everything in moderation (including moderation)
- Chocolate “good news about bad habits” (McDougall) → avoid overdoing one “superfood”

### 3G. Deliciousness
- Truly healthy food must taste great — especially with cancer-related taste changes, mouth pain/burning
- Classes always include demo + tasting for flavor-building

## Segment 5 — Q&A: Carbs, Cancer, Enzymes, Microbiome, Soy, Beans, Fruit (1:00:00–1:38:00)

**Speakers:** Laura Polt, Melvin Nario (moderator), audience questions

### Oats & Glycemic Load
- Oats = whole grain; OK unless celiac / rare cross-reactivity concerns
- Variety of grains preferred (quinoa/millet as oatmeal alternatives)
- Nario: prefer steel-cut over flavored Quaker-style products
- Sugar “feeding cancer” is oversimplified — issue is **high glycemic load / insulin resistance**
- Insulin as growth signal; cancer cells preferentially use circulating glucose; IGF-1 / growth hormone caution in cancer (Nario)
- Lower GL tactics: keep grains whole (not flour); pair carb + protein + fat; cool cooked potatoes (resistant starch); pasta al dente; sprouted grains/beans/breads (not ground to flour)

### Digestive Enzymes
- Polt: not a supplement specialist; retreats stock enzymes for plant-heavy / bean transitions; useful in food sensitivity / mold history; pancreatic cancer may require specialist dosing
- Emphasize chew well, don’t overfill stomach, papaya seeds as food enzyme cue
- Nario clinical note: high-dose proteases/lipases (e.g., lipase ~30,000–75,000 U/meal) theorized for cancer/immune support — test-guided in his practice

### Cancer-Type / Treatment-Specific Diets
- Depends on cancer **and** treatment late effects (e.g., founder’s stage IV colorectal radiation → could not tolerate high-fiber retreat diet)
- Colorectal: fiber + microbiome; red meat concern may relate to **heme** (not only fat); alcohol underappreciated carcinogen
- Cancer Choices sections: microbiome; probiotics/prebiotics

### Probiotics Caution (Nario)
- Contraindications emphasized: **acute pancreatitis**; **severely immunocompromised** patients — septicemia risk with probiotics
- Prefer earlier/preventive use vs late debilitated stage III–IV after heavy chemo/radiation/surgery
- Polt: Akkermansia hype; also linked to higher glioblastoma risk signal — microbiome interventions should be specific, not “willy-nilly”
- Immune checkpoint inhibitor response can depend on microbiome composition

### Soy & Breast Cancer
- Avoid **GMO soy** (unknowns; glyphosate → possible microbiome “plant species” impact)
- Early preclinical fear of phytoestrogens ≠ clinical Women’s Health Study signals; soy did not interfere with tamoxifen; may support aromatase-inhibitor effectiveness
- Harm signal with **fragmented soy** (soy protein isolate capsules/powders)
- Prefer **whole soy**: tempeh, miso; tofu less “whole” (fiber removed) but still food vs isolate
- Nario: whole-food soy / genistein as protective phytoestrogen in tested cancer plans
- More detail: Cancer Choices breast cancer handbook

### Beans, Gout, Lectins
- Nightshades more often arthritis triggers in Polt’s experience than beans; gout patients need individualized protein limits
- Lectins (Gundry): cook beans thoroughly; soak dried beans, discard soak water; kidney beans worst if undercooked
- Moderation: couple tablespoons beans with ~¾ cup cooked rice for complete protein — not a “heap”
- Elimination diet anecdote: jaw arthritis resolved in ~1 week after identifying food triggers (2007)
- Nario add-on: boiling / pressure cooking also reduces lectins

### Fruit Timing & Blood Sugar
- Fit for Life school: separate fruit from protein/fat; ~30–60 min before meal — individualize
- Berries often better tolerated with meals
- Nario cites Harvard Health letter: fruit after meals associated with weight lowering (fiber buffering)
- Whole fruit > juice; pair fruit with nut butter/cheese to blunt GL
- Prefer blender “whole-food” smoothies over extracted juice; Keith Block (integrative oncology) skeptical of juicing (carrot juice high GL)
- Parboil greens before juicing if hypothyroid tendency
- Low fruit intake associated with ~2× cancer risk (Nario citation); Spanish Mediterranean pattern higher fruit, still protective

## Segment 6 — Tip of the Week: Cleaning Produce When Organic Isn’t Affordable (1:39:47–1:42:30)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario, DO

### Dirty Dozen Context
- High-contamination produce (strawberry, spinach, kale cited) — up to ~95% contamination framing for Dirty Dozen items

### Protocols Taught in Fellowship
| Method | Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Baking soda** | 1 tsp per ~2 cups / 500 mL water | Soak ~15 min; rub manually; alkaline + abrasion; **studies favor baking soda over ACV** |
| **Apple cider vinegar** | ~¼ cup (~60 mL) per liter water | Need not be expensive organic ACV; antimicrobial; rub |
| **Neither available** | Running water + heat | Peeling/trimming outer skins also reduces surface pesticide |

### Pearl
- Choose **one** method — stacking both is optional overkill
- Washing minimizes surface residue; does **not** remove systemic uptake from contaminated soil (arsenic example)
