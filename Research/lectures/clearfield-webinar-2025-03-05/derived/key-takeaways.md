# Key Takeaways — Clearfield Webinar 2025-03-05

## Top 10 Clinical Pearls

1. **Culinary translation beats nutrition lectures alone.** Convert “eat greens” into five affordable, cultural, delicious preparations — slowly, meeting patients where they are.

2. **Use Polt’s eight principles as a standing order set:** whole, quality forms, natural, seasonal, local, tradition-aligned, balanced, delicious.

3. **Whole > fragmented.** Apple ≠ apple juice; bran+germ+endosperm ≠ white flour. Refining can remove ~95% of key grain nutrients before cosmetic enrichment.

4. **AICR New American Plate:** ~⅔ plants, ≤⅓ animal protein (or plant complete protein). Rainbow produce; no single superfood panacea.

5. **Soy for breast-cancer patients: whole foods (tempeh, miso), non-GMO; avoid soy protein isolates.** Clinical women’s data do not support blanket soy bans with tamoxifen.

6. **Glycemic load context matters more than “sugar feeds cancer” slogans.** Pair carbs, keep grains intact, use resistant starch / al dente / sprouts; insulin and IGF-1 are growth signals.

7. **Probiotics are not risk-free in oncology.** Caution in acute pancreatitis and severely immunocompromised patients (septicemia risk); microbiome tools should be specific (Akkermansia not universally benign).

8. **Beans: soak, discard water, cook soft; moderate portions.** Lectin fear is mostly undercooking; gout/arthritis still need individualization (nightshades often more relevant for joints).

9. **When organic isn’t affordable:** baking soda soak (~1 tsp/500 mL, 15 min + rub) outranks ACV in cited studies; white vinegar soak is a strong panel alternative for Dirty Dozen produce.

10. **Eating awareness is clinical, not fluff.** ¾-full, calm setting, midday main meal, overnight fast, breaths before eating — digestion and nourishment depend on autonomic state.

## Comparison Table: Whole Soy vs Fragmented Soy

| Dimension | Whole soy (tempeh, miso) | Soy protein isolate |
|---|---|---|
| **Food matrix** | Intact bean-based foods | Highly processed fragment |
| **Fiber** | Present (tempeh/miso) | Removed |
| **Breast-cancer counseling** | Favored if patient eats soy | Where harm signals clustered |
| **GMO** | Prefer non-GMO sources | Often commodity GMO feedstock |
| **Phytoestrogen framing** | Food-dose isoflavones (e.g., genistein discussed) | Concentrated / non-food use |

**Consensus from session:** Not “soy good/bad” — **form and dose** matter; whole-food soy ≠ xenoestrogen.
