## Section C — Oncology Nutrition Pearls & Q&A (5 questions)

### Q14. What is Polt’s soy recommendation for breast-cancer–concerned patients?
- A) Avoid all soy forever because phytoestrogens always drive ER+ tumors
- B) Prefer soy protein isolate capsules for purity
- C) **Favor whole soy foods (tempeh, miso); avoid fragmented soy isolates; prefer non-GMO** ✓
- D) Only GMO soy is studied enough to recommend

> *Women’s Health Study–era signals: soy did not interfere with tamoxifen and may support aromatase-inhibitor effectiveness; preclinical phytoestrogen fear was over-applied clinically.*

### Q15. Which probiotic caution does Nario emphasize for advanced/immunocompromised cancer patients?
- A) Probiotics always cure mucositis
- B) **Risk of septicemia in severely immunocompromised patients; also avoid in acute pancreatitis** ✓
- C) Probiotics are banned in all stage I disease
- D) Only Akkermansia is universally safe

### Q16. Name two practical tactics Polt teaches to lower glycemic load of carbohydrate foods.
**Answer (any two):** Keep grains whole (not ground to flour); pair carbs with protein/fat/fiber; cool cooked potatoes (resistant starch); cook pasta al dente; use sprouted grains/beans/breads; avoid extracted juices.

### Q17. For dried beans and lectin concerns, Polt’s primary cooking advice is:
- A) Eat them raw for enzymes
- B) **Soak, discard soak water, and cook thoroughly until soft (esp. kidney beans)** ✓
- C) Only canned beans are dangerous
- D) Never combine beans with rice

### Q18. True or False: Polt teaches that juicing is always superior to eating whole fruit for cancer patients.
**Answer:** **False.** Prefer whole fruit (or blender drinks that retain fiber). Keith Block is cited as skeptical of juicing; carrot juice can be high GL.

## Section D — Produce Cleaning & Practice Systems (2 questions)

### Q19. Per Nario’s tip of the week, which produce-wash method shows more promise in studies?
- A) Expensive branded organic ACV only
- B) **Baking soda soak (≈1 tsp / 2 cups or 500 mL water, ~15 min + rub)** ✓
- C) Dish soap
- D) Dry brushing without rinse

> *ACV (~¼ cup / liter) is acceptable; choose one method. Washing does not fix soil-uptake contaminants (e.g., arsenic).*

### Q20. Adam Crouch reports that pesticide-testing collaborators most strongly recommended which wash for fruits with nooks and crannies?
**Answer:** Straight white vinegar soak for 15–20 minutes, then rinse (may mildly alter flavor). Ozone water and commercial food washes were also tried; vinegar ranked strongest in their experience.
