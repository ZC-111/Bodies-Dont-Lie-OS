## Segment 2 — Metabolomics Framework: Pathways, Rate, Circadian Timing (14:47–25:02)

**Speaker:** Greg Nakarelli

### ★ Core Definition
> **Metabolomics** = study of metabolites in an organism (blood, urine, stool, saliva) to predict health outcomes, guide treatment/prevention, and describe phenotype. Contrasted with reductionist single-analyte medicine; aligned with **systems biology** and **multi-omics** (proteome + metabolome relationships).

### 2A. Core Metabolic Pathways Named
- Glycolysis / gluconeogenesis; pentose phosphate (NADPH); TCA cycle → oxidative phosphorylation
- Fatty acid synthesis / elongation / β-oxidation; ketone body metabolism
- One-carbon / methylation cycle; detoxification (phase I vs II)
- Neurotransmitter synthesis; urea cycle; nucleotide / heme / hormone / cholesterol metabolism

**Catabolic vs anabolic frame:**
- Catabolic: glycolysis, pyruvate processing, TCA, OxPhos, β-oxidation
- Anabolic: amino acid synthesis, PPP, fatty acid / hormone synthesis

### 2B. Metabolic Rate Layers
| Rate | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Basal metabolic rate** | Minimum energy to sustain life (awake, no food, no movement) |
| **Resting metabolic rate** | BMR + sleep + arousal metabolism |
| **Exercise metabolic rate** | Exercise metabolism + RMR |

- **Protein** contributes most to metabolic rate (enzyme synthesis / heat); carbs more short-term; lipids longer-view contribution
- **Circadian biology:** organ systems metabolically active at different times; shift work, sleep deprivation, travel, aging disrupt; adiponectin gene expression varies by time → links to insulin resistance / MetS
- **Testing rule:** sample timing matters; all-up or all-down pathway metabolites = red flag; high upstream + low downstream → enzyme deficiency (genetic or chemical), low cofactor/substrate (e.g., methylfolate for MTHFR / Hcy→Met), or mitochondrial block (↑ glycolysis / ↓ TCA → lactate, NADH, ↓ ATP)

## Segment 3 — Subjective / Objective Assessment & Metabolomics Visit Goals (25:02–34:55)

**Speaker:** Greg Nakarelli

### 3A. Subjective Checklist
Energy (AM, throughout day, postprandial fatigue); weight / waist change; diet diary / preferences / allergies / supplements; activity level; sleep quality/latency; family + personal history of diabetes, CVD, HTN, lipids, MetS

### 3B. Objective Checklist
- Body composition: BMI, waist-hip ratio (WHR), BIA
- Vitals: BP, temperature, RR, pulse
- Nutrition-focused physical exam (hair, smell, oral, skin, nails, vibratory sense)

### 3C. Body Composition Pearls
- **Visceral fat** (not fat mass alone) drives inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, MetS risk
- **Metabolically healthy obesity (MHO)** ≈ more subcutaneous fat, preserved insulin sensitivity / glycemia / BP
- **Metabolically unhealthy obesity (MUO)** ≈ ↑ visceral fat + IR / T2DM risk
- **Metabolic BMI:** metabolite-derived BMI predicting IR / genetic risk / cardiac events beyond anthropometric BMI
- **WHR risk:** women >0.85; men >1.0
- **BIA:** useful for trending on *one* device (FFM, BCM, TBW, ICW); cross-device comparisons unreliable; does **not** measure bone density

### 3D. Other Objective Signals
- High BP → endothelial / metabolic dysfunction (“metabolic hypertension”); **too-low BP** also concerning for CVD/MetS risk
- Low body temp (~96°F common) → low metabolic rate / mild hypothermia association
- Higher resting pulse quartiles → higher MetS risk (men and women)
- Goals of visit: track subj/obj data; clarify optimization vs condition-specific goals; teach metabolomics vs genetics (MTHFR risk ≠ methylation dysfunction until metabolites show it); set expectations; choose review depth

## Segment 4 — Benefits, Limitations, AI & Digital Twinning (34:55–~40:00)

**Speaker:** Greg Nakarelli

### Benefits
- Pathway-level personalization; better diet/lifestyle/supplement strategy; environment impact visible (genome sequence largely fixed; metabolome retestable)

### Limitations
- Usually one matrix at a time; timing confounds; supplements/meds alter results; must integrate full clinical picture

### AI / Machine Learning
- Pattern recognition across metabolite panels exceeds unaided human capacity
- Typical analytic path: GC-MS or LC-MS → ML associations → health predictions

### ★ Digital Twinning
> Create a digital copy of the patient (history, symptoms, diagnoses, current supplements) → simulate interventions (e.g., CoQ10, then carnitine) → score impact on **health scores** (insulin sensitivity, CVD risk, etc.) → pick the recipe that improves scores; failed twins discarded.

- Ultimate precision stack: genome + metabolome + microbiome + biometrics + history
- Framed as objectivity check against practitioner supplement bias

## Segment 5 — Metabolic Syndrome: Criteria, Pathogenesis, Microbiome, Mitochondria (≈40:00–52:27)

**Speaker:** Greg Nakarelli

### Definition & Diagnosis
Cluster: high BP, high blood sugar, excess belly fat, abnormal cholesterol → risk of CVD, T2DM, stroke, Alzheimer’s. Characterized by **insulin resistance, oxidative stress, inflammation**.

**Traditional MetS (any 3 of 5):** waist circumference, triglycerides, HDL, blood pressure, fasting glucose  
**Co-signals:** microalbuminuria, ↑ uric acid, 2-hr / OGTT glucose abnormalities  
**2023 prevalence map:** some U.S. regions >50% MetS — not improving

### Pathogenesis Drivers
↑ TNF-α, IL-6, CRP; excess FFA; high leptin / low adiponectin; ↑ ROS; RAAS activation → chronic inflammation + IR

### Microbiome Axis
| Unfavorable | Favorable |
|---|---|
| ↑ Firmicutes / Bacteroidetes imbalance; ↓ Bifido / Lactobacillus; ↓ / absent **Akkermansia** | Mediterranean-pattern flora; ↑ SCFA producers |
| Western diet metabolites: choline → TMAO (worse with dysbiosis); LPS / pro-inflammatory tryptophan metabolites → endotoxemia | Cranberry / pomegranate foods support Akkermansia |

### Mitochondrial Axis
High calorie load, mtDNA mutations, toxins, glucolipotoxicity → ETC leak → ROS → impaired insulin signaling → cell death / hyperglycemia → MetS picture

### Metabolite / Pathway Hits Associated with MetS
- Molecules: fucose (↓ with high TG — brown seaweed / fatty-acid burning signal), MMA (B12), creatine, pyroglutamic acid (glutathione), phosphatidylcholine / other glycerophospholipids, amino acids (Ser, Gly, Arg)
- Pathways: arginine–proline, glutathione / taurine, carnitines / FFA, TCA / ketone oxidation
- **Health scores** from metabolite panels (dyslipidemia, BP, fasting glucose, composite MetS) make quarterly trending clinically usable

## Segment 6 — Interventions: Exercise Metabolites, Diet, Biotics, Bioactives (52:27–55:02)

**Speaker:** Greg Nakarelli

### Traditional Toolkit
Lifestyle first; meds often include statins, fibrates, metformin, acarbose, GLP-1 agonists (presented as conventional list, not personal endorsement)

### Exercise Metabolite Shifts
- Carnitines ↑ fatty-acid oxidation capacity
- Indole lactic acid (anti-inflammatory)
- **BAIBA (β-aminoisobutyric acid):** muscle-derived; ↑ adipose browning, fatty-acid oxidation, brain benefits — also available as supplement
- α-Ketoglutarate: anti-aging / muscle / brain / bone associations

### Diet Patterns Cited
Mediterranean (best studied), DASH, plant-based; carnivore evidence mixed / genotype-dependent (e.g., ApoE + saturated fat)

### Biotics & Bioactives
- Probiotic strain example (human data): *Lactobacillus gasseri*
- Prebiotics: caution in SIBO / gut-reactive patients
- Postbiotics / bacterial metabolites (SCFAs, antimicrobials) — relevant after lifelong antibiotic exposure
- **Akkermansia:** often low/undetectable in metabolically unhealthy; test before empiric supplementation (mucin regulator — too much/too little both issues)
- Food bioactives: coffee, green tea, curcumin; **bergamot** (favorite for hypercholesterolemia / hypertriglyceridemia); **cinnamon**

### Speaker Takeaways Slide
Metabolomics = metabolites ↔ health/disease; always pair with subjective/objective exam; MetS dysregulates Arg/Pro, glutathione/taurine, fatty-acid pathways; digital twinning selects interventions to raise health scores
