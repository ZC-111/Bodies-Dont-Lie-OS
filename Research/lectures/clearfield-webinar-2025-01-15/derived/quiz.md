- A) Perfect circadian alignment
- B) **Enzyme deficiency/inhibition or low cofactor/substrate** ✓

### Q3. Why does Nakarelli emphasize sample timing for metabolite testing?
**Answer:** Organ systems and pathways are circadian; enzyme/gene expression (e.g., adiponectin-related) varies by time of day. Shift work, sleep loss, travel, and aging alter timing — mistimed samples confound interpretation.

### Q5. True or False: An MTHFR risk variant on a genetic test proves that the methylation cycle is currently dysfunctional.
**Answer:** **False.** Genetics = risk. Actual dysfunction is shown by metabolite shifts (homocysteine, methionine, and related methylation metabolites) plus clinical context.

## Section C — Interventions, Products & Clinical Judgment (6 questions)

### Q15. Which dietary bioactive did Nakarelli call one of his favorites for hypercholesterolemia / hypertriglyceridemia?
- A) High-dose fructose
- B) **Bergamot** ✓
- C) Trans fats

### Q16. What is Nakarelli’s main clinical concern about using statins as MetS therapy?
**Answer:** Statins can worsen mitochondrial dysfunction (a MetS hallmark), may contribute to myositis in poor metabolizers, and may increase arterial calcification in some; they don’t treat root cause. Soft-plaque cases may still benefit from calcification; lifestyle first (AHA) preferred for typical patients.

### Q17. Why is he cautious about rapamycin for anti-aging?
- A) It is too inexpensive
- B) **mTOR suppression can impair immune mobilization and has worsened autoimmunity / infection burden in some patients** ✓
- C) It always raises ApoB
- D) It has no biologic activity

### Q18. Theorem Aristotle metabolomics pricing quoted to the group was:
- A) $50 practitioner / $100 retail
- B) **$400 practitioner / $750 retail** ✓
- C) $600 practitioner / $1,100 retail (that was the microbiome test)
- D) Free with CME

### Q19. What analytic advantage does Theorem’s microbiome test claim over common 16S / targeted stool panels?
**Answer:** Whole-genome sequencing to strain level across the microbial census (bacteria, archaea, fungi, viruses), plus **quantitative counts** so digital-twin interventions and retests can show measurable shifts — unlike vague presence/absence reports or limited targeted panels (e.g., GI-MAP).

### Q20. Short answer: What is digital twinning used for in this workflow?
**Answer:** Build a data-rich digital copy of the patient, simulate candidate diet/lifestyle/supplement interventions, and select those that most improve metabolite-derived **health scores** — reducing guesswork and practitioner bias.
