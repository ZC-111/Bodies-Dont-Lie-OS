## Section A — Myths, ECS & Endocannabinoid Deficiency (8 questions)

### Q1. Why does Quinn say fatal cannabis overdose is unlikely compared with opioids?
- A) Cannabinoids are always non-psychoactive
- B) **Cannabinoid receptors are absent from the brainstem respiratory centers** ✓
- C) CBD completely blocks THC absorption
- D) FAAH instantly clears all exogenous cannabinoids

### Q2. True or False: Quinn teaches that CBD is “not psychoactive.”
**Answer:** **False.** CBD acts in the brain (e.g., anxiety pathways) so it is psychoactive, but it is **not intoxicating** like THC.

### Q3. What is cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome’s definitive management per this lecture?
- A) Ondansetron and continue THC at lower dose
- B) Switch from indica to sativa
- C) **Strictly stop THC; hot showers may temporarily stop vomiting** ✓
- D) High-dose CBD isolate only

### Q4. The endocannabinoid system’s three structural pillars are:
**Answer:** **Receptors** (CB1/CB2/CB3-class), **endogenous ligands** (anandamide, 2-AG, PEA, etc.), and **enzymes** that synthesize/degrade them (notably **FAAH**).

### Q5. Stress worsens endocannabinoid tone primarily by:
- A) Upregulating CB1 on muscle
- B) **Increasing FAAH, which breaks down endocannabinoids** ✓
- C) Raising dietary PEA absorption
- D) Blocking TRPV channels permanently

### Q6. Per Quinn’s framing, approximately what share of brain transmembrane receptors are cannabinoid (CB) receptors?
- A) 5%
- B) 15%
- C) **~58%** ✓
- D) 90%

### Q7. What age cutoff does Quinn emphasize for avoiding THC, and why?
**Answer:** **Under 25** — adolescent/young-adult brains remain vulnerable to THC effects on cognitive development.

### Q8. Name three lifestyle contributors to endocannabinoid deficiency syndrome discussed in the lecture.
**Answer:** Any of: **poor diet, lack of exercise, chronic stress, gut dysfunction / poor lifestyle hygiene**; cannabis may be used as an ECS “supplement” after fundamentals.

## Section C — Clinical Uses, Safety & Product Selection (6 questions)

### Q15. For anxiety, which product strategy does Quinn favor?
- A) High-THC flower smoked PRN only
- B) **CBD-dominant (or broad/full spectrum with calming terpenes); keep THC low** ✓
- C) Epidiolex for all adults with GAD
- D) Marinol alone at bedtime

### Q16. PEA (palmitoylethanolamide) is clinically appealing for pain because:
**Answer:** It is an **endocannabinoid-like** molecule that enhances ECS tone, reduces mast-cell activation, acts at GPR55/119, competitively interacts with FAAH pathways, has supportive pain meta-analyses (~300–1200 mg/day), and is underused partly because it is **hard to patent**.

### Q17. List four drug classes Quinn flags for cannabis interaction vigilance.
**Answer:** Any four of: **anticoagulants, sedative CNS depressants, SSRIs, antipsychotics (esp. youth + heavy THC), stimulants, immunosuppressants, opioids, PPIs, antiseizure medications**.

### Q18. What must a trustworthy cannabis COA cover before you recommend a product?
**Answer:** Third-party testing for **heavy metals, pesticides/herbicides/fungicides, molds/microbes, cannabinoid content/ratios, and terpene content**; plus understanding extraction residues and total THC (sum of related species), not a single Δ9 line.

### Q19. Oral Δ9-THC is converted in the gut/liver to what metabolite, and why does that matter?
- A) CBN — shorter action
- B) **11-hydroxy-THC — roughly 3–4× more potent and longer-lasting; useful for bedtime/pain, risky if patients redose early** ✓
- C) CBDVA — non-active
- D) Anandamide — identical to endogenous tone

### Q20. Opening tip: virgin coconut oil (VCO) vs refined coconut oil — what is the key clinical caution?
**Answer:** VCO retains polyphenols but can raise **both HDL and LDL** (lauric acid/saturated fat/MCT). Use in **moderation**; cardiology preference still leans to EVOO; ask whether any LDL rise is **large buoyant** vs **small dense** particles rather than treating total cholesterol alone.
