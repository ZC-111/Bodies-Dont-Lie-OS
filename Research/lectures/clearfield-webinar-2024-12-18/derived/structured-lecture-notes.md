## Segment 3 — When Hormone Tracking Helps (Clinical Settings) (13:30–23:28)

**Speaker:** Rosemary McKenzie

### ★ Core Thesis
> Hormone tracking is not only for "are they ovulating?" — it matters for **every woman with ovaries and fluctuating hormones**, and continuous urinary data beats a single serum snapshot.

### Clinical Settings
| Setting | Why track |
|---|---|
| Known/suspected hormone abnormalities | PCOS, endometriosis, PMS/PMDD — see underlying pattern |
| Coming off birth control | Watch return of **coordinated** hormones |
| Postpartum / post-miscarriage | Return of coordinated cycles |
| Hysterectomy with ovaries retained / uterine ablation | Lost menstrual "landmark" but hormones still fluctuate |
| Fertility / TTC | Full E3G + LH + PDG + FSH vs LH-only OPK |
| Perimenopause | Stage transition beyond STRAW FSH alone |
| Athletes / research | Follicular vs luteal phase response (glucose, training) |

### Why Urinary Metabolites vs Serum
- Urinary hormones reflect **prior serum** levels (slight delay; 2015 correlation data shown)
- Tracked metabolites: **E3G** (estrogen), **PDG** (progesterone), **LH**, **FSH**
- Small at-home monitor study (n=4): area-under-curve algorithms for E2/P vs E3G/PDG identified follicular→luteal transition
- Continuous pattern > one blood draw ("what were they 6 hours ago / yesterday / tomorrow?")
- Patient compliance: like a **continuous glucose monitor** — immediate feedback on lifestyle adherence

### Lifestyle Feedback Example
- PCOS patient with very high estrogen → vegan diet with high soy → diet change → urinary metabolites improved within **~3 weeks**

### Research Context
- Universities: Johns Hopkins, Melbourne, UBC, others
- University of Toronto pilot (n=49): Fitbit + CGM + urinary metabolites → clear **follicular vs luteal glucose shift**
- Upcoming ~50-patient, 3-cycle study with temperature, blood, serial ultrasound (Marquette third-party; expected ~May)

## Segment 4 — Fertility Pilot & Pattern Interpretation (23:28–41:30)

**Speaker:** Rosemary McKenzie

### IVF-Eligible Pilot
- Patients qualified for IVF (TTC >1 year); median age 34
- Diagnoses included PCOS, diminished ovarian reserve, endometriosis, hypothalamic amenorrhea
- Intake visit → home hormone monitoring with provider
- **44% pregnant after 2 months** working with provider
- Comparison benchmarks cited: PCOS conception ~25–30%; IUI ~20%; IVF ~47%
- **77%** successfully ovulating by end of pilot
- High engagement when a provider reviews the data with the patient

### Case Snapshots
1. **Hypothalamic amenorrhea:** Flatlined hormones (May) → conception cycle (September) after provider care + ovulation induction
2. **DOR + obesity + luteal defect + hypothyroidism:** Uncoordinated hormones → Metabolic nutrition, Trulicity, Clomid, HCG trigger, progesterone → conception cycle

### Three Pattern Questions
1. **Are hormones coordinated?** Estrogen rise → LH surge → PDG rise? Bleeding ≠ ovulation (withdrawal/breakthrough bleeds)
2. **Within reference ranges?** Urine units ≠ serum — learn the curve language; majority of points should fit typical pattern
3. **Cycle length & variability?** Ideal ~25–35 days with ≤3 days cycle-to-cycle variation (not "sometimes 25, sometimes 35")

### Hormone-by-Hormone Pearls

**LH (easiest)**
- Expect low baseline except **1–3 day** surge
- Problematic: chronically high baseline LH (desensitizes ovary — common in PCOS) or surge lasting ~5–8 days
- Acceptable surge shapes: classic spike, biphasic, or short plateau (still within 1–3 days)
- Analysis cited: 1,624 self-reported conception cycles

**Estrogen / E3G**
- Ask: Does E rise before LH then decline? Does it have a second (smaller) luteal rise? "Large hill then small hill"
- Flat/low follicular E → concern for follicular development (restrictive eating, low fat/fiber, over-exercise)
- Missing luteal E → concern for poor ovulation / LUF / suboptimal corpus luteum
- **Reverse pattern** (low follicular / high luteal E) → PMS, heavy periods; single blood test or DUTCH timed 7 days post-O often miss severity
- Case: high prolactin + reverse E pattern → treated → conceived within 3 cycles after wedding

**Progesterone / PDG**
- Nice bell curve, **higher than estrogen** on the graph
- Normal luteal length **11–16 days**; implantation often ~7–10 days after ovulation
- Luteal <11 days → risk early miscarriage / failed implantation
- Single mid-luteal serum P can look "fine" on one day while continuous data shows only one good day

**FSH (less data; ~300 patients in analysis shown)**
- Should surge **with LH** for ovulatory event; missing FSH surge → failed ovulation attempt
- Early-cycle high FSH → DOR / perimenopause
- Ongoing elevated FSH → perimenopause, POI/DOR, chemo/drug effects

## Segment 5 — PCOS, Postpartum & Perimenopause Cases (41:30–56:03)

**Speaker:** Rosemary McKenzie

### PCOS Case (26yo, irregular 31–52 days, TTC >1 year)
- Cycle 1: Near-normal looking but **warning** — E not timed with LH; reverse E pattern
- Cycle 2: Apparent LH surge that **failed** (non-ovulatory), then secondary E rise + second LH that finally triggered ovulation (confirmed by PDG)
- Clinical trap: "Test 7 days after ovulation" keyed off the **failed** surge → falsely low P / useless DUTCH timing; true luteal was later (~cycle day 37)

### Postpartum / Low-Hormone Cases
- Regular cycles ≠ optimal hormones
- Example: BMI 33.6, breastfeeding, 2 years postpartum — October chart decent → June chart collapsed E and worse luteal → investigate restrictive dieting to lose baby weight
- Short luteal / high luteal E / low PDG as recurring warning signs

### Perimenopause Deep Dive
- STRAW criteria: standardized but **not specific enough** (FSH variable → high → variable again)
- Stephanie Faubion / Hayes-type framing: track **FSH + estradiol** together — high E can suppress FSH mid-transition
- Age ≠ pattern: 42yo with ongoing high FSH, uncoordinated LH, low E, cycle day 311 chart; 49yo with high fluctuating E and no LH/P changes; 52yo still coordinated but **weak short luteal P**

### ★ Cooperative HRT Pearl
> In perimenopause, if she still has LH surges, time progesterone **after the LH surge** ("cooperative HRT") rather than blind cycle-day 15–28 or daily dosing — until coordination is lost, then daily/cyclical may fit better.

## Segment 6 — Q&A: Postmenopause, Cost, Practice Integration (56:03–1:18:42)

**Speakers:** Rosemary McKenzie, Bill Clearfield, Dr. Nario, Dr. Borja (Speaker 5)

### Postmenopause
- Least data; most use within ~2–3 years after final period
- Cannot back-calculate serum from urine metabolite value
- Use for **pattern/response**, not therapeutic serum equivalence

### HRT Visibility on Urinary Metabolites
| Route | Urinary metabolite effect |
|---|---|
| Oral / vaginal / injectable progesterone | Often **ceiling** PDG (~top of scale, e.g. 30) |
| Transdermal patch/cream/oil | Only **slight** serum → slight urine change; underlying ovarian pattern still visible |

### Cost & Workflow vs Cycle Mapping
- First morning urine → dip wand → analyzer
- **~3 months near-daily monitoring ≈ cost of one cycle map** → more data points, real-time, flexible cadence (qOD / q3d)
- Does **not** replace: testosterone/androgens, cortisol/adrenal pathways, full DUTCH metabolite panels (2/4/16-OH etc.)
- Continuous monitoring until pattern optimized; pause when stable; restart at life transitions

### Live Case — Post-OCP Amenorrhea (28yo)
- Off OCP since February (~10 months amenorrhea); acne; possible PCOS history
- Labs (May, random): total E ~86, T ~43, P ~1.1, FSH 4.4
- McKenzie: perfect candidate for **every-other-day** monitoring + intervention; "wait a little" is not enough at 10 months

### Device Logistics (pre-product-name section)
- FDA-cleared; patient can buy without Rx
- Analyzer (one-time) + single-use wands; E+LH+P on one wand; FSH separate wand
- HIPAA provider dashboard with same-day results
- Can start testing before first visit (during waitlist)

### Cooperative P Timing Example
- LH surge ~cycle day 18–19 → start bioidentical P ~**3 days after LH surge** (not blindly day 13)

### PCOS Hormonal Phenotypes Observed
- High estrogen PCOS
- Suppressed estrogen (long wait then rise)
- Reverse E pattern PCOS  
→ Continuous data revealing subtypes never visible on sparse labs

## Segment 7 — Mira Product Deep-Dive & Evidence (1:19:01–1:44:00)

**Speaker:** Rosemary McKenzie (Speaker 3) — recording paused then product discussion

### Mira vs Competitors
- **Mira:** immunofluorescent technology (lab-grade approach)
- Competitors cited (Inito, Premom, Clearblue LH strips, etc.): nano-gold technology
- ~**$330** for analyzer + 3 boxes of wands ≈ 3 months if testing ~20 days/cycle (skip heavy bleed days)
- Cost reducers: every-other-day; luteal-only denser testing; intermittent cycles of monitoring
- Provider options: patient discount code, affiliate (up to **17%**), drop-ship, bulk office stock; holiday promo through year-end
- Sample analyzer + wand pack via QR / form; dashboard + resources; max 2 devices/office (memory erasable / shareable)
- Wand shelf life ~12 months US / ~10 months international; ships widely

### Evidence & Comparisons
- Correlates with serum E2/LH/P (small n=4 shown; larger n=50 pending)
- Marquette University third-party evaluation favored Mira vs other urinary devices; expanding study vs blood + ultrasound
- Head-to-head with **DUTCH cycle map**: DUTCH may not index to ovulation — case ovulated day 10 with only one pre-O value on DUTCH; Mira values otherwise lined up with DUTCH when both run
- Saliva: no direct Mira-vs-saliva study; Mira positioned vs **blood + ultrasound** gold standards
- Dosing stance: not for therapeutic level calculation — track **response/pattern** and adjust by outcome

### Perimenopause Case (50yo)
- Heavy periods, breast tenderness, hair loss, mood crisis (anxiety/depression/suicidality)
- Started coordinated but low P → progressed to high fluctuating E (labeled estrogen dominance / anovulatory)
- Lifestyle + supplements + **transdermal progesterone** → less E fluctuation, patient felt better (PDG not ceilinged because transdermal)
- Note: other providers might choose estradiol patch on same chart — tool shows pattern; treatment still clinician judgment
- **Pellets:** no Mira data yet
- Reishi mushroom / "MiraRay"-type supplements discussed anecdotally for estrogen control — no Mira study data
