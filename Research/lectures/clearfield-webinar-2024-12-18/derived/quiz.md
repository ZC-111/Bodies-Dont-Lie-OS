## Section A — Clinical Indications & Fundamentals (7 questions)

### Q1. Beyond trying to conceive, which clinical setting did McKenzie emphasize as especially suited to urinary hormone tracking?
- A) Fully established menopause >10 years
- B) **Post-hysterectomy with ovaries retained or post-uterine ablation (lost bleed landmark)** ✓
- C) Acute MI risk stratification
- D) Male hypogonadism as first-line workup

> *Bleed is the usual "light post"; without it, hormones may still fluctuate and need tracking.*

### Q2. What urinary estrogen metabolite is typically tracked on these home monitors?
- A) Estrone sulfate only
- B) 16-OH estrone
- C) **E3G (estriol-3-glucuronide)** ✓
- D) Free estradiol in urine without conjugation

### Q3. Why does McKenzie prefer continuous urinary metabolite tracking over a single serum draw?
**Answer:** Serum is a snapshot; urinary serial data shows the full cycle pattern (yesterday / today / tomorrow), improving detection of uncoordinated hormones and response to interventions.

### Q4. In the IVF-eligible pilot she presented, what percentage of participants conceived after two months of provider-guided home monitoring?
- A) 20%
- B) 30%
- C) **44%** ✓
- D) 77%

> *77% was the rate who successfully ovulated by the end of the pilot — different endpoint.*

### Q5. True or False: A menstrual bleed on the chart always means the patient ovulated.
**Answer:** **False.** Bleeding can be withdrawal or breakthrough without ovulation; look for coordinated E rise → LH surge → PDG rise.

### Q6. Ideal cycle length variability from cycle to cycle should usually be:
- A) ≤7 days
- B) **≤3 days** ✓
- C) Any range within 25–35 days is fine
- D) Exactly 28 days every cycle

> *Cycles that bounce between 25 and 35 days "fit" the range but have too much variation.*

### Q7. Standalone LH ovulation predictor kits are limited because they track:
- A) Four hormones poorly
- B) **Only one of up to four trackable hormones (misses E, P, FSH pattern)** ✓
- C) Only FSH
- D) Only progesterone

### Q9. The preferred estrogen pattern McKenzie describes is:
- A) Flat follicular and high luteal
- B) Equal follicular and luteal hills
- C) **Large follicular hill, then smaller luteal hill after LH** ✓
- D) Only luteal estrogen, no follicular rise

### Q10. A "reverse" estrogen pattern (inadequate follicular E, excess luteal E) commonly presents with:
**Answer:** PMS symptoms and/or heavy periods; continuous tracking shows severity that one timed blood test or mid-luteal DUTCH may miss.

### Q11. What luteal phase length range did McKenzie cite as normal, and what risk attaches to luteal phases <11 days?
**Answer:** Normal **11–16 days**. Shorter luteal phases increase risk of early miscarriage / failed implantation (implantation often ~7–10 days post-ovulation).

## Section C — Perimenopause, HRT & Device Practice (6 questions)

### Q15. Why is STRAW staging alone often insufficient for perimenopausal women?
**Answer:** FSH is labeled variable then high then variable again; high estradiol mid-transition can suppress FSH, so a single FSH (or age alone) mis-stages the patient. Continuous E + FSH (+LH/P) patterns clarify where she is.

### Q16. What is "cooperative HRT" progesterone timing in a woman who still has LH surges?
- A) Fixed days 1–14 only
- B) **Start bioidentical progesterone after the LH surge (e.g., ~3 days post-surge) to support the natural luteal process** ✓
- C) Only continuous combined oral contraceptives
- D) Pellets exclusively

### Q17. How do oral/vaginal/injectable progesterone vs transdermal progesterone typically appear on urinary PDG?
**Answer:** Systemic oral/vaginal/injectable often drive PDG to a **ceiling** effect; transdermal usually causes only a **slight** rise, so the underlying ovarian pattern remains visible.

### Q18. Approximate cost comparison McKenzie gave for Mira-style monitoring vs one cycle map:
- A) Daily Mira costs 10× a DUTCH cycle map
- B) **~3 months of near-daily Mira monitoring ≈ one cycle-mapping test** ✓
- C) Mira requires insurance prior auth always
- D) Analyzer must be prescribed as a Schedule II device

### Q19. Which analytes does Mira currently *not* replace, per the lecture?
**Answer:** Testosterone/androgens, cortisol/adrenal metabolic pathways, and detailed estrogen metabolite panels (e.g., 2/4/16-OH) — still need other labs when those questions matter.
