### Q2. Why does Hartman discourage reflexive Tylenol for pediatric fever?
**Answer:** Fever is a host defense that delays viral replication; febrile seizures are usually not pathologic (trauma risk is the concern); acetaminophen depletes glutathione needed under oxidative stress.

### Q5. Name three non-antibiotic sore-throat tools Hartman uses in practice.

## Section B — Hormones & Cardiovascular Myths (6 questions)

### Q6. Clearfield cites total testosterone below which male threshold as associated with major depression risk?

### Q7. True or False: Per the lecture’s Morgentaler-aligned view, TRT reliably accelerates prostate cancer.
**Answer:** **False.** Incidence of positive biopsies was lowest in hypogonadal men on TRT; low T/DHT often track with higher PSA / larger prostates in practice.

### Q8. What is Clearfield’s typical physiologic total-T target window discussed (non-bodybuilder)?
- A) 1500–2000 ng/dL with E2 driven to zero
- B) **~700–900 ng/dL** ✓
- C) Always stay under 400 ng/dL
- D) Free T only; ignore totals

### Q9. How does the lecture distinguish TRT-related erythrocytosis from polycythemia vera?
**Answer:** TRT erythrocytosis: RBC↑ only, EPO high, no PV genetic mutations, hepcidin suppressed. PV: often WBC/platelet↑, EPO low, ~97% carry characteristic mutations — do not treat TRT erythrocytosis as PV with hydroxyurea without genetics / proper workup.

### Q10. Meta-analysis association discussed for vasectomy:
- A) Reduced prostate cancer risk
- B) **Increased prostate cancer risk** ✓
- C) No oncologic signals
- D) Only infertility risk

### Q11. Why does Hartman prefer unpasteurized dairy fats when discussing saturated fat myths?
**Answer:** He argues pasteurization increases trans fats in milk products, which he considers cardiotoxic; prefers grass-fed beef/butter and raw dairy when discussing ancestral fat sources.
