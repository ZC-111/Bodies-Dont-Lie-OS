## Section A — Nitric Oxide Fundamentals (7 questions)

### Q1. By approximately what age is NOS-pathway nitric oxide production functioning at ~15% of youthful capacity (per Shirley)?
- A) Age 30
- B) Age 40
- C) **Age 60** ✓
- D) Age 80

> *~50% by age 40; ~15% by age 60. Women hold capacity better until menopause.*

### Q2. What is the rate-limiting cofactor that keeps NOS coupled as a dimer?
- A) NADPH alone
- B) Arginine
- C) **BH4 (tetrahydrobiopterin)** ✓
- D) Nitrate

### Q3. When NOS is uncoupled, the electron from NADPH is handed primarily to:
- A) Arginine → nitric oxide
- B) **Oxygen → superoxide** ✓
- C) Nitrate → nitrite
- D) Glutathione → GSSG

### Q4. Why does Shirley warn against arginine supplementation in aging / chronically ill patients?
**Answer:** If BH4 is depleted and NOS is uncoupled, arginine feeding increases **superoxide** rather than NO. Positive arginine data were largely in young fit male athletes. Replete BH4 (and use the nitrate pathway) first.

### Q5. Which molecule uniquely reduces BH3 back to BH4?
- A) Vitamin E
- B) **Vitamin C** ✓
- C) Glutathione
- D) Melatonin

### Q6. Hemoglobin requires nitric oxide attached to a cysteine residue in order to:
- A) Bind carbon dioxide
- B) **Deliver / offload oxygen to tissues** ✓
- C) Form HbA1c
- D) Activate platelets

> *You can be oxygen-saturated and still hypoxic at the tissue if NO-deficient.*

### Q7. True or False: Under oxidative stress, upregulated iNOS always means beneficial high nitric oxide output.
**Answer:** **False.** Oxidative stress uncouples NOS isoforms; upregulated iNOS often behaves as a **superoxide generator**, not a reliable NO producer.

## Section C — Therapeutics, Monitoring & Adjuncts (6 questions)

### Q15. What dietary nitrate intake does the DASH diet deliver that Shirley uses as a "no overdose" benchmark?
- A) 100–200 mg/day
- B) 400–600 mg/day
- C) **1200–1500 mg/day** ✓
- D) 5000 mg/day

### Q16. A patient takes nitrate capsules or a large salad and saliva nitrite strips still fail to turn pink after 1–1.5 hours. What should you investigate first?
**Answer:** Oral microbiome disruption that blocks nitrate → nitrite reduction — mouthwash, fluoride/whitening toothpaste, antibiotics or herbal antimicrobials. Continued nitrate can help rebuild reducers over days–weeks.

### Q17. Which compounding stack was presented for night/morning intranasal use?
**Answer:** **Night:** progesterone loaded with cyclodextrin. **Morning:** oxytocin with cyclodextrin. Rationale: sleep via GABA-like progesterone metabolites; morning "reset"/clearance physiology via oxytocin.

### Q18. Per McConnell / Castelli, what are the three non-negotiables for high-dose niacin titration?
**Answer:** Always **with food**, in **divided doses**, **start low and build slowly** — never as a fasting bolus. Flush usually fades by ~2 months if compliant; muting flush may lose PPAR benefits.

### Q19. In homozygous MTHFR 677 TT, what riboflavin intervention was cited for blood pressure, and why does it connect to NO?
**Answer:** **1.6 mg riboflavin** → ~8–12 mmHg systolic drop (het/wild-type no benefit). Flavins are electron carriers on the NOS enzyme; folate/BH4 pathways also keep NOS coupled (CSPPT folate + ACE-I stroke data also cited).

### Q20. List three practical EMF-mitigation / NO-support counseling points for the "Archie Bunker" patient who won't change diet overnight.
**Answer (any three):** (1) Get phone **off the body** (pockets/bras); use speakerphone; (2) start daily **nitrate** capsules even before diet change; (3) **grounding** bed/mattress cover overnight; (4) add **melatonin** as oxidative-stress partner; (5) reduce evening screen light that keeps cortisol high; (6) consider saliva nitrite strip monitoring.
