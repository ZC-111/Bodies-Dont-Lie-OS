## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Creatine Monohydrate vs Pyruvate (14:03–19:10)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario, DO (Speaker 4)  
**Pearl add-on:** Stefan Hartman, DO (Speaker 5) — cystatin C

### ★ Core Thesis
> Creatine has left the gym and entered the exam room. **Monohydrate remains first-line.** Pyruvate looks better on paper, not on people. Creatine raises **serum creatinine**, not renal injury, in healthy individuals.

### Form Comparison
| Form | Composition | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Creatine monohydrate** | ~88% creatine by weight; near-complete GI absorption | Gold standard; hundreds of RCTs; saturates muscle + brain stores |
| **Creatine pyruvate** | ~60% creatine / 40% pyruvate; highly soluble | Higher plasma levels theoretically; **no proven superior tissue uptake or outcomes** vs monohydrate; more expensive, mixes easier |

**Citations referenced:** Jagim et al. 2008; Kreider et al., *JISSN* 2017

### Dosing Pearl — Muscle vs Brain
- **Muscle saturation:** 3–5 g/day monohydrate
- **Brain / cognition / neuroprotection:** neurons have slower transport and higher demand under stress → studies use **10–20 g/day** (mild TBI, depression adjunct, Parkinson's, cognitive fatigue)
  - Rae et al. 2003 — working memory
  - Lyoo et al. 2012 *Am J Psychiatry* — adjunct major depression
  - Dechent et al. 1999 *Am J Physiol* — MRS brain uptake

### Kidney Myth
- Creatine → creatinine conversion elevates serum creatinine → false alarms / unnecessary stopping
- Safety reviews: Poortmans & Francaux 2000; Gualano et al. 2012; long-term use (to ~5 years) safe in older adults and T2DM when kidneys healthy
- **Avoid / monitor closely:** advanced CKD, AKI, concurrent nephrotoxins

### Clinical Pearl (Hartman)
> On creatine users, order **cystatin C** (~$40 LabCorp) to assess kidney function without creatine-driven creatinine artifact — avoids unnecessary nephrology referral.

## Segment 3 — Dr. Anna Hurst: A Cleaner, Better You — Biotransformation & Detoxification (22:32–1:08:30)

**Speaker:** Dr. Anna Hurst (Speaker 6)

### ★ Core Thesis
> Lay “detox” ≠ medical detox from addiction. Clinically, use **biotransformation**: converting endogenous/exogenous toxins to excretable forms. Prep the terrain (mitochondria, nutrients, gut) before aggressive clearance — especially in chemically sensitive / frail patients.

### 3A. Practice Model
- Board-certified family medicine + naturopathic/functional blend in one clinic (dietitian, health coach, trainer, massage on staff)
- **History, history, history** — chronological 10-year timeline when visit time is short
- Labs: traditional ranges + **optimal** targets
- Genomics reserved for complex / multi-provider failures / strong family history (~40% of panel)
- Uses meds **and** nutraceuticals; both change biochemistry when pathophys is understood

### 3B. Biotransformation Phases
```
Lipid-soluble toxin
  → Phase 1 (CYP450) → reactive intermediates (half polar)
  → Phase 2 (conjugation: methylation, glutathione, acetylation…) → water-soluble
  → Phase 3 (GI clearance / microbiome) → feces, urine, sweat, breath
```
- Phase 2 backup = “6 lanes → 2 lanes” → recirculation of intermediates → nutrient drain
- **Phase 1 support:** B vitamins, milk thistle, cruciferous vegetables
- **Phase 2 support:** glutathione, NAC, methionine, **calcium D-glucarate** (also gut-bug–produced; estrogen metabolism)
- **Phase 3:** prebiotics/probiotics; treat dysbiosis
- Detox nutrients ≈ mitochondrial/Krebs nutrients (B1–B3, glutathione, CoQ10/ubiquinol, Mg, Fe)

### 3C. Clinical Red Flag — Do Not Aggressive-Detox First
> Frail patient with long allergy lists (perfumes, gasoline, meds) → nurse typing 15 minutes → **rebuild foundations first**, not jump to major detox protocols.

### 3D. Detox Genomics (selected SNPs discussed)
| Gene / pathway | Clinical angle |
|---|---|
| **GPX / GSTP / “G-” genes** | Glutathione / heavy metal / pesticide handling |
| **NQO1** | CoQ10 + vitamin E recycle; 1 copy ↓ ~67%; homozygous ↓ up to ~98% → prefer **ubiquinol** |
| **EPHX1** | Aromatic amines (pesticides, dyes, diesel, tobacco, plastics); aflatoxin; post–breast implant sensitivity clue |
| **ALDH1** | Alcohol intermediate (formaldehyde-like) more toxic (~13–16%); morning fog after wine |
| **GC** | Vitamin D carrier; double variant → may need ongoing D; don’t yank when levels “normalize” |

### 3E. Gut Toxins & “Leaky Gut”
- Prefer term **intestinal hyperpermeability** (medical literature)
- Endotoxins / exotoxins / LPS → inflammation; LPS–neurodegeneration link (microglia, amyloid, tau; *H. pylori* endotoxin examples)
- Toxins stored in **adipose** (white fat warehouse); weight-loss plateau on long fasts may be protective if clearance capacity is poor
- **Mold spores** → allergic; **mycotoxins** → immunogenic + hormonal disruption (IL-1β, MMP-9, TGF-β); #1 Rx = **remediate or leave the environment**

### 3F. Heavy Metals & Environment
- Blood metals = acute exposure; body-burden often needs **provoked** testing (limited published reference standards; interpret carefully)
- Taxi-driver study (2016): ↑ Cd/Pb/As + ↑ IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, homocysteine; ↓ glutathione
- Symptom/SNP maps for As, Pb, Cd, Hg reviewed; fracking downwind radioactivity / BTEX concerns cited
- Air pollution cohort (~4,000 nonsmokers NE US): ↑ fibrinogen, IL-6, myeloperoxidase

### 3G. Plastics / PFAS & Practical Avoidance
- 2024 bottled-water finding: ~**240,000 nanoplastic particles/L** (10–100× prior estimates)
- Hormone disruption: early puberty, endometriosis, fertility, PCOS stubbornness
- Tools: EWG Dirty Dozen / Clean 15; bamboo/100% cotton bedding; mattress off-gassing; ≤5 ingredient foods
- Sparkling water: some brands high PFAS; **Perrier (green bottle)** cited as relatively lower among discussed options

### 3H. Modified Citrus Pectin (MCP)
- Pediatric arsenic/lead/cadmium study: PectaSol ~15 g/day × 6 days (day 6 → 20 g) → ↑ urinary Cd ~150%, Pb >500% by day 6
- **Start low** — 15 g/day upfront → severe bloating
- Empirically used as binder for metals, plastics, forever chemicals; may improve galectin-3 / cystatin C on longevity panels

## Segment 5 — Q&A: Chelation, Genetics, Mold, Glutathione, Migraines, CBG (1:08:38–end)

### Chelation / Uranium

- Nario: **EDTA** (IV > rectal; oral absorption questionable) may lower uranium clinically; Hurst uses EDTA/DMSA (IV/rectal; DMSA also oral) — anecdotal ↓ metals

### Genetics / ApoE4
- Genetics fixed; **genomics/epigenetics** modifiable
- ApoE4 alone ≠ Alzheimer’s; needs co-factors (e.g., TOMM40 mitochondrial component; APOC1 “switch”)
- Use as **motivational map**, not fatalism; cost limits universal testing (~40% of her patients)

### Mold Protocol Philosophy
- At Cleveland Clinic, Hurst drafted literature-backed protocol interviewing Shoemaker, Neil Nathan, et al.
- Shoemaker labs “ahead of time” but limited specificity/validity for mold-only attribution
- Prefers **hodgepodge tailored to patient**: leave mold first, support homeostasis, empiric antifungals/binders (charcoal — vet literature) as needed; Andrew Campbell blood testing discussed by panel

### Forever Chemicals
- Quest PFAS blood test ~$300; insurance rarely covers
- Interventions: avoidance + glutathione / conjugation / liver pathway support guided by genomics when available

### Glutathione
- Prefers jumping to glutathione when GST/GPX SNPs impair NAC conversion
- Brand lean: **Pure Encapsulations** (Cleveland Clinic IRB quality experience)
- Maintenance ~**100–200 mg/day**; treatment/detox doses **500–1500+ mg** → warn for Herx-like symptoms
- Monitor **RBC glutathione** (commercial labs); keep high-normal
- Reduced GSH cheap / better taste; liposomal better intracellular delivery (mucosal GSH ↑ cited); ASEA water claim of ↑ intracellular GSH discussed by panelist

### Vertiginous Migraines
- Consider RBC GSH; timeline for environmental trigger; gentle T3 if DIO2 / low CNS T3; **always pair thyroid push with adrenal support** if reverse T3 high
- CBD + **CBG**: CBG = parent cannabinoid (non-psychoactive); more sleep than CBD; neuroinflammation/pain

### Advanced Detox Mentioned by Panel
- Plasmapheresis × ~5 sessions (tissue re-equilibration); cognition ↑ claim ~40%; expensive
- EDTA course teaching: often many sessions (e.g., 33× cited) for metals
