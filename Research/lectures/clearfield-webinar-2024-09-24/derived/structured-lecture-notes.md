## Segment 3 — Main Lecture: Drug-Induced Nutrient Depletion (DIND) (16:23–~32:00)

**Speaker:** Dr. Violet Bailey

### ★ Core Thesis
> Roughly **half of medicines in clinical use for ~20+ years** have documented nutrient-depleting effects. Polypharmacy multiplies interaction risk — and DIND is an unmet, underappreciated need.

### Framing & Sources
- References: **Dr. James LaValle** (*Drug-Induced Nutrient Depletion Handbook*, 2nd ed., out of print) and **Pam Smith** (anti-aging fellowship director)
- US polypharmacy: **2+ drugs ↑ ~20%** in 10 years; **5+ drugs ↑ ~70%**
- Sweden (≈2020 slide): **5–7 meds → 4×** drug–drug interaction risk; **8–10 → 8×**; **14+ → 20×**
- Bailey uses LaValle’s handbook on **every new patient** and has a billing code path for nutrition assessment labs when depletion is identified

### Drug-Class Depletion Map (as presented)

| Drug / class | Key nutrients depleted |
|---|---|
| **Oral contraceptives** | Fatty acids; B1, B2, B3, B6, B12; Mg, Se, Zn, tyrosine, CoQ10, vitamin D *(also ↑ SHBG → ↓ T; progestins ≠ progesterone)* |
| **Anti-ulcer / PPIs / antacids** | B12, fatty acids, D, Ca, Fe, Zn, protein; antacids also Ca, phosphate, folate |
| **Antibiotics** | B vitamins, vitamin K |
| **Anticonvulsants** | Fatty acids; D, K, Ca; class-specific (valproate: carnitine, copper, Se, Zn; barbiturates/phenytoin/carbamazepine/mysoline: biotin ± B1/B12) |
| **Diabetes meds** | CoQ10, fatty acids, B12; **metformin → B12 + folate**; TZDs → bone loss; sulfonylureas/biguanides → CoQ10 |
| **Antihypertensives** | B6, CoQ10, Ca, Mg, K, Zn; **ACE inhibitors → Zn**; hydralazine → B6 + CoQ10 |
| **Aspirin / NSAIDs / acetaminophen** | Aspirin: C, fatty acids, B5, Ca, Fe, Na, K; **acetaminophen → glutathione**; NSAIDs: folate, melatonin; broader anti-inflammatories: Ca, K, Zn, Fe, B6, C, D, fatty acids |
| **Statins / fibrates / bile sequestrants** | Statins: **CoQ10** (HMG-CoA block); fibrates: B12, E, Zn, Cu; gemfibrozil: CoQ10, D; sequestrants (cholestyramine): fat-soluble vitamins + B12, minerals, folate, β-carotene, fatty acids |
| **Beta blockers / digoxin** | CoQ10, melatonin; digoxin: riboflavin, CoQ10, melatonin |
| **Psych meds** | TCAs: riboflavin, CoQ10; MAOIs: B6; haloperidol: CoQ10, D, melatonin; lithium: inositol; benzos: melatonin |
| **Diuretics** | Loop/thiazide: Ca, Mg, K, B1, B6, C, Zn, CoQ10; K-sparing: Ca, Zn, fatty acids; digoxin-related: Ca, Mg, phosphate |
| **Corticosteroids** | A, C, D, K, B6, B12, fatty acids, Ca, Cr, Mg, Se, Zn |
| **Colchicine / sulfasalazine / methotrexate / laxatives** | Colchicine: B12, Ca, Na, K, β-carotene; sulfasalazine/MTX: folate (± Fe); laxatives: A, D, K |

### Folate Pearl (Bailey “bandwagon”)
- Avoid **folic acid** (synthetic): lowers NK cells; never completes the full path to MTHFR; associated with **↑ colon cancer ~14%** in a Mexican cohort cited
- Prefer **methylfolate / Quatrefolic / folinic acid** with adequate **B6 + B12** for methylation

### Tobacco as a “Drug”
- Cigarette smoke / secondhand smoke deplete antioxidants and micronutrients (**115 references** cited; Dr. Richard Lord letter of affirmation mentioned)
- Priority nutrients: **vitamin C, B6, Mg, Se, Zn, glutathione / NAC**
- Higher B6 associated with less lung cancer (multi-country study >500k); higher Mg associated with less stroke (Swedish cohort >20k)
- Smokeless tobacco: **no nutrient depletion claimed**, but **homocysteine ↑ ~10×** → suspect B6/B12/folate/TMG gap
- Vaping: no clear nutrient-depletion map yet; risk of popcorn lung / altered vitamin E forms

### Closing Triad of the Talk
1. ~Half of common drugs deplete nutrients  
2. Tobacco / secondhand smoke deplete antioxidants & micronutrients  
3. Replenishment of key depleted substances reduces morbidity/mortality and improves biomarkers (detailed in Bailey’s companion smoking lecture)

## Segment 4 — Clinical Q&A: Labs, CoQ10, Thyroid, Mold (32:10–46:35)

**Speakers:** Bailey, Clearfield, Dr. Harlan

### Practical Workflow
- For long-term meds: **check levels → supplement** what is depleted
- Statins: “if you wait 10 years you’ll run into trouble” without CoQ10 support

### CoQ10 Quality Check
- Open a liquid CoQ10 softgel onto fingers: **grittiness = crystals** → poor absorption
- Prefer **crystal-free** products (e.g., **Designs for Health / Xymogen** 50 mg cited)
- **MitoQ** (Australia/NZ): mitochondria-targeted CoQ10; Harlan pearl (via Mark Houston): cardiac-focused MitoQ may need **separate systemic ubiquinol** because cardiac MitoQ concentrates in heart muscle

### Preferred Testing
- **Genova Metabolomix / metabolomics** functional testing
- Example: normal serum B12 but ↑ **methylmalonic acid** = functional B12 insufficiency
- RBC Mg/Zn/Cu useful; intracellular commercial panels criticized (split-sample inconsistency story)
- Hair analysis: OK historically for heavy metals timeline; prefer **urine provocation** (DMSA **20–30 mg/kg**) for current body burden — nutrient accuracy unknown

### Thyroid Timing Pearl
- Draw labs **4–6 hours after thyroid dose** (T3 half-life ~4–6 h vs T4 days)
- Always co-check **vitamin D** — needed to move thyroid hormone from cell to nucleus

### Polypharmacy Case Approach (atorvastatin + acetaminophen + furosemide + metformin)
- Nutrition assessment + **nitric oxide tongue strip** (Berkeley Life)
- Age 60: endogenous NO production ~**15%** of youth; Bailey’s own SBP dropped **~15 points** when repleted
- Half of Bailey’s South Florida practice is now **mold / water-damaged buildings** — every patient is unique; start by listening

### Methylation Clue (Harlan)
- High **folic acid** on labs can signal poor methylation (prefer methylated B vitamins)
- Conventional conferences now acknowledging short-course PPI use (~5 days) — Bailey: “20 years behind”

## Segment 5 — Nitric Oxide Deep Dive (Panel) (46:35–1:08:26)

**Speakers:** Bailey, Clearfield, Harlan, Quinn/Burgess, Carol Peterson

### Testing & Products
- **Berkeley Life** strips (tongue 10 sec) + capsules; also Fullscript, doctor supplement stores, Emerson
- High-nitrate foods: soft lettuce, Swiss chard, arugula, spinach (bacon high nitrate but sat-fat caveat)
- Bailey: MTHFR **C677T +/+** yet still needs daily NO support
- **Methylene blue** may prolong NO effect (blue urine trade-off; erection benefit anecdotal)
- Emerging: hydrogen-sulfide pathways for longer-acting NO; oxalate concerns with some BT products; topical NO for wrinkles (expensive)
- Dr. Terry Hertog (Brussels): multi-hormone syringe for wrinkles — local + systemic hormone thinking

### Cholesterol Signal (Quinn/Burgess)
- ~**5 patients** with previously resistant lipids saw cholesterol drop on approved NO solutions (Elizabeth Shirley / Claudia network)
- Shirley: plaque imaging showed **no plaque** despite brothers with CABG/stents
- Research angle: bulk pricing ~**$25/month** (2 caps/day, 60 count) for committee work
- Most “NO” products contain **precursors (nitrates → nitrites → NO)**, not nitric oxide itself

### Bailey’s Lipid Stack (non-statin lean)
1. High fiber **20–30 g/day** (soluble product that “vanishes” in water)  
2. **Gamma-delta tocotrienols** to block LDL oxidation  
3. Fish oil for triglycerides  

### Other Clinical Uses Discussed
- **ED:** NO caps ~20–30 min prior (enhances, not cures)
- **Neuropathy:** early study signal; dialysis not an absolute contraindication in cited CKD 2–3 contexts
- BP effect: typically ≤ **~10 systolic / ~5 diastolic** (often ~8/5 with oral capsules); modulator more than hard hypotensive — caution if already low on antihypertensives
- eNOS uncoupling with age → peroxynitrite / inflammatory concern more than “too much NO”
- NO not shown depleted by the Rx classes in Bailey’s DIND talk (not studied)
- Estrogen–NO interaction: Bailey believes NO supports hormone signaling; will share handout

### Oral Microbiome Caveats (Quinn + Harlan)
- Mouthwash can kill tongue-crypt anaerobes that convert **NO3 → NO2** (Nathan Bryan teaching)
- Vigorous tongue scraping discouraged by biologic dentists for same reason
- Humming / nasal cells also cited in Nobel-era NO physiology
- Organic leafy greens may have **less** sprayed nitrate than conventional — ironic NO food tip
