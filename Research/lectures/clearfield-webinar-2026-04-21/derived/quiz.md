## Section A — Hormones & Open Panel (4 questions)

### Q1. Per Carol, when is oral progesterone often more effective than cream?
- A) Always — cream never works
- B) **When low thyroid or high adrenaline impair dermal delivery** ✓
- C) Only in peanut-allergic patients
- D) Only post-hysterectomy

> *Cool skin / poor peripheral flow and adrenaline-driven vasoconstriction reduce cream absorption.*

### Q2. Clearfield now uses aromatase inhibitors in men on testosterone primarily for which reasons?
**Answer:** Gynecomastia, emotional lability, and ED/libido problems attributable to high estradiol — not routine zeroing of E2.

### Q3. Why did the panel criticize the A4M sponsor arguing T for 18-year-old men with unmonitored E2 ~100+?
- A) Testosterone is illegal under 21
- B) **Lack of evidence-based foundation for fellows; extreme E2 without vascular assessment** ✓
- C) Only MDs may discuss hormones
- D) Estrogen is irrelevant in men so monitoring wastes money

### Q4. True or False: Driving male estradiol to zero with aromatase inhibitors is currently Clearfield’s preferred default.
**Answer:** **False.** That was the old “gym rat” approach; crushing E2 was associated with heart disease, osteoporosis, and dementia risk phenotypes.

## Section C — Helminths, Mold & Clinical Protocols (6 questions)

### Q11. Which helminth species does Dobner use therapeutically, and what immune effect is highlighted?
**Answer:** *Necator americanus* (human hookworm) — associated with increased T regulatory cell activity / immune “teeter-totter” rebalancing; worm secretions contain ~200 unique peptides.

### Q12. Blastocystis subtypes 4 and 7 are discussed as potentially:
- A) Always requiring triple antiparasitic therapy
- B) **Anti-inflammatory / immune-calming parasites** ✓
- C) Identical to *C. difficile*
- D) Soil-only organisms with no human relevance

### Q13. In the adolescent Crohn’s + mold case, why did Dobner delay binders?
- A) Binders are illegal
- B) **Patient was constipated — binders worsen constipation; motility (beets, vitamin C) came first** ✓
- C) Mold was already cleared
- D) Biologics had already cured him

### Q14. Approximate vitamin C strategy for bowel tolerance / depletion assessment:
- A) Fixed 500 mg forever

- C) IV only, never oral
- D) Only with inulin

> *Panel also noted ascorbate may have better GI tolerance; start ~4 g in that framing.*

### Q15. For MCAS / histamine-intolerant patients, Dobner’s unconventional adjunct is:
- A) Maximum-dose Lactobacillus monoculture
- B) **Very low-dose helminthic therapy to support diversity and Treg tone** ✓
- C) Daily alcohol mouthwash

### Q16. Stanford ferment vs fiber study takeaway (as taught):
**Answer:** Increasing fermented foods (~10 weeks) improved microbial diversity and reduced inflammation more than pushing fiber alone — sauerkraut/kimchi/yogurt/kombucha class interventions are clinically prioritized.

## Section D — Foundations, Neuro & Practice Pearls (4 questions)

### Q17. Parkinson’s disease is often preceded by approximately how many years of constipation (per Dobner’s literature framing)?
- A) 2 years
- B) 5 years
- C) **~20 years** ✓
- D) Constipation is unrelated

### Q18. Why does Dobner distrust “I’ve been on one probiotic forever” strategies?
**Answer:** She wants rotating exposure to different species/functions; a single product can overload a narrow set of microbes. Probiotics are usually transient “tourists,” not permanent colonizers — benefit is metabolic/genetic exchange, not guaranteed appearance on retesting.

### Q19. Highest-yield simplified actions Dobner gives overwhelmed clinicians:
**Answer:** (1) Ensure adequate water/hydration; (2) fermented foods or rotating probiotics until ferments are tolerated; (3) reduce food fear and pesticide exposure / support regenerative food systems — restoration over antimicrobial whack-a-mole.
