## Section A — Medical-Legal & Practice Context (3 questions)

### Q1. Per Dr. Nario’s tip, which phrase is safest for integrative marketing copy?
- A) “Clinically proven to cure neuropathy”
- B) “Treats stage 4 cancer”
- C) **“Research suggests this formula may support resilience during treatment”** ✓
- D) “All oncologists now use IV vitamin C”

> *Prefer support language and study-hedged claims; avoid cure/treat/proven and false standard-of-care implications.*

### Q2. What disclaimer should appear on websites promoting integrative therapies?
**Answer:** FDA evaluation disclaimer — statements not evaluated by the FDA; product not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

### Q3. True or False: Patient testimonials that imply typical or expected results (e.g., “this clinic saved my life from stage 4 cancer”) are tightly regulated and high-risk.
**Answer:** **True.** Prefer softer outcome language + “individual results may vary” and “not a substitute for standard care.”

### Q18. Medicinal mushrooms worsen gut Candida/mold overgrowth — true or false per this lecture?
**Answer:** **False (myth).** Presented as 1970s folklore without evidence; TCM/clinical framing uses Reishi (and related species) against Candida. Mushrooms are biphasic immunomodulators; theoretical PAM/toll concerns are unproven clinically.
