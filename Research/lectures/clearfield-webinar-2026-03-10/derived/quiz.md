## Section A — Public Health & Patho-Etiology (7 questions)

### Q1. According to Clayton’s national-survey framing, roughly what fraction of US adults had ≥1 chronic condition by 2020?
- A) ~25%
- B) ~45%
- C) **~60%** ✓
- D) ~90%

> *~10% in 1935 → ~45% by 2000 → ~60% by 2020. Children/adolescents rose from ~1–2% to >40%.*

### Q2. What four major patho-etiologic drivers does Clayton link between the industrial diet and modern chronic disease?
**Answer:** (1) Chronic inflammasome / inflammatory stress, (2) dysbiosis, (3) type B malnutrition, (4) glycemic mismatch (worsened by low physical activity).

### Q3. Why does Clayton argue classic “signs of aging” (coronary fatty streaks, rising BP, hippocampal shrinkage, etc.) are largely extrinsic?
**Answer:** Vestigial populations (Hadza hunter-gatherers; Tsimané forager-horticulturalists) with similar life expectancy show little of these changes when infectious/parasitic disease is stripped out — implying progressive chronic intoxication from modern diet/lifestyle rather than inevitable intrinsic aging.

### Q4. The epidemiologic crossover from infectious to chronic degenerative respiratory/public-health dominance begins in the early 1950s. Where does Clayton place the dietary root cause?
- A) Introduction of statins
- B) **Introduction of fast / industrial / ultraprocessed foods in the early 1940s** ✓
- C) Discovery of antibiotics
- D) Rise of cigarette filters

> *Long preclinical latency → look ~10 years earlier than the mortality crossover.*

### Q5. What is “type B malnutrition” as defined in this lecture?
- A) Classic scurvy / beriberi / pellagra
- B) **Multi-micronutrient and phytonutrient depletion, often with calorie excess from empty ultraprocessed calories** ✓

### Q6. True or False: Clayton claims higher healthcare spending in the US has tracked with rising healthy life expectancy.
**Answer:** **False.** Spend rose from ~$500B (1970) to ~$5.5T (2026, constant $2024) while healthy life expectancy fell (similar reverse trend in the UK).

## Section B — Omega-3, Polyphenols & Inflammasome (6 questions)

### Q8. At what approximate omega-6 : omega-3 ratio does Clayton’s large sample database suggest chronic inflammasome stress stops while acute response is preserved?
- A) 15 : 1
- B) 25 : 1
- C) **≤ 5 : 1** ✓
- D) 1 : 1 only

> *Historical ~2–4:1; Europe ~15:1; E. Europe/N. America ~25–45:1.*

### Q9. Why are long-chain omega-3 supplements (or oily fish) often required in modern diets even if ALA is present?
**Answer:** Desaturation/elongation enzymes prefer ALA ~4:1 over LA, but excess dietary LA from seed oils overrides that preference and chokes EPA/DHA synthesis — so LCPUFA must be supplied directly.

### Q10. How do polyphenols protect extracellular matrix in Clayton’s inflammasome cascade model?
- A) They replace collagen directly
- B) **They inhibit MMPs (bind divalent metal cations in active sites) and epigenetically downregulate MMP gene expression** ✓
- C) They raise omega-6 in membranes
- D) They act as probiotics

### Q11. Why must polyphenol-rich foods be eaten continuously, unlike omega-3 fatty acids?
**Answer:** The body treats polyphenols as foreign xenobiotics — they are rapidly metabolized and excreted. Omega-3s incorporate into membrane phospholipids, so timing is less critical.

### Q12. Which food category does Clayton highlight as the most concentrated practical polyphenol source — and as an explanation for healthy Indian centenarians despite high 6:3 ratios?
- A) Cold-water fish
- B) **Spices (turmeric, cardamom, paprika, etc.)** ✓
- C) Refined seed oils
- D) Sugar-sweetened beverages

### Q13. Omega-3 alone is insufficient to suppress chronic inflammasome stress in Clayton’s protocol. What must it be combined with?
**Answer:** The right polyphenols (or lipophenols) — framed as switching off chronic inflammasome stress nearly as effectively as a biologic/glycoprotein when combined correctly.

## Section C — Dysbiosis, Fiber & Clinical Application (4 questions)

### Q14. Why does Clayton downplay standalone probiotic capsules as a public-health antidote?
**Answer:** Colonization resistance — strains may appear in stool while taken but disappear when stopped; major regulators (e.g., EFSA) have essentially awarded no health claims to probiotic species. Prebiotic fibers that feed indigenous saccharolytic species are the durable lever.

### Q15. Approximate modern vs mid-Victorian vs Mesoamerican prebiotic fiber intakes cited:
**Answer:** Modern ~**4 g/day**; mid-Victorian ~**40 g/day**; archaeological Mesoamerican (roots/shoots/tubers/nopales) ~**140 g/day**.

### Q16. What sequential prebiotic blend strategy does Clayton describe for shifting the entire colon to saccharolytic fermentation?
**Answer:** Short oligosaccharides/FOS (fast) → inulin → 1,3/1,4 β-glucans → resistant starch (slowest, e.g., green banana), so fermentation proceeds from ascending colon distally.

### Q17. What clinical “event horizon” warning does Clayton emphasize for chronic degenerative disease reversal?
**Answer:** Intervene early — ideally in the preclinical/pre-symptomatic phase. After substantial irreversible loss of functional tissue, pharmaco-nutrition cannot restore what is gone (atheroma/OA/BMD gains cited only when enough tissue remains).
