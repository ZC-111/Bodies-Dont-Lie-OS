## Section A — Hormone Tracking Fundamentals (8 questions)

### Q1. What is the primary conceptual shift Rose McKenzie emphasizes in women’s hormone assessment?
- A) Replacing all serum testing with saliva forever
- B) **Moving from static snapshots to dynamic, ongoing urine metabolite mapping across cycles** ✓
- C) Using cycle-length apps as the gold standard
- D) Measuring only LH strips at ovulation

> *Static = infrequent serum, delayed mailed labs, mucus/temp proxies. Dynamic = real-time E3G, PDG, LH, FSH patterns.*

### Q2. Which urine metabolite primarily reflects serum estradiol (E2)?
- A) PDG
- B) **E3G** ✓
- C) LH
- D) FSH

### Q3. True or False: Same-morning serum and urine metabolite values should match if the assay is valid.
**Answer:** **False.** Urine metabolites are breakdown products and **lag** serum; do not compare them as same-day equivalents.

### Q4. A patient has a 32-day cycle and a clear LH/FSH surge on CD 18–19, but flat E3G rise and minimal PDG. What does this most suggest?
- A) Perfect ovulation; proceed with timed intercourse only
- B) Anovulation because cycle length is abnormal
- C) **Suboptimal ovulation despite “normal” length and trigger** ✓
- D) Premature ovarian failure only

### Q5. Why is fixed “cycle day 21” mid-luteal bloodwork often wrong?
**Answer:** Mid-luteal means ~**7 days after true ovulation**. If she ovulates on CD 20 and labs are drawn CD 21, that is not mid-luteal and yields less usable data.

### Q6. Normal luteal phase length (as taught) is approximately:
- A) 5–8 days
- B) **11–16 days** ✓
- C) 18–22 days
- D) Exactly 14 days only

### Q7. How does a typical Clomid chart differ from a letrozole chart on urine E3G monitoring?
**Answer:** **Clomid** blocks estrogen receptors → circulating estrogen/E3G often **rises markedly** (obvious “Clomid chart”). **Letrozole** blocks estrogen synthesis → E temporarily **falls** then rebounds; charts often look unremarkable unless the patient discloses the medication.

### Q8. In the fertility pilot Rose cited (~36 IVF-qualified candidates), what approximate conception rate was reported after ~2 months of restorative care + remote hormone monitoring?
- A) 10%
- B) 20%
- C) **44%** ✓
- D) 90%

> *Also: 100% engagement claimed; framed as near IVF (~47%) and above typical PCOS (~25%) / IUI (~20%) rates.*

### Q10. What is a “stutter-step” or non-ovulatory LH surge pattern in PCOS monitoring?
**Answer:** Body mounts an LH surge **without** subsequent PDG rise (failed ovulation), then later mounts a **second** estrogen rise + LH surge that **does** produce progesterone — true ovulation later than assumed.
