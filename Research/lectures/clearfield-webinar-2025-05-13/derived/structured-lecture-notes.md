## Segment 2 — Rose McKenzie: Dynamic Hormone Tracking with Urine Metabolites (21:33–1:12:18)

**Speaker:** Rose McKenzie — fertility awareness educator; formerly restorative reproductive health clinic; now trains providers on at-home hormone tracking

### ★ Core Thesis
> Women’s hormone care is shifting from **static snapshots** (cycle length apps, infrequent serum, delayed mailed labs, mucus/temperature proxies) to **dynamic, real-time urine metabolite mapping** across full cycles — revealing patterns serum alone often misses.

### Disclaimers
- Educational lecture first; product/pricing deferred to end
- Urine metabolites **complement**, do not wholesale replace serum

### Biomarkers Tracked (urine)
| Urine metabolite | Reflects |
|---|---|
| **E3G** | Breakdown of serum estradiol (E2) |
| **PDG** | Breakdown of progesterone |
| **LH** | LH surge / ovulatory trigger |
| **FSH** | FSH coordination with LH |

**Normal pattern:** rising E3G → coordinated LH + FSH surge → corpus luteum → robust PDG “bell curve.” Patterns should be **dynamic and coordinated**, not flat or mismatched.

**Important:** Serum vs urine delay — never compare same-morning serum and urine as “matching”; metabolites lag serum breakdown.

### Evidence Cited
- 2015 serum–urine agreement work (progesterone, LH, estrogen patterns)
- More recent E3G / PDG / LH studies: both modalities identify ~24h ovulation window and follicular→luteal transition
- Upcoming UBC work (Dr. Thomas Burchard): urine + serum + **transvaginal ultrasound** validation → toward quantitative cycle “gold standard”
- Universities using urine metabolites to control cycle phase in athlete, diet/IF, mental health, PMS/endometriosis research

### 2A. Fertility / Infertility Use Cases
- Identify **fertile window opening** (not just ovulation day) for timed intercourse
- Detect suboptimal ovulation despite “normal” cycle length / LH strip positives
- Time mid-luteal labs to **true ovulation + 7 days**, not fixed CD 18–21
- Track response to seed cycling, luteal P4 support, ovulation induction

**Case pearls:**
1. **40yo TTC 1 year:** 32-day cycle, LH/FSH trigger CD 18–19 — but flat E3G rise + minimal PDG → suboptimal ovulation; shouldn’t wait another year of timed intercourse alone
2. **39yo secondary infertility:** 28-day cycle, ovulates CD 20, **short luteal** despite adequate-appearing PDG — implantation window too short (normal luteal 11–16 days)
3. **Short luteal + uncoordinated mid-range estrogen** — another suboptimal pattern
4. **Pilot clinic (~36 qualified IVF candidates, median age 34):** remote restorative protocol + monitoring → **44% conceived** in ~2 months; **100% engagement**; 72% more accurate diagnoses; comparable to cited IVF (~47%) / better than typical PCOS (~25%) or IUI (~20%) rates in their framing
5. **Clomid vs letrozole charts:** Clomid (ER blockade) → circulating E / E3G **rises markedly** (visible “Clomid chart”); letrozole (aromatase block) → E temporarily **falls** then rebounds — chart often indistinguishable unless patient discloses meds

### 2B. Hormone Imbalance & Metabolic Health
- Short cycles can still be **ovulatory** (e.g., early coordinated LH on CD 5 with rising E) — distinguishes breakthrough bleed vs true ovulation misfire
- **PCOS case:** 31-day ovulatory cycle with **reverse luteal E3G** (luteal E higher than follicular peak) → warning; next cycle 40 days with **failed LH then successful second surge** (stutter-step ovulation) — mid-luteal serum timing would have been wrong if assumed CD ~23
- **Postpartum / breastfeeding case:** longitudinal drop in E3G + red-streaked mucus around ovulation → patient self-advocates before TTC; prompts detective work (calorie restriction, overexercise, fat/grain cuts, nutrient depletion)
- **Glucose × cycle study (n=49):** CGM + hormone monitor + Fitbit → glucose **lowest follicular** (high E → ↑ insulin sensitivity), **highest luteal** (P4 dominant → ↓ sensitivity); supports cycle-phase diet/exercise coaching (e.g., Alicia Beatty “In the Flow”; Aspect Health PCOS scoring worse post-ovulation)

### 2C. Perimenopause
- Not blanket testing — indicated for pattern assessment, short luteal / low P4, ovarian function via serial FSH, timed (“cooperative”) HRT, lifestyle-first early peri monitoring
- FSH is **dynamic cycle-to-cycle**, not a linear march upward; single or 6-week-apart FSH can mislead
- Estradiol patches may lightly affect urine E3G, but wild endogenous swings still visible
- Cases: 49yo **unopposed estrogen** (high fluctuating E3G, no LH/PDG); 52yo coordinated pattern with **low PDG** (candidate for luteal-timed P4 vs continuous P4 that may stop coordination); 50yo short 24-day still-coordinated cycles — longer coordination often preferred when clinically appropriate
- QR resources: indications for testing; natural P4 decline; E2–FSH relationship; decision flowchart

### Pearls
- Urinary metabolite literature expanding (incl. DIM effects on estrogen metabolism pathways — Mark Newman / “Doctor Scene” study cited by Nario: DIM shifts toward **2-OH** protective metabolites vs 4-OH)
- At-home tracking monitors **E2 breakdown (E3G)** — not full estrone/estriol pathway lecture
- **Bone protection goals:** keep serum thresholds you already use; urine mapping complements when patient “was fine on HRT, now isn’t” (e.g., endogenous E going rampant)
- **GLP-1 users:** hormone tracking can support fertility-awareness–based pregnancy avoidance **under educator guidance** — do not casually label as contraceptive (liability); hormonal OC may disrupt the hormones you’re trying to optimize
- **Saliva vs serum vs urine:** no three-way head-to-head study yet; choose tool by clinical question (saliva often claimed for topical cream lymphatic uptake — contested)
- **Men / postmenopausal ongoing urine goals:** not there yet — research gap; no established urine metabolite “goal values” for postmenopause
- Mood swings on day 1 of menses: likely multifactorial (glucose shifts + abrupt hormone drop); encourage hormone diary + intervention tracking
- Endometriosis: distinct patterns because ectopic tissue doesn’t slough like eutopic lining — emerging research
- Upcoming interest: ADHD symptom severity × cycle phase; ADHD + perimenopause pilots
