### Q3. Why does Shea warn against driving HbA1c “as low as possible”?
**Answer:** All-cause mortality vs biomarker level is typically **U-shaped**. Too-low and too-high HbA1c both associate with higher mortality; the optimal zone is in the middle. Extremely low GSP may also signal glycation defects or low albumin contexts.

### Q4. What vitamin D level does Shea consider a better longevity target than the common “30 ng/mL is enough” message?
- A) 20–30 ng/mL
- B) **Above 50 ng/mL** ✓

### Q5. Uric acid above which approximate level was linked to ~10 years lower life expectancy in the presented data?
- A) 5 mg/dL
- B) 7 mg/dL
- C) **9 mg/dL** ✓
- D) 12 mg/dL

### Q6. When should you order standard CRP vs high-sensitivity CRP?
**Answer:** Same protein — assay sensitivity differs. Use **standard CRP** for acute spikes (surgery, frank infection). Use **hsCRP** for chronic/low-grade inflammation and longevity risk where changes are small (e.g., COVID-range elevations that standard CRP may miss).

### Q13. How does optimized NAD status change response to anti-inflammation treatment in the data she presented?
- A) No difference
- B) **~80% responders with higher NAD vs ~30% with low NAD** ✓
- C) Low NAD always responds better
- D) Only IV NAD changes response rates

### Q14. Why does Shea prefer oral NMN slightly over NR (even though both raise NAD)?
**Answer:** NMN is a **one-step** precursor to NAD; NR requires **NR kinase** (NR→NMN) and some people may be enzyme-deficient. She also cites somewhat better functional benefits signals with NMN, while acknowledging both raise NAD similarly at comparable doses.

### Q15. What is the key limitation of IV NAD for “NAD optimization”?
- A) It is illegal in all states
- B) **It does not meaningfully raise intracellular NAD; plasma half-life ~6 hours; benefits are mainly extracellular/temporary** ✓
- C) It permanently locks NAD above 200 μM
- D) It cannot be combined with oral precursors

> *If using IV, she still recommends oral precursors afterward for intracellular optimization.*

### Q16. Starting NMN dose and expected optimization rates per Shea?
**Answer:** Start **1000 mg/day**. About **75%** reach optimal NAD; ~**25%** need ~1500 mg; ~**5%** need less than 1000 mg (and ~5% at 1000 mg may exceed 100 μM). **Test to titrate.** Recheck at **3–4 weeks**.

### Q17. Why is high-dose niacin particularly concerning as an NAD-raising strategy in this talk?
**Answer:** Niacin can push NAD to ~**150 μM** (above her safety window), increase **2PY/4PY** metabolites linked to vascular inflammation/CV risk, may associate with faster epigenetic aging in limited data, and high-NAD niacin users often **lack** the expected NAD health benefits.

## Section D — Clinical Integration (2 questions)

### Q19. A 45-year-old executive wants “anti-aging IVs,” already gets weekly NAD drips, and takes high-dose niacin for lipids. Labs show NAD 140 μM, hsCRP 2.8, vitamin D 22. Outline an approach using this webinar’s principles.
**Answer (model):**
1. Stop chasing higher NAD — **140 μM is above the 100 μM ceiling**; reduce niacin and/or drips; retest in 3–4 weeks aiming 50–75 μM
2. Prefer **oral NMN/NR** for intracellular NAD if supplementation still needed; do not rely on IV for intracellular optimization
3. Aggressively treat **inflammation** (hsCRP 2.8) and replete **vitamin D >50 ng/mL**
4. Consider oxidative stress + Klotho if available; fix “sub-health” drivers rather than adding more NAD theater
5. Use biomarkers before/after each change — personalization and prioritization over protocol stacking

### Q20. Name the five “big” biomarker domains Shea highlighted for her provider panel (~$650) and one reason each matters from this lecture.
**Answer:**
1. **NAD** — mitochondrial ATP + treatment-response amplifier  
2. **Klotho** — longevity/kidney-linked protein; dynamic illness marker  
3. **Oxidative stress** — highly prevalent in US; mortality/cancer signal  
4. **Senescence** — SASP burden; chemo response/survival associations  
5. **Inflammation (hsCRP)** — drive toward zero for survival benefit
