## Segment 3 — Conventional Longevity Biomarkers (16:25–~28:00)

**Speaker:** Dr. Shea

### Focus Categories
Eight biomarker categories highlighted; **autophagy** desired but no good clinical assay yet.

### Inflammation — hsCRP
- High hsCRP linked to worse survival; even hsCRP ~3 mg/L common in ~20–30% of “healthy” people
- Conventional CVD risk often framed as CRP >1 mg/L
- Individuals with **hsCRP <0.5 mg/L** have lower all-cause mortality than those around ~1 mg/L
- Clinical goal: drive CRP **as close to zero as possible** (CRP is one of few markers where lower is always better — not U-shaped)

### Glycation — HbA1c & GSP
- HbA1c associated with all-cause mortality in diabetics **and** non-diabetics
- Optimal zone is in the **middle** of the curve (U-shaped mortality) — too low and too high both harmful
- **GSP (glycated serum protein)** as additional glycation marker
- Very low GSP may signal glycation defect or low albumin (e.g., cancer); premature-aging case discussed

### Uric Acid
- Uric acid >9 → ~**10-year** decrease in life expectancy (presented survival curves)
- Uric acid / HbA1c ratio can improve mortality prediction vs uric acid alone

### Vitamin D
- Survival difference ~**50%** between optimal vs deficient groups (per slide)
- Does **not** consider 30 ng/mL optimal → target **>50 ng/mL** (IU/ng framing as stated)
- ~70–80% of Americans still deficient — cheap, high-leverage intervention

### Other Conventional
- ALP also linked to all-cause mortality in her framing
- LDH retained on her panel (biohacking / muscle damage vs mass; helps interpret CK with creatine use)

## Segment 4 — Cellular Senescence (28:00–31:31)

**Speaker:** Dr. Shea

### Concept
- Senescent cells refuse programmed death → secrete **SASP** (senescence-associated secretory phenotype) → local + systemic harm
- Bidirectional with aging; impacts mitochondria, DNA function, and **NAD**

### Current Assays (imperfect)
1. **Naive T-cell proportion** in circulation → immune senescence proxy (declines with age)
2. **β-galactosidase (β-gal)** — lysosomal enzyme relatively specific to senescence

### Clinical Signal
- Cervical cancer cohort: higher senescence → worse chemo response and shorter survival
- Hypothesis: reducing senescence may improve cancer treatment outcomes (trials planned / resource-limited)

## Segment 5 — Klotho (Clotho) Longevity Protein (31:31–41:10)

**Speaker:** Dr. Shea

### ★ Core Concept
> Klotho (named for the Greek goddess who spins the thread of life) is primarily kidney-secreted (some brain). Impacts kidney, heart, diabetes, cancer, COVID, Alzheimer’s risk signals.

### Factors That Lower Klotho
1. Oxidative stress  
2. Inflammation  
3. Psychological stress / depression  

### Dynamic Behavior
- Behaves more like **CRP** than a static trait — can drop rapidly with illness
- Personal case: mid-optimal (~5+) → **2.8** during COVID → recovered to >6 with general recovery (no Klotho-specific product)
- Avoid levels much below **~5**; extreme lows seen with cancer, long COVID, kidney disease, Alzheimer’s

### Raising Klotho — Honest State of the Field
- Gene therapy (e.g., **BioViva**) may work but cost/access limit mainstream use
- Bacterial recombinant Klotho proteins: **doubtful** (lack proper glycosylation)
- No validated single supplement protocol yet among clinics working with her
- Best current signal: **improve overall health** (↓ inflammation, ↓ oxidative stress) → Klotho often rises
- Mentions of ARBs/statins raising Klotho and low-phosphate diets (Nario podcast notes) — Shea: unvalidated; open to collaborative study
- Two US labs can measure Klotho clinically (San Diego standalone expensive test; **Infinity** as panel component)

## Segment 6 — Oxidative Stress & NAD / Mitochondria (41:10–51:05)

**Speaker:** Dr. Shea

### Oxidative Stress
- Measures ~**70%+** of Americans high vs ~**10%** Europeans → lifestyle/food drivers suspected
- Associated with cancer outcomes (cervical cancer survival data) and all-cause mortality
- Under-discussed vs inflammation; Shea prioritizes it heavily

### NAD — “Super Molecule of Life”
- Coenzyme for **500+** enzymes; critical for mitochondrial ATP (~95% of ATP from glucose path needs NAD)
- Declines early: ~20–25% of teenagers already suboptimal; only ~25% of 20–30yo at optimal; 50–60s often < half of teenage levels
- Sharp decline late 20s/early 30s tracks physiologic peak then drop

### Four Ways to Raise NAD
1. Give NAD itself (pros/cons)
2. Give **precursors** (building blocks)
3. Inhibit / decrease NAD consumption
4. Increase NAD-synthesizing enzymes  

**Best practical tools:** **NMN** and **NR** precursors outperform other approaches in her comparative data.

### Clinical Trial Signal (NMN multi-ingredient formula)
- Baseline ~28 μM → >100% increase in ~2 weeks; plateau thereafter
- Many individuals peak in <2 weeks

### Treatment Response Amplification
| NAD status | Anti-inflammation response | Oxidative-stress protocol response |
|---|---|---|
| Higher NAD | ~80% responders | Stronger response |
| Low NAD | ~30% responders | Weaker response |

> Optimize NAD so other protocols work better — not only for energy.

## Segment 8 — Q&A Deep Dive (1:05:32–1:49:00)

**Speakers:** Dr. Shea, Bill Clearfield, Dr. Nario, Benoit Tano

### Klotho Logistics
- Measure: two US labs (San Diego expensive standalone; Infinity panel)
- Raise: no single validated product; improve health; BioViva gene therapy option for interested patients
- Follow-up Klotho lecture requested → after New Year

### NAD Measurement
- Enzymatic chemical assay on **dried blood spot** / fingerstick
- Saliva strips discussed = **nitric oxide**, not NAD; saliva NAD not interpretable vs cellular NAD

### Precursors: NMN vs NR vs Niacin
- **NMN ≈ NR** for elevating NAD at comparable doses
- Personal preference **NMN**: one step closer to NAD; NR requires NR kinase (unknown % deficient); NMN may have additional biologic benefits
- **Niacin:** can raise NAD — sometimes into **harmful** range (~150 μM); ↑ nicotinamide metabolites **2PY/4PY** → vascular inflammation / CV risk signal; limited epigenetic-clock aging signal; monitor NAD if using niacin

### NAD Target Window
- **Optimal:** 40–100 μM (ideally **50–75**)
- **Do not exceed ~100 μM**
- ~5% of people on 1000 mg NMN exceed 100 μM
- High-niacin patients can have high NAD **without** benefit — do poorly

### Route of Administration
| Route | Intracellular NAD | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral precursors** | Best practical | Preferred |
| **IV NAD** | Does **not** raise intracellular NAD | t½ ~6 h; extracellular receptor effects only; add oral precursors after |
| IM / SQ NAD | Some increase | Rarely enough dose/frequency to optimize |

### Other Q&A Pearls
- **hsCRP vs CRP:** same protein; sensitivity differs — use hsCRP for chronic/low-grade; standard CRP for acute/surgery/infection spikes
- **HbA1c too low:** risk rises somewhere near **<4.7–5.0** in general population framing; diabetic optimal often cited ~5.2–5.6 range in her verbal sketch; <4.2 flagged on some lab reports
- **Telomeres:** hard to measure; little actionable increase; mostly genetic — deprioritized vs actionable markers
- **NMN & sports:** endogenous molecule; WADA policies — check current list; many athletes use NMN anyway
- **Kids:** <20 — supplement only if tested deficient/suboptimal; autism cases as young as 2 with benefit reports; pregnancy oxidative stress under-addressed — she favors prenatal NAD/precursor discussion (safety framing)
- **FDA & NMN:** notice that NMN should not be marketed as dietary supplement; **not actively enforcing**; NPA lawsuit; analogous to prior NAD/NDC and peptide situations
- **NMN dosing:** start **1000 mg/day** → ~75% optimize; ~25% need ~1500 mg; ~5% need less — **test to titrate**
- **Recheck NAD:** **3–4 weeks** after starting supplementation
- **Creatine caveat:** ↑ CK and creatinine can look like kidney injury — use cystatin C, ACR, dual eGFR estimates; LDH helps distinguish muscle damage vs mass
- Niacin for kidney failure (Tano): Shea — no data to confirm
