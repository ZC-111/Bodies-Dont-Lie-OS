## Section B — Dosing, Rhythm & Delivery (8 questions)

### Q8. What serum estradiol target does Gersh emphasize as a practical physiologic ballpark?
- A) 20–30 pg/mL
- B) **Around 100 pg/mL (at least >50; often up to ~150–160)** ✓
- C) 400–600 pg/mL
- D) Undetectable with vaginal therapy only

### Q9. Low vs high estradiol effects on immune cells (as taught):
**Answer:** Low estradiol is more **pro-inflammatory**; higher/ovulatory-range estradiol is more **anti-inflammatory**.

### Q10. What is the main problem with “tiny estradiol + nightly progesterone”?
- A) Progesterone raises estradiol receptors too much
- B) **Nightly progesterone downregulates estrogen receptors and blunts growth-factor benefits while still poorly managing a physiologic endometrium** ✓
- C) Tiny estradiol always causes clotting
- D) Nightly progesterone prevents all bleeding forever

### Q11. Gersh’s preferred cyclic progesterone pattern for many women is:
- A) 100 mg every night indefinitely
- B) **About 200 mg for ~14 days each cycle, then off** ✓
- C) 10 mg medroxyprogesterone acetate daily
- D) Progesterone cream 20 mg once weekly

### Q12. “If you grow it, you must shed it” refers to:
**Answer:** Physiologic estradiol drives **endometrial proliferation** along with beneficial systemic growth factors — the lining must be cyclically shed (or ablated), not endlessly suppressed with escalating continuous progesterone.

### Q13. Why does Gersh promote elective endometrial ablation around completion of childbearing?
- A) It replaces the need for any progesterone forever in all cases
- B) **It allows physiologic estradiol dosing with little/no bleeding, solving clinician fear of managing menses** ✓
- C) It is required before any patch therapy
- D) It prevents menopause itself

### Q14. Which delivery method does Gersh explicitly discourage as non-physiologic?
- A) Transdermal gel
- B) Brand-consistent patch
- C) **Pellets (and estradiol troches)** ✓
- D) Compounded BID cream

### Q15. Preferred timing to measure estradiol on topical therapy:
**Answer:** Morning **before** first application (or late day if needed); for patches, typically the **day after** placement — avoid levels within 1–2 hours of application; prefer not letting the day’s low point run too low.

## Section C — Clinical Applications & Panel Pearls (5 questions)

### Q16. For vaginal therapy when bladder health is a priority, Gersh prefers:
- A) Estriol only, because bladder is beta-dominant
- B) **Estradiol (bladder more alpha); estriol still acceptable for atrophy; may add vaginal DHEA** ✓
- C) Oral Premarin exclusively
- D) Testosterone pellets

### Q17. True or False: Gersh supports an automatic stop of HRT 10 years after menopause.
**Answer:** **False.** She compares ongoing physiologic hormones to thyroid replacement — no arbitrary end date; cells continuously renew and still need hormones, nutrients, sleep, and exercise.

### Q18. Early perimenopause / menopause facial hair and acne — Gersh’s testosterone advice:
- A) Always add testosterone to overcome SHBG
- B) **Often avoid adding testosterone — LH-driven ovarian T may rise while falling estradiol lowers SHBG, raising free T** ✓
- C) Use male TRT doses
- D) Testosterone is FDA-first-line for all postmenopausal women

### Q19. Multi-day fasting in reproductive-aged cycling women should be timed:
- A) Follicular phase to boost ovulation
- B) **Luteal phase after ovulation — follicular fasting may prevent or impair ovulation** ✓
- C) Only during menses
- D) Never if using any progesterone

### Q20. Panel (Carol) caution after hysterectomy regarding estrogen-only therapy and bone:
**Answer:** Estradiol **alone** may raise prolactin → raise PTH → pull calcium from bone; progesterone (Prior: often ~300 mg in osteoporosis contexts) is a key bone hormone — don’t assume “uterus gone = estrogen only forever.”
