## Section C — Clinical Use, Safety & Monitoring (6 questions)

### Q15. What maintenance vs complex-patient dosing did Lisa describe?
**Answer:** Maintenance **1–2 capsules/day**; complex multi-system chronic patients up to **3–4 capsules/day**.

### Q16. Which population should avoid the current 4-ingredient formula pending the pediatric/pregnancy-safe variant?
- A) Adults with diabetes

- D) Patients with low sIgA

### Q17. What is the main drug contraindication highlighted?
- A) Metformin
- B) **Warfarin (and strong blood thinners)** ✓
- C) Levothyroxine
- D) Vitamin D

### Q18. Compared with high-dose sulforaphane in sensitive patients, this stack is said to:
- A) Cause more Herxheimer reactions
- B) **Avoid Herxheimer / “hexarmor” die-off by modulating host immunity rather than massive pathogen kill** ✓
- C) Require hospitalization
- D) Suppress all probiotics

### Q19. Name three lab or stool markers Lisa suggested tracking after starting the formula.
**Answer (any three):** calprotectin, secretory IgA, zonulin, short-chain fatty acids, lymphocyte count / immune blood markers.

### Q20. A patient with chronic EBV, CMV, mycotoxicosis, and Lyme asks whether this will “Herx” them. How do you counsel using webinar points?
**Answer:**
1. Frame as immune **modulation** + barrier/gut support, not aggressive antimicrobial blast
2. Cite lack of Herxheimer reactions reported; gentler than sulforaphane for many sensitive patients
3. Consider **3–4 caps/day** for complex multi-hit cases (early clinical experience)
4. Screen for **warfarin**/anticoagulants; caution pregnancy/peds with full formula
5. Follow sIgA, zonulin, lymphocytes, and clinical infection burden; set expectations that combo product is early (~2 months market) while ingredient studies are stronger
6. Still address environment (mold), sleep, and cofactors — formula is adjunct, not sole therapy
