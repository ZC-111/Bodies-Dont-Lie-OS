## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Ayahuasca Pharmacology (11:49–18:00)

**Speaker:** Dr. Melvin Nario

### Botanical Combination
- **Banisteriopsis caapi** (vine) → β-carbolines = reversible MAO-A inhibitors
- **Psychotria** leaf → DMT (dimethyltryptamine)
- Without MAO inhibition, DMT is metabolized too quickly; with it → 4–8 hour serotonergic experience

### Mechanisms
- Agonizes 5-HT2A; modulates glutamate; ↓ Default Mode Network rigidity; ↑ global connectivity; may acutely ↑ BDNF
- More “network disruptor” than simple mood booster; ego quieting can be therapeutic or terrifying

### Human Evidence Cited
- 2019 double-blind RCT (Palhano-Fontes et al.): n=29 treatment-resistant depression; single dose ayahuasca vs placebo
  - Lower MADRS at days 1–2; large effect at day 7
  - Response ~64% ayahuasca vs ~27% placebo; remission trend ~36% vs ~7%
- Observational: ↓ depression / substance misuse in ceremonial settings; effects lasting weeks; no clear long-term cognitive harm in structured settings
- Limitations: small n, cultural context, variable dosing, no standardized protocols

### Risks & Contraindications
- Hypertensive episodes, serotonin syndrome (SSRIs/SNRIs/stimulants), mania/psychosis, CV strain
- Absolute: bipolar/psychotic disorders, active cardiac disease, concurrent serotonergic meds
- Legal: Schedule I in US; ceremonial/religious exemptions; patients often travel to Peru/Brazil; some religious retreats (FL, HI)

### Clinical Pearl
> Job is not endorsement police — screen meds, psych risk, CV vulnerability; counsel without judgment.

## Segment 3 — Dr. Eldredge Taylor: Stress Dominance & The Stress Connection (18:06–1:12:00)

**Speaker:** Dr. Eldredge Taylor

### ★ Core Thesis
> You cannot manage what you cannot measure. 75–90% (now likely 90–99%) of primary-care visits are stress-related — become an expert at identifying, measuring, and managing stress.

### 3A. Origin Story & Non-Diagnoses
- Trained as Emory OB/GYN: OCP → Premarin ± Provera → hysterectomy → psychiatry referral for residual symptoms
- Wife’s symptoms + John Lee progesterone/saliva tape → reframed practice; books *Are Your Hormones Making You Sick?* and *The Stress Connection*
- **Non-diagnoses** (labels that restate symptoms): chronic fatigue, fibromyalgia, PCOS-as-puberty mislabel, ADHD, IBS, bipolar — patients want *why*, not a scarlet letter

### 3B. Stress Physiology (Zebra Model)
- Short-term design: SNS → HPA → cortisol mobilizes glucose for fight/flight
- Reference: Sapolsky *Why Zebras Don’t Get Ulcers* (+ *Behave*)
- Acute stress reallocates energy: ↓ reproduction, ↓ thyroid, stop digestion / empty colon, hold water, stay awake
- **Chronic stress** skips mineralocorticoids (↓ aldosterone / fluid balance) and suppresses the rest of the endocrine cascade — hormone balancing fails while cortisol dominates

### 3C. Why Saliva for Cortisol & Steroids
- Endocrinology literature: salivary cortisol ≈ excellent index of **plasma free cortisol**; bypasses CBG alterations
- Fat-soluble steroids accumulate in tissue; blood = delivery; urine = metabolites; saliva ≈ tissue concentration via passive diffusion into acinar cells
- Free-hormone calculations from blood still miss tissue stores

### 3D. Cortisol Rhythm & High Performers
- Normal: high AM → dissipates → slight post-meal bump → low at night for repair/sleep
- Phase 1: AM cortisol sky-high (e.g. >400) — push mode
- Later: flat / inverted / “tired and wired” — low AM energy (caffeine/carbs fill gap); high PM → insomnia / 2–3 AM waking
- Office examples from Dec 2025–Jan 2026: real-estate agent, lawyer/finance after husband’s stroke — not rare; matches the 75–90% claim
- Personal case: ~2014 A4M lecture cognitive shutdown; saliva cortisol “shot”; ~6 months recovery; stopped travel overdrive

### 3E. Stress → Thyroid & Unified Model
- HPA activation ↓ TSH and blocks peripheral T4→T3 (↑ reverse T3 can block T3 receptor)
- TSH-only euthyroid claims fail under stress; need free T3, reverse T3, cortisol curve context
- Unified model: GnRH suppression, TSH/5′-deiodinase block, vagal withdrawal, gut/immune compromise, frontal cortex inhibition → loss of cognitive autonomy
- Zero-sum: treating low hormones / gut / brain fog without upstream HPA allocation fails
- Stressed clinicians default to group consensus / rigid protocols (“caterpillar line”)

### 3F. Four-Pronged Stress Treatment
1. **Remove** the stressor when possible (job, relationship, food sensitivity, glucose chaos)
2. **Change response** (meditation, breathwork, environment, support)
3. **Repair damage** (gut, neurotransmitters, nutrient repletion)
4. **Build resilience** for future stressors
- Cortisol is **capacity**, not the villain — want *responsive* rhythm, not chronically high or flat
- “Health is a rhythm, not a number”

## Segment 4 — Q&A: FSH, Perimenopause, Testing Compartments (1:12:00–1:36:00)

**Speakers:** Bill Clearfield, Dr. Taylor, panel

### 4A. FSH & Perimenopause
- Rising FSH stimulates ovarian cysts → estrogen production; older eggs ovulate poorly → **↓ progesterone**
- Perimenopause ≠ estrogen deficiency on saliva — often ↑ estrogen / ↓ progesterone
- Menopause: all stop ovulating (↓ progesterone); **not** all stop making estrogen (aromatase in fat/tissue)
- Blood postmenopausal estrogen often “absent” because it reflects ovarian delivery, not tissue aromatase; saliva reflects tissue

### 4B. DUTCH / Urine vs Saliva vs Blood
- DUTCH = metabolites (liver/kidney handling; COMT pathways; useful in cancer/detox context)
- Metabolites can be low while tissue estrogen is high (recirculation before metabolism)
- Taylor: too complex for routine clinical decisions / patient education; prefers compartment thinking:
  - Blood → total/delivery
  - Saliva → tissue physiology
  - Urine → detoxification pathways
- Mismatch with blood or DUTCH does **not** invalidate saliva — different compartments

### 4C. FSH Suppression Debate
- Clearfield cites emerging interest in lowering postmenopausal FSH (weight, osteoporosis, dementia risk)
- Taylor skeptical: elevated FSH is physiologic menopause for millennia; new disease burden more likely environment/toxins/lifestyle
- Opposes **unopposed estrogen** solely to suppress FSH; black-box / WHI history as caution against repeating Premarin-era mistakes
- Growth without differentiation → cancer risk framing

### 4D. Clinical Saliva Practice
- Target **progesterone : estrogen ≈ 200 : 1** (lab he uses)
- ~80% of his menopausal patients on progesterone alone do well; thinner / younger more often need estrogen
- Retest ~3 months; stop progesterone 48–72h before retest to detect tissue oversaturation
- Lab preference: **Doctor’s Data** (simple, inexpensive; used Genova/others historically)

## Segment 5 — Q&A: Cortisol/DHEA Support, Estrogen Forms, Progesterone Dosing (1:36:00–1:56:00)

### 5A. Cortisol & DHEA Support (Dr. Gerber)
- Mild: adaptogen / adrenal supplement (e.g. rhodiola-containing)
- Moderate: “super energy IV” weekly × 3 — vitamin C 15g, Mg, B5, B6, NAD 100mg (adrenals heavy users of C/B5/B6; NAD as citric-acid-cycle limiter)
- Severe flat curve: hydrocortisone pattern per *The Safe Uses of Cortisol* — e.g. 10 mg AM + 5 mg noon (~4h half-life); short bridge 2–3 months while rebuilding
- DHEA if low: women often 5–10 mg
- High nighttime cortisol: phosphatidylserine (e.g. “Serine Calm”) to help reboot pituitary rhythm / lower nocturnal cortisol

### 5B. Estrogen Distinctions
- Estradiol = major systemic hormone; balance with progesterone
- Estriol favored for vaginal dryness (protects breast framing); nightly × 1 week then 2×/week × 4 weeks
- Avoids oral estrogen (more conversion toward estrone; carcinogenicity concern)

### 5C. Progesterone Protocols
| Life stage | Form | Timing | Dose guidance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premenopausal | Cream (or oral if preferred) | Cycle days 12–26 | Cream 10–40 mg |
| Peri / irregular / menopause | Oral (daily “basement”) | Continuous | Oral 50–200 mg (often start ~100) |
| Sleep-primary goal | Prefer oral | Night | Allopregnanolone metabolite aids sleep |
| Postmenopause cream | Cream | 5 days on / 2 off | Prevent continuous tissue climb |

- Dose scales to **gap** vs 200:1 target (like BP drug titration)
- Imbalance either direction (excess E or excess P) is bad; receptor desensitization from excess P → “stopped working”

### 5D. Career Advice
- Get the MD/DO credential, then practice physiology-based medicine; start with stress when facing the “checks every box” patient
