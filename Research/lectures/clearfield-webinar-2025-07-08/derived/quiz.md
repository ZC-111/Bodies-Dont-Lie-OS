## Section B — PBM Fundamentals & Mechanisms (8 questions)

- B) **PBM typically refers to red / near-infrared light; phototherapy covers a broader light spectrum including UV/blue** ✓
- C) Phototherapy is only for skin; PBM is only for brain

### Q6. Roughly what fraction of sunlight energy is infrared?
- A) 10%
- B) 25%
- C) 40%
- D) **~50%** ✓

> *UV ~10%, visible ~40%, infrared ~50% — relevant to indoor lifestyle “IR deficiency.”*

### Q7. The primary mitochondrial chromophore discussed for red/NIR PBM is:
- A) Melanin
- B) **Cytochrome c oxidase** ✓
- C) Hemoglobin A1c
- D) Catalase

### Q8. Which statement best captures the hormetic dosing idea in PBM?
- A) Longer sessions always yield better outcomes
- B) **Find a sweet spot that mildly stresses cells to activate recovery without overdoing it** ✓
- C) Only continuous 2-hour sessions work
- D) Dose is irrelevant if wavelength is correct

### Q9. Beyond ~1 inch from the skin / with clothing intervening, what happens to therapeutic light delivery?
**Answer:** **Significant loss of dose**; clothing blocks light. Stay as close as possible / on bare skin for target areas.

### Q10. Which neurotrophic factor is upregulated by PBM and supports neuronal survival / synaptogenesis?
- A) VEGF only
- B) **BDNF** ✓
- C) Insulin
- D) Cortisol

### Q11. Far-infrared around ~3000 nm primarily acts by:
- A) Stimulating cytochrome c oxidase like 830–1070 nm
- B) **Heating via water-molecule interaction (systemic warmth), not classic mitochondrial PBM** ✓
- C) Blocking all ROS
- D) Inducing seizures

### Q12. True or False: Visible bright red light is required for deep/transcranial PBM to be working.
**Answer:** **False.** NIR in the mid-hundreds to ~1000+ nm is largely invisible; looking for “red glow” can mislead users seeking brain-depth effects.

## Section C — Clinical Applications & Safety (8 questions)

### Q13. In the former-athlete TBI/CTE-risk study discussed, cognitive improvements were reported after approximately:

- A) 4–6 Hz
- B) **9–11 Hz** ✓
- C) 20–30 Hz
- D) 40 Hz only

> *Slower peak alpha (~8 Hz, especially ~7 Hz) associated with cognitive impairment; observational reports of speeding toward healthy range with tPBM.*

### Q15. Which pulse rate has the strongest research signal for working memory / “brain brightening”?
- A) 1 Hz
- B) **40 Hz** ✓
- C) 1024 Hz
- D) Random noise

### Q16. Which is a true contraindication / major caution for transcranial PBM as presented?
- A) Epilepsy (absolute contraindication)
- B) **Active cancer (angiogenesis concern) and non-titanium metal implants in the head** ✓
- C) History of migraine
- D) Prior concussion

> *Epilepsy was explicitly NOT a contraindication for non-ocular head PBM; titanium skulls generally acceptable.*

### Q17. Hashimoto’s evidence cited by Clearfield used which common wavelength and session pattern?
**Answer:** **830 nm** laser/NIR; example protocol **10 sessions over 5 weeks** (often framed **2–3×/week × 5 weeks**); one 2013 study reported 47% reduced/stopped levothyroxine at 9-month follow-up.

### Q18. What pre-session habit did Jordan emphasize to improve benefit and reduce headache/fatigue side effects?
**Answer:** **Hydration / drink water before PBM** (IR–water interaction; dehydration worsens adverse responses).

### Q20. In the long-COVID cognitive study using Neuronic (+ Thor bed comparison arm), what exposure pattern produced significant cognitive gains in ~1 month?
