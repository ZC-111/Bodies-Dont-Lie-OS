### Q2. In Gallogly's language, what is a "beachhead"?
**Answer:** Being known in your market for **one message → one market → one offer** — the offer that gets patients *into* the office (not every service you eventually deliver). Military Normandy analogy: concentrate force on a narrow toehold.

### Q3. According to Gallogly, roughly what share of revenue typically comes from what share of offerings?
- A) 50% of revenue from 50% of offerings
- B) **80% of revenue from 20% of offerings** ✓
- C) 90% of revenue from new Facebook leads
- D) 100% of revenue from à la carte procedures

### Q4. What was Carolina Men's Clinic's core entry offer / guarantee?
**Answer:** Come once, meet a doctor, get a test dose; **if you don't see results in 12 minutes, you owe nothing** — focused solely on fixing men's erections.

### Q5. Dr. Nicole Garcia was offering men's/women's sexual health, HRT, and cosmetics and struggling at ~$50K/month. What change drove growth to $200K+/month?

### Q8. What is the true purpose of marketing/advertising in Gallogly's model?
- A) Maximize form fills

- C) **Get a customer who will pay premium prices** ✓

### Q9. Who is the core cash-pay customer demographic Gallogly emphasizes?
- A) College students
- B) **Age 55+ with disposable income** ✓
- C) Under-40 biohackers only
- D) Medicaid-only panels

> *He notes 55+ patients often spend 4–8× more than typical 40-year-olds.*

### Q10. Why do Facebook lead ads often fail for premium cash conversion?
**Answer:** They interrupt moderately curious people who fill forms to "make you go away." Interruptive leads lack **know / like / trust**, so they price-shop and rarely buy premium packages. Relationship content (especially video) outperforms interruptive lead gen for premium offers.

### Q11. How long do prospects typically research a provider online before contacting them, per Gallogly's cited studies?
- A) 24–48 hours
- B) 1–2 weeks
- C) **4–6 months** ✓
- D) 2–3 years

### Q12. What geographic dominance target does Gallogly recommend for content (not vanity influencer metrics)?
**Answer:** Dominate a **25–50 mile radius** around the practice — local authority, not 10K subscribers / million views.

## Section C — Conversion, Pricing & Packages (5 questions)

### Q13. What is Gallogly's minimum pricing rule relative to delivery cost?
- A) Costs should be 50% of price
- B) **All associated costs ≤ 25% of the price charged** ✓
- C) Match the conference average for PRP
- D) Always discount 20% for Medicare patients

> *Example: $250 cost → charge at least $1,000.*

### Q14. Name the three conversion tools Gallogly requires (beyond beachhead + education).
**Answer:** **Scripts**, a **signature solution** (package), and **premium pricing**.

### Q15. Why does Gallogly ban à la carte selling for cash programs?
**Answer:** Patients cherry-pick cheap pieces and disappear; it mirrors insurance medicine's step-wise delays; 55+ buyers want a **one-stop solution** they can commit to once — like refusing a cardiologist who offers "one artery at a time" or discount bypass specials.

### Q16. What happened when Dr. Amy Duffy raised her top package from $5,500 to $10,000?
- A) All patients left
- B) **She sold two $10K packages the following Monday and later doubled annual revenue ($1.2M → $2.4M)** ✓
- C) She had to finance new equipment
- D) She returned to insurance panels

### Q17. True or False: Raising prices tends to decrease patient compliance.
**Answer:** **False.** Gallogly argues **higher prices increase compliance** because patients have more skin in the game (plus psychological/placebo effects).

## Section D — Case Application & Action (3 questions)

### Q18. Summarize Dr. Ash Goyle's transformation metrics after adopting the system.
**Answer (key points):**
1. Regenerative spine cash focus; cases ~$5K–$19K
2. From ~$10K/month cash (mostly insurance) to **~$125–135K/month** cash
3. Dropped >50% of insurance; **60-day waitlist**; collects payment at "yes" (cash velocity)

### Q19. A rural orthopedic surgeon says, "My farmers won't pay more than $750." Using the Petropoli/Tripoli case, what counter-evidence does Gallogly offer?
**Answer:** Follow beachhead + scripts + signature packages + premium pricing anyway. After 5-1-30 reactivation: 12 consults → 4 procedures @ ~$20K ATV; later multi-joint packages $15.5–25K; practice became more profitable with fewer employees; went **insurance-free** with ~$17.5K average package — in rural upstate NY.

### Q20. A burned-out functional medicine clinician has five rotating offers, weak Facebook leads, and à la carte pricing. Outline Gallogly's first-week action plan from this webinar.
**Answer:**
1. Pick **one beachhead** (one message / market / entry offer) — pause rotating weekly promotions
2. Start **education content** (YouTube/local video + testimonials) aimed at 55+ searchers in a 25–50 mile radius; add a short in-room indoctrination video
3. Build **one signature package**; stop à la carte; set price so costs ≤25% of fee
4. Use a **script** to advise/guide/recommend the package (no discounting)
5. Immediate experiment: **raise prices 25%** without a committee
6. Optional: listen to *Practice Profit System*; get *Anatomy of a Seven-Figure Medical Practice*; consider structured mentorship if DIY stalls
