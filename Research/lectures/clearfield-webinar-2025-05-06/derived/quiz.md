### Q2. What approximate total-T window does Sammons prefer for physiologic restoration?
- A) 200–400
- B) 400–600
- C) **700–1100** ✓
- D) 1500–2000

> *Super-physiologic levels drive aromatization side effects without added benefit for most midlife men.*

### Q3. Why does Sammons insist on AST/ALT (and liver support) before or early in TRT?
**Answer:** Poor hepatic clearance lets hormones linger → increased aromatization → early “honeymoon” then return of fatigue, heaviness, and estrogenic symptoms.

### Q4. Homocysteine target discussed for methylation/hormone clearance readiness is:
- A) < 15 µmol/L
- B) **< 10 µmol/L** ✓
- C) < 5 µmol/L
- D) Any value is fine if MTHFR is heterozygous

### Q5. Name four root causes or contributors to low T beyond “aging.”
**Answer (any four):** hypothalamic/pituitary failure, testicular trauma/orchiectomy, TBI, mumps, Klinefelter, chemo/radiation, diabetes, hemochromatosis, OSA/hypoxia, alcohol, obesity, endocrine disruptors (plastics/BPA/phthalates), cortisol/stress, ultra-processed diet.

### Q6. True or False: Every man starting TRT should be placed on anastrozole within the first 8 weeks.
**Answer:** **False.** Cited ~1,700-man cohort: only ~2.5% needed AI; average start was nearly **1 year** after TRT initiation.

### Q9. Why does Sammons argue men still need estradiol?
**Answer:** E2 supports libido (sweet spot), bone mass/quality, tissue repair (local aromatization), gonadotropin feedback, and (with adequate T) better body composition and CV markers. Crushing E2 to zero recreates menopausal-type symptoms.

### Q10. Approximate E2 thresholds discussed for considering intervention:

**Answer:** Omni Calculator (search “testosterone to estradiol ratio”); literature ranges roughly 10:1–13:1 (Halasa prefers closer to ~30:1 for men).

### Q14. How does tamoxifen differ from anastrozole in this lecture’s framing?
**Answer:** Tamoxifen is a **SERM**, not an aromatase inhibitor. Men (especially prostate-cancer dosing) often have significant side effects (night sweats, sexual dysfunction, weakness). Prefer AI when aromatase blockade is the goal; don’t casually swap them.

## Section D — Integration, Safety & Practice Pearls (4 questions)

### Q17. How should TRT interact with untreated OSA or poorly controlled diabetes?
**Answer:** Best practice: optimize first. Practical: may start **concomitantly** if motivation is nil, but emphasize whole-picture care and monitor H&H / symptoms while fixing sleep and metabolism. TRT alone will not fix nightly hypoxia.

### Q18. What FDA update (Feb 2025) did Nario cite regarding testosterone and heart risk?
**Answer:** FDA **removed the cardiovascular black-box warning** on testosterone products (post-TRAVERSE). Residual concern highlighted: possible **blood-pressure** increase. Clearfield notes TRAVERSE used a single branded 1.62% patch product.

### Q19. A 52-year-old man has total T 480, fatigue, central adiposity, E2 38, and refuses injections. He wants “something short of full TRT.” What approach fits this webinar?
**Answer:**
1. Optimize sleep/OSA screen, A1c, liver clearance, methylation, lifestyle (lift, protein, disruptors)
2. Consider trial low-dose **anastrozole** (e.g., 0.5 mg 3×/week) as monotherapy to raise endogenous T via LH/FSH — transparent n-of-1
3. Natural supports: DIM, Ca-D-glucarate, iodine/progesterone strategy per Halasa if estrone-heavy
4. Recheck labs ~3 months; if still symptomatic, compounded **troche** TRT preferred over pellets
5. Track T:E2 ratio; don’t crush E2 preemptively

### Q20. What is OS01, and how should AMPK activation be cycled per Nario/Halasa?
**Answer:** OS01 is described as an oral AMPK activator hitting all **12** known human AMPK pathways (metabolism/mitochondria/insulin sensitivity). Dose cited **100 mg**; cycle **6 weeks on / 4 weeks off**. Halasa warns against chronic AMPK-on states — alternate with mTOR/repair signaling (avoid “always-on” longevity excess like continuous rapamycin).
