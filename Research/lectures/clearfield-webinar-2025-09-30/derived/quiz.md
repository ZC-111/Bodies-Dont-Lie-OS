## Section B — Rigidity & Muscle Spindles (7 questions)

### Q4. How does Garrison define rigidity (per the 2003 PD/NIH consensus he cites)?

### Q5. Approximate number of muscle spindles in the human body, and their firing rate?
**Answer:** ~**30,000** spindles firing roughly **50–100 times per second**.

### Q6. Why can a hyperactive spindle loop “outvote” voluntary movement?
- A) Spindles only fire during sleep
- B) **Myotatic reflex ~30 ms vs voluntary movement ~100–200 ms** ✓

### Q7. In complete quadriplegia, what happens to denervated limb muscles within ~4–6 weeks according to Garrison?
**Answer:** Severe atrophy plus extreme hypertonicity — muscle feels like a fibrous band driven by spindle feedback; voluntary innervation from brain is required to keep muscle “a muscle.”

### Q8. Which set of transmitters tends to **inhibit** muscle-spindle firing in his framework?
- A) Glutamate, ACh, NE, serotonin

- C) Substance P only
- D) Histamine and bradykinin only

### Q9. Why does Garrison emphasize substance P in aging tendons and rigidity?
- A) It only dilates blood vessels
- B) **Injured/stretched tenocytes make SP (and ↑ SP receptors); nerves take it up → sustained interneuron firing** ✓

### Q10. What bedside ROM metric does he use as a forearm rigidity biomarker (AMA / UPDRS-style)?
**Answer:** **Extended wrist rotation** — arm extended, thumb vertical; normal ~**70–90°** internal/external rotation; post-TBI and Parkinsonian patients often markedly reduced.

## Section C — Quorum Sensing, CLA & Clinical Protocols (7 questions)

### Q11. What is a quorum-sensing agent, in one sentence?
**Answer:** A molecule secreted in discrete amounts by microbes so its concentration measures bacterial density and, at a threshold (“quorum”), triggers coordinated group behaviors (e.g., biofilm).

## Section D — Integration & Clinical Judgment (3 questions)

### Q18. How does he link L-dopa to long-term treatment failure in Parkinson’s?
**Answer:** He proposes **Malassezia** (and possibly other skin microbes) become **more aggressive in the presence of L-dopa**, worsening pathogenic skin-biome behavior — a hypothesized reason L-dopa loses effectiveness over time.

### Q19. A 55-year-old with early tremor and forearm rigidity for 4 months asks about topical CLA. What expectation-setting matches the lecture?
**Answer:** Early disease may respond dramatically (“light switch”); multi-year rigidity with painful peri-muscular nodules may need QS agents **plus** careful mechanical disruption, and long-standing spinal cord peptide effects may limit full reversal.

### Q20. A patient with anxiety, PMS, poor dream recall, white nail spots, and “normal labs” is not improving on standard psych meds. Using only this webinar’s tip segment, what integrative workup/treatment axis would you consider?
**Answer:**
1. Consider **HPU** — urinary kryptopyrrole (light-protected)
2. Replete **P5P, zinc, magnesium** + antioxidants / anti-inflammatory diet
3. Frame as functional Zn/B6 drain from excess pyrroles, especially at hormone transitions
4. Reassess mood, dream recall, and stress tolerance after repletion
