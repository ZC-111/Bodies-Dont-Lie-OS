## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Ivermectin — Mechanism vs Hype (12:25–20:46)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario, DO (Speaker 4)

### Framing
- Goal: **scientific calibration, not advocacy** — open-minded without becoming “evidence indifferent.”
- Polarizing COVID-era drug now attracting longevity / oncology / neurodegeneration interest; **enthusiasm is ahead of the evidence**.

### Mechanistic pathways cited (beyond antiparasitic)
- AKT / mTOR, AMPK, PAK1
- Mitochondrial signaling
- Autophagy / mitophagy  
→ Metabolism, inflammation, cancer biology, aging-adjacent biology

### Longevity signal (preclinical only)
- **2018 rotifer study (*Biogerontology*)**: mean/median lifespan ↑ ~8–21%; some mobility / mitochondrial activity improvements
- Similar nematode findings at certain doses
- **Key limit:** no mammalian lifespan studies, no mouse longevity data, no human anti-aging trials — not mainstream geroscience (unlike rapamycin / urolithin A framing)

### Cancer interest
- In vitro: apoptosis, antiproliferation, anti-angiogenesis; mTOR / ROS signaling alterations
- NCI-linked preclinical exploration noted
- Ongoing Phase 1 → Phase 2 interest: ivermectin + checkpoint inhibitors in metastatic **triple-negative breast cancer**
- 2026 observational / self-report cohort (ivermectin + mebendazole, mixed cancers) — **not** oncologic standard of care

### Neurodegeneration
- 2025 streptozotocin Alzheimer’s model: ↓ amyloid-β plaque burden, neuroinflammation, pathological brain changes; ↑ neuronal-protection markers
- Proposed links: anti-inflammatory / oxidative-stress modulation + autophagy “cleanup”
- Animal only — **not** an Alzheimer’s therapy claim

### Stronger near-term applied signal — malaria transmission (BOHEMIA, 2025)
- Monthly ivermectin ↓ malaria incidence ~**26% in children**
- Mechanism is unusual: **not direct antimalarial cure** — humans become temporarily toxic to mosquitoes that feed on treated individuals → transmission control

### Dosing / safety Q&A (as stated)
| Context | Dose frame |
|---|---|
| FDA parasitic | ~150–200 µg/kg (short courses) |
| FLCCC-style high dose | ~0.4–0.6 mg/kg for ~5–7 days (not chronic daily) |
| Alzheimer’s animal models | ~2–3 mg/kg (very high vs clinical antiparasitic) |

- Common AEs: GI; higher / prolonged use → neurologic + hepatic risk signals
- Neuropathy reports tied to **high dose + months-long non-intermittent** use; overall clinical safety profile described as generally good at conventional intermittent use

### Clinical pearl
> Biology is fascinating; do not collapse mechanistic intrigue into clinical protocol without mammalian / outcome evidence.

## Segment 4 — Varanasi: Pharmacist Lens, Ayurveda Bridge & PK Deep Dive (1:00:37–1:23:52)

**Speaker:** Swathi Varanasi, PharmD  
**Cred:** Co-created / first resident of the (then) only US integrative-health residency pathway for pharmacists; work with Greenspace Herbs / Shafi / Amit

### Why bioavailability is a clinical problem
- Global botanical market projected >**$500B by 2030**
- Patients already use ashwagandha, berberine, curcumin for stress, metabolic health, inflammation, aging resilience
- Limitation: poor solubility, rapid metabolism, limited oral systemic exposure → consistent intake ≠ meaningful circulating actives

### Ayurveda frame (bridge, not replacement)
- Ayurveda = “science of life”; whole-person: digestion, metabolism, constitution, stress, sleep, environment, mind–body
- **Tri-dosha:** Vata (movement / nervous), Pitta (metabolism / transformation), Kapha (structure / resilience)
- Takeaway for non-Ayurvedic clinicians: **individual variability** — personalization aligns with systems biology / genomics / microbiome thinking
- Energetic language (*prana* / *qi*) mapped to electrochemical / biophysical physiology (membrane potentials, ion gradients, ETC, conformation)

### Pharmacology model shift
- Single-molecule / single-target pharma vs botanical **network** effects (St. John’s wort vs SSRI mild–moderate depression cited as network example — not casual substitution advice)
- Standardization of markers (withanolides, berberine content, curcuminoids) valuable **and** matrix co-factors matter
- Higher dose ≠ higher exposure ≠ better outcome → need **PK**

### Quantum Ayurveda definition (Varanasi)
- Trademark / Greenspace framework: resonance-informed botanical optimization
- Tools: AI molecular modeling + Raman fingerprinting + PK research
- Question: can molecular / energetic state be characterized, reproduced, and linked to patient-relevant exposure?

### PK Study 1 — Berberine QA
- Design: randomized, double-blind, crossover; **7-day washout**; single oral dose; plasma via validated **LC-MS/MS**
- Arms: **500 mg Berberine QA** vs **500 mg Berberine HCl (~97%)**
- Results (approx.):
  - **~18× higher Cmax**
  - **~4× higher total systemic exposure (AUC)**
  - Faster Tmax; longer elimination half-life

### PK Study 2 — Ashwagandha QA
- Design: randomized, double-blind, crossover; single dose; total withanolides by LC-MS/MS
- Comparator: standard extract **2.5% withanolides**
- Results (approx.):
  - **~5.6× higher Cmax**
  - **~18× higher AUC**
  - Longer half-life + mean residence time; **similar Tmax** → retention/exposure story more than “faster absorb only”

### PK Study 3 — Curcumin QA
- Comparator: **95%** standard curcuminoid extract
- **500 mg** single oral dose, **non-fasted** (more real-world than fasted PK)
- Plasma total curcuminoids over **24 h**
- Results (approx.):
  - **~20× higher AUC**
  - **~22× higher Cmax**
  - Detectable levels by ~**15–30 min**; faster peak

### Cross-study clinical message
- Not all extracts are interchangeable; label milligrams can mislead
- PK is first-layer evidence — **outcome trials still required**
