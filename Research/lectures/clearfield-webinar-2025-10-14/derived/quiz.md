## Section B — Progesterone After Hysterectomy (8 questions)

### Q7. Clearfield’s practice rule for estrogen therapy is:
- A) Estrogen alone if uterus removed
- B) **Never estrogen without progesterone (patients “pinky swear”)** ✓
- C) Progesterone only if endometrial hyperplasia documented
- D) Progestins preferred over micronized progesterone

### Q8. The Honeymooners metaphor maps to:
- A) Estrogen = Alice; progesterone = Ralph
- B) **Estrogen = Ralph (havoc); progesterone = Alice (cleanup)** ✓
- C) Both hormones = Ralph
- D) Progestins = Alice

### Q9. Why does the panel reject “no uterus → no progesterone”?
**Answer:** Progesterone receptors and neurosteroid roles exist outside the endometrium (brain/GABA, bone osteoblasts, breast quiescence, CV, kidney diuresis, immune NF-κB, thyroid axis). Hysterectomy removes the uterus, not those needs.

### Q10. Compared with natural progesterone, synthetic progestins are described as more likely to cause:
- A) Improved sleep and diuresis only
- B) **Weight gain, fluid retention, depression, headaches, bloating, acne, hair loss, insomnia** ✓
- C) Guaranteed pregnancy maintenance
- D) Lower breast-cancer risk than natural P in the WHI-style framing presented

### Q11. The neurosteroid cascade linking progesterone to calm is:
- A) Progesterone → cortisol → serotonin
- B) **Progesterone → 5α-DHP → allopregnanolone → GABA tone** ✓
- C) Progesterone → estradiol → dopamine only
- D) Progesterone → melatonin → orexin

### Q12. Oral vs topical progesterone for anxiety/sleep — Nario/Clearfield preference rationale:
**Answer:** **Oral** micronized progesterone at bedtime preferred when the goal is first-pass formation of **allopregnanolone** and GABA stimulation; topical is less systemic/sedating and better for local breast/ovarian use; avoid high-dose capsules in the morning.

### Q13. Carol’s pearl for patients who feel “drunk/foggy” on progesterone:
- A) Stop all hormones permanently
- B) **Consider impaired allopregnanolone clearance via sulfation and glucuronidation** ✓
- C) Switch immediately to medroxyprogesterone acetate
- D) Double the morning oral dose

### Q14. Approximate luteal-phase serum draw timing Clearfield uses for cycling patients:
- A) Day 1–3
- B) **Days 19–21** ✓
- C) Day 14 only
- D) Random anytime is ideal for cycles

## Section C — Hormone Testing Strategy (4 questions)

### Q15. Match the “dating app” persona: 24-hour urine is best described as:
- A) Reliable classic baseline buddy
- B) Free-spirited free-hormone adventurer
- C) **Deep-dive storyteller of metabolites and enzyme pathways** ✓
- D) Only useful for TSH

### Q16. Which statement about saliva testing is correct per Nario?
- A) Best lifelong sole monitor for high-dose cream HRT
- B) **Strong for free hormone, multi-point cortisol/CAR, early topical tracking; can skew with gingivitis or cream residue** ✓
- C) Always insurance-covered and cheap (~$20)
- D) Superior for synthetic progestin levels

### Q17. Dream Team combo recommended:
**Answer:** Serum for baseline (and insured BHRT monitoring) + saliva for active cortisol/early topical patterns + 24-h urine for estrogen metabolism/detox and oral HRT insight — always correlated with symptoms.

### Q18. Why might serum alone panic an outside clinician watching a woman on testosterone pellets?
**Answer:** Circulating levels may read **100–300** while Clearfield cites tissue/symptom targets nearer **~30–40**; pellet kinetics ≠ “range sheet” physiology — dose to symptoms, not fear of the printout.
