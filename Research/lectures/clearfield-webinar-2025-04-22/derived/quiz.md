## Section A — Muscle, Sarcopenia & Assessment (8 questions)

### Q1. After age 40, approximately how much muscle mass and strength are lost annually (per Clearfield’s intro framing)?
- A) 0.1% mass / 0.5% strength
- B) **1–2% mass / 1.5–5% strength** ✓
- C) 5–10% mass / 10% strength
- D) Muscle mass is stable until age 70

### Q2. Why does Meletis argue GLP-1 “no excess muscle loss” papers may not match real clinic patients?
**Answer:** Average patients are often **overfed and undernourished**. Cutting calories of a poor diet (without optimizing protein, training, mitochondria, etc.) predictably worsens muscle, myokines, and metabolic outcomes — same logic as unsupervised bariatric calorie collapse.

### Q3. Skeletal muscle represents roughly what fraction of metabolically active body mass in Meletis’s framing?
- A) 10%
- B) 25%
- C) **~40%** ✓
- D) 70%

### Q4. Adults over 60 in the lowest third of strength were cited as having approximately what relative all-cause mortality vs the upper third?
- A) 10% higher
- B) 25% higher
- C) **~50% higher** ✓
- D) No association after fitness adjustment

> *Meletis emphasizes strength remains inversely associated with death even controlling for cardiorespiratory fitness.*

### Q5. Which office metric does 2024 literature propose adding as a “new vital sign,” and why does Meletis like it?
- A) Waist-to-hip ratio only
- B) **Hand-grip dynamometry — simple, sit-down, correlates with morbidity/mortality/hospital outcomes, engages patients** ✓
- C) DEXA every visit
- D) Serum myostatin on every patient

### Q6. True or False: Meletis says get-up-and-go testing is worthless compared with grip strength.
**Answer:** **False.** He frames grip as a convenient, literature-supported metric that compares well — not as throwing other functional tests out with the bathwater.

### Q7. What is **anabolic resistance** in this lecture’s usage?
- A) Refusal to take anabolic steroids
- B) **Impaired muscle uptake/utilization of amino acids (e.g., leucine) despite availability** ✓
- C) Excess mTOR from all egg proteins
- D) High free testosterone blocking IGF-1

### Q8. Name three clinical states that raise myostatin (per lecture).
**Answer:** Any of: **aging, obesity/diabetes-metabolic syndrome, disuse/sedentary lifestyle, injury/immobilization (e.g., joint replacement).**

## Section B — Thyroid, Detox, Exercise & Myokines (6 questions)

### Q9. In euthyroid sick / severe calorie-restriction physiology as taught, which conversion is preferred vs “brake pedal”?
- A) T4 → reverse T3 preferred
- B) **T4 → T3 preferred; reverse T3 is the brake / non-productive pathway** ✓
- C) TSH must always rise first
- D) Only free T4 matters

### Q10. Why does Meletis warn “don’t die from dieting” during rapid fat loss?
**Answer:** Rapid adipose loss can **mobilize stored lipophilic toxins/hormones** (DDT/DDE, forever chemicals, etc.). Without phase I–II–III detox support, burden is redistributed (“feather duster”), stressing an already stressed system.

### Q11. What pre-conception weight-loss caution did he cite from literature counseling?
**Answer:** Avoid encouraging a woman planning pregnancy to lose **more than ~5 pounds in 6 months**, because mobilized toxins may enter the reproductive milieu.

### Q12. Myokines / exerkines are primarily released by:
- A) Bed rest
- B) **Muscle contraction / exercise** ✓
- C) High reverse T3 alone
- D) PPI therapy

### Q13. Lowering myostatin is linked to increased irisin and what adipose change?
- A) Whitening of brown fat
- B) **Browning of white fat (thermogenic / UCP-1-related calorie-burning phenotype)** ✓
- C) Pure visceral fat expansion
- D) No adipose effect

### Q14. The 1953 London bus study pearl: who had roughly twice the coronary disease?
**Answer:** Sedentary **drivers** vs walking **conductors** who collected tickets — early occupational evidence that putting muscle to work matters for heart outcomes.

## Section C — Fortetropin, Protocols & Q&A Pearls (6 questions)

### Q16. In the 21-day human RCT Meletis cited, Fortetropin increased average protein synthesis by about:
- A) 2%
- B) 8%
- C) **18%** ✓
- D) 50%

### Q17. Critical administration rule for Fortetropin:
- A) Must be baked into muffins to activate
- B) **Do not cook — heat destroys structural activity; mix cold into yogurt/smoothie/pudding; egg allergy is a contraindication** ✓
- C) Only IV compounding
- D) Requires daily myostatin blood tests

### Q18. Typical adult dosing range discussed for Fortetropin:
**Answer:** About **11.6–19.8 g once daily** (often described as a scoop-and-a-half of the original). Higher/heroic multi-dose strategies were used in a Duchenne case under clinical judgment.

### Q19. For patients on GLP-1s, Meletis’s integration stack emphasizes:
- A) GLP-1 alone is sufficient if scale weight drops
- B) **Multivitamin + mitochondrial support + amino acids + Fortetropin (+ training/protein sufficiency)** ✓
- C) Immediate follistatin peptide for everyone
- D) Stopping all protein to spare kidneys

### Q20. Which peptide did Meletis say he likes clinically, and what sleep-apnea access tip did he give for muscle patients?
**Answer:** **BPC-157** (~500 mcg; affordable compounding source discussed). For OSA workup when deductibles are high: **Lofta.com** remote sleep testing (~$189 cited) to get specialist-read testing and device pathway.
