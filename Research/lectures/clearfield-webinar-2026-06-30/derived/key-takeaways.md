# Key Takeaways — Clearfield Webinar 2026-06-30

## Top 10 Clinical Pearls

1. **ApoB < 65 = no new plaque. ApoB < 40 = plaque reversal.** Order ApoB on every patient ($5, no fasting required).

2. **Rosuvastatin 5mg + Ezetimibe 10mg costs < $1/month** and is more effective at lowering ApoB than a $740/month supplement stack.

3. **Ezetimibe has a strong brain-health signal** — 7× reduction in cognitive decline via 14-3-3/hexokinase disruption and autophagy activation. Near-zero side effects.

4. **Optimally dosed estradiol (FSH 5–24) may independently lower ApoB** by suppressing PCSK9 and upregulating LDL receptors. Track lipids when dosing HRT.

5. **Niacin remains the only monotherapy moving all lipoproteins favorably**, and lowers LP(a) up to 75%. Start 50mg/meal, titrate to 3g/day over months. Takes patience and education.

6. **Statin myalgia resolves in 93% of cases** with vitamin D targeting ~40 ng/mL. Try this before switching drugs.

7. **CAC score misses soft plaque** — the dangerous kind. Prefer CT angiogram for patients with risk factors.

8. **Screen children early for FH** (ApoB by age 9). 5 in 1,000 carry it. Early intervention prevents MI by age 40–50.

9. **Nattokinase may destabilize plaque** — Hartman reports a case of suspected plaque rupture leading to stroke. Weak evidence base despite social media hype.

10. **Regressive vicariation during detox should be benign and transient.** If symptoms become severe, prolonged, or involve new organ dysfunction, stop labeling it "detox" and work up the patient.

## Debate Summary: Statins vs. Niacin

| Dimension | Statin (Rosuvastatin) | Niacin |
|---|---|---|
| **ApoB lowering** | Strong (30–50%) | Moderate |
| **LP(a) lowering** | ❌ May increase it | ✅ Up to 75% |
| **Outcomes data** | 170K+ patients, 26 RCTs | Coronary Drug Project (older) |
| **Cost** | $0.42/month | $5/month |
| **Brain safety** | Desmosterol risk in ApoE4 | Neuroprotective |
| **Side effects** | Myalgia (vitamin D fixes), rare rhabdomyolysis | Flushing (resolves), rare hepatotoxicity |
| **Patient compliance** | Easy (one pill) | Harder (titration, education) |
| **Best for** | Primary prevention, high ApoB | LP(a), statin-intolerant, uninsured |

**Consensus:** Both have a role. Not either/or. Match to patient profile.
