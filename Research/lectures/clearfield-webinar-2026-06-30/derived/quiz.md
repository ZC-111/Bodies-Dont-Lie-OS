### Q5. Which imaging modality does Hartman prefer over CAC score, and why?
**Answer:** CT angiogram — because CAC detects only calcified plaque (which is relatively stable) and misses soft plaque, which is more dangerous and rupture-prone.

### Q6. In the PCSK9 mutant spectrum, a loss-of-function mutation results in:

### Q7. What did the retracted "Keto CTA Study" (Norwitz et al.) actually show?

### Q8. True or False: CRP elevation is required for ApoB-driven atherosclerosis to occur.
**Answer:** **False.** ApoB-containing particles cause arterial wall retention and plaque formation before CRP elevation. A person can develop carotid atherosclerosis with elevated ApoB even if hsCRP is low and insulin sensitivity is excellent.

### Q12. For statin-intolerant patients experiencing myalgia, what intervention resolves symptoms in 93% of cases?
- A) CoQ10 200mg/day
- B) Switching to pravastatin
- C) **Vitamin D supplementation targeting ~40 ng/mL** ✓
- D) Magnesium glycinate

> *Per Steven McConnell. Mechanism: statins deplete cholesterol precursors → disrupted vitamin D synthesis.*

### Q13. Why should statins be avoided (or used cautiously) in patients with elevated LP(a)?
**Answer:** Statins can increase LP(a) levels by up to 200% in some individuals. McConnell recommends niacin for LP(a) patients instead. Red yeast rice is the only "statin" that actually lowers LP(a) (~23% reduction).

### Q14. What is the significance of desmosterol monitoring in statin-treated patients?
**Answer:** Desmosterol is a cholesterol precursor needed for brain cell function. ApoB cannot cross the blood-brain barrier, so the brain depends on local synthesis. If statins drive desmosterol too low, cognitive impairment occurs — especially in ApoE4 carriers. Reversible if caught within ~5 years. Only Boston Heart Diagnostics still offers the assay.

## Section C — Niacin Deep-Dive (4 questions)

### Q15. What is McConnell's recommended starting dose for niacin, and how is it titrated?
**Answer:**
1. Start at **50mg per meal** (essentially nothing)
2. Titrate slowly over weeks
3. Always take with food
4. Target: 3g/day for maximum benefit
5. Flushing resolves within 2 weeks–1 month

### Q16. What outcomes data does McConnell cite for niacin?
- A) JUPITER trial
- B) **Coronary Drug Project — 90% relative risk reduction, NNT < 4** ✓
- C) FOURIER trial
- D) Heart Protection Study

### Q17. Name three non-lipid benefits of niacin cited in the discussion.
**Answer (any three):**
- Raises growth hormone → normalizes deiodinase (T4→T3)
- Anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, anti-fibrotic
- Reverses kidney disease (↑ GFR, ↓ PTH, ↓ FGF-23)
- #1 of 150 FDA-approved drugs for fatty liver
- Shrinking glioblastomas (Alberta research)
- Lowers oxidized phospholipids (only drug that does this)
- Forestalls dementia/Alzheimer's

### Q18. Why does McConnell dismiss the AIM-HIGH and HPS2-THRIVE trials as evidence against niacin?
**Answer:** He argues they used niacin improperly (wrong dosing, wrong populations). Cleveland Clinic's trial didn't even use niacin. The metabolite blamed on niacin (4-methylnicotinamide/4py) comes from junk food, not niacin therapy. A nephrology journal demonstrated the metabolite's benefit 6 months before the negative trial was published.

## Section D — Clinical Integration (2 questions)

### Q19. A 15-year-old obese male presents with ApoB of 130, TG of 200, and family history of paternal MI at age 40. What is your approach?
**Answer (per Hartman):**
1. Counsel firmly on lifestyle modification (diet, exercise) — age allows some time
2. If ApoB doesn't improve with lifestyle in 3–6 months → start pharmacologic lipid-lowering
3. Frame it clearly: "You could die like your father at 40, or make changes now"
4. Consider genetic testing for familial hypercholesterolemia
5. ~5 in 1,000 patients carry FH; early capture prevents MI by age 40–50

### Q20. A 57-year-old female with elevated LDL, elevated ApoB, positive CAC score, and no current medications is reluctant to take statins. How do you approach this using insights from this webinar?
**Answer:**
1. Start with **ezetimibe** (minimal side effects, cognitive protection, cheap)
2. Optimize **estradiol to FSH 5–24** (Clearfield protocol) → may independently ↓ ApoB
3. Check LP(a) — if elevated, start niacin (50mg/meal, titrate to 3g/day)
4. Consider rosuvastatin 5mg if ApoB still above target; address statin fears with data (20% dementia risk *reduction*, not increase)
5. If statin-intolerant: vitamin D to 40 ng/mL, switch to ezetimibe-only + niacin
6. Get CT angiogram (not just CAC) to assess soft plaque
7. Follow ApoB serially — target < 60 (prevention) or < 40 (established CVD)
