## 4. 60-second video script

**Hook (0–5s):** “Your LDL can look ‘fine’ while ApoB is still laying plaque.”  
**Beat 1 (5–20s):** ApoB counts atherogenic particles — ~$5, no fasting drama.  
**Beat 2 (20–35s):** Hartman’s first-line framing: rosuvastatin 5 mg + ezetimibe 10 mg for under a dollar a month.  
**Beat 3 (35–50s):** Muscle pain? Fix vitamin D toward ~40 ng/mL before quitting. CAC misses soft plaque — ask about CT angiogram.  
**CTA (50–60s):** “Next labs: order ApoB. Know your number.”  
**On-screen text cues:** `ApoB &lt;65` · `&lt;$1/mo` · `D ≈ 40` · `CTA &gt; CAC`

## 6. Patient takeaway card

**Title:** Know your ApoB  

**Plain-language bullets (5):**
1. ApoB estimates how many plaque-forming particles you have.  
2. Speakers framed &lt;65 as a prevention-style target (confirm with your clinician).  
3. A low-cost statin + ezetimibe combo was highlighted as highly affordable.  
4. Calcium scores can miss soft plaque — imaging choice matters.  
5. If statin muscles ache, ask about vitamin D before stopping cold.

**Ask your clinician:** “Can we add ApoB to my labs and review my target?”  
**Avoid / myth bust:** “High LDL is always safe on keto”; “Nattokinase is harmless plaque cleaner.”  
**Disclaimer:** use block above.

## 7. Clinician pearl card

**Title:** ApoB-first prevention — Hartman summary  

**Pearls (5, denser):**
1. Serial ApoB as primary marker (~$5–6); fasting optional.  
2. First-line framing: rosuvastatin 5 mg + ezetimibe 10 mg.  
3. Myalgia pathway: vitamin D ~40 ng/mL → 93% resolution claim (McConnell).  
4. Prefer CT angiogram over CAC for soft plaque risk.  
5. Preserve niacin branch for LP(a)/intolerance — tag disagreement; watch desmosterol in ApoE4.

**Monitor:** ApoB on therapy; vitamin D if myalgia; LP(a) when indicated; desmosterol if cognitive concern.

## 8. Do-not-say list

- Don’t present ApoB targets as universal guidelines without speaker attribution.  
- Don’t meme “statins always safe” or “statins always toxic.”  
- Don’t promote nattokinase for plaque.  
- Don’t erase the Hartman/McConnell niacin split.  
- Don’t cite the retracted keto CTA study as safety proof.  
- Don’t turn McConnell’s $5 niacin story into unsupervised mega-dosing advice.
