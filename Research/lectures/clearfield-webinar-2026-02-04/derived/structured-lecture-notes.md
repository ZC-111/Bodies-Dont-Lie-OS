## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Kratom (Mitragyna speciosa) (12:13–19:02)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario, DO (Speaker 1)

### ★ Core Thesis
> Kratom sits in a **gray zone** — not benign, not equivalent to prescription opioids. Clinical role is **neither endorsement nor condemnation**, but risk–benefit discussion, dose awareness, and patient education.

### Botany & Pharmacology
- **Botanical:** *Mitragyna speciosa* (Southeast Asian)
- **Primary alkaloids:** mitragynine, 7-hydroxymitragynine
- **Mechanism:** partial **mu opioid receptor agonist**; also adrenergic and serotonergic activity; strong **dose-dependent** pharmacology
- **Key distinction:** Kratom is **not an opioid drug**, but **is** an opioid receptor agonist

### Dose-Dependent Effects
| Dose range | Clinical pattern |
|---|---|
| **Low dose** | Stimulant-like: focus, alertness, sociability, ↓ anxiety, mood/energy support |
| **Higher dose** | Analgesia, sedation, dependence risk |

### Evidence Cited
- Limited controlled human data; much from animal models / surveys
- **2020 RCT** (*Journal of Psychoactive Drugs*): regular users, cold pressor test — pain tolerance **11 sec → 25 sec** at 1 hour post-kratom; placebo unchanged → measurable analgesia

### Risks
- Dependence with daily/escalating use; withdrawal
- Nausea, constipation, sedation at higher doses
- Most adverse outcomes: **high doses, concentrated extracts, poly-substance use** (alcohol, benzos, opioids)
- Café/bar framing → patients assume “safe” and often **don’t disclose** unless asked
- Speaker 3: blood/urine testing for kratom now entering clinical practice

### Clinical Pearl
> Ask why / how much / how often. Low-dose social use ≠ high-dose extract + polysubstance risk. Conversations matter more than ideology.

## Segment 3 — Siobhan Mitchell: Mitochondrial Therapeutics & Aging (19:02–1:00:00)

**Speaker:** Siobhan Mitchell, PhD — CSO, MitoQ  
**Credentials (intro):** PhD SUNY Albany; postdoc brain aging UW; Unilever / Nestlé / PepsiCo nutrition–cognition trials; senior director Noom; 80+ papers; 8 patents (aging, cognition, mood)

### ★ Core Thesis
> Mitochondria are the cell’s “CSO” — not only ATP, but calcium, apoptosis, stem-cell and **immune regulation**. Dysfunction begins in the **20s–30s** and is a root driver of many hallmarks of aging and early chronic disease.

### 3A. Why Mitochondria Matter Early
- Muscle ATP production and mitochondrial copy number decline from ~age 30 → 80
- Drivers: innate ROS, inflammaging, poor glucose control
- Environmental hits: **microplastics, infections, mold, pesticides, heavy metals**
- Many of ~12 hallmarks of aging (telomere attrition, senescence, mtDNA damage, ↓ NAD) can be framed as mitochondrial root causes
- Early mechanisms of T2DM and CVD linked to mitochondrial dysfunction

### 3B. Immune Axis — cGAS–STING & Inflammasome
- Infection (e.g., COVID) / Alzheimer’s-related pathogens → mitochondrial ROS + **mtDNA release**
- mtDNA sensed as foreign → cytokine cascade via **NLRP3 inflammasome**
- Unresolved inflammasome → long COVID, inflammaging, amyloid overproduction
- Framing: target mitochondria to modulate immune response, not only “energy”

### 3C. Nutrient Landscape — NAD Precursors vs PQQ vs Mitoquinol
| Nutrient | Mitchell framing |
|---|---|
| **Omega-3s, B vitamins, CoQ10, ergothioneine** | Foundational mitochondrial support list |
| **NAD precursors (NMN, NR, nicotinamide, NMN-H)** | Raise plasma NAD ≠ proven mitochondrial efficiency; clinical data thin vs hype |
| **PQQ** | Mitochondrial antioxidant; NADH oxidation; biogenesis; **good brain penetration**; cognition (young + old); popular in Asia |
| **Mitoquinol (MitoQ)** | Strongest **clinical dataset** among mitochondrial nutrients discussed |

### 3D. Mitoquinol Mechanism
- Designed ~25 years ago by Mike Murphy & Robin Smith (NZ)
- Problem: oral CoQ10 poorly enters mitochondria (~**5%**); large / amphipathic
- Solution: **triphenylphosphonium (TPP+)** tag → positively charged → accumulates in negatively charged mitochondria (up to ~**90%**)
- Synthetic: idebenone-like CoQ moiety + TPP+ (not found in nature)
- Effects: ↓ oxidative stress, membrane protection, ↑ endogenous antioxidants, support ATP under stress (hypoxia, infection)
- Mild mitochondrial uncoupling → balanced redox (not zero ROS)
- **Does not replace CoQ10 ETC role** — often co-prescribed with CoQ10/ubiquinol (Mark Houston / Nario protocol note)

### 3E. Pivotal Clinical Signals Presented

**1. Postmenopausal FMD (University of Colorado)**
- Late menopause (≥55) → better FMD than normal-onset menopause (extra estrogen years)
- Acute **160 mg** MitoQ → FMD improved toward premenopausal peers; ↓ oxidative stress; lipid shifts (palmitic acid called out)
- Chronic ~100-woman follow-up planned/ongoing; preliminary **cognitive** (verbal/visual memory) signal in ≥65s

**2. Sedentary / non-exercisers**
- Acute **80 mg** → improved FMD; exercise felt more fruitful
- Separate exercise literature: power, time-trial performance

**3. Liver health (largest MitoQ study — unpublished at talk)**
- n=179, concerned-but-not-diagnosed liver population
- **20 mg × 12 weeks**: ALT ↓ **7.5 U/L** vs ~4 U/L control; cholesterol benefit strongest if baseline lipids poor
- Subjective: perceived stress, fatigue/weakness, concentration
- Greater ALT effect if higher baseline inflammasome markers (lymphocytes, RDW, WBC)

**4. Type 2 diabetes — subclinical cardiac energy**
- **40 mg × 12 weeks**; MRS creatine–ATP ratio → **~15%** ↑ energy production
- Improved diastolic function (PEDSR) at rest and stress — prevent silent diabetic heart injury framing

**5. COVID exposure proof-of-concept**
- Post-exposure **20 mg**: infection ~**30%** vs **30/40** placebo-arm infection rate cited; shorter/ milder/delayed symptoms if infected
- Inflammasome mechanism hypothesized; larger studies planned; long-COVID work upcoming (~9 months)

**6. Preclinical — clonal hematopoiesis (DNMT3A)**
- CH common (~10–20% ages 60–70; >50% by age 80) → cancer + infection risk
- Mouse model: MitoQ preferentially pushed mutant overactive cells toward apoptosis; supported healthy stem-cell maturation
- Human study discussions underway

### 3F. Clinical Applications Summary Table (Mitchell)

| Agent | Stronger signals cited | Weaker / null signals cited |
|---|---|---|
| **PQQ** | Cognition (processing speed, flexibility), hypercholesterolemia, sleep quality (insomnia), CRP/inflammasome, blood flow | — |
| **NMN** | Sleep (elderly), insulin (prediabetes), hypertension (large doses), PAD | Plasma NAD rise ≠ outcomes |
| **NR** | Mild inflammasome (COPD), PAD, HF mitochondrial markers, subtle sleep metabolic rate | **2025 nulls:** long COVID, hypertension; limited functional benefit |
| **Mitoquinol** | Early CVD stiffness/HTN, diabetes CV protection, PAD, HF, fibromyalgia/CFS (pain + cognition), liver/ALT, hepatitis C ALT, kidney vascular health, COVID exposure PoC | Cost; GI AEs |

### 3G. MCRP (Mitochondrial Collaborative Research Program)
- Provides Mitoquinol / placebo / powder for investigators
- ~20 ongoing studies (clinicaltrials.gov); examples: PTSD–vascular function, stroke rehab (gait/QoL)
- Contact: s.mitchell@mitoq.com; MCRP site / mcrp@mitoq.com

## Segment 4 — Q&A: Dosing, Safety, CoQ10, Biomarkers (1:00:01–1:31:21)

**Speakers:** Siobhan Mitchell, Bill Clearfield, Melvin Nario, audience (chat)

### Dosing Philosophy
- Prefer **higher induction → maintenance titration** once symptomatic or biomarker goal met
- Accumulates in mitochondria; recycles like CoQ10; plateaus — no classic “adaptation”
- Highest cited continuous dose: **160 mg × ~6 months** without major issues; excess → **GI effects** (natural limiter)
- **Take 30 minutes before meals** — food markedly ↓ bioavailability
- Clinical dose framing: **20 mg capsules** common; practitioner brand ~**$60/month** via mitoq.com portal or Fullscript
- Use-case examples for “when to step down”: statin myalgia resolution; fertility metrics (Anne Shippy cited)

### Safety
- Main AEs: mild nausea / stomach upset (often days then resolves); occasional headache; few study dropouts for AEs
- If intolerant: lowest dose or short break then retry
- Thalassemia + mild ALT/AST: possible ALT benefit; limited human data

### CoQ10, Urolithin A, Combinations
- **CoQ10 + MitoQ:** compatible and often recommended — different jobs (ETC vs mitochondrial antioxidant targeting)
- MitoQ does **not** raise blood CoQ10; may raise SOD/catalase
- **Urolithin A:** pure mitophagy enhancer (Mitchell: Nestlé research background); intermittent boost may be preferable to constant mitophagy vs MitoQ’s antioxidant/redox balance
- Organ-specific SKUs (MitoQ Eye / Heart / Curcumin): same Mitoquinol core + co-ingredients (lutein, Mg, vit D, curcumin) — **not** organ-specific targeting tags
- Sinatra-style stack (CoQ10, PQQ, Mg, acetyl-L-carnitine, D-ribose) + MitoQ: mechanistically sensible; post-ischemia human data limited
- Gates Foundation–funded **preeclampsia** study starting (month of talk)

### Biomarkers to Follow
- BP; inflammasome markers (MDA, IL-6, etc.)
- Patient-reported energy / brain fog often most noticeable
- ATP increases mainly in **compromised** states (Alzheimer’s models, HF, diabetes) — not healthy organisms
- Telomere length / senescence / autophagy: strong **preclinical** signals (AMPK, NRF2, mTOR); human longevity endpoints lacking

### NAD Hype Pushback
- NMN-H marketed on plasma NAD — Mitchell: insufficient proof of mitochondrial benefit
- Literature does not match consumer/influencer expectations; expensive disappointment risk for patients

### Mitochondrial Transplantation
- Canadian “Mito Revolution”–style projects discussed
- MitoQ approached as preservative during transplant matrix + post-transplant support
