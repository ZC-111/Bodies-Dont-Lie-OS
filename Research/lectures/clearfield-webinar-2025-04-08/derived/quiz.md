## Section A — Foundations & Principles (6 questions)

### Q1. Which statement best captures Hakimi’s core framing of homeopathic prescribing?
- A) Match the ICD diagnosis to a fixed protocol remedy
- B) **Illness is specific to the patient; similars address the symptom picture, not the nosologic label alone** ✓
- C) Always use a single high-potency remedy for six months
- D) Homeopathy equals high-dose herbal supplementation

> *SOAP “does not work” as the primary driver; three patients with “sinusitis” may need three different pictures.*

### Q2. What are the three principles of homeopathy as taught in this lecture?
**Answer:** (1) Law of similars (like cures like); (2) law of highly diluted substances; (3) illness specific to the individual.

### Q3. How does Hakimi distinguish clinical from classical homeopathy?
- A) Clinical uses only nosodes; classical never does
- B) **Classical usually one remedy after a long interview; clinical often multiple remedies from ~300 pharm/tox-grounded medicines with shorter interviews** ✓
- C) Classical is FDA-regulated; clinical is not
- D) Clinical rejects the law of similars

### Q4. Why is turmeric powder that a patient calls “homeopathy” usually *not* homeopathy?
**Answer:** It is used in **material/supplement doses**. Homeopathic medicines are highly diluted preparations of natural substances selected by similars — not bulk nutraceuticals.

### Q5. What historical role does Constantine Hering play in this lecture?

- C) Authored the Flexner Report
- D) Invented repertory software

## Section B — Reactional Modes / Miasms (7 questions)

### Q7. Otter writes “soric” and “psychotic.” What are the correct clinical terms Hakimi intends?
**Answer:** **Psoric** (open elimination) and **sycotic** (blocked elimination; “Sy-” not “Psy-”), plus **syphilitic** and later **tuberculinic**.

### Q8. Which pattern best describes the **psoric** reactional mode?
- A) Progressive weight gain, fibroids, chronic catarrh without return to baseline
- B) **Acute eliminations via GI/GU/lung/skin/mucosa with return to prior health; pathway alternation and cyclic recurrence possible** ✓
- C) Ulcers progressing to necrosis and night bone pain as the primary theme
- D) Only autoimmune destruction

### Q9. Which cluster is most **sycotic**?
- A) Seasonal hay fever that fully clears between episodes
- B) **Blocked elimination after an insult → diet-resistant weight gain, cellulite, warts/polyps/fibroids/BPH, chronic mucosal discharge, depressive tendency** ✓
- C) Double-jointedness and scoliosis alone
- D) Fixed meal-time migraines only

### Q10. What etiologic question does Hakimi emphasize especially for sycotic shifts?
**Answer:** **“After what?”** — insults such as vaccines/injections, chronic medications (e.g., layered SSRIs), hormones, pollution/mold/metals, stress, or emotional trauma.

### Q11. Tuberculinic mode is best described as:
- A) A form of syphilitic destruction
- B) **A fast-eliminating subset of psoric mode with weak constitution, cold sensitivity, frequent infections, adenopathy, anxiety/irritability** ✓
- C) Identical to carbonic morphology
- D) Always requiring mercury remedies first-line

### Q12. Which medication list matches **psoric** mode remedies named in the lecture?
- A) Thuja, Medorrhinum, Nitricum acidum
- B) **Sulfur, Psorinum, Arsenicum album, Lycopodium** ✓
- C) Mercurius, Aurum, Syphilinum only
- D) Only Opium and Coca

### Q13. True or False: Hahnemann taught that every patient must be forced into exactly one miasm category.
**Answer:** **False.** Individualization comes first; chronic reactional mode is a *helper* when a pattern is clear. Mixed modes also exist.

## Section C — Morphology, Sensitive Type & Practice Integration (7 questions)

### Q14. Match the connective-tissue constitution:
- Fluoric → ?
- Phosphoric → ?
- Carbonic → ?

**Answer:** **Fluoric = lax** (double-jointed, asymmetry, scoliosis risk, later ptosis/prolapse themes); **Phosphoric = normal** ligaments (tall-thin, inflammatory cycling, mood fluctuation); **Carbonic = tight** (stocky, square teeth/vertebrae, constipation, sclerosis tendency).

### Q15. A patient has post-meal bloating below the belt line, must nap after eating and wakes **groggy/angry**, gets migraines if lunch is late, scratches rashes until they bleed for relief, and flushes with contradiction. Most likely sensitive-type example from the lecture?
- A) Nux vomica
- B) **Lycopodium** ✓
- C) Sepia only
- D) Carbonic morphology alone (no remedy type)

### Q16. How does Hakimi contrast Nux vomica’s postprandial nap with Lycopodium’s?
**Answer:** Both may need to sleep after eating, but **Nux vomica awakens refreshed**; **Lycopodium awakens groggy/irritable** — “never wake a Lycopodium from their nap.”

### Q17. Since when has homeopathy been FDA-regulated in the US according to Hakimi, and how should products be framed?
- A) 1994 DSHEA — as dietary supplements only
- B) **Since 1938 — as medications (with drug numbers), not casual “remedies”** ✓
- C) Never regulated
- D) Only state boards regulate; FDA has no role

### Q18. What did the EPI3 study (~8,559 patients) suggest in Hakimi’s summary?
**Answer:** Patients seeing homeopathy-involved physicians (including GP+homeopathy) had **comparable outcomes with less conventional medication use and fewer medication side effects** across MSK, URI, sleep, anxiety/depression domains.

### Q19. For suspected UTI that might progress to pyelonephritis, Hakimi’s sequencing is:
- A) Homeopathy alone for two weeks no matter what
- B) **Homeopathy for symptom support plus appropriate antibiotic coverage given acuity/risk** ✓
- C) Always repertory software first
- D) Immediate syphilinum

### Q20. Why does Hakimi decline marketed “COVID-19 homeopathic remedies” despite CDH tracking common symptom pictures (e.g., Mercurius, Argentum nitricum, Phosphorus)?
**Answer:** COVID has a known symptom **proving** picture, but he lacks rigorous **experimentation** showing those dilutions reliably treat COVID; he prefers remedies with established experimental track records.
