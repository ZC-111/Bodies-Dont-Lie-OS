### Q11. Which short-chain fatty acid is emphasized as the primary energy source for renewing GI epithelial cells?
- A) Acetate only
- B) Propionate only
- C) **Butyrate** ✓
- D) Lauric acid

### Q12. After a course of antibiotics, what postbiotic dosing strategy does Pelton recommend?
**Answer:** Take postbiotics during the antibiotic course but **not simultaneously in the stomach** (separate by a few hours); then **double or triple** the postbiotic dose for **10 days to 2 weeks** afterward to accelerate microbiome rebuild.

### Q13. For SIBO, why are live high-CFU probiotics often poorly tolerated, while a postbiotic-dominant ferment may be better tolerated?
**Answer:** In SIBO, colon-type bacteria ferment substrates in the **small intestine**, causing gas/bloating/pain. A postbiotic-heavy product delivers **acid-stable metabolites with very low live CFU**, so it is less likely to feed SI fermentation; still titrate (e.g., 1 capsule QOD → daily → full dose).

### Q18. What rapamycin regimen does Pelton estimate most longevity users follow, based on Mannick-type data?
- A) 1 mg daily indefinitely without monitoring
- B) **~5–6 mg once weekly (~85–90% of users; ~20% immune boost signal at 5 mg/wk)** ✓

### Q19. How does Pelton resolve the apparent paradox that rapamycin (mTOR inhibitor) can still support healthy muscle over time?
**Answer:** Continuous mTOR without autophagy is like a Formula 1 car with no pit stops. Autophagy clears damaged proteins/membranes (“flat tire”) so that when growth signals return, rebuild replaces dysfunctional parts — intermittent mTOR inhibition supports long-term tissue quality rather than endless growth of damaged machinery.

### Q20. Name RX Vitamins’ reflux product Pelton prefers and its two active components.
**Answer:** **Acid Block** — **DGL** (deglycyrrhizinated licorice) coats the esophagus; **sodium alginate** forms a floating foam barrier on gastric contents to reduce reflux (often chewed; bedtime use common).
