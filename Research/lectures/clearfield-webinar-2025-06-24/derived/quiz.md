## Section B — Why Standard Oncology Stalls (5 questions)

### Q5. According to Halasa, the two top barriers preventing eradication after bulk tumor shrink are:
- A) Poor patient compliance and insurance denial
- B) **Cancer stem cells and cancer-associated fibrosis** ✓
- C) Only Ki-67 elevation and hypercalcemia
- D) Excess HDL and low CRP

### Q6. Why are cancer stem cells relatively resistant to chemo/radiotherapy in this framing?
- A) They lack mitochondria entirely
- B) **They behave like normal cells with strong antioxidant defenses and immature MHC presentation** ✓
- C) They only live in bone marrow
- D) They exclusively use fatty-acid oxidation

### Q7. What metabolic shift (Warburg) does Halasa emphasize after mitochondrial injury from chemo/RT?
**Answer:** Shift from oxidative phosphorylation to **glycolysis** → lactic acidosis → more CSC differentiation + fibrosis stimulation + “cold” immune polarization (Treg/Th2/Th17).

### Q8. True or False: Halasa recommends abandoning conventional chemotherapy entirely in favor of ivermectin/mebendazole monotherapy.
**Answer:** **False.** He argues for **both aisles** — keep chemo/RT/immunotherapy but make them smarter (pharmacogenetics, ~10–20% photo-activated low dose, nanoparticle loading) while adding CSC/fibrosis tools.

### Q9. What delivery platforms does Halasa prefer for targeted integrative oncology agents?
**Answer:** **Cyclodextrin** loading (especially oral/peptides) and **platelet nanoparticles** for injectables/antibodies/chemo — aiming drug into pathology rather than naked systemic spillover.

- A) ACE2 cleavage

## Section D — Mebendazole, Dosing & Q&A Pearls (6 questions)

### Q15. Mebendazole’s mitotic mechanism is best summarized as:
- A) Topoisomerase I poisoning
- B) **β-tubulin binding → microtubule destabilization → mitotic arrest → apoptosis** ✓
- C) Pure aromatase inhibition
- D) Direct PD-1 antagonism

### Q16. Which CSC developmental pathways does mebendazole inhibit alongside anti-VEGF effects?
**Answer:** **Hedgehog (SMO/Gli1)** and **WNT/β-catenin**; also reduces VEGF (Avastin-like normalization of kinked vessels).

### Q17. Approximate dose ranges cited for ongoing CSC work:
- A) Ivermectin 1 mg weekly; mebendazole 10 mg once
- B) **Ivermectin ~12–26 mg/day or 200–400 µg/kg; mebendazole 100 mg BID–TID for 3–6 months** ✓
- C) Both agents only as single 1-gram IV boluses
- D) Pediatric OTC pinworm packs unchanged for all adults indefinitely

> *Stay within FDA-approved therapeutic index / max safe doses when repurposing.*

### Q18. For primary cancer prevention, Halasa’s preference is:
- A) Continuous high-dose ivermectin for all adults
- B) **Health optimization, polyphenols, hormone balance, personalized genetics — reserve ivermectin for treatment contexts** ✓
- C) ASEA water as monotherapy
- D) Fenbendazole alone based on social media

### Q19. Regarding brain tumors / BBB:
**Answer:** **Ivermectin** is framed as **not** a good CNS penetrator (neurotoxicity concern if it did); **mebendazole** may cross at higher doses (Hopkins pediatric glioma early-phase cited); **albendazole** also discussed as a brain-oriented alternative.

### Q20. What circulating tumor DNA assay was discussed as conventional-facing with ~95% PPV, and how does it differ from CD44-style CSC monitoring?
**Answer:** **Signature** — DNA-based ctDNA assay for solid tumors (biopsy + blood), often insurance-facing; complementary to **cell-based** CSC marker panels (e.g. CD44) rather than a full replacement.
