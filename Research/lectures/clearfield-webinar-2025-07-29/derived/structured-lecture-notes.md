## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Celtic vs Himalayan Salt (16:39–21:00)

**Speaker:** Dr. Nario (Speaker 2); Icelandic salt note from Speaker 4

### Celtic Salt
- Origin: Brittany, France — moist, grayish, clay salt flats
- Higher magnesium, potassium, calcium; more moisture; somewhat lower sodium per gram vs table salt
- Caveat: ocean-origin → reports of possible **microplastic contamination**

### Himalayan Salt
- Origin: ancient sea deposits, Punjab region of Pakistan; pink from iron oxide (trace)
- 80+ trace minerals in very small amounts; drier, crystalline
- Less ocean-pollutant exposure risk; purity depends on mining/source
- Iron oxide risk is negligible at culinary doses (industrial/cosmetic inhalation is the real concern)

### Clinical Pearl
> Natural salts can support electrolytes vs refined table salt, but **quality + moderation** matter. Prefer third-party–tested sources. For electrolytes → Celtic may edge ahead (Mg). For contamination concern → Himalayan (or Icelandic, per conference anecdote).

## Segment 3 — Dr. Jacqueline Sammons: Sexual Health as an Indicator of Whole Health (21:07–1:10:40)

**Speaker:** Jacqueline Sammons, DO — Transformation Health & Wellness Center, Anaheim CA  
**Training:** Traditional OB/GYN → integrative; WHI (2002) framed as under-educating providers/patients on HRT

### ★ Core Thesis
> Sexual health is a **vital sign of whole-person health** — not merely STI risk, birth control, or “partner duty.” Desire, arousal, orgasm, and pain-free intimacy reflect biochemistry, relationships, meds, and chronic disease.

### 3A. Framing & Practice Gap
- Media sexualizes appearance while shaming desire — especially for midlife women (“I feel bad for my husband”)
- Providers undertrained on **non-judgmental** sexual-history taking
- Most “sexual health” surveys track disease risk / partners / contraception — not desire, arousal, pleasure, satisfaction, or relationship quality
- WHO definition (used as starting point): physical, emotional, mental, and social well-being in relation to sexuality — **not merely absence of dysfunction**

### 3B. Domains to Assess
**Female:** relationship quality, desire/libido, arousal/lubrication, orgasm (some never climaxed), pain-free activity  
**Male:** relationship context, desire (not only firmness/duration), climax/ejaculation timing vs partner perception  
- Sexual assault history common (~1 in 4–5 women cited) — often never asked; pain → aversion → secondary desire loss

### 3C. Conditions & Medications Affecting Sexual Health
**Conditions:** CVD/HTN, sleep apnea (esp. men with ED — ask about snoring; home sleep studies preferred), diabetes (neuropathy/vascular), neuro disease, mental health, menopause, cancer (cortisol/survival mode; anatomy change from surgery/radiation)

**Medications (under-counseled):**
- Beta-blockers (e.g., propranolol), diuretics, opioids, prostate meds
- **Statins** (highly prescribed)
- Antiepileptics, H2 blockers, chemo agents
- SSRIs/SNRIs (huge libido hit); **Wellbutrin** often better for libido-sensitive patients
- Antipsychotics, benzos, muscle relaxants, sleep agents

### 3D. Bidirectional Benefits
Good sexual health associated with better CV outcomes, diabetes morbidity, mental health, cognition, QoL  
Meta-analysis filter: ~20,000 studies → **~63** usable — research gap is large

**Selected findings (as presented):**
- Female sexual function ↔ psychological, environmental, social, overall QoL
- Postpartum: sexual **frequency** not linked to better QoL (obvious clinically; abrupt progesterone/estradiol drop after placenta; breastfeeding suppresses estradiol)
- Menopausal symptom burden ↔ worse sexual function (bidirectional); desire linked to vitality; arousal/orgasm linked to lower physical pain
- Male sexual frequency/QoL ↔ overall QoL; satisfaction ↔ vitality in older age
- STI rates high in seniors/assisted living (cited ~8:1 women:men partnering ratios) — still ask older adults
- Psych: better sexual function ↔ fewer psych symptoms; women’s arousal inversely related to depression; men’s dysfunction strongly tied to depression (chicken/egg)
- Frequency: benefit plateaus around **~once/week** — useful counseling target

### 3E. Screening Tools
- Short 5-question screens to open the door (prefer brief over another long survey)
- Longer scored indexes available for research-oriented practices
- Normalize: “I ask all my patients…” — same tone as bowel habits / mold exposure / smoking

### 3F. Intervention Stack
1. **Lifestyle:** strength training (↑ testosterone/DHEA), diet, hydration, quit smoking, limit alcohol
2. **Stress boundaries:** stop over-volunteering; protect couple time; date night on calendar
3. **Counseling:** individual, couples, sex therapy — especially empty-nest reconnection
4. **Relationship assessment:** sometimes the mismatch *is* the diagnosis
5. **HRT** framed as replacing what ovaries no longer make — tissue integrity, injury prevention, adiposity, not only hot flashes
6. **PDE5 inhibitors** (Viagra/Cialis) when appropriate after CV screen
7. **Lubricants** — no shame
8. **Peptides** (kisspeptin, gonadorelin mentioned) for younger men wanting fertility-sparing support
9. Exploration (devices, literature) **after** addressing tissue pain/hormones — don’t dismiss dyspareunia with “just read erotica”

### Clinical Pearl
> Make sexual health part of the ROS. Comfortable providers create safe vulnerability — e.g., 75yo man who had never told anyone (including his wife) about loss of desire.

## Segment 4 — Q&A: HRT in Older Women, Labs, CVD Framing (1:17:37–1:27:00)

**Speakers:** Sammons, Clearfield

### Case Prompt
70yo menopausal woman, thyroid cancer 30 years prior, total hysterectomy — same workup as peri/new menopause.

### Sammons Workup (first pass)
- Estradiol, LH, FSH, progesterone, total + free testosterone, DHEA-S
- Thyroid: TSH, free T4, free T3
- Metabolic: A1c, fasting insulin; AST/ALT
- Methylation: MTHFR, homocysteine

### Clearfield Add-Ons
- Estrone, 25-OH vitamin D3, pregnenolone, prolactin, SHBG
- Thyroid antibodies at least once on everyone (~1/3 new patients positive in his locale)

### Vaginal Estradiol
- Framed as **near-universally safe** for chronic/recurrent UTI; black-box warnings under review for removal
- Protocol cited: fingertip at introitus **nightly × 2 weeks**, then **2 nights/week indefinitely**
- Cited 2016-era signal: vaginal estradiol associated with lower CV/stroke **mortality** (inflammation ↓, less bacterial seeding via recurrent UTI pathway)

### Systemic HRT (Sammons approach in 70s)
- Low-dose estradiol patch (or Vivelle-type) ± progesterone ~50 mg even post-TAH if sleep/nocturia/anxiety/sweats — estradiol/progesterone as “sisters”
- Monitor levels **q3–6 months**
- Female testosterone: **2.5–5 mg/day**; target levels ~**40–70** (not male range)
- DHEA important for brain + as testosterone precursor

### Risk Framing
- Literature risk ↑ for MI/stroke when HRT started **>10 years after menopause** or **age >60** — but trials used **oral CEE + MPA** (not bioidentical estradiol/progesterone)
- **Transdermal estradiol** safer than oral for VTE/stroke/CVD events
- Literature conflates progestin / progesterone / “progestogens” — chemical structure matters
- British Menopause Society (Clearfield): often no hormones after 60 — contrast with U.S. integrative practice

## Segment 5 — Hashimoto’s / TPO, Vitamin D, Insulin Resistance (1:27:20–1:36:50)

**Speakers:** Sammons, Clearfield

### Vitamin D & Thyroid Antibodies
- Sammons: optimize vitamin D serum ~**80–100**; antibodies often fall (studies cited); “directed minimalist”
- Dosing evolution: 50,000 IU weekly (COVID-era) ± viral “hammer” 50,000/day × 3; shifting toward **5,000 IU daily** with better serum response
- Clearfield rule of thumb: ~**+8 ng/mL per 1,000 IU/day**; recheck ~6 weeks ideal, often 3–4 months

### Clearfield Hashimoto Stack (as stated)
1. Optimize thyroid function: free T3 upper third (~**3.4–4.2**), TSH ~**0.8–2.0**
2. **Plant sterols** (e.g., Moducare BID) — ~50% antibody ↓ in a year
3. **LDN** 0–3.0 mg (thyroid sensitive; often need to ↓ thyroid dose)
4. **Iodine** if low (~60–65% screened low; replace even with autoimmunity — following Hartman)
5. **Selenium** 200 mcg BID — ~40% antibody ↓ in literature
6. **Black cumin seed oil** — additional ~15–20% antibody ↓

### Post-COVID Endocrine Signal
- Sammons: spike protein / infection / vaccine era associated with new Hashimoto’s, new diabetes, “turbo cancer” vignette in small practice; teens with rising insulin resistance (Collins chat)

### Insulin Resistance / Teens
- Check **leptin** when IR present
- Counsel teens directly (not only parents): whole foods, cut gluten/processed sugar, strength + cardio, outdoor time / less screens
- AAP approved **tirzepatide** down to age 6 for obesity — integrative use includes **microdosing** (e.g., volleyball athlete ~1.25 mg q2 weeks) for inflammation, tissue recovery, focus
- Elevated leptin → prioritize protein (80–100 g/day cited); consider mold/CIRS/EBV if lifestyle already solid

## Segment 6 — Sexual Health Beyond Hormones: Cancer, Disability, Bias (1:42:50–1:53:00)

**Speakers:** Nario, Sammons, Clearfield

### Key Points
- Sexuality ≠ automatic testosterone/estrogen prescription — psychology, connection, spirit matter

- Self-pleasuring framed as oxytocin/dopamine / immune-supportive in cancer contexts (Nario) — still taboo; providers should normalize carefully
- **Paraplegic case:** HRT stopped post-accident for VTE concern; later providers refused labs — “you’re paralyzed, you’re not having sex.” Sammons reframes: vitality, tissue integrity, mental health, non-penetrative intimacy still matter
- Gender bias: men get PDE5s freely while age-matched partners with dyspareunia/UTI/frozen shoulder are denied hormones
- Female T FDA framing limited (HSDD / transition) — clinical benefits broader (muscle, vessels, clarity, vigor)
- Young men with T ~270 at age 28 — endocrine disruptors discussed

### Practical Pearl
> Estradiol patch adhesive reaction: spray **Flonase (fluticasone)** on skin ~30 min before patch — patient-taught tip
