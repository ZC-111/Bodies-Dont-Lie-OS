## Section A — Sexual Health Framework (7 questions)

### Q1. Per the WHO framing Sammons used, sexual health is best defined as:
- A) Absence of STIs and unwanted pregnancy
- B) Ability to achieve orgasm on demand
- C) **A state of physical, emotional, mental, and social well-being related to sexuality — not merely absence of disease/dysfunction** ✓
- D) Concordance with partner’s libido

### Q2. What is the main practice gap Sammons identifies in typical “sexual health” surveys?
**Answer:** They focus on disease risk, partner count, barrier use, and contraception — not desire, arousal, pleasure, satisfaction, or relationship quality.

### Q3. A man presents with erectile dysfunction. Beyond PDE5 discussion, what sleep-related question should be asked immediately?
- A) Dream recall frequency
- B) **Whether he snores / has had a sleep evaluation (OSA is a major ED driver)** ✓
- C) Melatonin brand preference
- D) Caffeine after 2 pm only

> *Sammons prefers home sleep studies over lab nights when available.*

### Q4. Which antidepressant class is highlighted as relatively libido-sparing compared with SSRIs/SNRIs?
- A) Tricyclics as a class
- B) MAOIs
- C) **Bupropion (Wellbutrin)** ✓
- D) Mirtazapine only

### Q5. In the meta-analysis Sammons cited, roughly how many of ~20,000 screened studies made the final usable set?
- A) ~2,000
- B) ~630
- C) **~63** ✓
- D) ~6

### Q6. True or False: In the postpartum period, higher sexual frequency was linked to better quality of life in the cited analysis.
**Answer:** **False.** Sexual frequency was not associated with better QoL postpartum — tissue trauma, lochia, lactation, sleep disruption, and abrupt estradiol/progesterone collapse dominate.

### Q7. For counseling on sexual frequency and psychiatric/well-being benefit, what approximate cadence did Sammons cite as “enough” (plateau)?
- A) Daily
- B) **About once per week** ✓
- C) Once per month
- D) Three times per day

## Section B — HRT & Women’s Hormones (7 questions)

### Q8. Sammons’ vaginal estradiol induction protocol for GSM/recurrent UTI (as stated) is:
- A) Daily indefinitely from day one
- B) **Nightly × 2 weeks, then 2 nights/week ongoing** ✓
- C) Weekly only
- D) Only with systemic oral CEE

### Q9. Why does Sammons argue vaginal estradiol can reduce cardiovascular/stroke *mortality* even if MI still occurs?
**Answer:** Local estrogen lowers genitourinary inflammation and recurrent UTI burden → less bacterial seeding/inflammatory load affecting coronary vessels (mechanism as presented; cite 2016-era literature she referenced).

### Q10. Typical female testosterone dose and target range Sammons described:
- A) 20–40 mg/day; levels 300–500
- B) **2.5–5 mg/day; levels ~40–70** ✓
- C) 10 mg weekly injection only; levels unrestricted
- D) OTC DHEA only; no serum target

### Q11. Literature showing ↑ MI/stroke when HRT starts after age 60 / >10 years post-menopause largely used which regimen?
- A) Transdermal estradiol + oral micronized progesterone
- B) Vaginal estradiol alone
- C) **Oral conjugated equine estrogen + oral medroxyprogesterone acetate (progestin)** ✓
- D) Injectable estradiol cypionate monotherapy

### Q12. Compared with oral estradiol, transdermal estradiol is preferred in this discussion primarily because:
- A) It raises SHBG more
- B) **It has a better VTE / stroke / cardiovascular event safety signal** ✓
- C) It never needs monitoring
- D) It is FDA-mandated after 60

### Q13. True or False: After total hysterectomy, Sammons always withholds progesterone.
**Answer:** **False.** Progesterone is not strictly required for endometrial protection post-TAH, but she may still use it (~50 mg cited) for sleep, nocturia, anxiety, night sweats — estradiol and progesterone as synergistic “sisters.”

### Q14. Clearfield’s add-on labs for a new HRT workup (beyond Sammons’ first pass) include which of the following sets?
- A) Only CBC and CMP
- B) **Estrone, 25-OH vitamin D, pregnenolone, prolactin, SHBG, and at least one thyroid antibody panel** ✓
- C) Only AM cortisol
- D) Only PSA

## Section C — Thyroid, Metabolic & Practice Pearls (6 questions)

### Q15. Sammons’ vitamin D serum target when trying to lower TPO antibodies:
- A) 30–40 ng/mL
- B) 50–60 ng/mL
- C) **About 80–100 ng/mL** ✓
- D) >150 ng/mL routinely

### Q16. Clearfield’s rough rule: each 1,000 IU vitamin D daily raises serum 25-OH-D by about:
- A) 2 ng/mL
- B) **8 ng/mL** ✓
- C) 20 ng/mL
- D) 50 ng/mL

### Q17. Which agent did Clearfield say can reduce thyroid antibodies by ~50% over a year (plant-sterol “Pac-Man” approach)?
**Answer:** Plant sterols / Moducare (typically BID), as stated in-session.

### Q18. Celtic vs Himalayan salt — which concern is more specific to Celtic (ocean-harvested) salt in Nario’s tip?
- A) Excess iron oxide lung toxicity at culinary doses
- B) **Possible microplastic / ocean pollutant contamination** ✓
- C) Zero magnesium content
- D) Mandatory prescription status

### Q19. Patient tip for estradiol *patch adhesive* dermatitis:
**Answer:** Spray fluticasone nasal spray (Flonase) on the skin ~30 minutes before applying the patch to blunt local adhesive reaction.

### Q20. Short answer: Why is denying HRT labs to a paraplegic woman “because she’s not having penetrative sex” clinically wrong in Sammons’ framing?
**Answer:** Sex hormones support vitality, cognition, mood, tissue integrity, and non-penetrative intimacy — sexual health is whole-person health, not limited to able-bodied penetrative intercourse.
