## Section B — Endocannabinoid System & Mechanisms (6 questions)

### Q7. Which statement best matches Block’s CB1 / CB2 teaching?
- A) CB1 is gut-only; CB2 is brainstem-only
- B) **CB1 predominates in CNS functions; CB2 is largely peripheral and activates with injury/disease; brainstem respiratory centers lack cannabinoid receptors** ✓
- C) Both receptors concentrate in the medulla and stop breathing at high dose
- D) CB2 is the primary memory receptor in hippocampus

### Q8. Name the two principal endogenous ligands Block highlights, and which THC most closely resembles.
**Answer:** **Anandamide** and **2-AG**. THC most closely resembles **anandamide** (partial agonist / “bliss” etymology); 2-AG is described as a fuller agonist tone.

### Q9. How do endogenous endocannabinoids differ from plant cannabinoids in time/space of action?
**Answer:** Endogenous ligands are **site-specific, on-demand, and rapidly degraded**; plant cannabinoids act more **broadly** and **last longer**.

## Section C — Evidence, Public Health & Clinical Guardrails (5 questions)

### Q13. Per the 2017 National Academies report as presented, which indication has **conclusive** evidence, and which two have **substantial** evidence?
**Answer:** **Conclusive:** chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting (THC). **Substantial:** chronic pain; MS-related muscle spasm/spasticity.

### Q14. Surgeon General Jerome Adams’ core caution about modern cannabis is best summarized as:
- A) Cannabis is weaker than in the 1970s
- B) **Potency escalation (flower ~20–30% THC; concentrates ~90–95%) plus normalized use (including pregnancy data concerns) outpaced component-level medical research** ✓
- C) All cannabis should be smoked for bioavailability
- D) Epidiolex proves recreational concentrates are safe

### Q15. What does the Dunedin study finding cited by Block imply for adolescent counseling?
**Answer:** Long-term heavy use starting ~age 15 associated with ~**6 IQ points** lower by age 30; counsel delaying THC until brain maturation (~**mid-20s / ~25**).

### Q16. A naive patient redoses an edible after 30 minutes because “nothing happened.” What pharmacokinetic teaching from this webinar explains the risk?
**Answer:** Oral Δ9-THC undergoes hepatic conversion to **11-hydroxy-THC** (~**4×** potency and duration). Delayed onset + stacking → prolonged intoxication/paranoia; classic ER presentation.

### Q17. True or False: Block presents Rick Simpson Oil as having controlled, cancer-curative human evidence.
**Answer:** **False.** He frames RSO/cancer claims as largely **anecdotal**; distinguishes symptom/spiritual relief and in-vitro signals from proven human cancer cures.

## Section D — Products, Analogs & Practice Integration (3 questions)

### Q18. How did the 2018 Farm Bill + Epidiolex pathway reshape CBD markets — and what unintended analog emerged?
**Answer:** Legal hemp/CBD expansion and Epidiolex’s path out of Schedule I flooded the market and dropped prices; excess CBD can be acid-converted to **delta-8 THC**, often sold via gas-station loopholes with far less safety data than delta-9.

### Q19. Why does Block say physicians in legal states typically “recommend” or “order” cannabis rather than “prescribe” it?
**Answer:** Under federal **Schedule I**, formal **prescribing** would violate the Controlled Substances Act / DEA framework; state programs use alternate language to navigate the conflict.

### Q20. A 22-year-old with anxiety asks for high-THC flower “like what friends use.” Using this lecture, outline a safer counseling approach.
**Answer (key elements):**
1. Brain still developing → strongly discourage THC until ~25 when possible
2. Prefer **CBD-forward / historically balanced** chemistries over mega-THC; THC often **worsens** novice anxiety
3. Avoid concentrates/dabbing; discuss hypotension risk and cardiac red flags
4. If any product used: **start low, go slow** (perceptible doses may be 2.5–5 mg THC); never stack edibles early
5. Stick to indications with better evidence (pain, MS spasm, CINV) and demand known contents/label integrity
6. Frame Schedule I limits on research; document shared decision-making and local legal constraints
