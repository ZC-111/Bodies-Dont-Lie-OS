## Section A — Environment & Pathophysiology (8 questions)

### Q1. According to Dennis, approximately how much air does a person breathe per day, and why does that matter clinically?
**Answer:** Roughly **1,080–3,000 gallons/day**. No oral antifungal or nasal wash can overcome ongoing inhalation of that volume from a contaminated home/HVAC — hence “drying off in the shower.”

### Q5. Why must fiberglass be eliminated from HVAC systems in mold-reactive patients?
**Answer:** Fiberglass acts like “cotton candy for fungus,” grows mold, and sheds particles that seed intense eosinophil reactions in sinus (and sometimes lung) mucosa — including destruction down to bone.

### Q6. What settling-plate colony counts does Dennis target after a 1-hour SDA exposure?

### Q7. True or False: Consumer particle-counter “safe” cutoffs that compare indoor to outdoor air are medically adequate for Dennis’s patients.
**Answer:** **False.** Manufacturer averages lack medical input and run **too high** for hyper-reactive patients.

### Q8. What does a brown halo around sinus tissue on an SDA agar plate represent?

> *Tissue and halo mycotoxin assays match; after hundreds of cases, halo alone is treated as confirmatory visually.*

## Section B — Diagnosis, Hormones & Imaging (6 questions)

### Q9. Why does Dennis distrust IGF-1 as a standalone growth-hormone screen in mold-toxic patients?
**Answer:** In his series, patients with IGF-1 **above the mean of normal** still had ~**53%** chance of failing GH stimulation testing. Observed deficient IGF-1 values ranged **43–285**, including above lab “high normal.” Symptoms + stimulation testing beat the single number.

### Q10. If one mycotoxin is found in urine, what brain burden does Dennis claim is typical?
- A) None — urine clears the brain
- B) Equal one-to-one matching toxin only
- C) **About 10 or more mycotoxins present in the brain** ✓
- D) Only if MRI contrast is positive

### Q11. How does Dennis’s preference for urine mycotoxins vs Campbell-style blood IgG/IgE differ?
**Answer:** Urine reflects **recent excretion (~1–3 days)** — “is it happening now?” Blood IgG/IgE show sensitization without a clear temporal stamp. If forced to pick one, he chooses urine; clothing tap plates further confirm current environmental load.

### Q12. What P300 finding helps explain why mold patients retain verbal instructions poorly?
- A) Instantaneous ear-to-cortex latency
- B) **Delayed (~416 ms) or non-crossing waveforms vs ~300 ms normal** ✓
- C) Exclusive left-ear deafness
- D) Elevated brainstem volume only

> *Clinic pearl: make patients write instructions down.*

### Q13. What AI imaging claim is made for gliotoxin / mycotoxins?
**Answer:** Defense-derived algorithms trained on CT/MRI of mycotoxin vials can highlight toxin in patient scans; sites have been **biopsy/tissue-assay confirmed**. Contrast not required. Software commercialization currently limits access.

### Q14. In the Cushing pituitary adenoma case, how many mycotoxins were found in tumor tissue?
- A) 1

### Q15. What is the amphotericin nasal nebulization mix Dennis specifies?

- C) 50 mg / 30 mL sterile water
- D) 3 mg powder insufflated dry

### Q16. What voriconazole nasal mix and rationale does he give?
**Answer:** **12.5 mg / 30 mL distilled water.** Voriconazole preferred over itraconazole when brain penetration matters (higher cost).

### Q17. What is the correct sequence for daily nasal care?
- A) Nebulize first, then irrigate
- B) **Irrigate/clean first, then nebulize BID** ✓
- C) Oral antifungal only — skip topical
- D) Steroid spray alone for 2 weeks

### Q18. How should oral amphotericin be used vs IV, per Dennis?
**Answer:** Oral capsules (**~250 mg BID**) stay gut-localized for yeast and avoid systemic nephrotoxicity. IV/systemic amphotericin can destroy kidneys — do **not** try to make oral amphotericin absorbable (e.g., liposomes) for systemic effect.

## Section D — Clinical Integration (2 questions)

### Q19. A patient improves after sinus surgery and antifungals, then relapses within 30 minutes of sitting in a rental car. What is the teaching point?
**Answer:** Hyper-reactors cannot re-enter fungal environments — even brief exposures (cars, washers, belongings) can recreate near-baseline disability. Long-term success = **zero-mold housing + HVAC**, not meds alone.

### Q20. A colleague asks whether to lead with BEG spray for MARCoNS, Dupixent for eosinophilic asthma, or HBOT for brain fog in a mold patient still living in a wet basement. Integrate Dennis’s hierarchy.
**Answer:**
1. **Evacuate / remediate environment first** (wet basement = guaranteed failure)
2. Clean nose: irrigate → amphotericin/voriconazole nebulize; Candida diet; nystatin + probiotic with any antibiotics
3. Address sinus “manufacturing plant” (± surgery in ~50%) before expecting durable neurologic gains
4. BEG spray only if purulent secondary infection — won’t hold if fungus/cilia damage remain
5. Dupixent may block eosinophil symptoms but leaves mycotoxin load
6. HBOT 1.4–2.5 ATA / oxygen concentrator only **after** safe housing — otherwise relapse wastes money
7. Full pituitary / GH workup once load is falling
