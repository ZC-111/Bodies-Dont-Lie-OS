## Section A — PT-141 / Bremelanotide (5 questions)

### Q1. According to Nario, PT-141’s primary receptor target for sexual motivation is:
- A) Peripheral PDE5 in corpora cavernosa
- B) **Melanocortin-4 (MC4) in the hypothalamus** ✓
- C) Peripheral α1-adrenergic receptors
- D) GnRH receptors in the pituitary

### Q2. Why was combining PT-141 with PDE5 inhibitors historically discouraged?
**Answer:** Risk of **priapism** (erection lasting >4 hours). Newer trials explore combination as salvage for PDE5 non-responders (central + peripheral).

### Q3. FDA-labeled Vyleesi (bremelanotide) is approved for which population?
- A) Postmenopausal women with HSDD
- B) **Premenopausal women with hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD)** ✓
- C) Men with vascular ED only
- D) All adults with low testosterone

### Q4. True or False: Nario’s key male ED pearl is that some “erectile dysfunction” is motivational/reward-circuit failure, not vascular failure.
**Answer:** **True.** Men who can perform but lack interest may fail PDE5 monotherapy because the problem is central dopamine/motivation, not NO/vascular tone.

### Q5. Which larger biology does Nario link to the melanocortin system besides libido?
- A) Purely renal sodium handling
- B) **Appetite, energy balance, reward — with exploration alongside GLP-1 pathways** ✓
- C) Only skin pigmentation

### Q7. Which receptor subtype did Serna associate most strongly with uterine/breast proliferation and clotting risk when overstimulated?
- A) ERβ

- C) GPR30 only
- D) Androgen receptor

### Q8. Serna’s preferred local therapy cue for significant vaginal atrophy is:
**Answer:** Compounded **estriol (E3)** vaginal use roughly **2–3 times/week**, titrated to symptoms, rather than defaulting to branded products.

### Q9. Estetrol (E4) teaching points from the talk include:

### Q10. Why did Serna argue conjugated equine estrogens are harder on proliferative risk than bioidenticals (her framing)?

## Section D — Tano: Xenoestrogens, Metabolites & Practice (5 questions)

### Q16. Tano’s geographic epidemiologic hook was that Midwestern maps of what overlapped obesity, diabetes, heart disease, and cancer?
- A) Average gym membership density
- B) **Pesticide / herbicide spray intensity** ✓

### Q17. In the classic metabolite frame Tano used, which pathway is considered more protective vs more proliferative?
**Answer:** **2-hydroxyestrone (2-OH-E1)** = protective / easier clearance; **16α-hydroxyestrone (16α-OH-E1)** = proliferative / cancer-associated in the literature he cites.

### Q18. Why does Tano warn clinicians who prescribe DIM or I3C?
- A) They always raise estradiol 10-fold
- B) **They can paradoxically lower protective 2-OH-E1; always recheck the 2:16 ratio** ✓
- C) They are FDA-approved chemotherapy
- D) They permanently destroy ERβ

### Q19. Tano’s preferred first-line approach to hot flashes (when estrogen is not “absolutely necessary”) is:
- A) High-dose oral Premarin
- B) **Progesterone (progestin in his wording) — often sufficient** ✓
- C) Unopposed 2 mg ethinyl estradiol
- D) Aromatase inhibitors only

### Q20. Short answer: What minimal estrogen rescue did Tano describe for thin women with refractory hot flashes?
**Answer:** Roughly **1 mg BiEst cream** (~**20% estradiol / 80% estriol**), not high-dose systemic E2 — alongside detox, avoidance, and non-estrogen hormones (progesterone, DHEA) as needed.
