## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Bioregulators vs Peptides (15:25–22:50)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario, DO (Speaker 1)

### ★ Core Thesis
> Bioregulators are **short peptides (typically 2–4 amino acids)** that restore **tissue-specific gene expression** — epigenetic “Post-it notes,” not hormone pushers or receptor override drugs.

### Framing
- Peptide resurgence after FDA compounding shifts → patients already asking about **bioregulators** (often from Reddit / gyms)
- Clinicians need office-based conversations, not locker-room dosing advice
- Aging framed as loss of **signaling fidelity**: epigenetic drift, quieted repair genes, ↑ inflammatory genes, loss of tissue specificity

### Mechanism (as presented)
- Normalize gene expression → restore protein synthesis
- Improve mitochondrial and immune signaling
- Reduce **inflammaging**
- Low microgram dosing → favorable safety narrative vs milligram hormone/peptide stacks
- **Not FDA-approved** (used internationally); not “cosmetic anti-aging” — organ function restoration framing

### Named Examples
| Bioregulator | Tissue / focus |
|---|---|
| **Epitalon / Epithalon** | Pineal; telomerase; melatonin / circadian |
| **Thymalin** | Thymus / immune; T-cell differentiation |
| **Pinealon** | Pineal / cognitive support |
| **Vasogen** | Vascular endothelium |
| Cartilage-focused agents | Joint / cartilage repair |
| Liver-focused agents | Hepatic support |
| Thyroid bioregulator (mentioned) | May normalize hyper/hypothyroid patterns without forcing extremes |

### Evidence Highlights Cited
- **Khavinson et al., 2003:** fibroblasts + epithalamin → telomerase activation, telomere lengthening, cells exceed Hayflick limit **without** malignant transformation
- **Khavinson, Mechanisms of Ageing and Development, 2002:** thymalin → restored thymic function markers, improved CD4/CD8 ratios, better outcomes in hospitalized elderly → framed as **immunosenescence correction**, not immunostimulation

### Clinical Pearl
> Bioregulators ≠ steroids ≠ classic forcing peptides. Oral bioavailability is often emphasized because molecules are smaller than typical peptides. Commercial oral lines discussed (e.g., Integrative Peptides / Holtorf–related channels) — injectables less common in that channel.

## Segment 3 — Bill Clearfield: Vitamin C Effects on Hormones (25:38–1:05:39)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield, DO  
**Context:** Material from a Vitamin C conference (VCICI); CME-framed; ~97 references / ~47 slides; filler while Piper late

### ★ Core Thesis
> Adequate vitamin C supports **hormone homeostasis** across sex steroids, thyroid, stress axis, growth factors, and insulin — form and dose matter (ascorbate preferred; conference “adequate” oral target often **4 g/day**, plus **+1 g per chronic stressor**).

### 3A. Forms, Basics & Delivery
- Essential water-soluble vitamin; humans lack gulonolactone oxidase → dietary/supplement only
- **Ascorbic acid** (pH ~2–3): cheaper; more GI irritation >2 g; not preferred for IV (acidity)
- **Ascorbate** (buffered, pH ~6–9): better GI tolerance; standard for IV; adds **sodium load** → caution in HF / renal disease / Na-sensitive patients
- Both enter blood as ascorbate
- RDA: ~75 mg ♀ / ~90 mg ♂ — conference / clinical doses far higher
- Oral: tablets, powders, liposomal (Levy-style “shot”); saturable intestinal transport; excess urinary
- IV: **20–50 g/day** for cancer / chronic illness protocols (up to 5 days/week in conference framing)
- High oral/IV → ↑ urinary oxalate → stone risk
- Concentrated in adrenals, pituitary, thyroid, gonads

### 3B. Endocrine Axes — Clinical Doses Cited

| Axis | Key claims from talk | Dose / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Testosterone** | ↑ LH/FSH at pituitary; supports Leydig (LH) + Sertoli/spermatogenesis (FSH); preserves germ-cell DNA | **2 g/day** can ↑ T in infertile/older men (>41); may lower LH as T rises |
| **Estrogen** | Can ↑ estradiol; with OCP or BHRT may raise serum E2; can worsen **estrogen dominance** (↑ E2:P) | Lower-dose effects emphasized |
| **Progesterone** | Supports luteal phase; ↓ oxidative stress; GABA via allopregnanolone pathway | **750 mg ascorbate × 3 months** → **77% ↑** progesterone (luteal-defect context) |
| **Growth hormone / IGF-1** | ↑ GH release (DA/NE ratio); ↓ somatostatin; improves IGF-1 conversion | **250–1000 mg/day**; autism vignette: high GH + low IGF-1, often ↑ IL-6 |
| **Thyroid** | Aids T4→T3; ↑ levothyroxine absorption amid microbiome disruption; ↓ TPO Abs | **500 mg/day × 3 months** ↓ TPO in cited studies; hyperthyroid pts often low C |
| **Insulin** | Improves insulin sensitivity; ↓ FBG, fasting insulin, A1c | **>200 mg** for sensitivity signal; **≥1000 mg × 12 weeks** for stronger glycemic effect |
| **Cortisol / DHEA** | High dose normalizes elevated cortisol & DHEA-S; stress resilience | Target cortisol:DHEA ~**5–6:1** (abnormal ~10:1) |
| **Pregnenolone** | Cofactor for mitochondrial cholesterol → pregnenolone (“mother hormone”) | Low C impairs steroidogenesis cascade |
| **Prolactin** | Supports hypothalamic dopamine → dampens excess PRL | Deficiency linked to dysregulated PRL / fertility |
| **Melatonin** | Synergistic antioxidant / photo-protection with C | Melatonin framed as stronger ROS scavenger in skin vs C alone |

### 3C. Progesterone After Hysterectomy (Teaching Point)
Clearfield argues against “no uterus = no progesterone”:
- Diuresis, smooth-muscle relaxation, Ca/P excretion, ↑ BBT, sedation/analgesia, memory, osteoblast support
- Immunomodulation (IL-1β, TNF-α, NF-κB)
- TBI protocol cited: IV progesterone **8 mg/kg continuous × 24h** within 24h → ~**50%** mortality reduction at 1 month (as presented)
- Neuroprotection / dementia / glaucoma associations listed

### 3D. GABA Diagnostic Hint & 5α-Reductase Caution
- Clinical hint: prolactin **>6** (units not specified) associated with adequate GABA tone; **<6** → agitation / irritability → OTC GABA often preferred over benzos
- Pathway: progesterone → 5α-DHP → allopregnanolone → GABA
- **Finasteride / dutasteride** block same 5α-reductase → can impair GABA pathway; psychiatric AEs; ~120 suicides linked especially to **1 mg** in young men (18–45); avoid if personal/family bipolar, severe depression, severe sleep disorders

### 3E. Bone, CV, Labs & Lifestyle Depletion
- Bone: collagen hydroxylase cofactor; higher intake ↔ better BMD; osteoporosis risk ↓ up to **~33%** (as cited)
- BP: **>500 mg/day** modestly lowers BP in hypertensives; modulates homocysteine; ↑ NO bioavailability
- Adequacy labs: plasma ascorbate **>50 µmol/L**; leukocyte ascorbate **>15 µg** (tissue stores)
- Depleters: smoking (−25–50%), obesity (need ~2×), pollution/UV, low produce intake, inflammation, stress/surgery, NSAIDs/statins/steroids/antibiotics/chemo, alcohol, aging
- HOMA-IR quick calc cited: (fasting insulin × fasting glucose) / 405; goal **<1.9**, normal **<2.9**

### 3F. Practical Dosing Q&A
- **4 g/day** oral: split **2 g AM + 2 g PM** with meals
- Melatonin sleep stack: XR/IR **~1.5 mg** + vitamin D 2–5k IU + magnesium (glycinate if threonate unavailable), **2h before bed** — melatonin resets circadian rhythm, is not a hypnotic “sleeping pill”
- Cancer-context melatonin doses 10–40 mg mentioned by Nario; go low/slow (0.5–1 mg) in AFIRM style; grogginess = too high

## Segment 4 — Piper Dobner: Microbiome & FMT Q&A (1:06:11–2:13:42)

**Speaker:** Piper Dobner, ND — microbiome therapies; Senior Manager, Science & Education, SFI Health (formerly Klaire Labs); clinic **Biome Basics** (`hello@biome-basics.com`)  
**Prior talk:** FMT lecture with group ~prior year / Reno conference

### ★ Core Thesis
> Treat the microbiome as an **ecosystem** (diversity, biofilms, metabolites, horizontal gene transfer) — not a single missing “hero bug.” FMT is closer to **ecosystem transplant / genetic backup** than simple organism replacement. Chronic illness often needs **restoration**, not endless antimicrobial “clear then plant.”

### 4A. Beyond Single-Bug Thinking
- Analogy: forest ≠ only echinacea plant
- Shannon / beta diversity > chasing Akkermansia alone
- Transient pathogens on stool tests (e.g., some *E. coli*) may be observed, not always attacked
- Broad “targeted” antimicrobials still wipe whole community sections
- Biofilms: microbes rarely act as free-floating singles; synergy can amplify or cancel function

### 4B. Restoration vs Antimicrobial Suppression
- C. diff literature → antibiotic first, then FMT; autism (Adams/Kong, ASU) and planned Parkinson’s protocols also pretreat with antibiotics (“clear forest before planting”)
- Dobner skeptical that clear step is always required
- **June 2025-era study (as discussed):** FMT enema as **primary** C. diff therapy **non-inferior** to vancomycin (~70% each); **fewer relapses** in FMT arm — despite enema being among *least* effective delivery routes
- Gold-standard delivery: **colonoscopic**; capsules ≈ colonoscopy efficacy historically (**~96.2%** per 2017 Jambo comparison cited)
- Chronic IBS/IBD/SIBO/long COVID/autoimmune: prefer rebuild (diet, polyphenols, fermented foods) over reflexive Rifaximin cycles
- Acute life-threatening infection → antibiotics still appropriate
- History pearl: lifelong antimicrobial exposure almost always high in complex chronic patients
- Urobiome conference (UCSD): UTI antibiotics ↑ recurrence risk → antibiotic cycles
- Antipsychotics and other meds can act like antimicrobials on diversity
- Metabolically unhealthy (↑ waist, weight, HOMA-IR) → **lower** diversity than metabolically healthy

### 4C. Diversification Tools
- Sauerkraut / kimchi diversify more than fiber-first in depleted guts (fiber needs microbes that digest it)
- Farmer–doctor conference: soil ↔ gut analogy; permaculture restoration is **years**, not days
- Farmers use **sauerkraut juice** on soil → binds glyphosate (as reported)
- Antifungals (e.g., itraconazole) and antivirals also damage microbiome — not antibiotics alone
- Hookworm self-infection n-of-1: Tiny Health diversity ↑; immune “signal overload”; pinworm med → diversity crash (bacterial species also dropped)

### 4D. What FMT Actually “Replaces”
- Prefer **transplant of ecosystem** over “replacement”
- Donor-like stool signatures appear, but host community not simply wiped/swapped
- Horizontal gene transfer may introduce metabolite capacity without permanent colonization by named species
- Future: metabolomics > bug name lists

### 4E. Misconceptions, Mouse Data & Adverse Events
- Biggest misconception: FMT is a **panacea** / overnight miracle (C. diff can look miraculous; chronic disease often does not)
- Germ-free mice + wild-type FMT: at **~6 weeks**, mucus physiology normalizes (SI resumes healthy sloughing; colon recovers thicker protective mucus) — long after acute stool changes
- Dysbiosis pattern: SI mucus thickens (protect brush border, impairs absorption); colon mucus thins
- Theoretical **anaphylaxis** on consents; clinical pattern more often transient immune hyper-reactivity / lip swelling → antihistamines; true anaphylaxis rare in her practice
- Capsule risk: **choking** / inability to swallow; triple encapsulation aims for colon delivery
- Bloating common ≠ SIBO; use **bitter tinctures** 15 min before meals or q15min while bloated (2–4 weeks)
- Carnivore alters microbiome (yes); sourdough favored if wheat-tolerated (lactic fermentation; her starter originated from first donor, now ~23 years old)

### 4F. Dosing Strategies
| Indication | Approach (as practiced) |
|---|---|
| **C. diff (standard)** | Fast day prior; **30 capsules** empty stomach day 1 |
| **Virulent / failed pharma FMT / ≥3–4 recurrences** | Two-bottle plan: **30** then **10/10/10** over days 2–4 |
| **Autism / IBD literature style** | Bolus then daily or weekly for **4–8+ weeks** |
| Chronic complex illness | Individualize; consider retest at ~1 year |

- Pharma products **Rebyota** / Vowst changed distribution landscape (post–late 2022); some patients fail pharma products then seek compounded capsules
- Colonoscopic FMT: Dr. Sabine Hazan (Bay Area) cited for research / familial autism mapping

### 4G. Probiotics & Testing
- Adult colonization often **transient**; infants more durable (e.g., Bifidobacterium)
- Horizontal gene transfer model for why patients improve without species showing on retest
- **Rotate probiotics** — lock-and-key metabolite fit varies by person
- Stay with **same lab** for pre/post (Tiny Health, Nirvana Biome / Cosmos ID); diversity scores preferred; Vibrant for donor qPCR / pathogen panels (FDA screening)

### 4H. Donor Screening & Prep
- Preferred donor age tradition (Chinese lit): 8–18; she won’t take **<8**; parental oversight required
- Current donor: **one lifetime antibiotic**, never vaccinated; minimal medical interventions
- Garage lab: hood, autoclave, centrifuge, single-use plastics, mason-jar blender blades; freezer stock
- Sabbatical January → rescreen donor February; inventory backlog risk with single donor

### Sleep Side Note (Dobner)
- Prefers **phosphatidylserine** + sleep hygiene (Matthew Walker) over exogenous melatonin for circadian reset; melatonin is in SFI line but she emphasizes endogenous production
