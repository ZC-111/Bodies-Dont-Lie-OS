## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Vitamin K as Metabolic & Longevity Lever (15:04–27:18)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario, DO

### ★ Core Reframe
> Vitamin K is usually cast as Vitamin D’s sidekick (“take with D for bones / keep calcium out of arteries”). New evidence positions it as a **metabolic modulator, frailty signal, and longevity lever**.

### Forms
| Form | Chemistry | Sources | Primary role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **K1** | Phylloquinone | Leafy greens | Hepatic clotting factors; limited bone/vascular bioavailability |
| **K2 MK-4** | Short-chain menaquinone | Liver, egg yolks, meat | Extrahepatic; short half-life → higher/more frequent dosing |
| **K2 MK-7** | Long-chain menaquinone | Natto / fermented foods | Longer half-life (days), better tissue retention; Nario’s preferred standout |

### Classic Mechanisms (still true)
- Activates **osteocalcin** → bone mineralization
- Activates **matrix GLA protein (MGP)** → prevents arterial calcium deposition

### New Evidence — Glucose / Insulin Sensitivity
- **2026 meta-analysis (Ahmed et al.)**: K1 and/or K2 supplementation → ↓ fasting glucose, ↓ HbA1c, improved HOMA-IR
- Stronger effects often at **lower doses** (study range ~**90–200 µg**); sometimes &lt;12 weeks
- No consistent change in fasting insulin / β-cell function → suggests **peripheral insulin sensitivity**, not pancreatic stimulation
- Raises question: is osteocalcin also an underutilized **metabolic hormone**?

### Frailty & Functional Aging
- **Azuma et al. 2022** (~800 older adults, mid-70s): highest ratio of **undercarboxylated / total osteocalcin** (K insufficiency marker) → ~**2.5× odds of frailty**; trends toward slower gait / lower activity
- Extends K story beyond bones/arteries → muscle, mobility, resilience

### Warfarin Myth Debunk
- K **opposes warfarin** → big intake swings move INR; key is **consistency, not elimination**
- Far less relevant for DOACs (e.g., apixaban/Eliquis) that do not act via vitamin K pathways

### Magnesium Co-Requirement
- Magnesium = activator for D + K bone/vascular pathway
- Elemental Mg ~**300–500 mg**; literature lean toward **glycinate / citrate** (less focus on taurate/threonate for this pathway)
- Take with D/K

### Panel Sidebar — Vascular Fragility & Glycocalyx
- Family anecdote: maternal brain aneurysm; 23andMe → Pure Encapsulations pathway tool suggested impaired K2 synthesis; siblings with easy bruising improved after ↑ K2
- **Joanie**: glycocalyx / microvascular health research — unstable glycocalyx → ↑ stroke, dissection, miscarriage risk (lipid-envelope viruses / annexin exposure)

## Segment 3 — Sam Fillingane: Multifunctional Cardiogram (MCG/MCGID) (29:13–1:26:46)

**Speaker:** Sam Fillingane, DO — Medical Director, CardioVision (sideline); Associate Clinical Professor, William Carey University COM; National Cardiovascular Laboratories (medical director / medical education)

### ★ Core Thesis
> Cardiovascular disease is preventable and reversible if inflammation is controlled. The MCG is an AI-enhanced 2-lead EKG that surfaces early plaque, soft plaque, small-vessel disease, mitochondrial dysfunction, and rhythm risk — replacing much of what CAC alone cannot see.

### 3A. Device Basics
- **Multifunctional Cardiogram (MCG / MCGID)** — four clips (wrists + ankles); leads II + V5 (two planes)
- Patient supine ~**7–8 minutes**; five × **82-second** acquisitions (extra segment if quality poor); door-to-done ~**10 minutes**
- ~**3 million** people tested; ~**25 years** R&D; CardioVision database (~100K) used to map frequency-domain “barcodes”
- Uses **frequency domain** (removes time domain) — analogy: radio dial → each frequency = different cardiac information
- Software footprint claimed larger than Google’s production stack (speaker claim)
- Sees **26** cardiac domains; safe for pregnancy, infants, frail, healthy; repeatable monthly–yearly
- Development delayed by COVID; market entry ~**2022** (ready ~2000)

### 3B. What It Sees Beyond CAC / Stress Testing
| Domain | Clinical value |
|---|---|
| Early coronary plaque | Detects ~**10%** plaque (vs stress/nuclear often needing 70–80% stenosis) |
| Soft + calcified plaque | CAC misses soft plaque |
| Small-vessel disease | Rarely tested in US (CPET scarce); scorable over time |
| Heart stiffening | Cardiomyopathy pathway vs ventricular hypertrophy pathway |
| Mitochondrial dysfunction | Score 1–10; office detection in minutes |
| Rhythm risk | VF / AF potential months ahead (0–5 scale) |
| Congenital defects | High capture rate in screened cohorts |
| Pre-op risk | Ischemia, mito, stiffening, rhythm before anesthesia |

### 3C. Cardiovascular Score
- Scale **0–22**; **&gt;4** = significant; average ACS / MI around **~4.6** (large-vessel)
- Small-vessel MI risk band cited ~**6–8**
- Yearly physical use-case: track 1.8 → 2.5 (worsening) vs 6.8 → 4 → 2.2 (improvement)
- Florida ER study (n=455): score **&lt;4** → **0** acute coronary events in that cohort; 11,000-patient follow-on pending
- Goal: cut ER cath decision from ~**90 min** to ~**10 min**

### 3D. Small-Vessel Disease Emphasis
- Circulation 2007: ~**49% women / 15% men** with small-vessel disease; newer estimates **60–70%** of women before large-vessel disease
- Drives MI risk + heart-failure / cardiomyopathy pathway
- Clinical phenotype: extreme exhaustion (grocery-aisle fatigue), DOE; often told “not your heart” after clean cath → convert to large-vessel ACS in ~**6 months**
- MCG both detects and **scores** response to therapy

### 3E. Mitochondrial Dysfunction & Rhythm
- Mito dysfunction linked to CVD, autoimmunity, recurrent cancers
- Rhythm score: 1–2/5 mild risk; **4–5/5** high risk (VF sudden death; AF → stroke, EF drop 10–15%)
- Japanese Rhythm Society AF data (speaker): **5/5** → **93%** developed AF within 3 months; post-ablation recurrence predicted when risk returned to 5/5 (~**90%** within 8 months)

### 3F. Accuracy & Safety Claims
- AHA Science Forum Innovation of the Year **2017** (pre-market)
- Sensitivity/specificity for ischemia historically better than SPECT/nuclear; aligns with angiography / CTA (speaker)
- Sensitivity climbed from ~84–86% (~2010) to **~93–94%** with more AI training data
- No radiation, no contrast; LBBB does **not** negate reading (unlike conventional EKG interpretation limits)
- Pacemakers mess up readings; avoid high electrical interference rooms; prefer no caffeine 4h (still test if present)
- Quality flags: Good / Marginal / Poor — Poor → repeat

### 3G. Practice Economics & Access
- Device ~**$60,000**; typical charge **$400–500**/test; insurance generally not yet covering (needs dedicated code; ~$3M coding pathway cited)
- ~**250** units US + ~250 Japan; 16 countries / 5 continents
- Access via purchase **or** Amy Spahi / CardioVision screening-day teams (10–20 patients/day)
- Contact (speaker): sam.fillingane@gmail.com → intro to Amy Spahi (Jacksonville)

## Segment 4 — Case Studies: Reading the 26-Parameter Report (56:00–1:26:00)

**Speaker:** Sam Fillingane

### Report Logic (always start here)
1. **Myocardial damage** → metabolic disease driving CV inflammation? (5×82-sec rows)
2. **Myocarditis** (acute) vs **myocardial remodeling** (chronic)
3. **↓ vs ↑ myocardial compliance** → plaque &lt;30% vs &gt;30% occlusive
4. **Rheumatic** (left-sided) vs **pulmonary** (right-sided) + **global asynchrony** (caret at V5 = left; Lead II = right)
5. **Congenital heart disease** flag → echo + bubble (or equivalent ASD/VSD protocol)
6. **Heart stiffening**: cardiomyopathy (septal involvement / rhythm risk) vs **ventricular hypertrophy** (diabetes / HTN pathway)
7. **Mitochondrial** markers (↓ EF, acute power failure) → 0–10 style composite
8. **Rhythm potential**: AF / VF scores

### Case 1 — 73yo Woman (High Risk)
- Ischemia scores average ~**7** (high myocardial dysfunction category when &gt;7)
- Metabolic disease 5/5; acute myocarditis 0/5; remodeling 5/5 → chronic inflammatory load
- ↑ compliance 5/5 → plaque **&gt;30%**; left-sided disease + global asynchrony at V5
- Congenital 5/5 + cardiomyopathy 5/5; VF **5/5** → cath / possible ICD urgency

### Case 2 — 60yo Physician (Mild Score, Serious Underbelly)
- Year 1 average ~**1.7–1.8** (Category B mild) but: metabolic 5/5; acute + chronic positives; left-sided disease; mito **~9/10**; AF 3/5
- Year 2 (meeting retest, minimal formal follow-up): average **~0.3**; plaque signals ↓; cardiomyopathy 3→0; VH 4→2; mito 9→2; AF 3→2 → **plaque reverse trajectory** documented on serial MCG

### Case 3 — 13yo Soccer Syncope (Hypertrophic / Arrhythmia Cardiomyopathy Catch)
- Syncope ~1 min on field; PCP then adult cardiologist cleared after exam/EKG
- Father requested MCG before return-to-play: non-zero scores (abnormal for age-13 zeros expected)
- Metabolic 5/5; congenital 3/5 + cardiomyopathy 4/5; AF 2/5; VF 1/5
- Pediatric congenital specialist → echo/EKG/MRI → **arrhythmia cardiomyopathy**; ICD at age 14 after rhythm worsened
- Teaching point: MCG prevented catastrophic return-to-play clearance

## Segment 5 — Eleven Inflammatory Disease States & Reversal Playbook (1:04:00–1:26:00 + closing Q&A)

**Speaker:** Sam Fillingane

### ★ Operating Rule
> **The doctor (and patient) who controls inflammation wins.** Inflammation drives plaque, glycocalyx injury, and the ~**66%** of MIs from plaque rupture (not 95%→100% occlusive growth).

### The 11-State Checklist (grew from 8 in 1998 → 11)
1. Diabetes / prediabetes  
2. Dyslipidemia (small-dense / oxidized LDL — not “cholesterol number” alone)  
3. Hypertension  
4. Obesity  
5. Sleep disturbance / sleep disorders  
6. Chronic kidney disease  
7. Mental health disorders  
8. Genetic disorders  
9. Autoimmune / inflammatory / toxin / chronic infection cluster  
10. Gut biogenome issues  
11. Mitochondrial dysfunction  

Most advanced patients carry **5–6** of 11. Treat each from an **inflammatory** root, not the obvious surface marker.

### Diabetes Inflammation Source (worked example)
- Glucose is substrate for ATP — **not** the primary toxin
- Type 2 / prediabetes: lowered hepatic carb tolerance → &gt;**25 g carbs** flips switch → **3–4 days** of de novo lipogenesis (TG-rich VLDL → small-dense LDL) + JNK1 inflammatory activation
- Non–insulin-resistant person: switch ~**90 g carbs / 4h**; anyone &gt;90 g flips it
- Other flippers: alcohol (≈1 oz hard liquor ♀ / 2 oz ♂), **high-fructose corn syrup** (no glycogen storage pathway)

### Sleep → Adrenaline Inflammation
- Stage 3–4 sleep manufactures serotonin / dopamine / NE stores
- Poor deep sleep → depleted neurochemicals → adrenal epinephrine compensation → hyperinflammatory CV cascade
- CPAP alone fixes OSA only ~**50%** if substrate (e.g., MTHFR / methylfolate → NO + brain chemicals) missing — “oven + ingredients” baking analogy

### Exercise Equalizer
- **50 min aerobic / day**, target HR ~**70%** (~115 bpm older / ~120 younger)
- Removes ~**93%** of circulating inflammatory factors that day; returns if skipped next day → aim **6–7 days/week**
- Preference for **elliptical** progression; casual walking/golf/tennis often insufficient for CV reversal intensity

### Case Volume Claim
- ~**8,000–10,000** patients with ~95% occlusions reversed without stents if stabilized ~6 months (speaker clinical series)

## Segment 6 — Q&A: Access, MCAS/Long COVID, MTHFR, Top Anti-Inflammatory Levers (1:26:46–2:14:54)

**Speakers:** Sam Fillingane, Bill Clearfield, Melvin Nario, panel

### Device / Systems
- Four largest Florida hospitals using MCG in ER pathways (Jacksonville origin via prison hospital → Orlando / Miami)
- Cardiologist “naysayer” strategy: send validation document with report — hard to ignore left-sided advanced disease flags
- Interpretation still clinician-taught (Fillingane trains purchasers); AI does not practice medicine yet

### Myocarditis / Vaccine / Long COVID → MCAS
- Acute inflammation flags under myocarditis channel; serial scores track clearing
- Long COVID often tied to **mast cell activation** (~**17%** population MCAS prevalence cited; COVID as trigger)
- Starter stack: **H1** (Allegra/Zyrtec/Claritin) + **H2** (Pepcid BID) + **quercetin** ~1000 mg BID–TID (up to ~3000 mg/day discussed); typical course **3–6 months** (longer if ongoing major stressors)
- Peptides: speaker defers to integrative colleagues (no personal experience)

### Nitric Oxide / MTHFR Pearls
- Methylfolate for MTHFR → NO surge in ~**2 weeks** can convert “95% angina” phenotype toward functional 50% via vasodilation
- Sleep/brain chemicals need **adequate** methylfolate: **15 mg** for C677T (7.5 mg ineffective in cited psych studies); A1298C may need **15 mg TID × 3 months** then taper (safety data ≈ placebo in speaker’s review)
- Integrative docs often underdose methylfolate (NO only, not brain chemicals)

### Top Anti-Inflammatory Priorities (requested “top 5” → expanded)
1. **Carb control** — ≤25 g / 4–5h + intermittent fasting for diabetics/prediabetics  
2. **Sleep** — OSA recognition + substrate (methylfolate) + CPAP when indicated  
3. **CKD caution** — diuretic-driven injury; prefer early **aldosterone blockade** (spironolactone) so Lasix can be minimized  
4. **Autoimmunity** — improve mitochondrial function to allow DMARDs to work  
5. **Gut biogenome** — patterns + probiotic/prebiotic pairing (prebiotics alone = common error)  
6. Genetics: ~**5%** Factor V Leiden / prothrombin; Type **1.5** diabetes ~**10%** of diabetics — early insulin ± GLP-1 can blunt autoimmunity

### Other Mentions
- Vasceze / nitrate-nitrite pathways for NO (speaker)
- Colchicine: speaker never needed it in large reversal series (studies mixed)
- Amyloxanax / peptide arterial clearing claimed by audience — speaker interested, inflammation tap still primary
