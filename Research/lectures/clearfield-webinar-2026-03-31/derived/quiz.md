## Section A — Vitamin K & Metabolic Health (6 questions)

### Q1. According to Nario’s tip, which vitamin K form has the longer half-life and is his preferred standout for bone/vascular tissue retention?
- A) K1 phylloquinone
- B) K2 MK-4
- C) **K2 MK-7** ✓
- D) Synthetic menadione (K3)

> *MK-7: days vs hours; fermented foods / natto; better absorbed and retained.*

### Q2. The 2026 Ahmed meta-analysis associated vitamin K supplementation with which metabolic pattern?
- A) ↑ fasting insulin and β-cell output
- B) **↓ fasting glucose, ↓ HbA1c, improved HOMA-IR (peripheral sensitivity)** ✓
- C) No glucose changes; only INR effects
- D) Worsened insulin resistance at doses &gt;1000 µg

### Q3. What laboratory ratio did Azuma et al. (2022) use as a vitamin K insufficiency marker linked to ~2.5× frailty odds?
**Answer:** Undercarboxylated osteocalcin / total osteocalcin ratio (higher ratio = more K insufficiency).

### Q4. True or False: Patients on warfarin should eliminate all dietary vitamin K.
**Answer:** **False.** Large swings change INR; keep intake **consistent**. Interaction is far less relevant for DOACs (e.g., apixaban).

### Q5. What elemental magnesium range did Nario cite as the activator co-factor for D + K pathways, and which forms had more literature support in his tip?
**Answer:** ~**300–500 mg elemental** magnesium; **glycinate and citrate** emphasized over taurate/threonate for this pathway discussion.

### Q6. Name the two vitamin K–activated proteins classically tied to bone mineralization and arterial calcium diversion.
**Answer:** **Osteocalcin** (bone) and **matrix GLA protein / MGP** (prevents vascular calcium deposition).

## Section B — MCG Device Fundamentals (6 questions)

### Q7. How long does a typical MCG acquisition take from hookup to finish, and what lead configuration is used?
**Answer:** About **10 minutes** total (five × ~82-second segments). **Two-lead** setup: Lead **II** and **V5** via four clips (wrists + ankles).

### Q8. What cardiovascular score threshold did Fillingane emphasize as “significant,” and what average ACS score was cited from the Florida hospital cohort?
- A) &gt;2 significant; average ACS 1.8
- B) **&gt;4 significant; average ACS ~4.6** ✓
- C) &gt;10 significant; average ACS 15
- D) Score only useful if CAC &gt;400

### Q9. Why does Fillingane argue MCG can replace calcium scoring for many use-cases?
**Answer:** CAC sees **calcified** plaque only. MCG also detects **soft/non-calcified** plaque, early (~10%) plaque, small-vessel disease, mitochondrial dysfunction, stiffening, and rhythm risk — domains CAC does not cover.

### Q10. In the n=455 Florida ER experience, what happened when the MCG cardiovascular score was under 4?
- A) Half still ruled in for ACS
- B) **Zero patients had a heart attack / ACS event in that under-4 group** ✓
- C) Scores under 4 were discarded as false negatives
- D) All patients still went to cath within 90 minutes

### Q11. Which patient population most commonly has undiagnosed small-vessel disease in the epidemiology Fillingane cited?
- A) Young athletic males
- B) **Women (historically ~49%, newer estimates 60–70% before large-vessel disease)** ✓
- C) Only patients with CAC &gt;1000
- D) Only pacemaker patients

### Q12. Which hardware/clinical factor most reliably invalidates or messes up an MCG reading?
- A) Left bundle branch block
- B) Pregnancy
- C) **Pacemaker** ✓
- D) Age &lt;18

> *LBBB does not negate MCG per Fillingane; pacemakers do. Also avoid strong nearby electrical interference; Poor quality → repeat.*

## Section C — Report Interpretation & Cases (4 questions)

### Q13. On the MCG report, what does five-of-five positivity under **increased** myocardial compliance indicate about plaque burden?
**Answer:** Plaque development that is **&gt;30% occlusive** (decreased compliance maps to &lt;30%).

### Q14. Global asynchrony with the caret pointing at V5 suggests disease predominantly on which side of the heart?
- A) Right-sided
- B) **Left-sided** ✓
- C) Neither — it only means AF risk
- D) Congenital only

### Q15. In the 13-year-old soccer syncope case, which pairing of positives most raised concern for a significant congenital / cardiomyopathy problem?
**Answer:** Concurrent positives for **congenital heart defect** and **cardiomyopathy** (plus non-zero age-inappropriate cardiovascular scores and rhythm potential) → pediatric specialty workup → arrhythmia cardiomyopathy / later ICD.

### Q16. A physician with a “mild” Category B score (~1.8) still had mito dysfunction ~9/10 and left-sided disease. What teaching point does this illustrate?
**Answer:** The **summary score alone is incomplete** — review all 26 parameters. Mild aggregate scores can hide high-risk mitochondrial, plaque laterality, stiffening, and rhythm findings that change management and serial monitoring.

## Section D — Inflammation Model & Clinical Integration (4 questions)

### Q17. In Fillingane’s diabetes example, what dietary threshold typically flips the hepatic de novo lipogenesis / JNK1 inflammatory switch in type 2 / prediabetes?
- A) Any fat gram over 10 g
- B) **&gt;~25 grams of carbohydrate** ✓
- C) Only sucrose &gt;90 g
- D) Protein &gt;40 g

> *Also: alcohol and HFCS flip the same switch; even metabolically healthy people flip at ~90 g carbs/4h.*

### Q18. How much aerobic exercise did Fillingane prescribe as the daily “inflammation equalizer,” and what acute effect on circulating inflammatory factors did he claim?
**Answer:** **~50 minutes/day** aerobic at ~**70%** max HR (≈115–120 bpm), preferably **6–7 days/week** — removes ~**93%** of circulating inflammatory factors that day (returns if skipped).

### Q19. Outline Fillingane’s starter long-COVID / MCAS calming stack discussed in Q&A.
**Answer:**
1. **H1 blocker** (less-sedating: Allegra / Zyrtec / Claritin)
2. **H2 blocker** (e.g., Pepcid AC BID)
3. **Quercetin** mast-cell stabilizer ~**1000 mg BID–TID** (up to ~3000 mg/day discussed)
4. Typical duration **3–6 months** (longer if major ongoing stressors)

### Q20. A 55-year-old woman with extreme grocery-aisle fatigue, DOE, “normal” prior cath, and menopausal status presents. Using this webinar, what is your integrative workup/action plan?
**Answer (model):**
1. Suspect **small-vessel disease** phenotype (women disproportionately affected; prior cath can miss SVD)
2. Obtain **MCG** for CVD score + SVD / mito / stiffening / rhythm parameters; do **not** rely on CAC alone
3. Screen the **11 inflammatory states** (esp. dysglycemia, sleep/MTHFR, autoimmunity/MCAS, gut)
4. Start inflammation control: carb threshold teaching if insulin resistant; daily aerobic target; fix sleep substrate (methylfolate dosing if MTHFR) ± CPAP
5. Ensure **K2 (MK-7) + D + Mg** for vascular calcium trafficking / metabolic support per Nario tip
6. Serial MCG every 3–6 months to confirm scores move toward &lt;4 and domain positives clear
7. Escalate (echo/bubble, specialty) if congenital / cardiomyopathy / high VF-AF flags appear
