## Section A — Vitamin C & Iron Safety (4 questions)

### Q1. Why can high-dose vitamin C harm patients with elevated ferritin?
- A) It blocks iron absorption entirely
- B) **It reduces Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺, enabling Fenton chemistry and free-radical tissue injury** ✓
- C) It depletes vitamin D stores
- D) It raises TSH

> *Nario tip of the week: match near gasoline metaphor; injury risk in liver, heart, endocrine glands.*

### Q2. Before starting high-dose oral vitamin C, which lab strategy does Nario emphasize?
**Answer:** Full iron panel including ferritin **and** transferrin saturation (serum iron ÷ TIBC × 100), not ferritin alone — because ferritin is also an acute-phase reactant and may reflect inflammation rather than hemochromatosis.

### Q3. Per the tip ranges stated, which ferritin pattern warrants investigating iron overload?
- A) Man 80 ng/mL; woman 40 ng/mL
- B) **Man >300 ng/mL or woman >200 ng/mL (danger near ~1000)** ✓
- C) Any ferritin above 50
- D) Only low ferritin with anemia

### Q4. True or False: Elevated ferritin always means hereditary hemochromatosis and is an absolute contraindication to any vitamin C.
**Answer:** **False.** Ferritin rises with inflammation; hemochromatosis typically appears near the thousands. High-dose C requires caution and iron management (phlebotomy/chelation when indicated), but Nario frames it as dose-dependent risk, not automatic absolute contraindication at modest elevations.

## Section C — Testing & Differential Diagnosis (5 questions)

### Q11. Why do CDC/FDA positions reject urine mycotoxin tests as diagnostic (per Campbell)?
**Answer (any three):** Not FDA-validated; exposure ≠ disease (dietary ppb ubiquitous); lack of lab standardization; risk of misdiagnosis/unnecessary treatment; urine often measures metabolites and can be inaccurate (~80% for T-2 in cited April study); OATs microbial markers often don't measure claimed targets.

### Q12. What mycotoxin testing strategy does Campbell recommend?
- A) ERMI environmental score alone
- B) Urinary mycotoxins every 30 days
- C) **Serum IgG and IgE to 14 mycotoxins; retest at 6+ months** ✓
- D) Organic acid test fungal markers only

### Q13. In the Johns Hopkins Lyme referral series Campbell cites, what fraction had no active Lyme disease?
- A) 16%
- B) 50%
- C) **84%** ✓
- D) 99%

### Q14. Which Lyme-related test approach does Campbell favor?
- A) CD57 and urine PCR
- B) Western blot only (subjective banding)
- C) **Multi-peptide ELISA** ✓
- D) Lymphocyte transformation test

### Q15. True or False: A positive Borrelia IgG years after clinical recovery proves active infection requiring years of IV antibiotics.
**Answer:** **False.** Antibodies can persist for years after healed infection; 5–20% of normals may be positive. Treat the patient, not the lingering serology. Prolonged antibiotics for PTLDS lack sustained benefit in five cited trials and cause substantial adverse events.

## Section D — Treatment Protocols & Clinical Integration (5 questions)

### Q16. Campbell's core pharmacologic antifungal dose for mycotoxin illness is:
- A) Fluconazole 150 mg once weekly
- B) **Itraconazole 100 mg twice daily with food** ✓
- C) Amphotericin B IV only
- D) Nystatin swish and swallow alone

### Q17. Why is itraconazole preferred over fluconazole for indoor mold illness?
**Answer:** Fluconazole covers Candida (yeast) but has **no activity against multicellular molds**. Itraconazole covers Candida **and** molds such as Aspergillus, Penicillium, Fusarium, and Stachybotrys that produce mycotoxins.

### Q18. Ponikau/Mayo chronic sinusitis study key finding and treatment?
- A) 4% mold; treat with antibiotics indefinitely
- B) **96% mold in sinus material; Amphotericin B nasal spray + itraconazole/Sporanox** ✓
- C) 96% bacteria; surgery only
- D) Viral only; steroids alone

> *~90% respond to itraconazole; ~10% need brand Sporanox. Sinus mycotoxins can reach brain via cribriform plate.*

### Q19. Name three interventions Campbell says lack supporting evidence for clearing human mycotoxin illness.
**Answer (any three):** Binders (charcoal/zeolite/clays) in humans; ozone; plasmapheresis; HBOT as curative; fasting; Clorox/vinegar DIY remediation as definitive; ERMI as clinical decision tool; Shoemaker/Nathan-style protocols as presented.

### Q20. A 38-year-old woman has brain fog, POTS-like symptoms, chronic sinusitis, “chronic Lyme” label after 2 years of rotating antibiotics, and lives in a water-damaged duplex. Outline Campbell-aligned next steps.
**Answer:**
1. Stop treating a Lyme label alone — reassess with clinical criteria; prefer multi-peptide ELISA if Lyme still in differential; do **not** rely on urine PCR/CD57/LTT
2. Order **serum IgG/IgE to 14 mycotoxins**; plan 6-month retest
3. Identify/remove exposure (HVAC, bathrooms, leaks) via building professionals — Clorox DIY not sufficient
4. Start **itraconazole 100 mg BID with food**; switch to Sporanox if generic batch intolerance or non-response; consider Amphotericin nasal spray if chronic fungal sinusitis
5. Add evidence-dosed eight-supplement kit (Detox Pure) + low-histamine anti-inflammatory diet
6. Avoid binders-as-primary-therapy, GSH/NAC if gliotoxin/brain MS-type picture, and urine-mycotoxin chasing
7. Screen endocrine effects (zearalenone pattern), consider spore-forming probiotics and melatonin 3 mg per his microbiome/toxin-clearance rationale
8. Expect clinical course nearer **6 months** with full stack + exposure control rather than years of antibiotics
