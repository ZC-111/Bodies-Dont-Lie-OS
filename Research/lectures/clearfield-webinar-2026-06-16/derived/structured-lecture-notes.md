## Segment 4 — Carbon 60: Structure, Clean Process, Mechanism (22:51–32:43)

**Speaker:** Bob Greska

### What is C60?
- Discovered 1985; Nobel Prize 1996 (“buckyball”)
- **60 carbon atoms** in soccer-ball sphere (12 pentagons + hexagons) — only fully spherical fullerene in the C60→C70→C84 series
- C70 = rugby-ball shape; less stable when donating electrons

### Clean vs solvent-extracted product (vendor thesis)
| Dimension | Greska / C60.com claim | Typical competitors (as described) |
|---|---|---|
| Process | Solvent-free conversion of carbon feedstock | Toluene / solvent extraction from soot; some sublimation cleanup |
| Particle size | ~**20 nm** spheres | Often ~**1200 nm** / crystalline aggregates |
| Color | True black (tight lattice absorbs light) | Purple tint = solvent-swollen lattice |
| “Purity” marketing | Misleading if only C60:C70 ratio | Clean = no metals/S/Cl/Na/K; trace O₂ only (~0.5%) |
| Mixing | ~2 minutes | Up to ~2 weeks to break clusters |
| Bioavailability | High (fits mitochondria-scale niches) | Lower; large crystals may lodge in liver/kidney (mouse anecdotes cited) |

- Claim: **~14 billion** 20 nm C60 molecules could fit in one RBC volume (illustrative)
- Third-party testing / transparency claimed on website (www.c60.com — use `www` to avoid copycat SEO)

### Mechanism framed
1. Mitochondria generate free radicals while making ATP
2. Aging → slower free-radical clearance → oxidative cascade / early apoptosis
3. Spherical C60 donates electrons **without collapsing** → “antioxidant” that stays structurally stable
4. Size claim: ~**200,000× smaller** than food polyphenol antioxidants → reaches intracellular / mitochondrial targets
5. Attracts positively charged radicals via charge complementarity (“magnet” metaphor)
6. Toxins defined as electron stealers — neutralize charge → pass via GI tract

### Topical water mist
- Claimed first successful **non-hydroxylated** C60 in water (no OH adducts that enlarge/chemically modify molecule)
- Trace glycerin added for proprietary tactile feel / reverse-engineering deterrence
