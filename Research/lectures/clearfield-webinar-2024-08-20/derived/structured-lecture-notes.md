## Segment 3 — Dr. John Newstead: Cancer Treatment–Induced Osteoporosis — Framing (15:21–28:21)

**Speaker:** Dr. John Newstead (Speaker 5)

### ★ Core Thesis
> **Osteoporosis care is about fractures (and in cancer, overall survival) — not surrogate markers.** DEXA T-score changes from FDA drugs do not reliably predict anti-fracture effect.

### Origin Story
- Early practice: BMD rising on therapy; mother-in-law’s BMD rose on Fosamax — then she fractured
- DEXA predicts fracture in only **~44% of women** and **~21% of men** with osteoporosis

### Cancer Survivorship Context (ASCO 2019)
- Survivorship + osteoporosis at “near epidemic” scale
- Early breast cancer + non-metastatic prostate cancer ≈ **40%** of survivors
- Breast cancer + fracture → hospitalization: up to **83% ↑ relative mortality risk**
- 122,000 cancer patients: fractures (except forearm) ↑ mortality; HR still **1.93** out to 10 years

### Fracture Burden Snapshot
| Population | Bone loss / fracture signal |
|---|---|
| US adults >50 | ~50% osteopenia or osteoporosis |
| Breast Ca + aromatase inhibitors | **~20%** fracture within 5 years; **~80%** AI bone loss |
| Prostate Ca + ADT | Similar fracture burden; BMD loss by **3–6 months**; hip Fx RR ↑ ~30% |
| Stomach cancer survivors | >50% osteoporosis by 10 years |
| Chronic glucocorticoids | **30–50%** fracture (spine / femoral neck) |

### Medication-Induced Risk (beyond chemo)
- **Glucocorticoids:** ↑ fracture risk *before* DEXA changes; destroy collagen + muscle → falls; bone quality ≠ mineral alone
- **SSRIs:** ~1 fracture per 19 women treated 1–5 years; ~21% cancer survivors on SSRIs
- **Acid blockers:** OR ~1.59 after 4 years; up to ~30% cancer patients still on them after year 1
- **Hypnotics:** insomnia in ~40% cancer survivors vs 10–15% general; ~33% ↑ fracture risk
- **Antihypertensives / fall risk:** ~95% of fractures follow a fall

### Disclosures
- Founder, NBI; MK-4 orphan designations (AML, MDS, HCC, APL); bone products since 2007

## Segment 4 — Screening, Exam Clues & Limits of Surrogate Tests (28:21–45:29)

**Speaker:** Dr. John Newstead

### Physical Exam Pearls
- Thinning / friable skin → shared type I collagen story with bone
- Wall–occiput distance (kyphosis), weight <112 lb, height loss, tooth loss
- **Gait instability / shoe wear pattern / sit-to-stand** = top fall (hence fracture) predictors
- Age predicts fracture better than DEXA alone

### Screening Reality Check
| Group | Screening rate (cited) |
|---|---|
| Eligible women | ~30% screened (70% missed) |
| Eligible men / Medicare ≥65 | ~94–96% **not** screened |
| AI users with baseline + follow-up | ~34.5% |
| ADT men with baseline DEXA | ~92% **not** tested |
| Non-Hispanic Black patients | Lowest screening rates |

### Guidelines & Tools
- ASCO: non-metastatic cancer + ≥1 risk factor → central DEXA
- AI: baseline then every 1–2 years; tamoxifen bone loss mainly **premenopausal**
- **FRAX:** not validated in cancer / stem-cell transplant; ignores AI, ADT, transplant inputs; not for age <40
- Hip fracture ≥65: up to **36%** dead within 1 year; ~50% of survivors never regain prior pain-free function

### Labs — Use Cautiously
- **NLR:** >3 → osteopenia association; **4.68** → osteoporosis association (inflammation → osteoclast drive)
- **Bone turnover markers:** adjunct only; high pre-analytical noise; CTX / NTX can differ **5–6×** between labs
- Recommended monitoring pair: serum **CTX** + **P1NP** (mainly therapy compliance)
- Fasting; same lab/machine; same season; follicular phase if premenopausal; elevated ≤1 year post-fracture
- **MK-7 vs MK-4 teaching point:** all K forms ↓ undercarboxylated osteocalcin, but **MK-7 has never improved BMD or shown fracture reduction**; **MK-4** has (>25 trials; fracture outcomes in postmenopausal OP)

### Metastasis Signals (cancer niche use of BTMs)
- P1NP elevated in ~70% bone mets (breast/ovarian series); high specificity, ~50% sensitivity in cited study
- Prostate: P1NP rise detectable ~**8 months** before positive bone scan in 2007 study
- ALP cutoffs discussed for bone mets (cross-reactivity with liver ALP ~20%)

## Segment 5 — Lifestyle Foundations & Nutrient Hierarchy (45:29–1:04:10)

**Speaker:** Dr. John Newstead

### Evidence Gap Warning
- Most drug data = postmenopausal OP, not cancer bone loss
- Example: oral bisphosphonate **+ PPI** → **higher** fracture risk than PPI alone

### ASCO Basics (then what’s missing)
- Calcium, vitamin D, exercise, stop smoking, limit alcohol
- Guidelines lack: diet pattern, protein targets, sleep, social support, environmental fall-proofing, **MK-4**

### Calcium Dosing Reality
- RDA women ~1200 mg/day total; upper limit ~2000–2500 mg from **all** sources
- Avg US woman diet ~800 mg → often only **~400 mg** supplemental calcium needed
- Excess supplemental calcium → CV concern; prefer food first

### Lifestyle Interventions with Data
| Lever | Signal |
|---|---|
| Mediterranean diet adherence | ~**21% ↓** hip fracture; higher BMD; ↓ cancer mortality |
| Protein | Explains ~2–4% of BMD variance; target ~**1.3 g/kg/day** (~0.6 g/lb) |
| Sleep <5 h | Lower BMD (WHI); sleep behavior ~**17%** of fracture variance (UK Biobank) |
| Melatonin 1–3 mg | Dose-dependent BMD ↑ in osteopenia (3 mg > 1 mg); **no fracture outcomes** |
| Social support (≥3 close people) | ↓ osteoporosis risk; ~25–30% ↓ all-cause mortality; faster recovery after fracture |
| Steps | **7,000–7,500**/day → 50–70% ↓ all-cause mortality (10k steps = marketing myth) |
| Resistance training | Improves BMD + fracture risk; “if you’re not pushing weight, you’re not pushing aging” |

### Fall / Med Reconciliation
- ~94% of people >65 prescribed a fall-risk med
- Post-fracture discharge: patients often swapped from one fracture-promoting med to another — **no net benefit**

### Supplement Evidence Bar
> Prefer nutrients with **fracture outcomes + safety**, not only BMD or mechanism stories.

| Nutrient | Newstead stance |
|---|---|
| Ca + D | Yes — fracture data (~16–22% relative ↓) |
| **MK-4 45 mg/day** | Yes — BMD + fracture data; Japan-approved since 1995 |
| Strontium ranelate | Fracture data but VTE/CV harm (~1:1 help:harm); **not** US; citrate ≠ ranelate evidence |
| Magnesium | Essential generally; **no** convincing fracture RCTs for OP indication alone |
| Boron | No BMD/fracture RCTs for bone (Clearfield notes SHBG use separately) |

## Segment 6 — MK-4 Evidence, Pharmacotherapy & Cancer Scenarios (1:04:10–1:23:44)

**Speaker:** Dr. John Newstead

### MK-4 vs MK-7
- Different molecules (side-chain length); meta-analyses often blur “vitamin K2”
- Cockayne 2006 (retracted studies omitted in subset): MK-4 45 mg → ~**60% ↓** vertebral Fx, ~**76% ↓** non-vertebral
- Later metas: fracture endpoints that work are **MK-4** studies (RR ~0.47; ~60–62% ↓ fractures)
- MK-4 + D3 > MK-4 alone for BMD; safe even in dialysis (mineral caution still applies)
- Clinical OP dose **45 mg/day** (historically TID 15 mg); cancer trials up to 135 mg

### Drug Landscape (high level)
- Primary prevention covering vertebral **and** hip: only **zoledronic acid (Zometa)** among options discussed
- Prefers **anabolics (teriparatide/Forteo)** over bisphosphonates/SERMs for fracture reduction; osteosarcoma package warning = rat mega-dose artifact
- Bone-modifying agents often ↓ skeletal-related events (SREs) **without** overall survival gain
- **Denosumab** superior to zoledronic acid for first/subsequent SREs (2022 comparison) — still no OS difference in that frame
- AI bone loss: **bisphosphonates lack fracture reduction data** in this setting; denosumab has fracture + DFS / near-OS signals in cited ABCSG-style follow-up; rebound fracture risk after stop (~36 months)
- GIO: bisphosphonates ↑ BMD without clear fracture ↓ in systematic analysis; MK-4 stops glucocorticoid bone loss
- ADT: denosumab FDA-labeled for ↑ bone mass in high-risk men on ADT; emphasize **protein + exercise** against sarcopenia; MK-4 attenuated GnRH-agonist bone loss in women (6-mo trial)

### SRE Prognosis Reminder
- Hypercalcemia median survival ~3 mo; cord compression ~4 mo; pathologic fracture ~12 mo; bone-only breast mets OS ~2–3 years

### Closing Management Checklist
1. Mediterranean diet + adequate protein  
2. Resistance + balance training (modify yoga poses that load weak pelvis)  
3. Sleep + social support  
4. Calcium (food-first) + vitamin D aiming serum **30–44 ng/mL** for lowest hip Fx risk  
5. **MK-4 45 mg/day** (± melatonin ~3 mg; higher OK in oncology settings)  
6. Deprescribe fall/fracture meds when possible  
7. For every drug: *Has it reduced SREs and improved OS in **this** clinical situation?*

## Segment 7 — Q&A: Products, Hormones, Intake Strategy (1:23:44–end)

**Speakers:** Newstead, Clearfield, panel

### Clinical Pearls from Q&A
- **OsteoStrong / vibration:** useful as resistance/balance; no fracture outcome data; expensive
- Preferred calcium form: **calcium citrate** (absorbs at higher pH; better with hypochlorhydria / PPI; oxalate-stone friendly)
- “K2 drives Ca to bone not arteries”: soft claim; MK-4 improves BMD and keeps serum Ca normal; coronary decalcification data preliminary
- **DIM / natural AIs:** case of male OP with normal T attributed to chronic DIM → low E2; estradiol maintains male bone too
- Men on TRT: always check T **and** E2; Newstead typically keeps E2 ~**20–40**; short intermittent AI if symptomatic — avoid driving E2 to zero
- Product line (disclosure): Osteo-K / Osteo-K Minis / Osteo MK-4 — all **45 mg MK-4 + 2000 IU D3**; calcium 1000 / 400 / 0 mg as citrate; BID dosing for half-life/compliance; “Strong Bones Guarantee”
- Clinical trials used TID 15 mg; single daily 45 mg not the studied schedule
- Ca + D alone ~16–22% Fx ↓; add MK-4 45 mg → **>70%** relative fracture reduction in outcomes literature he cites
- Estradiol HRT: ~40% overall Fx ↓, ~28% hip Fx ↓ when started peri–early menopause (data thinner after ~60)
- Intake: diet recall, meds, exercise first; **rarely** run CTX unless patient needs a moving number for anxiety
- Collagen peptides: BMD data, no fracture outcomes → second-line after MK-4/D/Ca
- Irish moss / trace minerals: not for OP indication per Newstead; boron lacks OP fracture RCTs (Clearfield: boron for high SHBG)
