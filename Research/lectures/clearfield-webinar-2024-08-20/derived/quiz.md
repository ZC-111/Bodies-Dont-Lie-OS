# Quiz — Cancer Treatment–Induced Osteoporosis & Fracture-First Bone Care

**Source:** Clearfield Webinar 2024-08-20 — Dr. John Newstead lecture + Q&A  
**Level:** Integrative/functional medicine practitioner  
**Format:** 20 questions (multiple choice + short answer)

## Section A — Framing, Epidemiology & Screening (7 questions)

### Q1. According to Newstead, what should be the primary clinical endpoint in osteoporosis care?
- A) Raising DEXA T-score by ≥1 SD
- B) Normalizing undercarboxylated osteocalcin
- C) **Reducing fractures (and overall survival in cancer patients)** ✓
- D) Lowering CTX below the lab mean

> *Surrogate markers (DEXA, BTMs) are conversation tools — not proof of protection.*

### Q2. How well does DEXA predict who with osteoporosis will fracture?
- A) ~80% of women and ~60% of men
- B) **~44% of women and ~21% of men** ✓
- C) Nearly 100% if T-score < −2.5
- D) Only useful in men

### Q3. Among women with breast cancer on aromatase inhibitors, approximately what fraction fracture within five years?
- A) 5%
- B) 10%
- C) **20% (about 1 in 5)** ✓
- D) 50%

### Q4. True or False: Glucocorticoid-related fracture risk only appears after DEXA worsens.
**Answer:** **False.** Fracture risk rises before densitometric changes; glucocorticoids also impair collagen and muscle, increasing falls.

### Q5. What approximate share of eligible women receive osteoporosis screening in the general population figures Newstead cited?
- A) 70%
- B) 50%
- C) **30%** ✓
- D) 10%

### Q6. Why is FRAX limited for many oncology patients?
**Answer:** It has **not been validated** in cancer or stem-cell transplant populations and does not accept key inputs such as aromatase inhibitors, ADT, or transplant — and is not applicable under age 40.

### Q7. After hip fracture in a woman ≥65, what upper-range one-year mortality did Newstead cite?
- A) 5%
- B) 12%
- C) **Up to ~36%** ✓
- D) 60%

> *Often-quoted figure is ~20%; range extends to 36%. ~Half of survivors never regain prior pain-free function.*

## Section B — Testing & Lifestyle Foundations (6 questions)

### Q8. An NLR (neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio) of ~4.68 was associated with:
- A) Normal bone only
- B) Osteopenia only
- C) **Osteoporosis** ✓
- D) Osteopetrosis

### Q9. Which statement about vitamin K forms is correct per Newstead?
- A) MK-7 has strong fracture-outcome RCTs at 45 mg/day
- B) Any K form that lowers undercarboxylated osteocalcin reduces fractures
- C) **MK-4 has BMD and fracture outcome data; MK-7 has neither fracture reduction nor BMD improvement in the trials he reviewed** ✓
- D) MK-7 is interchangeable with MK-4 in meta-analyses

### Q10. What daily protein target does Newstead prefer for older adults protecting bone/muscle?
**Answer:** About **1.3 g/kg/day** (≈ **0.6 g per pound** body weight) — above the standard RDA, which many seniors fail to meet.

### Q11. Mediterranean diet adherence was linked to approximately what reduction in hip fracture risk?
- A) 5%
- B) **21%** ✓
- C) 50%
- D) No association

### Q12. Why does Newstead caution against stacking high-dose supplemental calcium on top of a typical US diet?
**Answer:** Average dietary intake is already ~800–1000 mg; pushing total intake with large supplements toward/over the upper limit (~2000–2500 mg from all sources) has **no proven bone benefit** and may raise **cardiovascular risk**. Often ~400 mg supplemental Ca (or food-only) is enough to reach ~1200 mg total.

### Q13. Walking how many steps/day was associated with a 50–70% reduction in all-cause mortality in the data he cited?
- A) 3,000
- B) **7,000–7,500** ✓
- C) Exactly 10,000
- D) 15,000

> *10,000 steps originated as a Japanese pedometer marketing campaign, not outcome science.*

## Section C — Nutrients, Drugs & Cancer Scenarios (7 questions)

### Q14. Which four nutrients did Newstead say have both BMD improvement and fracture-reduction evidence (with major caveats on one)?
**Answer:** **Calcium, vitamin D, MK-4, and strontium** — but he rejects US **strontium citrate** (no trials; false BMD inflation) and is wary of European **strontium ranelate** (VTE/CV harm roughly offsetting fracture benefit).

### Q15. What MK-4 dose is repeatedly tied to fracture outcomes in the literature he emphasizes?
- A) 45 mcg/day
- B) 1 mg/day
- C) **45 mg/day** ✓
- D) 135 mg only

### Q16. Combining oral bisphosphonate therapy with a PPI for PPI-associated bone risk was reported to:
- A) Fully neutralize PPI fracture risk
- B) **Increase fracture risk more than PPI use alone** ✓
- C) Improve survival in breast cancer
- D) Replace the need for DEXA

### Q17. In metastatic bone disease comparisons Newstead cited, denosumab vs zoledronic acid primarily differed in:
- A) Clear overall survival superiority for denosumab
- B) **Better prevention of skeletal-related events without a significant OS difference** ✓
- C) Denosumab worsening hypercalcemia
- D) Zoledronic acid eliminating need for calcium

### Q18. For aromatase-inhibitor bone loss, which statement matches his reading of the evidence?
**Answer:** **Bisphosphonates have not been shown to reduce fractures** in AI bone loss despite guideline enthusiasm; **denosumab** has stronger fracture (and disease-related) signals but carries **rebound fracture risk after discontinuation**.

### Q19. Preferred calcium salt when hypochlorhydria or acid blockade is likely?
- A) Calcium carbonate with meals only
- B) **Calcium citrate** ✓
- C) Coral calcium exclusively
- D) Calcium oxide

### Q20. A midlife man with osteoporosis, normal testosterone, and years of DIM use — what mechanism should you consider?
**Answer:** DIM can lower estradiol; **estradiol (not testosterone alone) maintains bone mass in men**. Excess aromatase inhibition / E2 suppression is a plausible driver — stop/reassess E2 and bone trajectory.
