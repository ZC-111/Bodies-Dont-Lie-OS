## Section B — Revenue, Preeminence & Costs (8 questions)

### Q6. In Tanno’s economics framing, revenue equals:
- A) Profit minus taxes
- B) **Price × quantity demanded** ✓
- C) Fixed cost ÷ variable cost
- D) Collections minus bad debt only

### Q7. What is the key difference between skills and preeminence?
**Answer:** Skills are the shared trained competencies of a specialty (most graduates can do the same procedures). Preeminence means your capability is so differentiated you are effectively **irreplaceable** — patients seek you specifically.

### Q8. Total profit, as defined in the lecture, subtracts which cost stack from total revenue?
- A) Rent only
- B) **Fixed + variable + psychic + opportunity costs** ✓
- C) Malpractice premiums only
- D) Advertising spend only

### Q9. Psychic cost best describes:
- A) The cost of EMR software
- B) **Mental/burnout burden from mispricing, information asymmetry, thin skills, slow results, and over-volume** ✓
- C) Patient copays
- D) Opportunity cost of capital alone

### Q10. Clearfield’s TMS pricing story illustrated which principle?
- A) Always charge the manufacturer’s minimum forever
- B) **Price above what the market will bear → zero demand; optimal price restores volume** ✓
- C) Insurance always maximizes revenue
- D) Lowering price always reduces total revenue

### Q11. Tanno’s preferred price-adjustment tactic when demand is soft:
- A) Raise prices immediately
- B) Cut to the absolute floor on day one
- C) **Start high, then drop sequentially so the new price feels like a bargain (hard to raise later)** ✓
- D) Never change list price

### Q12. Opportunity cost is:
**Answer:** The value of the next-best alternative use of your time/money (e.g., investing capital elsewhere or leaving medicine for another career).

### Q13. When demand is price-inelastic, raising price tends to:
- A) Destroy all volume
- B) **Preserve buyers who still pay — often because of preeminence / perceived unique value** ✓
- C) Only work for Medicaid panels
- D) Require seeing twice as many patients

## Section D — Legal, Regenerative & Systems (3 questions)

### Q18. True or False: U.S. law requires a nationally recognized functional-medicine diploma before an MD may discuss integrative approaches.
**Answer:** **False.** Tanno: no such national diploma requirement — but training quality, contracts, and state guidelines still matter; weekend “expert” claims are risky.
