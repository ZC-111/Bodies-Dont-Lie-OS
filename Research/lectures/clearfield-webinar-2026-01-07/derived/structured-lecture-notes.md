## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Collagen as Signaling Therapy (09:13–15:50)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario, DO (Speaker 4)  
**Comment:** John Neustadt (Speaker 5) — product formulation / third-party testing

### ★ Core Thesis
> Collagen supplements are **messengers, not building materials**. Hydrolyzed peptides (glycine, proline, hydroxyproline fragments) signal fibroblasts, chondrocytes, and osteoblasts — patients are “borrowing amino acids,” not becoming a cow.

### Collagen Types (clinical focus)
| Type | Tissue focus |
|---|---|
| **Type 1** | Skin, hair, nails, bone |
| **Type 2** | Cartilage / joints |
| **Type 3** | Skin elasticity, blood vessels |

*(~10 collagen types exist; Nario emphasizes mastering 1–3 clinically.)*

### Source Comparison
| Source | Types | Evidence / niche | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bovine** | 1 + 3 | Strongest RCT bulk — skin, BMD, connective tissue | Most cost-effective; closest pattern to human |
| **Porcine** | 1 + 3 | Structurally similar; wound matrices | Often under-labeled in supplements |
| **Marine** | Type 1 only | Skin hydration / fine wrinkles | Limited joint/bone data; often costly per gram |
| Chicken / eggshell | Niche | Less central to tip | Mentioned briefly |

### Evidence Cited
- **Proksch et al. (~2014):** 2.5–5 g/day collagen peptides × 8 weeks → ↑ skin elasticity, ↓ wrinkle volume, ↑ dermal collagen density; effects persist after stop
- **König et al. (2018):** 5 g/day × 12 months → ↑ BMD femoral neck & spine; ↑ P1NP (formation); ↓ degradation markers

### Clinical Pearl — Dose & Cofactors
> Once hydrolyzed, **dose, consistency, and peptide profile matter more than animal species**. Target **10–20 g/day** hydrolyzed peptides for **≥8–12 weeks**. Cofactors: vitamin C, zinc, copper. Common failure: half-scoop in coffee twice weekly → “doesn’t work.”

### Neustadt Product Note
- NBI bovine hydrolyzed types 1+3: **10 g/scoop**, high solubility, no smell/flavor; finished-product heavy-metal testing (Prop 65); available Fullscript / Emerson / website
- **Critical caveat (segue to main talk):** collagen has **BMD data**, **no fracture outcomes data**

## Segment 3 — John Neustadt: Osteoporosis Testing & Fracture Risk (15:50–1:07:23)

**Speaker:** John Neustadt, ND — founder/president NBI; Bone Health & Osteoporosis Foundation corporate advisory roundtable; author *Fracture Proof Your Bones*

### ★ Core Thesis
> A test is only as valuable as its ability to **predict fractures** (or, secondarily, improve adherence). Fracture risk is **multivariable** — DEXA T-score alone is a weak, overweighted signal.

### Disclosures / Product Frame
- NBI formulas: clinical-trial doses **MK-4 45 mg/day (divided)** + **D3 2000 IU/day** ± calcium variants; “Strong Bones Guarantee” (refund if fracture between qualifying BMD tests)
- Collagen: useful BMD adjunct, **not first-line** — prefer dietary protein amino acids; no fracture outcomes

### 3A. Three Pillars of Bone Evaluation
1. **Physical exam / functional fall-risk assessment** (underused)
2. **Imaging / densitometry** (DEXA, QCT, ultrasound, REMS)
3. **Labs** (vitamin D, NLR, bone turnover markers — adjunctive)

### 3B. Physical Exam & Fall Risk (★ Practice Pearls)

**Osteoporosis risk clues (incl. unconventional):**
- Low weight; height loss ≥2 cm over 1–3 years
- **Thinning / friable skin** — collagen in skin and bone; often missed; may prompt densitometry

**Screening gaps:**
- >95% of Medicare-age highest-risk population undertested
- Medications rarely prompting screen: SSRIs/SNRIs, PPIs, aromatase inhibitors
  - **SSRIs 1–5 years:** ~1 hip fracture per 19 women
  - **Aromatase inhibitors × 5 years:** ~20% expected to fracture
- Cancer-related screening rates near nonexistent for many cancers

**FRAX caveats:**
- Misses many bone-toxic meds (except prednisone)
- Invalid for ADT, aromatase inhibitors, hematopoietic stem-cell transplant, age <40
- Counseling pearl: invert the number — 10% 10-year risk = **90% chance of no fracture**; not an emergency

**Fall risk (95% of fractures follow a fall):**
- Gait, balance, chair-rise, hallway walk observation
- Footwear: >50% of falls in adults >70 at home barefoot/socks; barefoot/socks **11.2×** fall risk vs sneakers (age ≥65); 72% of surgical hip-fracture cohort wore socks/slippers/barefoot (mean age 78) → recommend **lace-up indoor sneakers**
- **One-legged stand (5- and 10-sec):** inability × 10 sec → **84% higher all-cause mortality** (~10y follow-up, n≈1700); inability × 5 sec → **213% greater risk** of injurious fall
- Walking ability Q (1 km / 0.5 km / 100 m): limited ability → **32–219%** higher fracture risk independent of age, weight, prior falls/fractures

**DEXA predictive humility:**
- Strong & steady + no recent falls/Fx: DEXA predicts hip Fx in only **5.3% women / 4.3% men <70** with osteoporosis; **14.6% / ~11% if >70**
- Overall: DEXA predicts fracture in only **44% of osteoporotic women / 21% of men**

### 3C. Imaging Modalities

| Modality | Role | Fracture prediction | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Central DEXA** | Gold standard diagnosis/monitoring | Modest; BMD explains <½ hip Fx risk | Artifacts (OA, AAC, BMI extremes, >300 lb); up to **90%** reports with ≥1 error; same machine/clinic for serial |
| **TBS add-on** | Lumbar bone quality | Modest incremental info (French n=560; Manitoba n>29k) | No outcome trials: ↑ TBS → ↓ Fx proven |
| **QCT / biomechanical CT** | Can repurpose abdominal CT | ≈ DEXA for hip Fx (13 studies; 2 male studies superior) | Higher radiation; detects osteoporosis ≠ predicts Fx |
| **Heel ultrasound** | Portable, radiation-free | Similar accuracy to DEXA for Fx prediction (USPSTF); not superior | Device-specific validation required; WHO T-score definition does not apply |
| **REMS** (FDA 2018) | RF echographic multispectrometry | Study 1: ≈ DEXA; Study 2 + fragility score: may be **worse for women lumbar/hip prediction vs 2008 DEXA benchmarks**, possibly **better for men**; low fragility score → high NPV for non-fracture | Not insured; out-of-pocket; no data that improving score lowers Fx |

**Pharmacotherapy & BMD:** among persons on FDA osteoporosis drugs, **Δ BMD is not a good predictor of anti-fracture effect** (Annals 2014). BMD accounts for only **4–28%** of vertebral Fx risk reduction from drugs. Mother-in-law Fosamax anecdote (↑ BMD → still hip Fx) motivated Neustadt’s specialty focus (2005–06).

### 3D. Laboratory Testing

**Vitamin D:** maximum hip-fracture reduction associated with **30–44 ng/mL** (same range linked to fall reduction). Most reach this with **~2000 IU D3/day**. Higher targets (60–80) may be pursued for systemic/immune goals — separate from hip-Fx optimum.

**Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR):**
- ≥ **3.17** associated with osteopenia
- ≥ **4.68** associated with osteoporosis  
→ systemic inflammation signal + hip-Fx association in older women

**Bone turnover markers (BTMs):** useful adjunct for **drug adherence**, not routine fracture crystal ball.
- Preferred pair (IOF/IFCC & European advisory boards): **CTX + P1NP**
- Resorption markers change in ~3 weeks; formation markers take months
- High inter-/intra-assay CV (e.g., CTX/NTX can differ **up to 5.6-fold** between labs; urinary NTX intra-assay CV ~26.6%)
- Pre-analytical: morning fasting; seasonal (higher winter); cycle phase (follicular preferred); no exercise 48h; post-Fx elevation up to 1 year; heart failure / matrix diseases raise markers
- Predicting “fast bone loser”: ~**coin toss (40–55%)**
- Gradient of risk for Fx prediction only ~**1.2 SD** incremental after clinical factors + BMD

### 3E. Surrogate Marker Trap — Vitamin K Forms

| Form | Under-carboxylated osteocalcin | BMD | Fracture outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vitamin K1** (5–10 mg trials) | Improves | No ↑ BMD / no stop of loss | One Fx trial: **no reduction** |
| **MK-7** (various; up to 320 μg) | Improves | Never shown to stop/reverse BMD loss; highest-dose study: **faster hip BMD loss** vs control | **No Fx trials** |
| **MK-4 45 mg/day divided** | Improves | ↑ BMD; maintains strength | **>70% fewer fractures** in clinical trials |

> Treating a surrogate (ucOC, CTX) ≠ proven fracture reduction. Demand **fracture endpoints**.

### 3F. Lifestyle Anchors (Q&A / radiation-spine case)
- Protein **≥0.6 g per lb body weight/day**
- Fiber **≥30 g/day** (gut–bone axis)
- Sleep **≥6 h** — UK Biobank: ~**70% of incident fractures** attributed to sleep issues (speaker claim)
- Deprescribe fall-risk meds (~**>90%** of elderly on such meds, per speaker)
- Strength + stability training

## Segment 4 — Q&A: Men’s Bone Health, HRT, Drugs, Nutrients & Strontium (1:07:28–1:47:01)

**Speakers:** Neustadt, Nario, Clearfield, Speakers 6–9

### Men’s assessment (20% of osteoporosis cases)
- NLR, vitamin D, PTH, **testosterone + estradiol**
- Bone benefit of T largely via aromatization → keep **estradiol >20** in men
- Fan of physiologic HRT in men and women; strength/stability still primary
- Occult celiac workup if unexplained osteoporosis (can be sole manifestation)

### HRT & FDA labeling
- Never monomodal therapy in Neustadt’s practice
- HRT: ~**20–40%** fracture-risk reduction association (speaker recall)
- FDA-approved for **prevention**, not **treatment** of osteoporosis — Neustadt believes treatment use is justified and hopes for future approval; clinic anecdotes of BMD rise with HRT alone insufficient as proof

### Silent vertebral fractures
- Neustadt: prevalence uncertain; clinical vs incident vs asymptomatic definitions muddy research
- Speaker 6 (imaging): AI on lateral CXR/spine flags anterior height loss (~20% threshold for “vertebral body fracture” reporting) — insurers push capture because asymptomatic vertebral Fx escalates cost/risk
- After osteoporotic fracture: ~**30–31%** refracture within 5 years

### Calcitonin / bisphosphonates / anabolics
- Nasal calcitonin: mainly for **acute vertebral Fx pain**; can ↑ BMD
- Bisphosphonates: atypical femoral Fx + ONJ (rare but significant); typically limit **3–5 years**; **>50% discontinue within year 1**; need ≥80% possession for benefit
- **Primary prevention:** oral bisphosphonates → vertebral only; **only IV zoledronic acid (Zometa)** shown for **both primary vertebral and hip** Fx reduction
- Prefer agents with long real-world track record (≥5–10 years): **teriparatide (Forteo)** — osteosarcoma black box from high-dose mouse data; millions of human doses, no attributable osteosarcoma per Neustadt; **denosumab (Prolia)** also solid secondary Fx reduction (e.g., ~68% RRR vertebral vs ~20% hip — secondary prevention frame)

### Boron, magnesium, vitamin C
- Neustadt lens: **no fracture-outcome RCTs** for boron, Mg, or vitamin C as bone supplements
- Mg: epi/food-frequency signals ≠ prospective Fx trials; one 1990s MgO trial ↑ BMD but underpowered for Fx
- Clearfield: boron **3–9 mg** for SHBG lowering → ↑ free sex hormones (secondary bone rationale); Neustadt uses boron in joint product (OA pain data)

### Strontium — strong “no” from Neustadt
- All Fx RCTs = **strontium ranelate** (EU drug), **not** US supplement **strontium citrate**
- 4/5 large trials: ~**45% vertebral** RRR, **no hip**; 1/5 hip benefit → hip efficacy doubtful
- Artifacts DEXA upward (US machines don’t correct)
- Safety (ranelate): ~**1 clot/stroke/MI per fracture prevented** framing — no citrate safety data
- Prefer MK-4 data (>70% Fx reduction) without that risk profile
- Competitor algae calcium + strontium citrate stacks (e.g., AlgaeCal / StronBoost 680 mg) — marketing extrapolation flagged

### Closing clinical rule
> When evaluating **any** test or therapy: (1) Does it **predict fractures**? (2) If you improve the number, has that been shown to **reduce fractures**?
