## Section A — Collagen Clinical Use (5 questions)

### Q1. Nario’s core framing of oral collagen peptides is that they act primarily as:
- A) Intact triple-helix building blocks deposited into skin
- B) **Bioactive signaling peptides (glycine/proline/hydroxyproline fragments)** ✓

### Q2. Which collagen types are the clinical “need to know” trio emphasized in the tip?
- A) Types 5, 6, and 10
- B) **Types 1, 2, and 3** ✓
- C) Types 4, 7, and 9
- D) Only type 2

### Q3. For measurable clinical benefit, Nario’s practical hydrolyzed collagen dosing/timeline is:
- A) 1–2 g every other day for 2 weeks
- B) **10–20 g/day for a minimum of 8–12 weeks** ✓
- C) 50 g once weekly indefinitely
- D) Topical only; oral dosing irrelevant

### Q4. The König 2018 bone study cited used which dose/duration of collagen peptides?
**Answer:** **5 g/day for 12 months** — ↑ BMD at femoral neck and spine, ↑ P1NP, ↓ degradation markers.

### Q5. True or False: After hydrolysis, animal species (bovine vs porcine vs marine) matters more than dose and peptide profile.
**Answer:** **False.** Nario’s pearl: once hydrolyzed, **dose, consistency, and peptide profile** matter more than species. Marine is mainly type 1 / skin niche; bovine 1+3 has the strongest general evidence base.

### Q10. Why does Neustadt invert FRAX percentages when counseling patients?

## Section C — Labs, MK Forms & Imaging Alternatives (4 questions)

### Q13. Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio thresholds cited:
**Answer:** NLR **≥3.17** associated with osteopenia; **≥4.68** associated with osteoporosis (inflammation / immune-activation signal).

### Q14. Which BTM pair do IOF/IFCC prefer for research and therapy monitoring?
- A) Osteocalcin + ALP only
- B) **CTX and P1NP** ✓
- C) Urinary NTX exclusively
- D) TRAP5b alone

### Q15. Among vitamin K forms discussed, which has clinical-trial evidence for both BMD improvement and large fracture reduction (~>70%)?
- A) Vitamin K1 5–10 mg
- B) MK-7 320 μg
- C) **MK-4 45 mg/day in divided doses** ✓
- D) All K forms equally for fractures

> *K1: no BMD gain / no Fx reduction in cited trials. MK-7: never shown to stop/reverse BMD loss; no Fx trials. All K forms lower under-carboxylated osteocalcin — surrogate ≠ outcome.*

### Q16. REMS vs DEXA — Neustadt’s balanced takeaway:
**Answer:** REMS is FDA-cleared (2018), radiation-free, portable, useful when DEXA T-scores are invalid (e.g., younger patients). Fracture prediction is **not clearly superior** to DEXA; one fragility-score study suggested **worse** prediction in women vs classic DEXA benchmarks and possibly **better in men**. Low fragility scores have strong **negative** predictive framing. Not insured; no proof that improving the score lowers fractures.

## Section D — Therapies, Nutrients & Controversies (4 questions)

### Q17. For **primary** fracture prevention, which agent did Neustadt highlight as shown to reduce both vertebral **and** hip fractures?
- A) All oral weekly bisphosphonates equally
- B) **IV zoledronic acid (Zometa)** ✓
- C) Nasal calcitonin alone
- D) Strontium citrate

### Q18. Target estradiol floor Neustadt likes in men being assessed for bone health:
- A) Zero (aromatase-inhibitor style)
- B) **Above ~20** ✓
- C) Only total testosterone matters
- D) Estradiol is irrelevant in men

### Q19. Why does Neustadt reject extrapolating European strontium ranelate trials to US strontium citrate?
**Answer:** Ranelate is the studied **drug** form (not available in US); citrate is a **supplement** with **no** fracture RCTs and **no** safety data. Ranelate: mostly vertebral benefit (~45%), doubtful hip benefit (1/5 trials), DEXA artifact, and serious vascular clot/stroke/MI signal roughly “one clot per fracture prevented.” Cherry-picking efficacy without safety is disingenuous.

### Q20. True or False: Boron, magnesium, and vitamin C each have prospective clinical trials proving fracture reduction as bone supplements.
**Answer:** **False.** Neustadt’s rule: demand fracture endpoints — those three lack them. Mg may help BMD in limited data; boron has joint/OA and SHBG-modulation rationales (Clearfield 3–9 mg) but is not a primary anti-fracture monotherapy based on cited evidence.
