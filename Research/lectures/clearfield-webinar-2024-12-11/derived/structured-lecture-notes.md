## Segment 2 — Soy Protein Quality & Muscle Building (11:04–19:00)

**Speaker:** Mark Messina

### Macronutrient Profile of Soybeans
- Most legumes: ~26–28% calories from protein; soybeans up to **~42%**
- Soybeans ~**50%** calories from fat (unlike nearly fat-free beans); chickpeas are a partial exception
- Soy ingredients: soy flour (~50% protein) → soy protein isolate (~**90%** protein); base for meat/dairy analogs and protein fortification

### Protein Quality
- Quality = digestibility + essential amino acid content (EAA dominates)
- **PDCAAS** (regulatory standard): soy protein isolate scores **1.0** (max) — at/above beef, well above many plant proteins
- “Complete vs incomplete protein” is **nonsense**: all proteins contain all 9 EAAs; some are *limiting* in amount (e.g., almond very low in one EAA) — a missing EAA would score 0

### Muscle & Strength
- ★ Hierarchy: **resistance exercise ≫ total protein ≫ protein type**
- Target for hypertrophy-minded intake: ~**1.6 g/kg** (2× RDA of 0.8 g/kg)
- Whey often favored for leucine content, but **2018 meta-analysis of 9 RCTs** (Messina co-author): soy ≈ whey/animal protein for strength and lean mass with resistance training
- Arnold newsletter cited: soy-heavy vegan diet can support muscle growth equivalently (Arnold himself not vegan)
- Expert consensus paper: protein *type* plays a **minor** role

### Clinical Pearl
> Exercise is the cake; total protein is icing; protein type is the cherry.

## Segment 3 — Cholesterol, Soybean Oil & Heart Claims (19:00–25:00)

**Speaker:** Mark Messina

### Soy Protein → LDL
- Direct LDL reduction ~**3–4%** (not via PUFA alone); first clinical data **1967**
- Population relevance: each **1% ↓ LDL** ≈ **1–2% ↓ CHD risk** over time
- **FDA health claim (1999):** 25 g soy protein/day as part of a diet low in saturated fat and cholesterol may reduce heart disease risk

### Soybean Oil
- World’s #2 edible oil (behind palm); **#1 in the US**
- Accounts for **>7%** of US calories; **>40%** of US linoleic acid *and* ALA intake
- Composition: ~16% SFA, moderate MUFA, ~**62% PUFA**; provides both essential FA (linoleic + ALA)
- **FDA heart claim (2017)** for soybean oil based on cholesterol-lowering when replacing SFA

### Intrinsic vs Extrinsic LDL Effect (Jenkins)
- Intrinsic = soy protein itself (~3–4%)
- Extrinsic = displacing SFA-rich protein foods with PUFA-rich soy → Jenkins estimate **8–10%**; Messina prefers **~6–8%** combined

## Segment 4 — Isoflavones, Breast Cancer & Skin (25:00–40:00)

**Speaker:** Mark Messina

### Isoflavones — Unique to Soy (practically)
- Japan intake: **30–50 mg/day**; US/Europe/Canada: **≤3 mg/day**
- Rule of thumb: **3–4 mg isoflavones per gram soy protein**; 1 traditional serving (cup whole-bean soy milk or 3–4 oz tofu) ≈ **~25 mg**
- Classified as phytoestrogens but **clinically ≠ hormone estrogen**
- Recent meta-analysis of RCTs: no effect on endometrial thickness, vaginal maturation index, FSH, or estrogen levels in postmenopausal women → do **not** assume estrogen-like systemic effects

### Hypothesized Benefits (time limited to breast + skin)
- Breast/prostate cancer risk reduction, skin, endothelial function, hot flashes, memory

### Breast Cancer — Prevention
- Ecological rates: North America ~3× Japan, ~7× China (1990 incidence) — hypothesis-generating only
- Within-population meta-analyses: higher soy/isoflavone intake associated with lower breast cancer risk
- ★ Messina’s 30-year view: **maximal protection requires childhood/adolescence intake**; adult intake may help but early-life signal is stronger (obs + animal + mechanisms)

### Breast Cancer Survivors — The 20-Year Controversy
- #1 client concern raised to dietitians (2023 survey worse than 2022)
- Conceptual fear: ER+ tumors + “phytoestrogen” label
- Rodent fear base: athymic ovariectomized mice models (1998 onward)
- **No definitive RCT** of recurrence/mortality with soy vs control
- Surrogate RCTs: soy/isoflavones **do not** ↑ breast density or cell proliferation (14 studies) — even at **100–235 mg/day** isoflavones (far above Japanese intake)
- Contrast: combined HT ↑ proliferation 4–10× in 12 weeks; tamoxifen/AIs ↓ density & proliferation
- Clearfield clarification: synthetic **progestin** (not bioidentical progesterone) is the HT proliferation driver — Messina defers

### Observational Benefit Signal
- **Shanghai Breast Cancer Survival Study (2009):** post-diagnosis soy → ↓ death & recurrence (n≈5,000; 444 deaths; 534 recurrences/BC deaths)
- Meta-analysis: soy isoflavones → **~26% ↓ recurrence**, especially postmenopausal & ER+ (theoretically most “estrogen-sensitive”)
- **ACS 2022:** post-diagnosis soy isoflavone intake associated with lower recurrence risk
- **AICR:** soy among 5 factors that may reduce BC mortality (with fiber, activity, overall diet, healthy BMI)

### Skin Photoaging RCT
- 6-month parallel RCT: postmenopausal women, Fitzpatrick I–III
- 30 g/day soy protein (isoflavones) vs 30 g casein
- Soy: **−6.5% wrinkle severity**, **−2.5% pigment intensity**, ↑ cheek hydration vs casein
- Dose context: 30 g soy protein is high vs Japan (~9 g/day protein from soy), but **~50 mg isoflavones** = ~2 traditional servings

## Segment 5 — Feminization Myths, Thyroid & Allergy (40:00–47:00)

**Speaker:** Mark Messina

### Male Hormones & Fertility
- Meta-analysis of **41 RCTs** (~1,700 men): **no effect** of soy/isoflavones on total T, free T, estradiol, or estrone
- 3 intervention studies on sperm/semen in healthy men: **no adverse effects**
- Two case reports (2008-era + 2024): oligospermia normalized with **80 mg/day** isoflavones × 3 months → pregnancy; intriguing but n=2

### Feminization Case Reports (context for later Q&A)
- Primary scare: 2008 gynecomastia case; later cases involved extreme intake (**~360 mg isoflavones/day** — e.g., 12 cups soy milk/day or ~20 servings/day vegan) ≈ **9×** Japanese male intake
- Long-term trials on breast tissue: no feminizing effects at normal intakes

### Thyroid
- Animal work since **1933**; human meta-analysis **2019** (18 RCTs): **no significant effect** on T4 or T3
- **FDA 2017** full safety review of soy protein: rejected safety concerns except **soy protein allergy**

### Allergy
- Soy is one of US “Big 9” (~90% of food allergies) but **lowest prevalence** among them except sesame
- ~**3/1000** adults (US/Canada survey estimates); ~**70%** of children outgrow by age 10
- Adult-onset possible; highly refined soybean oil **not** allergenic (insufficient protein)
- FAO global priority allergen list **excluded soy** (low prevalence, potency, severity)

## Segment 6 — Ultra-Processed Foods & Nova Critique (47:22–55:00)

**Speaker:** Mark Messina

### Why This Matters for Soy
- New plant-based meats (soy/pea/wheat concentrates) and **~90% of plant milks** (including whole-bean soy milk) classified as **ultra-processed (Nova group 4)**
- PubMed: ~**600–650** UPF papers/year (4× since 2019); 41% of US adults trying to avoid UPFs
- US diet: ~**57%** calories from UPFs; ~**73%** of marketplace foods classified UPF
- Epidemiology consistently links UPF intake to adverse outcomes (cancers, dementia, obesity, frailty, IBS, etc.)

### Nova Classification Problems
- Coined Brazil **2009**; classifies by **processing extent**, not nutrient profile
- Fruit + milk smoothie = group 1; add protein concentrate → group 4 (despite better nutrition)
- Plain yogurt = group 1; flavored yogurt = UPF
- Tofu / soy milk / soy burgers lumped with **Twinkies**

### Clinical Counterexample
- Meta-analysis **17 RCTs**, soy milk (group 4) vs cow’s milk (group 1), ~4 weeks median, ~500 mL:
  - LDL **−6%**, HDL **+4%**
  - SBP **−8 mmHg**, DBP **−5 mmHg** (Messina cautious on BP magnitude)
  - CRP **−22%**
- Conclusion: Nova is too blunt for consumer guidance

## Segment 7 — Seed Oils Defense (55:00–1:06:00)

**Speaker:** Mark Messina

### “Hateful Eight”
Canola, corn, cottonseed, soy, sunflower, safflower, grapeseed, rice bran

### Claims vs Evidence
| Claim | Messina position |
|---|---|
| Modern / highly processed | Acknowledge both |
| High n-6:n-3 ratio | Ratio **irrelevant** as diet-quality metric (AHA & many orgs) |
| Linoleic → inflammation | **Refuted** by RCT meta-analyses (2012, 2017, 2021) |
| Linoleic → oxidative stress | Double bonds oxidize in bottles (store cool/dark); humans have antioxidant systems; 2019 review: no ↑ lipid peroxidation / LDL oxidation *in vivo* — more data welcome |

### Mechanism Clarification
- Fear: linoleic → arachidonic acid → inflammatory eicosanoids
- Reality: dietary linoleic **does not meaningfully raise** tissue AA; some AA metabolites are anti-inflammatory

### Intake Targets
- AHA: linoleic acid **5–10%** of calories
- NHANES: US ~7.5–8% calories from PUFA (~90% linoleic) — mid-range; still exceed SFA target (11–12% vs ≤10%)

### Outcomes Evidence
- Nordic Nutrition Recommendations review (2024): replace SFA with n-6 PUFA → better lipids, ↓ CVD risk, better glucose/insulin, possible ↓ mortality; n-6 & n-3 may ↓ T2D risk; n-6 biomarkers associated with ↓ cancer mortality
- Ecological “seed oils rose, disease rose” = same fallacy as TV stations ↔ cancer

## Segment 8 — GMO Soy, Glyphosate & Intake Recommendations (1:06:00–1:10:30)

**Speaker:** Mark Messina

### GMO Context
- Grown in 24 countries since 1990s; evaluated by USDA, FDA, EPA
- **2020:** ~94% US soy, 96% cotton, 92% corn genetically modified
- Roundup Ready: inserted enzyme resistant to glyphosate’s inhibition of aromatic AA synthesis (Phe/Tyr/Trp)
- EPA: glyphosate **not likely carcinogenic to humans** (agency position cited)
- Nutrient composition GMO ≈ non-GMO
- Practical: nearly all US **tofu and soy milk** from **non-GMO** beans; animal feed absorbs most US GMO soy (~95% of crop)

### Intake Recommendations ★
| Goal | Guidance |
|---|---|
| General adult benefit | **~2 servings/day** (~50 mg isoflavones; 15–25 g soy protein) |
| Cholesterol claim | **25 g soy protein/day** |
| Upper practical limit | **≤4 servings/day** (not proven harmful above; diversity concern — avoid >~30–40 g protein from one source) |
| Preferred form | **Whole soy foods first**; supplements OK for hot flashes if food adherence poor |

## Segment 9 — Extended Q&A (1:10:35–1:57:00)

**Speakers:** Messina, Benoit Tano, Bill Clearfield, Dr. Nario, Stefan Hartman

### Disentangling Components
- Soy **oil** = fatty acids + some vitamins; **no** protein/isoflavones → won’t help hot flashes
- Isoflavone absorption similar from foods, SPI, and pills
- Trials prefer pills/powders for blinding, dosing, compliance
- Cholesterol lowering = **protein**; isoflavones don’t meaningfully lower LDL
- Foods also displace less healthy choices + provide ALA/fiber/etc.

### Gynecomastia / Extreme Dose Context
- Documented feminization cases ≈ **360 mg isoflavones/day** (extreme); clinical breast-tissue trials negative at normal doses
- Shanghai benefits attributed primarily to **isoflavones** (food matrix tofu/soy milk)

### Miso & Fermented Soy
- Japan soy split ≈ half tofu, half natto/miso
- Fermentation hydrolyzes glycosides → aglycones → possibly faster absorption / higher Cmax
- Miso weaker for cancer-risk signals in epi; may help heart disease; **high sodium** caveat (also plant burgers)

### Where Bad Rap Came From
- **Not** primarily GMO/glyphosate — **animal studies + extreme case reports + small outlier RCTs**
- Breast: 1998 mouse ER+ tumor stimulation → decade of rodent papers before Shanghai benefit signal
- Thyroid: animal enzyme inhibition not replicated in human RCTs
- Nutrition trials often underpowered → chance outliers stick in public mind

### Vegans Not Overconsuming
- Typical vegan soy isoflavone intake ~**20 mg/day** — far below Messina’s informal upper (~100 mg)

### Muscle Again (sarcopenia Chinese study)
- Habitual diet vs whey vs soy vs soy+whey (16 g) in sarcopenic adults already at ~1 g/kg → **all three protein arms** improved mass & performance

### Isoflavone Profile (Genistein / Daidzein / Glycitein)
- Native soybean ratio ≈ **50% genistein / 40% daidzein / 10% glycitein**
- 1990s supplements often **glycitein-heavy** (processing artifact) → ineffective for hot flashes
- **2012 meta-analysis:** only **high-genistein** supplements efficacious for hot flashes

### Genistein & Cancer
- Messina entered field (NCI, 1989) via genistein enzyme-inhibition story (TK etc.) — *in vitro* concentrations not achievable by diet
- Still plausible early-life chemoprevention via ERβ mechanisms
- Nario clinical note: genistein appears on genetic-supplement panels for ER-dependent cancers; typical protocol doses discussed ~125–200 mg 2–3×/day as part of multi-agent plans — Messina notes usual research doses ≤100 mg total isoflavones (~50 mg genistein); one marker study used 235 mg isoflavones / 150 mg genistein → no ↑ proliferation, ↑ apoptosis

### Seed-Oil Debate with Stefan Hartman
- Hartman: wants future lecture on processing/Maillard/funding history; ecological trends “everyone sicker”
- Messina: judge by **individual-level** prospective cohorts (often NIH-funded, e.g., EPIC); higher linoleic/seed-oil intake associated with **better** chronic-disease outcomes
- Omega-6:3 ratio debate: Bill Harris (Omega-3 Index) argues ratio uninformative — improve status by **raising omega-3**, not cutting linoleic; conversion LA→AA overstated

### Estrogen Receptor Selectivity
- Isoflavones bind ERs, **prefer ERβ** (anti-proliferative) over ERα (proliferative)
- Messina co-authored large review (~30K words, 700 refs): soy/isoflavones do **not** warrant endocrine-disruptor classification; wealth of human feeding studies unlike BPA
