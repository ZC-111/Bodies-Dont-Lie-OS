## Section A — Protein, Cholesterol & Basics (6 questions)

### Q1. What PDCAAS score does soy protein isolate receive?
- A) 0.7
- B) 0.85
- C) **1.0** ✓
- D) 1.2

> *1.0 is the maximum under PDCAAS; soy isolate matches or exceeds beef and most plant proteins on this scale.*

### Q2. According to Messina’s hierarchy for building muscle, which factor ranks highest?
- A) Protein type (whey vs soy)
- B) Total daily protein
- C) **Resistance exercise** ✓
- D) Leucine timing within 30 minutes of training

> *“Exercise is the cake; total protein is icing; protein type is the cherry.”*

### Q3. Approximately how much does soy protein directly lower LDL cholesterol?
- A) 10–12%
- B) **3–4%** ✓
- C) 15–20%
- D) No measurable effect

> *Modest but population-relevant; each 1% LDL reduction ≈ 1–2% CHD risk reduction over time.*

### Q4. What daily soy protein intake underpins the FDA heart-health claim?
**Answer:** **25 grams** of soy protein per day as part of a diet low in saturated fat and cholesterol (approved 1999).

### Q5. True or False: “Incomplete proteins” lack one or more of the nine essential amino acids.
**Answer:** **False.** Messina calls this “complete nonsense.” All dietary proteins contain all nine EAAs; some are low (*limiting*) relative to requirements. A truly missing EAA would yield a PDCAAS of zero.

### Q6. Roughly what share of US calorie intake comes from soybean oil, and what share of both essential fatty acids does it supply?
**Answer:** Soybean oil provides **>7%** of US calories and **>40%** of US intake of both linoleic acid (n-6) and ALA (n-3). It is the #1 edible oil in the US.

## Section B — Isoflavones, Breast Cancer & Skin (6 questions)

### Q7. Typical isoflavone intake in Japan vs the US/Europe/Canada is approximately:
- A) Japan 5–10 mg/day; West 30–50 mg/day
- B) **Japan 30–50 mg/day; West ≤3 mg/day** ✓
- C) Both regions ~25 mg/day
- D) Japan 100–150 mg/day; West 30–50 mg/day

### Q8. A practical rule for estimating isoflavones from traditional soy foods is:
- A) 1 mg per gram of protein
- B) **3–4 mg per gram of protein** ✓
- C) 10 mg per gram of protein
- D) Isoflavones track fat content, not protein

> *One serving (cup whole-bean soy milk or 3–4 oz tofu) ≈ ~25 mg isoflavones.*

### Q9. Messina’s preferred explanation for soy’s breast-cancer risk reduction emphasizes:
- A) Adult supplementation after age 50 only
- B) Soy oil linoleic acid content
- C) **Early-life (childhood/adolescence) soy intake** ✓
- D) Glyphosate-free certification

### Q10. Which statement best summarizes the clinical marker data on soy and breast tissue?
- A) Soy increases mammographic density like combined HT
- B) **Neither soy nor isoflavone supplements increase breast density or cell proliferation across multiple RCTs** ✓
- C) Only genistein increases proliferation; daidzein does not
- D) Effects appear only above 50 mg/day

> *Even doses of 100–235 mg/day isoflavones showed no adverse marker effects; contrast with combined HT raising proliferation 4–10× in 12 weeks.*

### Q11. What did the Shanghai Breast Cancer Survival Study (2009) and later meta-analyses suggest about post-diagnosis soy?
**Answer:** Higher post-diagnosis soy/isoflavone intake associated with **lower mortality and recurrence** (Shanghai n≈5,000). A later meta-analysis reported ~**26% lower recurrence**, especially in postmenopausal and ER+ survivors. ACS (2022) and AICR statements are supportive of safety/benefit.

### Q12. In the 6-month skin photoaging RCT, what dose and comparator were used, and what improved?
**Answer:** **30 g/day soy protein** (with isoflavones) vs **30 g casein** in postmenopausal women (Fitzpatrick I–III). Soy improved wrinkle severity (~−6.5%), pigment intensity (~−2.5%), and facial hydration. ~50 mg isoflavones ≈ two traditional servings.

## Section C — Controversies: Hormones, Thyroid, UPF, Seed Oils, GMO (5 questions)

### Q13. A meta-analysis of 41 RCTs on soy/isoflavones in men found:
- A) Lower total testosterone and higher estradiol
- B) Reduced sperm count at 25 mg isoflavones/day
- C) **No effects on total T, free T, estradiol, or estrone** ✓
- D) Gynecomastia in 12% of subjects

> *Documented feminization case reports involved extreme intakes (~360 mg isoflavones/day).*

### Q14. The 2019 thyroid meta-analysis (18 RCTs) concluded soy isoflavones:
**Answer:** Have **no significant effects on T4 or T3**. FDA’s 2017 soy protein safety review rejected thyroid and other safety concerns except soy protein allergy.

### Q15. Why does Messina criticize classifying soy milk/tofu with Twinkies under Nova?
**Answer:** Nova sorts foods by **degree of processing**, not nutrient quality. Adding protein concentrate turns a group-1 smoothie into an ultra-processed food; soy milk (group 4) vs cow’s milk (group 1) RCTs showed soy milk improved lipids, BP, and CRP — so the label is too blunt for consumer guidance.

### Q16. Which claim about linoleic acid / seed oils does Messina say clinical meta-analyses have refuted?
- A) Seed oils are modern dietary additions
- B) **Linoleic acid increases inflammatory biomarkers** ✓
- C) Seed oils require industrial processing
- D) Soybean oil is widely used in the US

> *RCT metas in 2012, 2017, and 2021; LA does not meaningfully raise tissue arachidonic acid as feared.*

### Q17. What practical intake does Messina recommend for adults seeking soy benefits?
- A) 1 serving/week
- B) **About 2 servings/day (~50 mg isoflavones; 15–25 g soy protein); upper practical limit ~4 servings/day** ✓
- C) 8–10 servings/day to match rodent chemoprevention doses
- D) Isoflavone pills only — avoid whole soy foods

## Section D — Clinical Integration (3 questions)

### Q18. A postmenopausal breast cancer survivor asks if she must avoid all soy. Using this lecture, what do you counsel?
**Answer:**
1. No definitive recurrence RCT exists, but **marker RCTs show safety** (no ↑ density/proliferation) even at high isoflavone doses
2. Observational data (Shanghai; metas) and **ACS/AICR** statements support post-diagnosis soy / lower recurrence
3. Prefer **traditional soy foods** (~1–2 servings/day) over megadose supplements unless targeting hot flashes with evidence-based genistein-rich products
4. Distinguish soy from combined HT (synthetic progestin drives proliferation risk)

### Q19. A male patient fears soy will “kill his testosterone.” He currently drinks 1 cup soy milk daily. Approach?
**Answer:**
1. Cite **41-RCT meta-analysis**: no effect on T or estrogen at typical intakes
2. His intake (~25 mg isoflavones) is **below Japanese male averages**, far from case-report extremes (~360 mg/day)
3. Three semen RCTs in healthy men: no adverse sperm effects; rare case reports even suggest oligospermia improvement at 80 mg/day (n=2 — not practice-changing alone)
4. Reassure; encourage dietary diversity rather than soy avoidance for hormone fear

### Q20. A patient on a carnivore forum wants to eliminate all “seed oils” and UPFs, including soy milk and tofu. How do you use Messina’s framework?
**Answer:**
1. Separate **junk-food UPFs** from nutrient-dense processed soy — Nova is not a nutrient score
2. Cite soy milk vs dairy RCTs (lipids, CRP) and Nordic reviews favoring n-6 PUFA replacing SFA for CVD outcomes
3. Omega-6:3 **ratio** is a poor metric; improve omega-3 status rather than demonizing linoleic acid (Bill Harris)
4. For soy specifically: **~2 servings/day** of tofu/soy milk is evidence-aligned; US retail tofu/soy milk often non-GMO if that is their concern
5. Avoid ecological correlations (“oils up, disease up”) as causal proof
