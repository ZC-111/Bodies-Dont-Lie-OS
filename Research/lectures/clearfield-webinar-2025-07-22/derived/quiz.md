## Section A — Tip of the Week & Lipid Timing (4 questions)

### Q1. After an acute viral illness, how long should you wait before ordering a lipid panel for baseline decisions?
- A) 48 hours after fever resolves
- B) 1 week
- C) **4–6 weeks post-recovery** ✓
- D) 6 months

> *Nario tip of the week: acute illness distorts lipids; wait 4–6 weeks.*

### Q2. During acute illness, which pattern is expected?
- A) LDL↑, HDL↑, TG↓
- B) **LDL↓ / HDL↓ (~10–30%), TG↑ (~10–50%)** ✓
- C) All lipids unchanged
- D) Only ApoB rises

> *IL-6 shifts liver toward acute-phase proteins; LDL receptors clear LDL; VLDL/LPL/cortisol drive TG up.*

### Q3. True or False: Post-COVID patients can show persistent dyslipidemia even without diabetes or obesity.
**Answer:** **True.** Wang et al. 2023 was cited for ongoing metabolic/endothelial disruption after COVID.

### Q4. A patient’s LDL remains high despite lifestyle and low-dose statin. Per Nario, what should you look for before escalating lipid drugs?
**Answer:** Occult/chronic inflammation (sinusitis, periodontal disease, Hep C, NASH), hormones, insulin resistance, steroids/meds — “cholesterol may be the signal, not the problem.”

## Section D — Clinical Cases & Stem Cells (4 questions)

### Q17. A 42-year-old on 12.5 mg iodine for 1.5 years has serum iodine 1245 (ref 40–92). First steps discussed?
**Answer:**
1. Repeat the iodine level (error / recent iodinated contrast)
2. Prefer urine iodine for monitoring (Hartman teaching)
3. Don’t ignore extreme elevation — consider gradual iodine reduction
4. Check vitamin D (inverse with TPO), sex hormones, full thyroid Abs
