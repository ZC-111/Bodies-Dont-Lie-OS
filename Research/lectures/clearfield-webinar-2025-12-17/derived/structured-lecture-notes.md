## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Nandrolone (Deca-Durabolin) in Longevity Practice (11:24–21:47)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario (with Stefan Hartman clinical add-ons)

### ★ Core Clarifications
> Nandrolone is a **19-nortestosterone–derived anabolic steroid** with strong anabolic / weaker androgenic effects. Patients are already asking about it via gym culture — better they hear it from clinicians than Reddit.

### Regulatory / Practical Facts
- **U.S. FDA:** approved only for **anemia of chronic kidney disease** (not osteoporosis)
- **Europe/Oceania:** historically used for postmenopausal osteoporosis
- **Deca-Durabolin** commercially discontinued for business reasons (not safety) — available via compounding for off-label use
- **Therapeutic doses cited:** 50–100 mg IM every 3–4 weeks (Reed et al. 1994; Johansson et al. 1999)
- **Abuse doses:** 200–600 mg/week stacked with testosterone; WADA-banned

### Clinical Themes
1. Bone density / frailty — ↑ osteoblastic activity + lean mass
2. Sarcopenia / cachexia in chronic illness
3. Joint recovery — collagen-synthesis signals (Urban et al. 1995)

### Panel Pearls (Hartman)
- Compounded at ~200 mg/mL; hard to get lower concentrations
- Half-life similar to testosterone cypionate; subjective effect ~6 days → microdosing may reduce side effects
- High intermittent doses can block DHT receptors → **ED** in men
- Women: very low dose (e.g., ~2 mg/week reported for Stefan’s mom); virilization risk if overdosed
- Monitor lipids, hematocrit, hormones; controlled substance / off-label caution

## Segment 3 — Dr. Felicia Gersh: Vasomotor Symptoms — Mechanisms & Why They Matter (29:20–1:07:35)

**Speaker:** Felicia Gersh, MD

### ★ Core Thesis
> **Hormones are not optional for optimal health.** Vasomotor symptoms (VMS) are markers of hypothalamic estradiol deficiency — not cosmetic inconvenience. Estradiol is the gold-standard therapy.

### Definitions & Timeline
- **Menopause:** 12 consecutive months without vaginal bleeding; U.S. typical age 45–55
- **Perimenopause:** often begins ~10 years before final menstrual year → can start mid-30s
- **Early menopause:** 40–45; **premature:** <40; **late:** >55 (~≥10% early)
- Tubal ligation can impair ovarian blood flow → early menopause (Gersh personal case at 43)
- VMS can persist **7–10 years** average; reports into 70s–80s; majority of women untreated

### #1 Symptom Cluster
- Word recall / memory problems often outrank hot flashes as presenting complaint
- Linked: sleep disruption, anxiety, depression, irritability, focus issues
- SWAN: higher VMS with **high FSH + low estradiol**

### Mechanism — KNDy Neurons & Neurokinin B
```
↓ Estradiol
    → dysregulation of hypothalamic thermoregulatory center
    → KNDy neurons (kisspeptin / neurokinin B / dynorphin)
    → NKB → NK3 (and NK1) receptors
    → exaggerated heat-loss responses, skin blood-flow chaos
```
- Same peptide circuitry overlaps reproduction (GnRH → LH/FSH)
- Core temperature tightly linked to ovulation / implantation biology

### Risk Factors for Severe VMS
- Obesity (weight loss helps)
- Smoking / secondhand smoke (~**60%** ↑ significant symptoms)
- Pre-existing mood disorders; childhood trauma/abuse
- Lower socioeconomic / educational status
- Worse baseline anxiety/depression → worse VMS

### Personal Narrative
- Paternal grandmother: severe untreated VMS → anxiety, depression, insomnia → suicide — frames VMS as life-altering, not “just hot flashes”

## Segment 4 — Non-Hormonal Pharma vs Integrative Options (1:07:35–1:43:42)

**Speaker:** Felicia Gersh, MD

### Pharmaceutical Ladder (when estradiol contraindicated / refused)

| Option | Status | Gersh stance |
|---|---|---|
| **Estradiol** | Gold standard | First-line for nearly all appropriate candidates |
| **Paroxetine (Brisdelle)** | FDA-approved for VMS | SSRIs/SNRIs: similar benefit class-wide (fluoxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, sertraline, venlafaxine) |
| **Gabapentin** | Off-label; supportive data | Preferable to NK antagonists if non-hormonal needed |
| **Clonidine** | Off-label | Largely shunned / not recommended |
| **Fezolinetant (Veozah)** | NK3 antagonist | ~$600/month; weeks to full effect; **FDA liver-injury warning** — last-line for Gersh |
| **Elinzanetant (Linquzet*)** | Dual NK1/NK3 antagonist (newly approved / pre-market at talk) | Same mechanistic class concerns |

\*Transcript brand spelling varies (“Lin kuet”); dual NK1/3 antagonist for VMS.

### Why NK Antagonists Worry Gersh
- NKB/NK3 signaling also relevant in **liver** → systemic blockade can cause serious hepatotoxicity
- Cannot selectively block only hypothalamic receptors
- No head-to-head vs estradiol; estradiol clearly superior on cross-study read

### Conventional Absolute Contraindications Noted (with Gersh nuance)
- ER+ breast cancer / endometrial adenocarcinoma (generally no estradiol)
- Homozygous Factor V Leiden, prior PE/DVT listed as absolute by many — Gersh personally more flexible but does not instruct violating official absolutes

### Phytoestrogens & Lifestyle
- **Foods:** organic minimally processed soy (tofu, miso, tempeh, edamame) — isoflavones; flax — lignans; other legumes
- Avoid “faux food” ultra-processed soy analogs
- **Siberian rhubarb root** stilbene phytoestrogen — data suggested better VMS effect than Veozah in Gersh’s read; inexpensive
- **Ashwagandha:** phytoestrogen + calming/sleep benefits; useful adjunct even on HRT
- Layers, cold liquids/ice, plant-based diet, weight loss, exercise, cooling bedding
- Workplace accommodations helpful but **treatment > changing clothes 20×/day**

### Autonomic / Gut / Neuroinflammation Axis
- Menopause → ↓ vagal tone, sympathetic overdrive
- Gut barrier impairment (“leaky gut”); **enteroendocrine / GLP-1–producing cells** decline with age → appetite/glucose dysregulation → ↑ diabetes risk post-menopause
- Bidirectional vagus–gut signaling; neuroinflammation (infection, lymphoma/leukemia) can mimic VMS physiology
- Estradiol modulates immune tone and inflammation control

## Segment 5 — Bioidentical HRT Protocols: Estradiol, Progesterone, Testosterone (1:40:47–2:29:04)

**Speaker:** Felicia Gersh, MD (Q&A with Clearfield / Nario)

### Estradiol Dosing Philosophy
> Standard of care doses often only suppress VMS — **inadequate for bone, brain, metabolic optimization.** Dose to comfort/tolerance and physiologic levels, not “lowest that stops flashes.”

| Approach | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Patch (recent menopause, <5 yr)** | Often start **0.1 mg**; 0.05 if breast-tender; upper buttock absorption better than abdomen |
| **Target levels** | ~**100 pg/mL** achievable with 0.1 patch in many; not chasing 300+ |
| **Forms** | Patches, gels, compounded creams; **avoid spray** (levels often stay menopausal) |
| **Compounded cream** | Absorbs less than commercial; site matters (thin skin > thick); high end cited ~5 mg BID with level monitoring |
| **Dual 0.1 patches / dual high gel packs** | Rare, poor absorbers — not routine super-dosing |

### Progesterone
- Prefer **vaginal** micronized progesterone (avoids first-pass)
- Oral: **80–90%** metabolized (allopregnanolone + ~30 metabolites) → inadequate parent hormone
- Typical start **200 mg** vaginal; range **200–400 mg**; measure cyclically
- Bleeding on physiologic cyclic HRT = expected growth-factor biology, not failure
- **Topical progesterone:** no reliable commercial product (21-carbon molecule; poor transdermal reliability). Acceptable “cheat” post-hysterectomy with monitoring; rectal alternative if vaginal refused
- Wiley protocol: good concept, **impractical / unphysiologic dosing** (estradiol levels ~800 pg/mL reported)

### Testosterone in Women
- Limited data; only FDA niche = hypoactive sexual desire
- Gersh heuristic: aim ~**50th–70th percentile** of lab range (often ~25 to upper 30s ng/dL depending on assay) — self-authored, not guideline
- Prefer **cream** titrated low→up (1→2→3 mg BID; max ~5 mg BID = 10 mg/day); **no pellets** (uncontrolled, often supra-physiologic)
- Oophorectomy: ovaries ≈ **25%** of T; brain has **no T sensors** → adrenals don’t compensate → **replace promptly**
- Abrupt stop of exogenous T also an “emergency”; natural aging decline is not
- Peri-menopause acne/whiskers often = **↓ SHBG from low E2** → more free T; fix with estradiol first, not more T
- Assays for free T unreliable at female ranges; use total T + SHBG thoughtfully

### Perimenopausal Surges
- Aging resistant follicles → escalating FSH/LH → intermittent **hyperstim-like estradiol overshoots**
- Rule out other pathology before labeling everything “perimenopause”
- Can start estradiol when symptomatic even if FSH still 10–20, after workup

### “Estrogen Dominance” Reframe → **Estrone Dominance**
```
Inflammation / adipose → ↑ aromatase → androgens → estrone
↓ conversion estrone → estradiol
Estrone → ERα (proliferation + innate immune activation)
→ more inflammation / cancer-permissive milieu
```
**Fix:** anti-inflammatory lifestyle + gut/liver detox pathways + **give estradiol + progesterone** (E2 down-regulates aromatase when plentiful)

### Endometriosis / Fibroids / Ablations
- Endometriosis + HRT: poor-quality data; focus pelvic anti-inflammation, higher vaginal progesterone (receptor resistance), **CBD** (oral/suppository) via endocannabinoid anti-inflammatory tone
- Fibroids: limited data; hysterectomy option if progressive growth
- **Endometrial ablation:** Gersh favorite — physiologic cyclic hormones without bleeding

### Labs
- Monitors with **blood**, not saliva
- Circadian / clock biology: ~1/3 genes are clock genes; ~90% associated — estradiol receptors in master clock; critical for VMS control
- Nitric oxide: E2 modulates eNOS/nNOS; oral + gut microbiomes contribute alternate NO pathways

## Segment 6 — Estetrol, BiEst/Estriol Critique & Close (2:39:41–2:48:26)

**Speakers:** Felicia Gersh, Melvin Nario, Bill Clearfield, Stefan Hartman

### Estetrol (E4)
- Fetal-liver weak estrogen; not present in adults physiologically
- Preferentially activates ERα subtype not linked to breast-cancer activation (αB vs αA per Gersh framing)
- In oral contraceptive **Nextstellis**; menopause indications emerging — Gersh skeptical of routine adult use

### Estriol / BiEst
> Would **never** use BiEst for menopause. Estriol does **not** protect bone. Body makes estriol from estradiol as needed.

- Dominant pregnancy estrogen; β-receptor bias down-regulates α → maternal immune tolerance of fetus — also ↑ infection severity risk (COVID/flu analog)
- May have disease-specific roles (e.g., MS) as a **drug**, not as menopausal HRT substitute
- Estradiol data: can induce apoptosis in some breast-cancer contexts / help certain resistant cases — do not treat E2 as “pro-cancer”
