## Section A — Vasomotor Mechanisms & Epidemiology (7 questions)

### Q1. According to Gersh, what is the gold-standard treatment for menopausal vasomotor symptoms?
- A) Fezolinetant (Veozah)
- B) Paroxetine
- C) **Estradiol** ✓
- D) Gabapentin

> *Estradiol modulates the hypothalamic thermoregulatory center; NK antagonists and SSRIs are alternatives when hormones are contraindicated or refused.*

### Q2. How long can average vasomotor symptoms persist after the menopausal transition?
- A) 6–12 months
- B) 2–3 years
- C) **7–10 years** ✓
- D) They always resolve within 1 year of the final menstrual period

### Q3. Which hypothalamic neuronal group and peptide pathway did Gersh emphasize in VMS pathophysiology?
**Answer:** KNDy neurons (kisspeptin / neurokinin B / dynorphin); neurokinin B signaling via NK3 (and NK1) receptors. Loss of estradiol regulation → thermoregulatory dysregulation.

### Q4. The SWAN study association highlighted for higher vasomotor symptom burden was:
- A) High estradiol + low FSH
- B) **High FSH + low estradiol** ✓
- C) High progesterone alone
- D) Low SHBG alone

### Q5. Smoking / secondhand smoke increased the chance of significant vasomotor symptoms by approximately:
- A) 10%
- B) 25%
- C) **60%** ✓
- D) 90%

### Q6. True or False: Official menopause (12 months without bleeding) must occur before vasomotor symptoms can begin.
**Answer:** **False.** Symptoms often begin years earlier in perimenopause (which can start ~10 years before the final menstrual year).

### Q7. What did Gersh identify as the #1 symptom women complain of entering menopause (often overlooked vs hot flashes)?
**Answer:** Word-recall / memory problems (interlinked with sleep disruption, mood, and VMS).

### Q16. Gersh’s usual starting estradiol patch dose for a woman <5 years into menopause is:
- A) 0.025 mg
- B) 0.0375 mg
- C) **0.1 mg (step down to 0.05 if breast tenderness)** ✓

> *0.1 patch often reaches ~100 pg/mL; place upper buttock for better absorption than abdomen.*

### Q17. True or False: Gersh considers commercially available topical progesterone cream a reliable way to achieve systemic physiologic levels in women with a uterus.
**Answer:** **False.** No product reliably/dependably delivers progesterone (21 carbons) through skin; vaginal (or rectal) preferred. Topical may be used cautiously post-hysterectomy with monitoring.

### Q18. After bilateral oophorectomy, why does Gersh say testosterone replacement is urgent?
**Answer:** Ovaries produce ~25% of women’s testosterone; the brain has no testosterone sensors, so adrenals do not compensate — abrupt ~25% drop warrants prompt replacement (unlike gradual natural aging decline).

- C) Estriol raises FSH too much
- D) Estriol is identical to estetrol

### Q20. Nandrolone tip: In the U.S., nandrolone is FDA-approved for:
- A) Postmenopausal osteoporosis
- B) Male hypogonadism as first-line TRT
- C) **Anemia of chronic kidney disease** ✓
