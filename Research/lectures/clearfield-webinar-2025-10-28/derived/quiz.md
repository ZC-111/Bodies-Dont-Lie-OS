### Q7. Why does Meletis insist on supporting adrenal **medulla** as well as cortex?
**Answer:** Cortex supplies cortisol/cortisone; medulla supplies adrenaline/NE. Cortex-only strategies (hydrocortisone or adaptogens alone) miss catecholamine support. He adds substrates such as N-acetyl-tyrosine and notes locus coeruleus NE dumps are poorly dampened when cortisol is low.

### Q9. What happens clinically if you aggressively uptitrate thyroid hormone without supporting adrenals/mitochondria?
- A) Permanent remission of long COVID

### Q10. Why might some patients prefer desiccated porcine thyroid over synthetic T4±T3, per Meletis’s provocation?
**Answer:** Porcine products contain a non-standardized amount of **T2**, which may uniquely stimulate mitochondrial/AMPK-related pathways in some responders — hypothesis, not definitive proof.

### Q11. In the prostate-cancer patient on androgen deprivation + statin, what mitochondrial/steroid concern does Meletis highlight?
**Answer:** Driving cholesterol extremely low (case ~97) plus near-zero testosterone collapses pregnenolone/DHEA steroidogenic substrate at the mitochondrial StAR/CYP11A1 step — “poisoning” pathways needed for hormones and cellular resilience.

## Section D — Clinical Integration & Controversies (3 questions)

### Q18. A 58-year-old postmenopausal woman has monthly UTIs, GSM, and refuses systemic HRT. Outline an evidence-first local plan from this webinar.
**Answer:**
1. Start **low-dose vaginal estrogen** (cream/ring/tablet): daily induction 2–4 weeks → 1–3×/week maintenance
2. Counsel on lactobacillus/pH mechanism and non-antibiotic prevention guidelines
3. If estrogen contraindicated (e.g., AI for breast cancer): consider **vaginal DHEA/prasterone** with shared decision-making
4. Add vestibular **testosterone** if dyspareunia/androgen-receptor–rich pain is prominent (Clearfield/Caroline dosing; watch total androgen load)
5. Hormone-avoiders: trial **hyaluronic acid** for tissue comfort (not equivalent UTI prevention evidence)

### Q19. A long-COVID patient with severe fatigue/brain fog asks about oxaloacetate + NR together. How do you counsel using Meletis’s framework?
**Answer:**
1. Stabilized oxaloacetate can be trialed; expect possible benefit within ~3–6 weeks; ~40% may be strong responders
2. Cost is high (~hundreds/bottle) — set expectations; ~2 bottles may clarify response
3. NAD precursor (e.g., NR) is synergistic in concept, but avoid stacking to super-physiologic excess
4. Start low/go slow (esp. MTHFR/overmethylation-prone); consider NAD lab check after weeks if using IV + oral
5. Address adrenals, thyroid, sleep/apnea, and mito-burdening meds in parallel — don’t rely on one molecule

### Q20. Compare metformin vs methylene blue as mitochondrial interventions based on this discussion.
**Answer:**
- **Metformin:** Complex I effect; longevity/cancer off-label interest; AMPK benefits; watch **B12 deficiency** (~50%); mixed mito cost vs metabolic gain
- **Methylene Blue:** Bypass/electron-donor narrative popular online; highly dose-dependent; Meletis had adverse subjective effects (MAO-related); Speaker 7 saw one dramatic responder, many non-responders
- **Shared pearl:** Neither is a universal fix — identify the patient’s specific “hurdles” (adrenal, thyroid, sleep, toxins, meds) rather than band-aiding all mito dysfunction with one agent
