## 🎯 Infographic 1: "Under-Muscled, Not Just Over-Fat"

**Title:** Muscle = Organ of Longevity

```
Skeletal muscle ≈ 40% body mass
  │
  ├── Endocrine organ (myokines)
  ├── Nutrient sensor (AAs, leucine)
  ├── Glucose sink (~80% of meal glucose)
  ├── Amino-acid reservoir (repair)
  └── “Body armor” (falls / fractures)
```

**Quote callout:** “We’re not over-fat — we’re under-muscled.” — Gabrielle Lyon (*Forever Strong*), cited by Quinn

**Footer:** Start with the minutes the patient actually has (even 2–3), not a 150-minute lecture.

## 💪 Infographic 2: "1RM Decision Tree — Strength vs Hypertrophy"

**Title:** One-Rep Max Made Simple

| Goal | Load | Reps (typical) | Look / feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | ~50–80% 1RM | ~5 (lower) | Stronger, not always bigger |
| **Hypertrophy** | ~30–50% 1RM (or 6–12 zone) | 10–20 / 6–12 | Size + some strength |
| **Aging default** | Mix both | Periodize | Function first |

**Rule:** 1RM = heaviest load with **form intact** — no ego lean-back curls.

## 🍬 Infographic 4: "GLUT4 — Why You Don’t Need to Carb-Load to Train"

**Title:** Exercise Moves the Glucose Door

```
REST: GLUT4 stored in vesicles
EXERCISE: GLUT4 → muscle membrane
  → insulin / glucose enter working muscle
  → some GLUT4 stays on surface
  → circulating insulin burden ↓
  → insulin resistance improves
```

**Callout:** Acute uptake needs translocation; **expression also rises after exercise**.

## 🥩 Infographic 5: "Protein & Leucine Targets"

**Title:** Amino Acids Build — Labels Lie

| Target | Number |
|---|---|
| Protein / meal | **30–50 g** |
| Leucine / meal | **~2.5 g** |
| Daily protein heuristic | **~1 g per lb goal body weight** |
| Hand-size chicken | ~30+ g protein |
| Ensure/Boost absorption (Quinn) | Often **only 10–20%** of labeled protein + sugar load |

**Formula example:** Goal weight 175 lb → ~175 g protein/day (food + quality shakes; absorption ≠ label).

**Footer:** Protein alone doesn’t hypertrophy — need mechanical stimulus (or leucine focus if unable to train yet).

## 🧱 Infographic 6: "Isometrics Ladder"

**Title:** Time Under Tension Without the Shear

```
START HERE
  Yielding holds: wall sit, plank, push-up position
  Overcoming holds: push wall / doorway 3–6 sec × 3
        ↓
  Bands (still controlled)
        ↓
  Free weights / machines
```

**Why first:** Connective tissue “locks,” less tendon shear, minimal DOMS → patients come back.

**Office kit:** Doorway push, chair sit, hand clasp — between patients.

## 📉 Infographic 7: "Sarcopenia Clock — And the Reversal Message"

**Title:** 3–5% Per Decade — Until You Fight Back

```
Age 30–40+:  −3 to 5% muscle mass / decade
Age 60+:     Faster loss
Function:    Mass AND strength/power decline
Outcome:     Falls, slow healing, IR, frailty

REVERSAL: Possible at ANY age / ANY starting fitness
Biggest % gains: the most deconditioned starters
```

**Hormone sidebar:** Keep hard sessions ~**≤1 hour** to favor testosterone vs cortisol.

## ✅ Infographic 8: "Functional Fitness Checklist"

**Title:** Can You Still Do Life at 70–80?

| Test | Target (Quinn board) |
|---|---|
| Overhead lift | 25–50 lb |
| Grocery carry | 2–4 bags, 50–100 ft |
| Breath hold | >30 sec |
| Single-leg stand | 30 sec |
| Hang / bar | >30 sec |
| Push-ups | ~20 women / ~40 men (age-adjusted) |
| Plank / wall sit | 1 minute |
| Floor get-ups | 10× no grab |
| Chair stand | No hand push-off |
| Waist vs height (men) | **2 × waist (in) < height (in)** |

**Grip caveat:** Useful sarcopenia clue — not the whole story. Prefer hang + crutches / carry competence.
