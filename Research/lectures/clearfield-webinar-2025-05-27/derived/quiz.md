## Section A — Muscle Physiology & Longevity (7 questions)

### Q1. Quinn’s core framing of skeletal muscle is that it is primarily:
- A) A passive locomotion tissue
- B) **The organ of longevity and a proxy for total health** ✓
- C) Only relevant for athletes under 40
- D) Secondary to VO₂ max for aging

> *“We’re not over-fat, we’re under-muscled” (Gabrielle Lyon). Muscle ≈ 40% body mass; endocrine + nutrient-sensing.*

### Q2. Approximately what fraction of dietary glucose is disposed toward muscle when mass is adequate?
- A) 20%
- B) 40%
- C) **~80%** ✓
- D) 100%

> *Without sufficient muscle, glucose recirculates toward visceral fat / unwanted storage.*

### Q3. Which myokine is highlighted for white-to-brown fat conversion and osteoblast support?
- A) Myostatin
- B) **Irisin** ✓
- C) Myostatin + IL-1
- D) Only BDNF

### Q4. What is myostatin’s role in Quinn’s balance model?
- A) Primary driver of hypertrophy
- B) **Atrophy / anti-growth brake so every daily lift doesn’t endlessly grow muscle** ✓
- C) Exclusive brain neurotrophin
- D) GLUT4 transporter

### Q5. True or False: “Lactic acid” is the preferred framing for the burn of hard efforts.
**Answer:** **False.** Quinn emphasizes **lactate** (from pyruvate under low O₂) as fuel, buffer, and hormone-like signal to heart, liver, and brain — not a toxin to “flush.”

### Q6. What training pearl does Quinn give about lactate burn and hypertrophy?
**Answer:** Aim for lactate burn roughly **~10% of the time** as a stimulus for muscle size / capacity, not every set forever.

### Q7. The Henneman size principle states that:
- A) Larger people always recruit more fibers
- B) **The nervous system recruits only the motor units needed for the task (energy conservation)** ✓
- C) All motor units fire at once for every lift
- D) Grip strength equals total health

- B) **Lower relative load / higher reps (e.g., ~30–50% or 6–12 rep ranges discussed)** ✓

### Q10. Yielding vs overcoming isometrics — which is which?
**Answer:** **Yielding** = hold against gravity (wall sit, plank). **Overcoming** = push/pull an immovable object (wall, doorway). Both create time under tension with connective tissue more “locked.”

### Q11. Recommended progression to reduce DOMS dropout in novices:
- A) Max deadlift day 1
- B) **Isometrics → bands → weights** ✓
- C) Daily 2-hour marathon + heavy squats
- D) Protein only, no movement

### Q13. Office micro-dose example Quinn gives busy clinicians:
**Answer:** Between patients — squats/half-squats/wall sits; hands push or pull hard for **3–6 seconds × ~3**; toothbrushing squats; doorway pushes / chair sits.

### Q14. For women, Quinn’s cited research framing is to lift:

## Section C — Protein, mTOR, Aging & Assessment (6 questions)

### Q15. Per-meal protein / leucine targets discussed:
**Answer:** Roughly **30–50 g protein per meal** and about **2.5 g leucine per meal** (three meals). Goal-body-weight heuristic: **~1 g protein/day per pound of goal weight**.

### Q16. Why does Quinn criticize hospital Ensure/Boost-style drinks for muscle building?
- A) They contain too much leucine
- B) **Low labeled protein with poor absorption (~10–20%) plus high sugar** ✓
- C) They are too expensive to absorb
- D) They suppress GLUT4 permanently

### Q17. Leucine primarily activates which complex in skeletal muscle for MPS?
- A) AMPK only
- B) **mTORC1** ✓
- C) Myostatin receptor exclusively
- D) PCSK9

### Q18. mTOR clinical balance pearl for cancer vs muscle:
**Answer:** mTOR is not purely “bad.” **mTORC1** (growth) vs **mTORC2** (survival) act cell-specifically. Cancer care: avoid flooding sugar/insulin. Muscle/angiogenesis needs some mTOR tone — chronic blanket rapamycin is not what serious bodybuilders take.

### Q19. Sarcopenia rate and reversibility:
**Answer:** About **3–5% muscle mass loss per decade** after mid-adulthood (worse after 60). Quinn stresses it is **reversible at any age**; the most deconditioned often see the largest percent gains early.

### Q20. Name two caveats Quinn gives about grip strength as a health marker, plus one preferred whole-body proxy.
**Answer:** Grip is a useful sarcopenia signal (forearms/wrists) but **not** the sole measure — his father had crushing chef grip yet poor overall health. Prefer broader proxies such as **ability to crutch**, hang time, chair stands, grocery carries, floor get-ups, or **2× waist < height** (men).
