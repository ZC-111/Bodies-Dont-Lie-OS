- Immediate steps already taken: showers; topical **carbon-60 (C60)** spray; hydration; sauna for dermal elimination
- Poison control: watch for systemic signs; ED if worsening

### Panel detox suggestions
| Method | Role | Notes from discussion |
|---|---|---|

## Segment 3 — Tip of the Week: Bioglutide (NA-931) — Oral Quadruple Agonist (16:53–20:17)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario, DO

### ★ Core framing
Investigational **oral** agent targeting **four** pathways at once:
1. GLP-1  
2. GIP  
3. Glucagon  
4. **IGF-1** (the differentiator — lean-mass preservation)

### Why it matters for anti-aging / obesity medicine
- Current GLP-1 class concern: **sarcopenia** during aggressive weight loss
- 2025 review (*Acta Diabetologica*): muscle loss may worsen frailty, insulin resistance, long-term metabolic resilience
- Thesis: future of obesity medicine = **body composition**, not maximal thinness

### Early data (as presented; largely sponsor / conference abstracts)
| Phase | Finding |
|---|---|
| Phase 1 (ENDO / ADA 2025) | Up to **~6.4%** weight loss in 28 days; mild GI AEs; **no major muscle-loss signal** |
| Phase 2 | **~13.8%** weight loss at 13 weeks; ~70% achieved >12% loss in some analyses; GI tolerability milder than class expectation |
| Phase 3 | Ongoing as of 2026 (per speaker) |
| Comparator reality | Injectable GLP-1s still lead (~26% weight loss class benchmarks) — orals not yet equivalent |

### Caveats emphasized
- **Investigational** — research-only / black-market research labels circulating
- Long-term safety unknown, especially **IGF-1 → cancer risk** question (no cancers in early phases cited)
- Company-sponsored early data; likely eventual big-pharma path (Eli Lilly mentioned as rumor)

### Panel aside (Dr. Patel)
- Clinical combo approach: GLP-1 + tirzepatide-class + third agent + B-complex/B12 to preserve muscle vs single-molecule high-dose

## Segment 4 — Stefan Hartman: Building a DPC Practice (28:40–1:06:11)

**Speaker:** Stefan Hartman, PA — Iron Direct Primary Care (started 2021)

### ★ Core thesis
> Free yourself from being a cog in the system: create a **membership-based, high-automation, low-overhead** practice. Government incentives favor LLC/S-corp entrepreneurship and employment.

### Origin story (as told)
- Started with ~$100K training loans, <$10–20K cash, **zero patients**
- Door-knocking in low-income neighborhood → no signups
- Grew to **3 providers, ~1,200 patients**, beachside practice with regenerative tech
- **$0 paid marketing** (no Facebook/Instagram ads) — organic referral network only

### Entity & tax architecture
1. Form **LLC** (FL: sunbiz.org / sunshine.org as cited)
2. Elect **S-corporation** ASAP; bring on **shareholders** (family, collaborating physician)
3. Quarterly profit → **shareholder dividends** (avoids Medicare / SS / unemployment taxes on that portion)
4. Still pay yourself a **W-2 wage** (Gusto) into personal checking (SSN-tied, separate from EIN business account)
5. Business credit cards for write-offs; track in **Xero** (or similar)
6. **SIMPLE IRA** with employer match (including matching yourself)

### Provider compensation philosophy
- Industry-leading split: providers keep **~65% of received payments** after transaction fees
- Goal: retain clinicians; avoid “corporate greed / high-turnover” primary care culture
- Collaborating MD (Dr. Goodman) as **shareholder**, not $2–3K/month supervising fee that bankrupts startups

### Complex-case economics (Clearfield challenge)

- Answer: membership buys **time and access**, not consumables; C-peptide → insulin when insulinopenic; months–years of FM detective work; patients pay labs/Rx separately

### Controlled substances & scope
- **No chronic opiates / benzos** (contractual)

### DPC vs concierge
- **Concierge** = still bill insurance + cash membership (“double dipping”) → Medicare fraud risk
- **DPC** = cash membership only; **opt out of Medicare**
- Opted-out docs can still refer to Medicare-participating specialists/imaging; referrals generally still recognized
