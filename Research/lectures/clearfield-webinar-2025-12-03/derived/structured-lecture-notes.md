## Segment 2 — Coimbra Protocol Overview & Cautionary Case (≈25:00–43:00)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield, DO

### ★ Core Thesis
> High-dose vitamin D3 under strict medical supervision can overcome **acquired vitamin D resistance** in autoimmune disease — but misuse can cause irreversible kidney damage, stones, arrhythmias, and death.

### 2A. Two “Coimbras” — Don’t Confuse Them
- **Coimbra Group (Europe):** Collaboration of ~42 European academic societies named after Coimbra University, Portugal — **not** this protocol
- **Coimbra Protocol:** High-dose vitamin D3 therapy developed by **Dr. Cicero Coimbra**, neurologist/professor, Federal University of São Paulo, Brazil (~2001–2002 onward)

### 2B. Origin Story
- Parkinson’s patient on **10,000 IU** vitamin D3 → tremor improved; concurrent **vitiligo** improvement sparked autoimmune hypothesis
- Reported use in: MS, vitiligo, psoriasis, RA, lupus, Hashimoto’s, alopecia areata, Crohn’s, others
- Coimbra clinical claim: **~95% suppression of MS activity** (observational / anecdotal — not RCTs)

### 2C. Cautionary Case — “Tom” (Wilkes-Barre, ~1990–91)
- 44yo brittle diabetic with stents; new-onset refractory seizures; Ca²⁺ ~**15.2** mg/dL; AFib/flutter; never woke up; died ~2 weeks later
- Self-dosed vitamin D from Sam’s Club candy dishes: ~**150,000 IU/day** for months (no monitoring, no low-Ca diet, no fluids protocol)
- Hospital had no vitamin D reagents; level eventually **>900**; dialysis/plasmapheresis failed
- Pearl: Always check vitamin D; never DIY mega-dosing

### 2D. Non-Negotiable Guardrails (introduced early)
- Strict medical supervision + informed consent
- **≥2.5 L fluid/day** (mandatory)
- **Low-calcium diet <500 mg/day**
- Dose by **PTH response**, not by 25-OH-D alone
- Monitor ionized + total Ca, renal function

## Segment 3 — Mechanism: Acquired Vitamin D Resistance (≈43:00–53:00)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield

### 3A. Vitamin D as Immune Pro-Hormone
- ↑ T-regulatory (Treg) cells → immune tolerance
- ↓ Th17 (and Th1) inflammatory drive → relevant to MS, psoriasis, RA, lupus, Hashimoto’s
- Epigenetic influence on hundreds–~1,200 immune-related genes
- Supports growth hormone / cortisol physiology; low D linked to higher autoimmune risk

### 3B. Resistance Hypothesis (analogous to insulin / thyroid resistance)
**Genetic contributors:**
- VDR polymorphisms (SNPs)
- CYP2R1 (25-hydroxylase) and CYP27B1 (1α-hydroxylase) variants → impaired activation

**Environmental / acquired contributors:**
- EBV, Lyme (*Borrelia*), CMV, herpes, hepatitis, *Yersinia*
- Mold / mycotoxins (hat tip Dr. Campbell)
- High cortisol → weakens VDR signaling
- Aging → ↓ skin production, absorption, renal activation

### 3C. Immune Reset Goal
```
Th17 / Th1 excess  →  High-dose D3 saturates resistant receptors  →  ↑ Treg, ↓ IL-6 / TNF-α / IL-17  →  Immune tolerance
```

### 3D. Starting Dose Framework (individualized)
| Condition class | Typical start |
|---|---|
| Average autoimmune | ~**1,000 IU/kg/day** (e.g., 100 kg → 100,000 IU/day) |
| Protocol range cited | **40,000–300,000 IU/day** |
| Psoriasis / RA / lupus / IBS | **300–500 IU/kg/day** |
| Hashimoto’s | **150–300 IU/kg/day** |
| Vitiligo study (Cinnamoré 2013) | Fixed **35,000 IU/day × 6 months** |

Labs: baseline → monthly × 3–4 months → at least q3 months thereafter

## Segment 4 — PTH as the Therapeutic Compass (≈53:00–1:03:00)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield

### ★ Clinical Pearl
> **25-OH-D always rises with supplementation** — even if receptors are resistant. **PTH suppression** is the biomarker that vitamin D is actually binding receptors and exerting biological effect.

### Targets
- LabCorp PTH reference often **15–65 pg/mL**
- Therapeutic PTH: **lower third of reference range** — Clearfield cites ~**10–13 pg/mL** sweet spot; Q&A also frames **~10–30** as acceptable lower-third window
- Aim: **≥30–40% PTH drop from baseline**, but **keep PTH detectable** (do not drive to zero → bone/renal risk)
- Expect 25-OH-D often **150–200+ ng/mL** (sometimes 200–400) on protocol — do not dose to “normal” 30–100 or clinic optimal 50–80

### Evidence Snapshot (Lemke MS cohort)
- ~41 MS patients; ~35,000 IU/day
- PTH fell from ~25–55 into ~**15–20** range and plateaued over a year

### Lab Caveat
- Quest vs LabCorp reference ranges differ (example: TPO Ab cutoffs) — always interpret against the reporting lab’s range

## Segment 5 — Diet, Hydration, Cofactors & Monitoring (≈1:03:00–1:22:00)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield

### 5A. Low-Calcium Diet (<500 mg/day) — No Cheat Days
**Avoid:** dairy, calcium-fortified plant milks/juices/cereals, nuts/seeds (almonds, cashews, pistachios, sesame/tahini, chia, flax), high-Ca greens, canned fish with bones, hidden dairy in soups/dressings/protein bars/creamers, Ca carbonate antacids

**Prefer:** grilled meats, fish, rice/pasta/potatoes, non-creamy vegetables, olive/avocado/coconut oil, limited butter, fruits/veg with attention to Ca content; keep a food diary

### 5B. Hydration
- **≥2.5 L/day** low-calcium fluids — mandatory to flush urinary Ca, prevent nephrocalcinosis / stones
- Citrate support (lemon water or potassium citrate) may bind urinary Ca — avoid K-citrate in renal failure / hyperkalemia

### 5C. Mandatory / Key Cofactors
| Cofactor | Dose / note |
|---|---|
| **Magnesium** (glycinate; taurate if sleep issues) | ~200 mg BID; avoid citrate (GI); **RBC Mg**, not serum |
| **Riboflavin (B2)** | 50–200 mg/day — energy, immune, ↓ stone risk |
| Omega-3 | 2–4 g/day × ≥6 months |
| Vitamin K2 | 100–400 mcg/day — directs Ca to bone; caution with warfarin; **not** a substitute for Ca restriction |
| Methylated B vitamins | Homocysteine / MTHFR support; avoid chronic very high B6 |
| Selenium | 200 mcg/day × ~4 months — may ↓ thyroid Abs ~40%; caution toxicity at higher doses |
| Zinc | 15–30 mg; watch copper depletion |
| Iron (if deficient) | Bisglycinate ~25–65 mg elemental after confirming deficiency |
| Vitamin A | Mentioned cautiously (fat-soluble); separate URI short protocol tip given |

**Jeff Bowles note (Q&A):** some high-D protocols push **1,000–1,500 mg elemental Mg** plus boron + B5

### 5D. Monitoring Panel
- **Calcium homeo:** total Ca, ionized Ca, phosphate, PTH
- **Renal:** creatinine, BUN, cystatin C; **24-h urinary Ca** (most sensitive pause/reduce trigger)
- **Imaging:** DEXA; annual kidney US if long-term
- **24-h urine Ca rule:**
  - **<250 mg/L** → may continue
  - **>250 mg/L** → **stop D × 3 days**, restart at **half dose**, maintain fluids

### 5E. Exercise Is Prescribed
- Weight-bearing / aerobic exercise counters bone loss from Ca restriction + high D bone turnover — not optional

### 5F. Research Highlights
- **Cinnamoré 2013:** 35,000 IU × 6 months in vitiligo/psoriasis — no toxicity under protocol; **>80%** significant improvement reported
- Larger observational series (~319 autoimmune pts; mean ~35,291 IU; MS subset mean ~53,000 IU): Ca, Cr, eGFR, 24-h urine Ca stayed within reference with strict diet/hydration
- Genetic subgroup (~130): ~**75%** carried multiple VDR-related mutations
- MS Society does **not** endorse Coimbra dosing

### 5G. What Can Go Wrong
1. **Nephrocalcinosis** — often irreversible structural/functional kidney damage  
2. **Nephrolithiasis** — obstruction, infection, surgery  
3. **Cardiac** — shortened QT, AFib/flutter, arrest (Tom’s terminal pathway)  
4. Case: MS patient on 100,000 IU/day with hypercalcemia + renal insufficiency — found to have **MEN1**-related PTH elevation pre-protocol → baseline screening / genetics matter  
5. Partial adherence (skipping Mg/cofactors, cheating on dairy) drives toxicity

### Unknowns
- No long-term powered RCTs; bone density with chronic Ca restriction; CV/cancer outcomes; remission durability; safety beyond 3–5 years
- Coimbra anecdotal: benefits may persist ~**1.5–2 years** after achieving therapeutic levels (uncertain)

## Segment 6 — Protocol Summary & Positioning (≈1:22:00–1:29:00)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield

### Protocol Checklist
1. Confirmed autoimmune disease with inadequate response / intolerance to standard care  
2. Physician-supervised; **never DIY**; do **not** stop DMARDs / MS agents without a plan  
3. Start ~1,000 IU/kg/day (or disease-specific lower start); titrate to PTH lower-third  
4. Fluids ≥2.5 L + Ca <500 mg/day + Mg + B2 + other cofactors  
5. Serial labs; pause/halve on high urinary Ca  
6. Adjunct option — not a replacement for evidence-based therapy  

### Eligibility Mindset
- Not for mild asymptomatic Hashimoto’s (e.g., TPO 15, few symptoms)
- Candidates: severe MS, refractory high-antibody thyroid disease, committed patients who will follow diet/fluids/labs

## Segment 7 — Q&A: Dosing, Safety, Alternatives (≈1:29:00–end)

**Speakers:** Clearfield, Nario, Vargas, Serna, others

### Clinical Q&A Pearls
- **VDR SNPs:** Yes — protocol conceptually targets receptor resistance / need for high doses
- **How to know dose is right:** Serial PTH q4 weeks from a fixed start (e.g., 35,000) until lower-third (~10–30); never to zero
- **Vitiligo start (conservative):** 35–40,000 IU/day, adjust by PTH; study alternative = 1,000 IU/kg/day
- **Routine 5–10k IU** improves D status but does **not** reverse autoimmune disease the way protocol doses aim to
- **CYP inducer caution (Nario tip):** high D in polypharmacy autoimmune patients (e.g., lipids meds)
- **Sri Lanka / Vargas:** acupuncture + homeopathy → near-total vitiligo reversal in ~6 months in some UN-program cases (no high-dose D)
- **Acupuncture points as injection portals:** Clearfield — less product (Botox/fillers/joints), fewer complications; Dr. Tan’s “magical points” referenced
- **Boron:** Flechas / BoroTabs discussion (osteopenia); Clearfield uses boron to ↓ SHBG ~50–60% over 3–5 months; Life Extension / Hakala sources mentioned
- Prefer **daily D3**, not D2 or weekly megadoses
- Patient selection / compliance: long-suffering patients may engage better; non-adherent patients are liability risks on this protocol
- K2 important to divert Ca from soft tissue — but does not replace Ca restriction
