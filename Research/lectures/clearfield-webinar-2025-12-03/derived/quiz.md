### Q5. Why is PTH preferred over 25-OH-D as the dosing compass?
**Answer:** 25-OH-D rises with supplementation even if receptors are resistant (false sense of adequacy). PTH suppression signals that active vitamin D is binding receptors and exerting biological effect — similar to preferring RBC magnesium over serum magnesium.

- D) Driven as high as possible without lab monitoring

### Q7. Which environmental factors does Clearfield list as contributors to vitamin D resistance?
**Answer (any 4):** EBV, Lyme/Borrelia, CMV, herpes, hepatitis, Yersinia, mold/mycotoxins, high cortisol, aging.

## Section C — Safety & Monitoring (4 questions)

### Q15. What 24-hour urinary calcium threshold triggers holding therapy?
**Answer:** If 24-h urinary calcium is **>250 mg/L**, stop vitamin D for **3 days**, then restart at **half the prior dose** while maintaining fluids. Levels **<250 mg/L** generally allow continuation.

### Q16. Which monitoring set is appropriate for calcium homeostasis on protocol?

- B) **Total calcium, ionized calcium, phosphate, and PTH** ✓

- D) TSH only

## Section D — Clinical Integration (2 questions)

### Q19. A 70 kg patient with vitiligo asks for “the study dose.” What are two evidence-based starts discussed, and how do you titrate?
**Answer:**
1. Fixed **35,000 IU/day × 6 months** (Cinnamoré vitiligo/psoriasis study; >80% significant improvement reported under strict monitoring)
2. Alternatively **~1,000 IU/kg/day** (higher)
3. Clearfield’s conservative preference: start **35–40,000 IU/day**, check **PTH every 4 weeks**, increase until PTH is in the lower third (~10–30 / ~10–13 sweet spot), never to zero
4. Enforce fluids ≥2.5 L, Ca <500 mg/day, Mg + B2, serial Ca/renal labs

### Q20. A patient with mild Hashimoto’s (TPO 15, minimal symptoms) vs a patient with severe refractory MS both ask about Coimbra. How do you triage?
**Answer:**
1. Mild/low-antibody, minimally symptomatic Hashimoto’s — **not** a typical Coimbra candidate; continue foundational care (gluten avoidance, nutrient repletion, treat comorbidities)
2. Severe MS / refractory autoimmune disease with commitment to diet, fluids, cofactors, and frequent labs — possible **adjunct** under informed consent
3. Do **not** stop disease-modifying drugs without a plan; never DIY OTC mega-dosing
4. Screen baseline Ca/PTH/renal (± genetics if indicated); discuss irreversible nephrocalcinosis risk
5. Consider complementary modalities discussed (acupuncture/homeopathy for vitiligo in Vargas’ experience) when high-dose D is declined or inappropriate
