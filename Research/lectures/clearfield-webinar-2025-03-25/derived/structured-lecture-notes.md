## Segment 1 — Opening Fragments: Testosterone & Free Androgen Index (00:00–04:30)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield (Speaker 1)

### Key Points
- **Male total testosterone:** optimal ~700–900 ng/dL; free ~2–4%
- **Female total testosterone:** lab normal 2–45 ng/dL; symptoms of deficiency often appear around 10; excess symptoms often >40; practical optimal ~30–40
- **SHBG:** transport protein for testosterone (nmol/L); lab-calculated free T often inaccurate
- **Free androgen index (FAI):** total T ÷ (SHBG × 0.288)
  - Males: normal ~30–130; optimal ~53–108
  - Females: normal ~0.4–8.4; practical free % target ~2–4%
- **Boron:** mineral used to lower SHBG

### Clinical Relevance
- Prefer FAI / free % over sole reliance on lab-calculated free testosterone
- Female T interpretation is symptom-driven within a narrow window

## Segment 3 — Laboratory of Functional Medicine: Serum Markers & CBC Pearls (14:00–23:00)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield

### ★ Core Frame
> Eleven-test functional lab overview (Part 1). Organic acids (OAT) and DUTCH deferred to a later part.

### 3A. Vascular / Inflammatory Serum Markers
| Marker | Clinical Meaning | Interventions Mentioned |
|---|---|---|
| **Lp-PLA2** | Plaque vulnerability | Niacin, omega-3s, statins, fibrates |
| **Myeloperoxidase (MPO)** | Plaque rupture risk (worse signal) | Quercetin, pomegranate seeds (also raise T), curcumin, aged garlic |
| **LDH** | Tissue damage; formerly on CMP | Isoenzymes 1–5 localize organ injury |
| **hsCRP** | Vascular inflammation | Goal **<1**; omega-3 2–4 g/day + curcumin |

**LDH isoenzymes (quick map):**
1–2: cardiac, renal, RBC  
3: lung / internal organs  
4: WBC, lymph nodes, pancreas, kidney  
5: liver, skeletal muscle  

**Disease-load triad (sed rate + CRP + LDH):**
- All three rising → increasing disease load / systemic inflammation
- ↑ CRP + ↑ sed rate + normal LDH → inflammation/infection without tissue damage
- ↑ CRP + ↑ LDH + normal sed rate → tissue damage with cell lysis

### 3B. CBC Pearls Often Ignored
| Finding | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| High MCH | B12 deficiency / macrocytic anemia |
| Low MCH | Leaky gut; ↓ absorption B6, Zn, Mg (± Cu) |
| High MCHC | Methylation defect |
| Low MCHC | Hypochromic anemia, leaky gut, spherocytosis |
| High RDW | Oxidative stress, anemia, thalassemia, chronic disease |
| Low RBC + Hct + Hb + platelets | Bone marrow suppression / nutrient deficiency |
| Neutrophil:lymphocyte ≈ 2:1 | Desired; higher → inflammation; ~1:1 → immune suppression / allergy |
| High monos | Viral infection, poor NK function, autoimmunity |
| High EOS | Allergy, infection, cancer, parasites, alcohol |
| High basophils | Mast cell / histamine (allergy, parasites, venom, hyperthyroid) |
| **High basophils + monos + EOS** | **Parasitic gut infection** (clinical tip) |

## Segment 5 — Advanced GI Testing: Stool, SIBO, Mucosal Barrier (26:00–41:00)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield

### 5A. Comprehensive Stool (qPCR DNA panel)
- Pathogens: *C. difficile* A/B, *Yersinia*, *H. pylori* (+ virulence factors), opportunistic/dysbiotic organisms, Candida, parasites/worms
- Commensals: watch **Akkermansia** (low = concern); Firmicutes:Bacteroides ratio (↑ → weight gain/obesity; ↓ → insulin resistance / inflammation)
- Biomarkers & treatments:

| Biomarker | High / Low Meaning | Interventions Mentioned |
|---|---|---|
| Steatocrit / elastase | Fat maldigestion / pancreatic insufficiency | Pancreatic enzymes ± bile acids |
| β-glucuronidase | Dysbiosis; estrogen recirculation | High fiber, **calcium D-glucarate**, pre/pro/postbiotics |
| Secretory IgA | High: infection/dysbiosis/food sensitivity; Low: stress/immune compromise | Colostrum when abnormal |
| Anti-gliadin IgA | Gluten immune response | Remove gluten |
| Eosinophil activation protein | Allergy / infection | Elimination diet, low-histamine foods, treat parasites, DAO support |
| Calprotectin | IBD gold-standard marker (± IBS) | Gut repair: L-glutamine, Zn carnosine, DGL (watch HTN), biotics, elimination diet |
| Zonulin (>50) | Intestinal permeability | 4 Rs: remove, replace, repair, regenerate; anti-inflammatory diet |

- **H. pylori resistance genes:** positives = antibiotics that will *not* work (e.g., amox/clarithromycin resistance → choose negatives)

### 5B. SIBO Breath Test (lactulose)
- **Hydrogen:** ≥20 ppm rise over 90 min = positive
- **Methane:** ≥10 ppm at any point = positive
- Rx path: hydrogen → rifaximin; methane → rifaximin + metronidazole (10–14 days)
- Non-Rx path (longer, ≥6 months): caprylic acid, oil of oregano, peppermint, green tea, grapefruit seed extract, pre/probiotics, **low-FODMAP**

### 5C. Mucosal Barrier Assessment
Four pillars: **zonulin**, **DAO**, **histamine**, **LPS** (+ DAO:histamine ratio)
- High zonulin → bacteria/yeast/gluten / leaky gut
- Low DAO → atrophied microvilli / permeability / cell dysfunction
- High histamine → MCAS / degranulation → DAO enzymes + mast-cell stabilizers
- High LPS → bacterial overload / neuroinflammation → antimicrobials (berberine, oregano, garlic) + gut repair
- Low LPS → immune deficiency → immunoglobulins + vitamins A & D

**Indications:** autoimmunity, food sensitivities, headaches, arrhythmias, digestive sx, depression/anxiety, any suspected leaky gut

## Segment 7 — IgG Food Sensitivity Testing (44:00–50:00)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield

### Key Concepts
- Targets **chronic disease burden**, not IgE anaphylaxis (peanut / stop-breathing allergy)
- Delayed symptoms (1–3 days) → patients rarely connect Saturday food to Tuesday symptoms
- Limitations: company variability, false positives, critics question evidence base — interpret with clinical history; watch cross-reactivity
- Females may show more IgG reactivity than males
- Evidence signals cited: IBS improvement, migraine reduction, IBD associations, cognition (gut–brain)

### Elimination Protocol
1. Eliminate top 1–2 daily offenders ≥3 weeks (prefer **6 weeks**) — IgG half-life ~23 days
2. Reintroduce slowly one at a time
3. If symptoms return → remove another 6 weeks
4. Full mapping can take ~6 months; often hand off to dietitian

## Segment 8 — Heavy Metals, Environmental Toxins & Mold (50:00–1:01:00)

**Speaker:** Bill Clearfield

### Exposure Symptom Clusters
- **Acute:** nasal/sinus, eye irritation, sore throat, cough/wheeze, rash, SOB
- **Chronic:** allergy, fatigue, headaches, brain fog, GI, memory/mood/personality change, immune weakness, reproductive/organ toxicity

### Testing Ladder
1. **Hair metals** (~$65) — inexpensive screen; mercury (fillings/sushi), titanium (joint replacements), uranium (regional), mineral pattern (e.g., low Mo)
2. Escalate to **urine / blood** metals if hair positive
3. **Mold:** remove patient from exposure first; DIY air kits; professional air + swab; urine mycotoxins (recent 1–3 day exposure; no FDA-approved human urine assay) vs **serum IgG/IgE** (Campbell camp — chronic/repeated exposure; now ~14 molds; IgE → MCAS)

### Detox Foundations
- Liver: silymarin/milk thistle, NAC, I3C
- Daily BMs, fiber, phytonutrients, ↓ processed foods / chemical exposure
- **HEPA filters** for heavy-metal patients
- Physical activity
- Supplements: curcumin, **ALA ≥300 mg BID** (neuropathy), pectin/PectaClear, flaxseed, rice bran fiber, quercetin
- **Trick:** Epsom salt baths 1–2×/week often restore molybdenum in 3–5 weeks (MTHFR cofactor)

### Clinical Anecdotes
- Builder eating sushi 4–5×/week → high Hg with cognitive depression / recurrent infections
- Historical thallium poisoning case (early 1990s) — lifelong caution when thallium appears on urine
