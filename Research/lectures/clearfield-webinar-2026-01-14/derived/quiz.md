## Section A — Humanin & Mitochondrial Peptides (4 questions)

### Q1. Humanin is best described as:
- A) A nuclear-encoded 24-aa peptide sold as an FDA-approved supplement
- B) **A mitochondrially encoded 24-aa peptide; biomarker/mechanism signal, not an approved therapy** ✓
- C) Synonymous with MOTS-c
- D) A CB1 partial agonist from Cannabis sativa

> *Nario: not a supplement/FDA-approved; patients confuse it with MOTS-c.*

### Q2. Which triad of actions did Nario emphasize for humanin?
**Answer:** (1) Prevent stress-induced mitochondrial apoptosis; (2) improve metabolic resilience (insulin/glucose); (3) reduce oxidative/inflammatory stress — protecting neurons, cardiomyocytes, endothelium.

### Q3. Which lifestyle stack was presented as the practical way to support endogenous humanin?
- A) Nightly humanin SQ injections only
- B) **Exercise (endurance + resistance), caloric restriction/fasting, NAD/phospholipid mitochondrial support, sleep/circadian hygiene** ✓
- C) High-dose niacin + statin
- D) Amyloid monoclonal antibodies

### Q4. True or False: Nario framed humanin as blocking p53 and promoting cellular proliferation.
**Answer:** **False.** He emphasized it does *not* shut down p53 or promote proliferation, and does not block immune-mediated apoptosis — selective for mitochondrial stress–induced apoptosis.

## Section B — ECS, Older Adults & Drug Substitution (5 questions)

### Q5. In the Israel older-adult observational cohort (~119 patients), what fraction stopped at least one medication after adding medical cannabis?
- A) 20%
- B) 36%
- C) **53%** ✓
- D) 75%

> *Only 20% added a new medication; opioids were the largest stop category (28 patients).*

### Q6. Why does Sulak argue THC is unlikely to cause opioid/benzo-like respiratory depression?
**Answer:** CB1 receptors are dense in the CNS but sparse/absent in brainstem cardio-respiratory centers where opioids/benzos suppress breathing.

### Q7. Match the product term:
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Full spectrum | Multiple cannabinoids **including** residual THC |
| Broad spectrum | Extract with **THC removed** |
| Isolate | Single purified compound(s) |

### Q8. THC’s relationship to anandamide at CB1/CB2 is closest to:
- A) Full inverse agonist
- B) **Partial agonist (pharmacologically similar)** ✓
- C) Pure antagonist
- D) Irreversible covalent binder

### Q9. Cannabis drug substitution in older adults disproportionately replaces drugs on which list?
**Answer:** The **Beers Criteria** (potentially inappropriate medications in older adults) — opioids, anxiolytics, some antidepressants, NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, antipsychotics.

### Q10. Approximately what percentage of people with dementia develop behavioral/psychological symptoms (agitation, exit-seeking, aggression, etc.) at some point?

### Q11. What is currently the only FDA-approved drug specifically for dementia-related agitation discussed, and its major boxed warning?
**Answer:** **Brexpiprazole (Rexulti)** — black-box warning for increased mortality in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis (and suicidality language shared with antipsychotics).

### Q15. Geneva observational data suggested that around what total daily cannabinoid exposure in severe elderly dementia was unlikely to produce clinically relevant CYP interactions in their cocktail study?

- C) ≤200 mg

### Q16. What is Sulak’s conservative “night priming” strategy to reduce THC adverse effects?
**Answer:** Give a **subtherapeutic night dose** (e.g., half gummy) for ~**1 week** (or even 2–3 nights) before daytime titration — adverse-effect tolerance builds faster than therapeutic tolerance.

### Q17. Typical THC total daily range Sulak oriented to for dementia agitation protocols (gummy stages) is:
- A) 50–100 mg
- B) **~6–15 mg/day (with some patients needing 20–25; neuroprotection cases may start ~0.5 mg)** ✓
- C) Exactly 40 mg fixed
- D) Only CBD isolate ≥700 mg

### Q18. CBG’s distinctive CNS mechanism highlighted for sleep/agitation support was:
- A) Full CB1 agonism only
- B) **α2-adrenoceptor agonism (clonidine/guanfacine-like sympathetic dampening)** ✓
- C) MAO inhibition
- D) NMDA open-channel block like memantine

### Q19. For a mildly symptomatic 101-year-old seeking possible neuroprotection (not agitation), Sulak’s starting suggestion was:
**Answer:** ~**0.5 mg THC once daily** — e.g., a steeped cup of **cannabis tea** (hot water typically yields ~0.5 mg THC; more acidic cannabinoids also extract).

### Q20. Sulak’s dosing motto for cannabis (including dementia) is:

- D) CBD-only forever; never use THC in elderly
