## Segment 2 — Tip of the Week: Humanin — Mitochondrial Survival Peptide (11:52–16:32)

**Speaker:** Melvin Nario, DO (Speaker 3)

### Core Framing
> Humanin is **not a therapy yet** — it is a **biomarker, mechanism, and longevity signal**. Patients already ask about injectable “research peptides”; clinicians need literacy without endorsement.

### Biology
- **24–amino-acid peptide** encoded by **mitochondrial DNA** (not nuclear) — distinct from brother peptide **MOTS-c** (ASR: “mod c”)
- Discovered **2001** in Alzheimer’s / glial-stress research
- Metaphor: if apoptosis is the demolition crew, humanin is engineering saying “damaged, not condemned”

### Three Primary Actions (as presented)
1. Prevents **stress-induced mitochondrial apoptosis** (not physiologic apoptosis)
2. Improves **metabolic resilience** (insulin sensitivity, glucose handling)
3. Reduces **oxidative and inflammatory stress**

### Clinical Relevance
Protects cells that “should not be dying”: **neurons, cardiomyocytes, endothelium**

### Human Data Cited
| Citation (as presented) | Signal |
|---|---|
| Conte et al., 2020 | Circulating humanin declines with age; correlates with AD, CVD, T2DM |
| Yen / Yin et al., 2018 | Mitochondrial SNP → lower humanin → accelerated cognitive aging (US cohorts) |
| Yen et al., 2020 | Children of centenarians → higher circulating humanin vs age-matched controls |

### Safety Framing for Peptide Anxiety
- Does **not** block immune-mediated apoptosis
- Does **not** shut down p53
- Does **not** promote proliferation (cancer scare context)
- Selectively blocks **mitochondrial stress–induced** apoptosis; some data suggest tissue protection under stress

### How to Support Endogenous Humanin (no injection required)
- Endurance + resistance training
- Caloric restriction / fasting
- Mitochondrial support strategies (NAD, phospholipids cited)
- Sleep + circadian rhythm

### Clinical Pearl
Patients often confuse humanin with **MOTS-c**; both are mitochondrial peptides but are not interchangeable. Exercise remains the most accessible “humanin protocol.”

## Segment 4 — Cannabis in Older Adults & Drug Substitution (21:42–36:21)

**Speaker:** Dustin Sulak

### Epidemiology
- Past-year cannabis use in older adults climbing (≈0.5% → 2% by 2015; then ≈2.5% → >4% by 2018)
- UCLA geriatrics survey (2020): **15%** cannabis users; **61%** first use after age 60; **75%** rated somewhat/extremely helpful

### Quality-of-Life Paradox
Cannabis may improve HRQoL / “feeling like myself” even when pain scales change only modestly — qualitative theme: *“cannabis gave me my life back.”*

### Israel Observational Study (~119 older adults, 6 months)
- Did **not** decrease ADLs; improved IADLs (4.1 → 4.7), Geriatric Depression Scale (6.5 → 5), pain VAS (8.8 → 5.5)
- Common AEs: dizziness, dry mouth, psychoactive sensation, fatigue
- **53% stopped ≥1 medication**; only 20% added a new med
  - Opioids stopped in 28 patients (major substitution signal)
  - Also ↓ benzos, neuropathic agents, antidepressants, antihypertensives

### Broader Israel Survey (~900 patients, 2018)
- Pain and QoL shift favorable; **14.4%** stopped opioids; **3.7%** reduced opioid dose; **7.5%** stopped benzos

### Beers Criteria Framing
Drug-substitution list overlaps **Beers Criteria** (potentially inappropriate in older adults): opioids, anxiolytics, some antidepressants, NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, antipsychotics.

### Why One Plant Replaces Many Classes
**Endocannabinoid system (ECS):**
- Dense **CB1** in CNS (notably **not** brainstem cardio-respiratory centers → no opioid/benzo-like respiratory depression)
- **CB2** in immune / connective tissue
- Body-wide homeostatic negative-feedback system (autocrine/paracrine)
- **THC ≈ partial agonist** at CB1/CB2, pharmacologically similar to **anandamide**
- Overuse can **dysregulate** ECS — respect titration

### Product Definitions (clarified live)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Full spectrum** | Multiple cannabinoids **including** residual THC |
| **Broad spectrum** | Full extract with **THC removed** |
| **Isolate / distillate** | Single compounds reconstituted |

Israel older-adult datasets: earlier study more THC-dominant; later more CBD / ~1:1-ish full-spectrum — **not** broad-spectrum.

## Segment 5 — Dementia Burden, Failed Targets, Antipsychotic Harm (36:21–48:00)

**Speaker:** Dustin Sulak

### Scale of the Problem
- ~**6.5–7 million** older Americans with Alzheimer’s (2022→present framing)
- Prevalence 6–19% among ≥65 (race/SES dependent); pathology precedes diagnosis by years–decades
- 2016 formal care cost ~$28K/patient → ~$196B/year (2016 dollars)

### Standard Cognitive Drugs
- Cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil) + NMDA antagonist (memantine): ~**6 months** cognitive benefit window; weaker in severe disease
- Multi-factorial disease (metabolic, inflammatory, infectious, neurotoxic) → single-target drugs limited

### Amyloid Antibody Critique (as presented)
- Image-integrity scandal around key amyloid–memory paper (Lesné / U Minnesota framing, Feb 2025 news cited)
- Meta of antibody trials: statistically tiny cognitive deltas; **not clinically meaningful** on CDR / ADAS-Cog at population level
- Safety: ARIA-edema RR ~**9.79**; hemorrhage risk elevated (~21% framing with RR ~1.21)
- Cost: **$25–35K/year** — “crazy” given falsified mechanistic foundation narrative
- **No trial showed halted decline or return to baseline cognition**

### Behavioral Symptoms (BPSD)
- ~**80%** of dementia patients develop agitation, exit-seeking, aggression, hypersexuality, night wandering, self-injury
- Gold standard: **non-pharmacologic** (music, aroma, light, massage, exercise, animal therapy) — cannabis should **enhance**, not replace
- Antipsychotics / benzos: behavioral control at high cost; antidepressants / anticonvulsants weak for BPSD

### Antipsychotic Outcomes Cited
- Canadian nursing-home cohort (~500K): **26%** off-label antipsychotics for BPSD; **68%** worse at follow-up; **27%** higher likelihood of worse behaviors vs non-users
- **Brexpiprazole (Rexulti)**: only FDA-approved drug for dementia-related agitation; ~$2,000/month cash / ~$21 Medicare; CMAI ↓ ~23–24 points / 12 weeks — but **black-box** ↑ mortality in elderly dementia-related psychosis

## Segment 6 — Preclinical Cannabinoid Neuroprotection (48:00–54:12)

**Speaker:** Dustin Sulak

### Preclinical Themes (cell / animal / C. elegans)
- Neuroprotection ↓ cell death; ↑ acetylcholine; ↓ amyloidogenesis; ↓ oxidative stress / neuroinflammation / Aβ-evoked apoptosis
- Regulate NMDA; stimulate neural progenitor proliferation / neurogenesis
- Animal reviews: improved memory/learning, ↓ anxiety-like behavior, ↓ Aβ, enhanced motor function, autophagy/immune modulation

### Highlight Mouse Studies (THC alone)
1. **3 mg/kg/day × 28 days** in aged mice → maze performance ≈ **2-month-old** mice; ↑ dendritic spine density; gene-expression pattern shifted toward young brain
2. **Ultra-low 0.002 mg/kg single injection** in 24-month mice → cognitive improvement lasting **~7 weeks**; MRI: better retention of brain volume/density vs vehicle

### Clinical Pearl
Preclinical doses that remodel cognition can be **extraordinarily low** — supports “start low” clinical philosophy.

## Segment 7 — Clinical Evidence: Cannabinoids for Dementia / Agitation (54:12–1:13:58)

**Speaker:** Dustin Sulak

### Evidence Base Size
~**26** observational reports / series / controlled trials (mostly small, often open-label)

### Key Studies Summarized

| Study | Design / dose | Outcomes (as presented) |
|---|---|---|
| Israel AD (n=11) | THC 2.5 mg BID → max 7.5 mg BID; 7/11 stayed lowest | NPI 44 → 12; minimal AEs |
| Geneva (n=10 women) | CBD:THC ≈2:1 on chocolate; avg ~9 mg THC + 18 mg CBD/day | NPI 71 → 38; CMAI 74 → 47; morphine/benzo/antipsychotic deprescribing; nurses: calmer, less rigidity, less screaming |
| Geneva follow-up (n=19) | Similar oil/jelly; up to ~13 mg THC | CMAI 85 → 53; challenges ↓; CYP1A2 & 2C19 slight ↓ (not clinically relevant per authors); no 3A4 change; many meds discontinued |
| Israel RCT 2022 (n=40 cannabis / 20 placebo) | CBD-dominant drops (~12 mg CBD + 0.5 mg THC/drop); avg end ~7.5 mg THC + ~175 mg CBD | AE rates ≈ placebo; sleep disturbance ↓; ≥4-pt CMAI: 60% vs 30%; ≥8-pt: 50% vs 15% |
| Italy 2023 (n=30, no psych meds yet) | THC-dominant galenical; 10 → 20 → 10 mg THC/day over 12 weeks | Agitation, NPI domains, CMAI improve; **MMSE orientation/registration** signal of cognitive improvement |
| Brazil case (75M Alzheimer) | **0.5–1 mg THC/day** | MMSE 21 → 25–27; sustained ~42 months; regained independent transit |
| Phase 2 RCT 2025 (n=14/arm) | **0.33 mg THC + 0.25 mg CBD** once daily in EVOO × 26 weeks | MMSE ↑ ~1.7 pts vs expected decline 1.5–2; clinically meaningful responders 64% vs 33%; **NNT ≈ 3** |

### Swiss PK Cocktail Takeaway
Around **≤40 mg total cannabinoids/day** in elderly polypharmacy dementia → clinically relevant CYP interactions unlikely in that cohort.

### Access Barrier
Schedule I + facility federal-funding fear → hard to deliver medical cannabis in nursing homes despite family motivation.

## Segment 8 — Practical Protocol: Hemp Gummies, Dosing, Nursing Support (1:13:58–1:30:29)

**Speaker:** Dustin Sulak (Healer program described without heavy branding)

### Why Gummies / Farm Bill 2018
- Hemp = **<0.3% THC by weight**, hemp-derived THC
- Product cited at **~0.1% THC by weight** — heavy matrix dilutes % while delivering clinical mg doses
- Two-stage blister protocol, **TID**; total THC range ~**6–15 mg/day** (matches many agitation studies)
- Cuttable / dissolvable for dysphagia / refusal; palatability critical

### Conservative Night-Priming Trick
> Half gummy **at night for ~1 week** (or even 2–3 nights) at subtherapeutic dose → adverse-effect tolerance builds faster than therapeutic tolerance → then titrate daytime doses.

### Real-World Feedback (~2,000 unique customers; 31 survey responses)
- Top indications: agitation, restlessness/irritability, sundowning, sleep, paranoia, hallucinations
- Onset: days–2 weeks for many; 1–2 months for those needing Stage 2
- ~Half “some improvement,” half “substantial”; few no-change / worsening
- Goals: reduce Seroquel / Ativan / Depakote / Abilify; ↓ caregiver burnout; keep patients at home
- Facility pushback surprisingly low when framed as OTC hemp with clinician order template

### Adverse Effects & Cannabinoid Roles
- Most common AE: **increased agitation from too much THC** (paradoxical)
- Sleep disturbance: sometimes flip **CBG ↔ CBD** (night vs day gummy)
- **CBG**: α2-adrenoceptor agonist (clonidine/guanfacine-like sympathetic dampening); helpful for sleep/anxiety; rare paradoxical reaction; little human BP effect
- Falls: 2009 study 1.5 mg THC → **no clinically significant gait change**; falls not a signal in dementia cannabis literature per Sulak
- THC daily range for dementia: ~**0.5–15 mg** (some need 20–25)
- CBD for agitation: less central unless high enough for residual THC or antipsychotic-range CBD (GI AEs; LFT monitoring at very high doses)

### Functional Recovery Anecdotes
- Patient outdoors daily after ~1.5 years on treatment (first outdoor trip in a year)
- Lewy body dementia patient playing piano again after ~2 years

### Regulatory Cliff (late 2025 framing)
- Appropriations / Rand Paul hemp redefinition: **≤0.4 mg THC per package** would wipe full-spectrum hemp
- Trump EO: cannabis Schedule I → **Schedule III**; CMS pilot up to **$500/patient/year** full-spectrum CBD reimbursement — contradictory signals
- Research options discussed: caregiver-app incentives vs Georgia memory-care **placebo run-in** trial

## Segment 9 — Q&A: Minor Cannabinoids, Dosing Philosophy, Special Cases (1:30:29–2:01:29)

**Speakers:** Sulak, Nario, Speaker 8, Doug (Speaker 9), Speaker 1 (Amen/SPECT), Cheryl (chat)

### Other Cannabinoids
- **CBDa / CBGa**: promising preclinical neuroprotection / anti-tau / NF-κB; CBDa more bioavailable/potent but spicy + poor BBB yet still active → hard to formulate in palatable gummies
- Future wish-product for **MCI / early cognition**: capsule with ultra-low THC + CBDa/CBGa (+ PEA mentioned as ECS-adjacent add)

### Routes
- Transdermal THC: theoretically viable (neuropathic-pain oil study rubbing away from feet); little dementia data; avoid first-pass
- Oral THC metabolites: 11-OH-THC slightly stronger/longer — not categorically superior to topical for dementia goals

### Dosing Motto
> **Start low, go slow, but don’t be afraid to go all the way.**  
Most: **1–5 mg THC per dose**; some thrive at **20 mg/dose**. Interindividual >> weight/age/sex. Titration may upregulate receptors/endogenous ligands; chronic overuse → CB1 downregulation.

### 101-Year-Old Mild Dementia (Cheryl)
- If goal = neuroprotection / cognition (not agitation): start **~0.5 mg THC once daily**
- Practical: steeped **cannabis tea** often delivers ~0.5 mg THC/cup (hot water solubilizes limited THC; more acidic cannabinoids)

### High-Dose CBD (700 mg)
- Broad neuroprotection (ischemic/chemical/mechanical/infectious/prion models) — mechanisms largely **outside** classic ECS (weak anandamide reuptake effect debated)
- At 700 mg: monitor **LFTs**; GI AEs; antipsychotic-comparable serotonin/dopamine receptor effects
- Often less CBD + a little THC goes further for many goals

### Cancer / Tippens Protocol Digression
- Anticancer cannabis literature often needs **gram-scale** cannabinoids; rare dramatic responses; dose cuts can allow recurrence (melanoma anecdote)
- Reliable cannabis role in oncology: **symptom control, QoL, chemo/radiation tolerability**, healthy-tissue protection — disease modification uncertain

### Osteopathy / Biodynamic Craniosacral
- On cannabis responders: greater system flexibility, softer end-range, deeper treatment tolerance (selection bias acknowledged)

### Amen SPECT vs Alcohol
- Cross-sectional SPECT confounders (sick users); Sulak: heavy cannabis misuse ≠ as bad as heavy alcohol; oral vs inhaled — oral for duration, inhaled for episodic/breakthrough
